# Cheatsheet — Duarte's Judgment Calls

## Thresholds & Defaults
| Decision | Duarte's number |
|---|---|
| Content vs. slot length | Plan for **60%** (40 min of a 60-min slot) |
| Audience patience ceiling | **30–40 min** before squirming |
| Executive summary slides | **~10%** of appendix length (50 appendix → 5 summary) |
| Executive rehearsal frame | Build for 30, rehearse as if cut to **5** |
| Slide comprehension | **3 seconds** (glance test) |
| Text field cost | Default text box ≈ **80 words** ≈ 20 sec of lost listening at 250 wpm |
| Typefaces per deck | **1**, at most 2 |
| Colors per deck | **3** complementary + 2 neutrals |
| Quote slide length | **≤ 30 words**, one quote at a time |
| Cutting an hour to 40 min | Cut **~1/3** of slides |
| Slide count, high-energy style | ~5/min (145 slides in 40 min); moderate = 1–2/min |
| Remote slide change interval | **~20 seconds** |
| Interpreter (any type) | Prepare **half** the material for the slot |
| Interpreter lead time | Notes sent **1 week** ahead; hiring up to **1 month** ahead |
| Tech setup minimum | **30 minutes**, walkthrough preferred |
| Calming breath cycle | **4** deep hold-and-slow-release breaths (<1 min) |

## Decision Rules
- **If it's a topic, not a sentence with POV + stakes** → you don't have a big idea; don't generate content yet.
- **If an idea doesn't support the big idea** → cut it, however interesting. If you don't filter, the audience will — and resent you.
- **If the audience is senior** → lead with conclusions and recommendations; a big reveal at the end will be interrupted away.
- **If the content is understandable without a presenter** → it's a document. Circulate it; don't project it.
- **If a slide takes more than 3 seconds** → it's a document, a build, or two slides.
- **If bullets on a slide don't need to be associated** → give each its own slide. Slides are free.
- **If bullets *do* feel related** → circle the verbs/nouns, name the relationship, replace with a diagram.
- **If the data has no z-axis** → no 3-D. Ever.
- **If one number carries the message** → show that number, huge, with no chart.
- **If you're deemphasizing chart context** → you must supply it out loud; don't redistribute that slide.
- **If you don't know a Q&A answer** → say so and commit to follow up. Never fake it; they see through it.
- **If a question seems trivial** → look for the question behind the question before dismissing it.
- **If the room's body language turns** → change course mid-talk; enduring it costs more than admitting it.
- **If the backchannel turns hostile** → engage and acknowledge; make people feel heard rather than ignoring or defending.
- **If you're tempted to flag your own sound bite for sharing** → don't. Overt flagging reduces sharing.
- **If your first visual metaphor arrives instantly** → discard it; it's everyone's first idea.
- **If you're running long** → drop to the false ending. That's why you planted it.

## Choosing the Vehicle
- Two direct reports, informal → conversation or whiteboard, no deck
- Venture capitalists, small room → formal stand-up presentation
- Analysts → a carefully written memo may outperform slides
- Young professionals / broad reach → video, streamed or posted
- Decision still open, want input → sketch live; a slick deck signals "final"
- Commencement, eulogy, wedding toast, layoff announcement → **no slides**
- Status update → distribute a document; don't hold a meeting to read it

## Analytical vs. Emotional Mix
| Topic | Audience | Lean |
|---|---|---|
| Layoffs, launches | Any | Emotional, but never sentimental |
| Science, engineering, finance | Specialists | Analytical + at least one human anchor |
| Cost cutting | Managers who must fire people | Emotional first, numbers second, or you read as inhumane |
| Cost cutting | Executives owning the bottom line | Numbers first — but acknowledge the human cost |

## Resistance Diagnosis
| Signal | Type | Response |
|---|---|---|
| Counterarguments, competing data, "have you seen X?" | Logical | Read the opposition; build a nuanced POV |
| Bias, dogma, moral objection, raw nerve | Emotional | Proceed carefully; explicitly honor the value at risk |
| "We can't afford / reach / staff that" | Practical | Name the sacrifice; visibly shoulder part of it |

## Tells & Smells
- Deck ends with a "why it matters" slide → self-focused structure; move it to the front.
- You can describe the talk as a noun phrase → no big idea.
- Middle of the talk is all one side of the contrast → tension collapsed; add toggles.
- Ending is a task list → the new bliss is missing.
- Slide won't fit on a sticky note → concept too complex for one slide.
- Eye doesn't know where to start on a slide → hierarchy failure, not content failure.
- Audience leaning back, arms crossed → resistance. Fidgeting, checking e-mail → boredom.
- No laughs, no nods, no notes across several minutes → you're not connecting; intervene.
- You can't find the verbal tic → you haven't watched yourself on video yet.
- Nobody asks a question → they've disengaged, or you never made room; either is a problem.
