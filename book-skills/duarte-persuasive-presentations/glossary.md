# Glossary

**Analytical/emotional balance** — the deliberate mix of logical and emotional content, set by topic and audience; no presentation should be devoid of emotion (Ch 3)

**Anti-cliché rule** — discard the first visual concept you think of, since it occurs to everyone else too (Ch 8)

**Appropriate drama** — matching a story's emotional scale to the stakes of the room, so you don't read as manipulative (Ch 6)

**Audience as hero / presenter as mentor** — the stance that the audience is the protagonist and the presenter is the Yoda-like mentor giving a gift, a tool, and a way to get unstuck (Ch 1)

**Backchannel** — the stream of online chatter before, during, and after a talk (Ch 12)

**Backchannel revolt** — coordinated online rejection of a speaker's message (Ch 12)

**Big Idea** — the one key message, stated as a complete sentence containing your point of view and what's at stake (Ch 2)

**Build** — animating a list so each item appears as you cover it, controlling pacing (Ch 8, Ch 9)

**Clean surface** — a blank slide with master prompts turned off, used as the starting point for design (Ch 8)

**Comfort monitor** — the presenter-facing teleprompter screen showing notes the audience never sees (Ch 7, Ch 10)

**Common ground tiers** — lots / moderate / minimal, describing how much overlap you share with an audience (Ch 1)

**Concrete comparison** — a simile or metaphor used as a sound-bite device, or a physical analogue used to convey magnitude (Ch 3, Ch 9)

**Consecutive interpretation** — the interpreter shares the stage and translates after each point (Ch 11)

**Controlling idea** — screenwriting's term for the big idea (Ch 2)

**Convergent thinking** — the elimination phase: sorting and filtering generated ideas against the big idea (Ch 3)

**Curator of content** — the presenter's role once the highest-leverage audience subgroup is chosen (Ch 1)

**Divergent thinking** — the generation phase, where new options emerge (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Dynamic opposites** — canonical contrast pairs (sacrifice/reward, stagnation/growth, budget/quality) used to build tension (Ch 2)

**Emotional texture** — emotion layered onto an otherwise analytical case, usually via story (Ch 3, Ch 6)

**Emotive anecdote** — a gripping personal story used as a S.T.A.R. moment (Ch 6)

**Executive summary slides** — front-loaded summary at roughly 10% of deck length, with the rest as appendix (Ch 1)

**False ending / real ending** — two rehearsed exit points allowing a graceful early close (Ch 7)

**Five arrangement principles** — flow, contrast, white space, hierarchy, unity (Ch 9)

**Five segmentation lenses** — politics, demographics, psychographics, firmographics, ethnographics (Ch 1)

**Four calls to action** — the audience acts as doers, suppliers, influencers, or innovators (Ch 2)

**Glance test** — the standard that any slide be comprehensible in three seconds (Ch 8)

**Grandmother test** — rework language until a non-expert would understand the central terms (Ch 3)

**Graphic recorder** — a practitioner who visually synthesizes a talk in real time on a large mural (Ch 7)

**Inventory of Personal Stories** — a catalog of remembered events and the emotions they summon, built from prompts and reused across talks (Ch 6)

**Mind mapping** — solo associative brainstorming producing a web of concepts and connections (Ch 2)

**New bliss** — the rewarded state the audience reaches by adopting your idea; the destination of the ending (Ch 4, Ch 5)

**Physical cues** — involuntary audience body language read as live feedback (Ch 12)

**Planted question** — a question a facilitator raises because the audience is too intimidated to ask it (Ch 11)

**Pre-selling** — meeting tough critics in advance so their objections don't surface as roadblocks (Ch 2)

**Pressure-testing** — handing your big idea to someone else to attack (Ch 2)

**Reciprocity** — the currency of social media; spreading others' ideas so yours will spread (Ch 12)

**Reexperiencing** — narrating a story from inside the moment rather than reporting it (Ch 11)

**Resistance types** — logical, emotional, practical (Ch 2)

**Resonance / resonant frequency** — the physics metaphor for tapping something already inside the audience so they move on their own (Ch 1)

**Rhythmic repetition** — a sound-bite device repeating a phrase across sentences or slides (Ch 3)

**Simultaneous interpretation** — booth-based real-time translation delivered to audience earphones (Ch 11)

**Slide cemetery** — a holding area at the end of the file for cut slides, available during Q&A (Ch 7)

**Slideument** — Garr Reynolds' term for a text-dense document masquerading as a slide deck (Ch 7)

**Slogan** — a concise, memorable statement engineered for pickup (Ch 3)

**Sound bite** — a crafted, repeatable phrase delivered without fanfare (Ch 3)

**S.T.A.R. moment** — Something They'll Always Remember: a climactic shocking statistic, evocative visual, memorable dramatization, or emotive anecdote (Ch 6)

**Subtext (in Q&A)** — the larger issue or unspoken motive behind a surface question (Ch 11)

**Toggling** — alternating between what is and what could be through the middle of a talk (Ch 4, Ch 5)

**Transformation arc** — the mapped move from where the audience starts to where you want it to end up (Ch 1)

**Verbal tic** — a filler word substituting for a pause (Ch 11)

**Visual taxonomy** — the five diagram relationship types: network, segment, join, flow, stack (Ch 9)

**Walk-in / walk-out slide** — the slide displayed as people arrive, and the one left up as they leave (Ch 8)

**What is / what could be** — the juxtaposition of status quo and better way that generates a presentation's conflict (Ch 4, Ch 5)

**Whispering interpretation** — the interpreter whispers audience comments to the presenter (Ch 11)

**White space** — open space around elements; raises perceived value and sharpens focus (Ch 9)
