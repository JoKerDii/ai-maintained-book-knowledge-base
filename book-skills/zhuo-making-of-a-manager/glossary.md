# Glossary — The Making of a Manager

**360-degree feedback** — Feedback aggregated from multiple colleagues for a more objective, complete view; gathered a few times a year (Ch 4).

**Absentee manager** — The over-delegating extreme: little support, no hard calls, lost credibility as "overhead" (Ch 9).

**Additive vs. multiplicative** — Doing the work yourself adds a fixed amount; improving purpose/people/process multiplies the team's output (Ch 1).

**Apprentice** — Transition path where your manager's growing team is split and you take a portion; the easiest, most-supported route (Ch 2).

**Behavioral feedback** — Feedback on themes/patterns across many tasks; personal, needs specific examples, given in person (Ch 4).

**Bench (great bench)** — A layer of leaders strong enough to run things without you; tested by the "extended vacation test" (Ch 7).

**Brilliant jerk / divider effect** — A talented person who belittles others and makes the whole team less effective (opposite of a multiplier) (Ch 3).

**Calibration** — Making your self-view (or your read of a report) match reality by seeking others' honest input (Ch 5).

**Compliment sandwich** — Burying criticism between praise; Zhuo rejects it as insincere and diluting (Ch 4).

**Concrete vision** — A bold, measurable, easily-repeated statement of the outcome (not the how); five people should repeat it identically (Ch 8).

**Context switching** — Rapidly shifting focus across many topics; a defining reality of managing large teams (Ch 9).

**Culture** — The norms and values that govern how things get done; revealed by what a team will give up for its values (Ch 10).

**Curse of knowledge** — Experts forget what it's like to see material fresh; fixed by sending materials in advance (Ch 6).

**Debrief (retrospective / postmortem)** — A blameless review of what went well/badly and what to change; learnings written down and shared (Ch 8).

**Delegation** — "The art of knowing when to dive in yourself and when to step back and entrust others" (Ch 9).

**Disagree and commit** — Backing a decision fully once made, even if you argued against it (Bezos) (Ch 6, Ch 8).

**Dream-manager questions** — First-1:1 questions a New Boss asks reports to learn their ideal manager (Ch 2).

**Dunning-Kruger effect** — Cognitive bias where the less-skilled overrate themselves; a reason calibration matters (Ch 5).

**Fixed vs. growth mindset** — Fixed: actions governed by fear of judgment; Growth: feedback is fuel and ability can improve with effort (Dweck) (Ch 5).

**Funnel (recruiting funnel)** — Viewing hiring as consistent conversion rates (emails → meetings → interviews → offers → accepts) (Ch 7).

**Imposter syndrome** — The universal feeling of being a fraud; hits managers hard because they're looked to for answers and constantly do new things (Ch 5).

**Managing is caring** — You can't fake respect; care means helping reports succeed and telling them the truth, unconditionally (Ch 3).

**Micromanager** — The over-involved delegation extreme: stifling, distrustful, drives talent away (Ch 9).

**Multiplier effect** — The leverage a manager creates by improving the team rather than personally producing (Ch 1).

**New Boss** — Transition path: hired to lead an established team; gets a "newbie window" and blank slate (Ch 2).

**Newbie card** — The ~3-month window where a New Boss is expected to ask everything; use it to ramp fast (Ch 2).

**Output vs. activity** — Measure results (orders won), not busyness (calls made) (Grove) (Ch 1).

**Parkinson's Law** — "Work expands to fill the time available"; beaten by smaller milestones (Ch 8).

**Pareto / 80/20 principle** — Most results come from a minority of causes; focus is identifying which (Ch 8).

**Pioneer** — Transition path: founding member now responsible for a growing team; most autonomy, least support (Ch 2).

**The Pit** — The low point where imposter syndrome and self-doubt peak; escaped with a six-tactic toolkit (Ch 5).

**Playbook** — A codified checklist for a repeated task (run a meeting, close a hire) others can execute (Ch 8).

**Portfolio approach** — Splitting the team's work across short/medium/long time horizons (thirds) (Ch 8).

**Purpose, People, Process** — The why/who/how; the three buckets every managerial task sorts into (Ch 1).

**Successor** — Transition path: taking over your departed manager's entire role (Ch 2).

**Survival mode** — When the org is "on fire," you abandon long-term investment and do whatever it takes now (team hierarchy of needs) (Ch 1).

**Task-specific feedback** — Same-day feedback on a concrete action; focused on the *what*, not the *who* — the easiest type to give (Ch 4).

**Trust test (three statements)** — Reports bring you their biggest challenges; you exchange critical feedback without it being personal; they'd gladly work for you again (Ch 3).

**Twice as good ("How can I be twice as good?")** — The frame for on-the-job self-improvement (Ch 5).

**Weak hire** — A unanimous but unenthusiastic "hire"; Zhuo says reject it — bet only on passionate advocacy (Ch 7).

**Would-I-rehire question** — "Assume the role were open — would I rehire this person or gamble on someone else?"; used for struggling reports/managers (Ch 3, Ch 9).
