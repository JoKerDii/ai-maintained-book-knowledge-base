# Cheatsheet — The Making of a Manager

Decision rules and heuristics for acting the way Zhuo would. Keep beside you.

## The one thing to never forget
**Your job = get better outcomes from a group of people working together.** If an action doesn't improve the team's outcome (via purpose, people, or process), it's probably not your job.

## Should I even be a manager? (decide before saying yes)
| If you… | Then… |
|---|---|
| Are more motivated by an *outcome* than by a *specific role/craft* | ✅ strong fit (covers most weaknesses) |
| Would say "No problem!" to 70% of your day in meetings | ✅ you get energy from people |
| Can stay steady through others' emotional crises | ✅ fit |
| Want the *title*, *freedom to call shots*, or feel *obligated* | ⚠️ wrong reasons — "I should/can" ≠ "I want to" |
| Have one craft you can't give up | ⚠️ consider the IC track — it's a transition, not a promotion |

## Diagnosing poor work (in order)
1. Are expectations aligned? (does "great" mean the same?) → if no, set them.
2. Is it motivation? (unclear picture / aspiration mismatch / no consequences) → address it.
3. Only then: is it a skills gap? → train or hire. (Skills gaps don't close in months.)

## The trust test — you're doing well if all three are true
1. Reports bring you their **biggest challenges** unprompted.
2. You **exchange critical feedback** without it being personal.
3. They'd **gladly work for you again.** (Unsure? It's probably a no.)
→ Multi-week "everything's fine"? **Prod further.** Aim for 1:1s that feel *a little awkward*.

## Feedback decision rules
- **Give more than you think** — reports' #1 ask is "more feedback"; aim ≥50% positive.
- **Before the work** → set expectations. **Same day** → task-specific. **Across patterns** → behavioral (in person, with examples). **Few times/yr** → 360.
- **Critical feedback** → direct + dispassionate: *"When I observed [X], I felt concerned because…"* No sandwich. No disguised "questions." Never when upset ("Let's talk later").
- **Bad-news decision** → state the decision first; don't fake a joint discussion.
- Feedback failed? Check in order: often enough? → heard? (make it safe) → actionable? (specific + what-success-looks-like + next steps).

## People moves — act fast
- ~80% of the time, pep talks for a fundamentally mismatched report fail. Drawn-out "help" is worse *for them*.
- Test: **"If they weren't already here, would I recommend another team hire them?"** / **"Would I rehire them if the role were open?"** If no → move them (better role) or let them go — respectfully, decisively, not up for debate.
- **Never tolerate a brilliant jerk** — the team is *better off* when they leave.

## Meetings — the ruthless-efficiency rules
| Question | Rule |
|---|---|
| Why meet? | Define the **great outcome** first, then pick ONE type: Decision / Info / Feedback / Ideas / Relationships |
| Who's invited? | Only those needed for that outcome + decision-maker + affected stakeholders. Cut the rest. |
| Prepared? | Send agenda/materials **the day before** (curse of knowledge) |
| Ideas? | Solo silent writing (Post-its) *then* discuss |
| Airtime? | Cover interruptions, open doors for the quiet, cap overtalkers |
| End? | Agreed next steps + recap (decision, owners, next check-in) |
| Do you need to be there? | If the right outcome happens without you → skip it, get notes. Cancel meetings good for no one. |

## Hiring rules of thumb
- Frame it as **building the future**, not filling holes. It's *your* job, not the recruiter's.
- Interviews predict poorly → de-bias: **past work + honest references + multiple independent interviewers + prepared identical questions.**
- **Reject weak hires** (unanimous but unenthusiastic) and **anyone toxic** (bad-mouths past employers, blames others, arrogant).
- Bet on a **passionate advocate**. **Hire people capable of more.** Prioritize **diversity**.
- Leadership hire? Go **slow**, do homework — bad ones are disproportionately disruptive.
- Recruiting is a **funnel** — effort reliably pays off. Ask: *"If hiring were the only thing that mattered, what would I do differently?"*

## Execution rules
- Vision must be **concrete** (5 people repeat it identically) — kill *help/improve/enhance*.
- **80/20**: rank every list; do #1 before #2; "if only one goal, which?"
- Every problem gets **one owner**; every task a **who + by when**.
- Break big goals into **milestones** (defeat Parkinson's Law).
- **Reversible decision?** Move at ~70% info (Bezos). Then **disagree and commit**.
- Prize **execution over strategy** — a fast runner beats a slow one who knows the path.

## Delegation dial (calibrate constantly)
```
Micromanager ————————————|———————————— Absentee
 (stifling, talent      SWEET SPOT      (no support,
  leaves)          dive in AND step      overhead)
                    back per person
```
- **Keep** only work at the intersection of *most important to org* AND *only you can do best*.
- **Delegate** anything a report can do as-well-or-better; giving big problems = a sign of trust (declare ownership publicly).
- Rule of thumb: **double your leadership capacity every year**; aim to put yourself out of a job.
- **People trump projects** — "Do we have the right people on the right problems?"

## Culture levers (all four, or it won't stick)
1. **Talk** about what matters — 10 times, 10 ways (repetition ≠ condescending).
2. **Walk the walk** — a say/do gap is the fastest trust-killer. (Don't state a value you won't live.)
3. **Align incentives** — watch for traps (individual-over-team, short-over-long, rewarding no-conflict, the squeaky wheel).
4. **Invent traditions** that make values concrete.
→ Real culture = **what you'll give up for your values**, not the slogan.

## Self-management defaults
- Everyone feels like an imposter — normal. Choose the **growth mindset** (feedback = fuel, not verdict).
- In the Pit: don't pay the "double tax," assume your story is irrational → seek the truth, keep a *Little Wins* journal, ask for help.
- Treat your manager as a **coach, not a judge**. Ask "how can I be **twice as good**?"
- Training worth it? Ask "**One year from now, will I be glad I did this?**"
