# Chapter 10: Nurturing Culture

## Core Idea
Culture is "the norms and values that govern how things get done" — and it exists whether or not you tend it. You shape it not with slogans but by **what you're willing to give up for your values**: talking about them relentlessly, walking the walk, aligning incentives, and inventing traditions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Culture = what you'll sacrifice for your values**: an org's real culture shows not in its website copy but in the trade-offs it accepts. ("Full ownership" sounds nice — but are you willing to tolerate the chaos of everyone doing what they think best, and accept blame for others' mistakes? "Nothing at Facebook Is Somebody Else's Problem.")
- **Know the kind of team you want to be**: an exercise in three parts — *Understand your current team* (3 adjectives, proudest moments, what you do better than most, what 5 random members would say you value, top complaints); *Understand your aspirations* (5 adjectives you'd want an observer to use — and their double-edged-sword pitfalls); *Understand the difference* (score 1–9, biggest gaps, obstacles, one-year picture).
- **Never stop talking about what's important**: assume a message must be heard **ten times, said ten different ways**, to stick. Repetition isn't condescending — it makes you a more authentic, inspiring leader (Sheryl Sandberg's "hard conversations" became company vernacular this way).
- **Always walk the walk**: people watch bosses for say-one-thing-do-another gaps — one of the fastest ways to lose trust. "If you're not willing to change your behavior for a stated value, don't bring it up in the first place."
- **Create the right incentives**: reward behavior that matches your values and hold people accountable when it doesn't. Even good intentions create bad incentives — dig into the gap between stated values and actual behavior.
- **Invent traditions that celebrate your values**: rituals create actions people bond around (hackathons made "Be Bold" and "Move Fast" concrete; Mark's Friday Q&A models "openness").

## Key Concepts
- **Subcultures can flourish** within a larger org (growth team = data-informed; infra = long-term; design = holistic) — but not ones that fight the parent culture (you can't be a siloed team inside a company that values openness).
- **Common incentive traps**: rewarding individual performance above all (undercut colleagues); short-term gains over long-term investment; lack of perceived conflict (people hide disagreements); the squeaky wheel (matching outside offers teaches everyone to interview elsewhere).
- **Recognize hard, value-aligned acts**: when someone passes up a lucrative-but-unethical deal, fires a toxic star, or openly admits a mistake — thank them and acknowledge it was hard.

## Mental Models
- Your team's culture is its personality — it exists by default; if you dislike the vibe, examine why and act.
- Beware simple incentive rules promising amazing results (lines-of-code, pay-by-the-word, "show three explorations") — they're rarely simple and leave collateral damage. Better: discuss *why* the value matters and let people apply judgment.
- Success is "the sum of the millions of actions taken by your team during small, quotidian moments," not a few sweeping decisions.

## Anti-patterns
- **Say/do gaps**: preaching frugality then buying a fancy couch; demanding punctuality while always late; championing diversity but promoting only clones.
- **Mandating a value structurally without living it**: the "show three explorations" rule produced deliberately-worse throwaway designs and busywork.
- **Punishing/dismissing conflict**: a manager who's "so happy everyone gets along" teaches the team to hide disagreements; resentment grows.

## Worked Example
**"Ask for feedback" — the value Julie wasn't living.** A bright, determined report asked how to accelerate her learning. Julie enthusiastically answered "Ask for feedback" and spent the 1:1 explaining how. Weeks passed with no change from the report. Then, in 360 reviews, that report told Julie directly: "You rarely ask me or others on the team for feedback, and I'd love to see you do more of it." Eye-opening — Julie preached feedback but didn't live it, and the report noticed. Chastened, she resolved to practice until it became habitual. Lesson: be the first to live any value you want the team to hold.

## Key Takeaways
1. Culture is defined by the trade-offs you'll accept for your values, not by slogans.
2. Deliberately map your team's current culture vs. your aspirations, and close the gaps.
3. Repeat what matters ten times, ten ways — it lands, and it inspires.
4. Walk the walk; a say/do gap is the fastest trust-killer.
5. Audit incentives for traps; reward value-aligned behavior and address violations.
6. Invent rituals that make abstract values concrete and shared.
