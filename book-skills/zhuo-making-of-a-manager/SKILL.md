## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The one-line definition (Ch 1).** A manager's job is to **get better outcomes from a group of people working together**. It's not about title or employment — the moment you stop trying to do something alone, you're managing. Your job is *not* to do the work yourself, even if you're best at it; that's *additive*. Improving the team is *multiplicative*. Judge a manager on two halves (Chris Cox): present **results** + the future-facing **strength and satisfaction of the team**.

**Purpose, People, Process (Ch 1).** Every managerial task sorts into the *why* (purpose — a shared, concrete picture of why the work matters), the *who* (people — right skills, motivation, trust), and the *how* (process — how the team decides and works together). Ask which lever most improves the outcome, and act there. Exception: in **survival mode** (org on fire), drop the long game and fight the fire.

**Great managers are made, not born — but you must *want* the day-to-day.** Management is a *transition*, not a promotion. Wanting the title, the money, or "the shots" are the wrong reasons; adaptability and liking people are the right traits.

**Trust is the foundation (Ch 3).** With a small team, people dominate. You have trust when: reports bring you their biggest challenges, you exchange critical feedback without it being personal, and they'd gladly work for you again. **Managing is caring** — you can't fake respect, and it must be *unconditional* (about the whole person, not their performance) or honesty dries up when things get hard. Run weekly 30-min **1:1s** focused on the report (not status); prepare questions; let them lead. Play to strengths — invest in rising stars, not just strugglers. **Make people moves quickly** and never tolerate a "brilliant jerk."

**Feedback (Ch 4).** Feedback = anything that inspires a helpful behavior change (praise counts; it can start *before* the work). Four types: set **expectations** up front → same-day **task-specific** → in-person **behavioral** → periodic **360**. "Every major disappointment is a failure to set expectations." Deliver critical feedback **directly and dispassionately** — no compliment sandwich, no disguised "questions," never while upset. It only counts if it's *specific*, *heard*, and *leads to action*. Aim ≥50% positive.

**Managing yourself (Ch 5).** Everyone feels like an **imposter** — normal. Get brutally honest about your strengths, weaknesses, and **triggers**, then **calibrate** against others. Choose the **growth mindset** (feedback is fuel, not a verdict). Have a "**Pit**" toolkit for peak self-doubt. Treat your manager as a **coach, not a judge**; ask "how can I be **twice as good**?"

**Meetings (Ch 6).** Don't ask "what's the purpose?" — ask "what's a **great outcome**?" Pick one of five types (Decision, Info, Feedback, Ideas, Relationships), invite only who's needed, send materials the day before (curse of knowledge), make it safe to contribute, and skip/cancel meetings that don't earn their cost.

**Hiring (Ch 7).** "**Hiring is not a problem to be solved but an opportunity to build the future.**" Interviews predict poorly, so de-bias with past work, honest references, multiple independent interviewers, and prepared questions. **Reject weak hires** (unanimous but unenthusiastic) and toxic candidates; bet on **passionate advocates**; prioritize **diversity**; **hire people capable of more**. It's *your* job, not the recruiter's.

**Making things happen (Ch 8).** Prize **execution over strategy**. Start with a **concrete vision** (measurable, repeatable — kill *help/improve/enhance*), **focus** (80/20 — "innovation is saying no to 1,000 things"), give every problem **one owner** and every task a **who + by-when**, break big goals into milestones (Parkinson's Law), and iterate via **debriefs** and **playbooks**.

**Scaling & delegation (Ch 9).** As the team doubles, the job shifts to vision, hiring leaders, and **delegation** — "knowing when to dive in and when to step back." Avoid the micromanager and absentee extremes. Giving big problems is a **sign of trust**. Keep only work at the intersection of *most-important-to-org* AND *only-you-can-do-best*; **put yourself out of a job**. Struggling manager? "Would I rehire them if the role were open?"

**Culture (Ch 10).** Culture = the norms/values that govern how work gets done — revealed by **what you'll give up for your values**. Shape it four ways: **talk** about what matters (10×, 10 ways), **walk the walk**, align **incentives**, and invent **traditions**.
