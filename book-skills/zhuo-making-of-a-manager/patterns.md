# Patterns & Techniques — The Making of a Manager

Concrete, reusable methods from the book. Each is a thing you can *do*.

## The Purpose/People/Process Audit
**When to use**: any time your team's outcomes are lagging and you're unsure where to intervene.
**How**: sort the problem into why (purpose), who (people), or how (process). Ask which lever most improves the collective outcome, and act there rather than on symptoms.
**Trade-offs**: in survival mode, skip this and fight the fire directly.

## Diagnosing Poor Work (skill vs. motivation)
**When to use**: a report's work isn't great.
**How**: run a series of conversations — (1) Are expectations aligned (does "great" mean the same to both)? (2) Is it motivation (unclear picture, aspiration mismatch, no consequences)? (3) Only then, is it a skills gap? Fix the earliest failing link.
**Trade-offs**: skills gaps rarely close in a few months; be honest about timelines.

## The Weekly 1:1
**When to use**: with every direct report, ≥30 min weekly.
**How**: focus on the report, not status. Prepare questions each morning. Use Identify → Understand → Support question banks; let the report lead. Never use it for status updates (use another channel).
**Trade-offs**: at ~8+ reports the math breaks — that's the signal to develop managers beneath you.

## Setting Expectations Up Front
**When to use**: before any project, promotion timeline, or launch commitment.
**How**: agree on what great/mediocre/bad looks like, offer starting advice and pitfalls, and state how you'll be involved. Readjust the instant reality shifts.
**Trade-offs**: none — "every major disappointment is a failure to set expectations."

## Delivering Critical Feedback
**When to use**: something isn't working and must be addressed.
**How**: deliver directly and dispassionately. Template: "When I [observed] your [output], I felt concerned because… I'd like to understand your perspective and resolve this." Be specific, clarify what success looks like, suggest next steps. Ask "Does this resonate? Why or why not?" No sandwich; don't disguise concerns as questions; don't engage while upset.
**Trade-offs**: for a *decision* (not feedback), state the decision first and don't open it to debate.

## The Four Feedback Types (cadence ladder)
**When to use**: building a complete feedback practice.
**How**: (1) expectations before work; (2) task-specific, same-day, ≥50% positive; (3) behavioral, in person with examples; (4) 360 a few times/year (ask: what should X do more of? what should X stop?).
**Trade-offs**: task-specific is easiest but insufficient alone — reports also want career/behavioral feedback.

## The Brutal-Honesty Self-Inventory
**When to use**: understanding how *you* manage (esp. new managers, or in the Pit).
**How**: list strengths (3-word descriptions from loved ones; proudest qualities; success traits; common praise) and weaknesses/triggers (inner-critic complaints; wished-for gifts; triggers; common improvement feedback). Then calibrate with your manager and 3–7 close colleagues.
**Trade-offs**: asking for feedback is uncomfortable; frame it as growth, not a worthiness test.

## Escaping the Pit (six tactics)
**When to use**: peak self-doubt/imposter syndrome.
**How**: (1) don't beat yourself up (no "double tax"); (2) assume your story is irrational — seek the truth; (3) visualize success/past wins; (4) ask people you can be real with; (5) keep a *Little Wins* journal; (6) set boundaries and self-care.

## The Five-Type Meeting Design
**When to use**: before scheduling any meeting.
**How**: define the *great outcome*, then pick one type — Decision / Sharing Information / Feedback / Generating Ideas / Strengthening Relationships — and design for it. Don't make one meeting do two jobs.
**Trade-offs**: decision meetings make poor idea-generation forums (and vice versa).

## Meeting Preparation & Follow-up Ritual
**When to use**: any meeting, especially recurring or large.
**How**: send agenda/materials the day before (beats curse of knowledge). End with agreed next steps; send a recap (decision, action items + owners, next check-in).

## The Calendar Audit
**When to use**: feeling overwhelmed/behind.
**How**: for one week, log every meeting — did you participate, were you critical, did you get value? Drop the "no"s; ask for pre/post notes instead. Cancel meetings valuable to no one.

## The Future Org Chart
**When to use**: every January (or planning cycle).
**How**: map expected headcount, level per role, needed skills/strengths, gaps, and diversity additions. Use it as the rubric that stops you from hiring the next available person.

## Smart-Bet Hiring
**When to use**: every hire (interviews are unreliable predictors).
**How**: examine past work (portfolios/teach-a-class); get honest references; use multiple interviewers who record rationale independently before debrief; prepare identical questions; reject weak hires and toxic candidates; bet on passionate advocacy; hire people capable of more; prioritize diversity.
**Trade-offs**: leadership hires are disproportionately disruptive — slow down and do homework.

## Concrete Vision Test
**When to use**: writing any team goal or roadmap.
**How**: strip squishy words (help/improve/enhance); make it bold, measurable, repeatable. Ask five people who heard it to repeat it — they should match.

## Break-It-Down (defeat Parkinson's Law)
**When to use**: any big goal with a far deadline.
**How**: split into near milestones; give each a *who* and a *by when*; buffer for the planning fallacy; work backward from the target date.

## Debrief / Retrospective
**When to use**: after a project, on a cadence, or after any error.
**How**: 1–2 hrs; what went well / badly / to change. Blameless — collective language ("our process failed"), objective timeline. Write down and share learnings.

## Delegating a Hard Problem
**When to use**: growing a report and freeing your own time.
**How**: confirm you truly believe they can solve it; hand it over; publicly declare them the owner (empowers them); stay "on the boat" coaching — don't walk away.

## Put-Yourself-Out-of-a-Job Rule
**When to use**: continuously, as the team grows.
**How**: spend your energy only at the intersection of "most important to the org" AND "what you're uniquely best at." Delegate everything a report can do as well or better. Aim to double leadership capacity yearly.

## The Would-I-Rehire Test
**When to use**: a report or manager is persistently struggling.
**How**: ask "Assume the role were open — would I rehire this person or gamble on someone new?" If no, make the move (help them find a better role or let them go), respectfully and decisively.

## Culture-Building Loop
**When to use**: shaping how your team works.
**How**: (1) map current vs. aspirational culture; (2) talk about what matters ten times, ten ways; (3) walk the walk; (4) align incentives and address violations; (5) invent traditions that make values concrete.
