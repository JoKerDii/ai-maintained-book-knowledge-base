# Seeing Like a State — Cheatsheet

## 1. Is this scheme headed for failure? (the four-element test)

Score each. **All four present = Scott's recipe for disaster.** Fewer than four = risky, not lethal.

| # | Element | Present when… | Mitigation |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Administrative ordering** of nature/society (**state simplification**, **legibility**) | A standard grid replaces diverse local practice; the metric will be enforced, not just observed | Keep the old records; measure what you suppressed |
| 2 | **High-modernist ideology** | Confidence outruns evidence; "rational design of social order"; **visual/aesthetic order** treated as proof of function | Demand a falsifiable prediction and a failure criterion |
| 3 | **Authoritarian executor** willing to use coercive power | No veto point; dissent is reframed as obstruction; emergency powers | Restore a real veto held by someone who bears the cost |
| 4 | **Prostrate civil society** unable to resist | Those affected have no voice, no exit, no organization | Fund/convene the affected before, not after |

**Scott's summary:** legibility supplies the *capacity*, high modernism the *desire*, the authoritarian state the *determination*, an incapacitated society the *terrain*.

> Elements 1 and 2 alone are ordinary and often benign. Under representative institutions, high modernism "could spur reform." The lethal ingredient is that **nobody can say no**.

## 2. Tells & smells

| If you see… | You're probably in… |
|---|---|
| The plan is judged by how it looks from above (grid, symmetry, aerial view) | **Aesthetic order mistaken for functional order** (ch4, ch7) |
| A lavishly subsidized, closely supervised pilot cited as proof of scale | **Miniaturization** (ch7) |
| Practitioners' workarounds described as non-compliance | Suppression of **mētis** (ch9) |
| One number now defines success for a many-purpose activity | **Thin simplification** / hegemonic metric (ch8, ch10) |
| First cycle exceeds forecast; second cycle collapses | The **monocropping** signature — scientific forestry, *Waldsterben* (ch1) |
| The map is being enforced as law | **Facts on paper remaking facts on the ground** (ch1, ch2) |
| Nobody can explain how the system actually runs day to day | Formal order **parasitic on informal process** (ch9) |
| Rollout is nationwide, simultaneous, irreversible | Scope has defeated foresight (ch10) |

**Fastest single diagnostic:** run a **work-to-rule** thought experiment. If following the documented rules exactly would halt the work, the documentation is not the system — and any plan built from it is thin.

## 3. Designing interventions: Scott's four rules of thumb (ch10)

| Rule | Decision test |
|---|---|
| **Take small steps** | Can I stand back and observe before the next move? Haldane: a mouse survives the mineshaft, "a horse splashes." |
| **Favor reversibility** | If this is wrong, what does undoing it cost? Leopold: "keep all the parts." |
| **Plan on surprises** | Does the design admit uses I haven't imagined? Land that grows several crops; adjustable floor plans. |
| **Plan on human inventiveness** | Am I assuming later participants will improve on my design — or that they'll degrade it? |

## 4. Choosing between formal rule and local judgment

- Environment **repetitive, stable, predictable** → fixed routines are legitimately efficient. Codify.
- Environment **mutable, indeterminate, particular** → **mētis** governs. The harbor pilot outranks the captain *inside the harbor*.
- Generic rule + local case → budget for translation. "The more general the rules, the more they require in the way of translation."
- Rules of thumb are *summaries of* practice — teach them after competence, like grammar after speech.

## 5. The two maps test (ch10)

Draw **Map 1** (routes the design provides) and **Map 2** (time-lapse of everything unplanned people actually do). If Map 2 is doing load-bearing work your design doesn't represent, the design is not viable alone. "Planned Brasília is, in a thousand ways, underwritten by unplanned Brasília."

## 6. Institutional litmus test

**Does this form enhance the skills, knowledge, and responsibility of those inside it?**
- **"Canned"** — self-sufficient, receives no input, permits no modification (Iwo Jima statue, Taylorist line, superquadra).
- **Mētis-friendly** — completed by its users (Vietnam Memorial, common law, language, democracy).
> Narrow environments produce a less resourceful population — which then genuinely needs supervision. The plan manufactures the subjects it assumed.

## 7. Guardrails against misreading Scott

- He is **not** anti-state, anti-planning, anti-science, or anti-modernity. The state is "the ground of both our freedoms and our unfreedoms."
- Legibility has legitimate uses he credits explicitly: **public health, taxation fairness, rule of law, poverty relief, citizenship**.
- His target is precisely "an imperial or hegemonic planning mentality that excludes the necessary role of local knowledge and know-how."
- He does **not** romanticize the local; mētis coexists with domination, monopoly, and exclusion.
- Markets are **not** the escape hatch. Large-scale capitalism "is just as much an agency of homogenization, uniformity, grids, and heroic simplification as the state."

## 8. Transfers

| Domain | The state simplification | The suppressed mētis |
|---|---|---|
| Software architecture | The canonical model / schema / rewrite plan | On-call intuition, undocumented workarounds, why that config exists |
| Org design | Org chart, job descriptions, levelling rubric | Who actually gets things unblocked |
| Metrics / KPIs | The single north-star number | Everything the number had to exclude to be countable |
| Platform policy | Uniform global rule, enforced at scale | Local context, edge cases, community norms |

Design rule for all four: keep the intervention small, reversible, and diversity-preserving; and keep a channel open to the people whose knowledge the model excluded.
