# Chapter 11: Strategies, Meta-Programs & Modeling

## Core Idea
Excellence is not a trait but a replicable *sequence* of internal representations (a strategy) plus a set of perceptual habits (meta-programs) and prioritized values. NLP elicits that hidden syntax, then either installs it in yourself, refines it, or models it from someone who already runs it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Spelling Strategy**: Install a good speller's visual-recall strategy.
  - When to use: poor spelling, or any rote-memory task that benefits from eidetic recall.
  - How: View the correct word; relax (anchor calm); look up-left and picture it; clear state; recall the image and write it down, check; then write it backwards from the image, check. Loop on errors. Add color/size/font to hard letters.
- **Elicitation Of Learning Strategies**: Uncover the syntax you use to learn well.
  - When to use: to transfer your best learning approach to a weak subject, or model another's.
  - How: Pick a subject you excel at; list goals/outcomes; name the evidence procedure (how you knew you succeeded); list actions and problem-solving moves; spot the dominant rep system; elicit the full sequence (what stimulated you, how you represented the outcome, progress evidence); test.
- **The Walt Disney Strategy** (Dilts): Cycle three creative roles in separate physical locations.
  - When to use: developing a project, plan, or idea needing both vision and feasibility.
  - How: Anchor Dreamer, Realist, Critic to three spots plus a meta-position. Dreamer brainstorms dissociated; Realist associates and storyboards the plan; Critic turns objections into *questions* (not attacks) back to the Dreamer. Cycle until the plan fits at every location.
- **New Behavior Generator**: Build a needed "part" (e.g. assertiveness) as an outcome-based strategy.
  - When to use: person lacks an adaptive behavior they could naturally express.
  - How: Identify the part/need; elicit supporting state & resources (recall partial past expressions); create 3rd-position scenes of expressing it; ecology check; edit scenes for objections; run in 1st position and anchor; instruct subconscious to build the part; test.
- **Problem Definition**: Re-frame a problem you've defined as unsolvable.
  - When to use: stuck, feeling the problem is outside your control.
  - How: Apply meta-model to the definition (vagueness, distortions); turn the noun into a verb ("how do you *social-anxiety* yourself?"); examine ecology/secondary gain; surface hidden presuppositions and challenge them; use the as-if frame ("how would I see this with high self-esteem?"); test.
- **Problem Solving Strategy (I)** (Dilts): Solve via metaphor across spatial positions.
  - When to use: a stuck personal problem needing a fresh angle.
  - How: Anchor Problem, Meta, and Resource positions. In Resource, access a rich unrelated state; build a metaphor for the problem inspired by it; solve the *metaphoric* problem; from Meta, translate that solution back to the real problem; check; repeat with other resources.
- **Problem Solving Strategy (II)**: Align a team via a shared kinesthetic resource.
  - When to use: a problem owned by 2+ people, or latent conflict among them.
  - How: Each recalls a resourceful experience and expresses its *movement* nonverbally; mirror partner; rotate through 2nd and 3rd positions; return to 1st side-by-side facing the same way; converge on a shared movement ("we zone"); use it to re-group whenever conflict surfaces while solving the real problem.
- **Meta-Programs Identification**: Pace another's meta-programs to build rapport.
  - When to use: sales, leadership, coaching.
  - How: Assess their meta-programs; communicate in the same patterns; notice where your own driver meta-programs blind you; test for improved rapport/empathy.
- **Limiting Meta-Programs Challenging**: Fix communication breakdowns caused by meta-program misuse.
  - How: Identify their meta-programs and any violations/inappropriate use; evaluate effectiveness for the context; invite them toward a more effective meta-program; test the improved exchange.
- **The Meta-Programs Change Pattern**: Expand your own meta-program flexibility.
  - How: Identify your typical/least-flexible meta-programs and the situations where they fail; explore alternates (especially the opposite, e.g. away→towards); future-pace; ecology check across logical levels; install provisionally with a review date; re-evaluate.
- **Values Hierarchy Identification**: Surface and rank your operating values.
  - How: Relax; elicit values via 3 questions (what matters most; what you'd want said at your 80th birthday; the one trait you'd keep); rank by pairwise comparison; ecology-check the ordering against current life.
- **Honored Values Elicitation** (Galatiltyte): Elicit *heartfelt*, felt-sense values via deep listening.
  - How: Listen past primitive/negative content for the value beneath; ask the outcomes they most want, then the values those outcomes serve; connect each value to current needs and behaviors. Yields "handles" for further intervention.
- **Basic 2nd Position Modeling**: 4-person drill (Modeled, Subject, Modeler, Observer). Modeler enters 2nd position, mirrors subtle muscle movement, then stands in; feedback loops refine accuracy.
- **2nd Position Intuitive Modeling**: Two Modelers enter a "not-knowing" 2nd position with the Person Being Modeled on a simple skill, write what they infer is happening *internally*, then compare and collaborate to describe the skill.
- **Basic 3rd Position Modeling**: Two Modelers observe from 3rd position, gathering physiology, rep systems, language, meta-programs; describe internal state; compare; collaborate on the model.
- **Basic States Of Excellence Modeling**: Modeler A models from 2nd position; Modeler B asks *why* (beliefs/values/meta-programs/meta-outcomes) and *how* (goals, TOTEs); also model the *opposite* (stuck) state and contrast.
- **Mapping Anyone's Brain**: Non-verbally map a person's eye-accessing cues (Vr, Vc, Ar, Ac, K) in under three minutes by walking them through recall/construct tasks per modality, building a spatial map of their representational keys.

## Key Concepts
- **Strategy**: An ordered sequence (syntax) of internal representations producing a result (e.g. spelling = Visual recall → Kinesthetic check).
- **TOTE**: Test–Operate–Test–Exit; the feedback loop underlying every strategy — test against the goal, operate, re-test, exit when criteria met.
- **Meta-program**: An observable, context-dependent *tendency* in how attention is sorted (towards/away, etc.) — a continuum, never fixed or a label for a person.
- **Modeling**: Extracting another's strategy/state of excellence so it can be reproduced; rests on perceptual positions.
- **2nd position**: Stepping into the other person's experience (empathic, "as if I am them") — the engine of modeling and rapport.
- **3rd position**: Detached observer/meta-position viewing both parties; used to gather structure and resolve conflict.
- **Values hierarchy**: Your "most important to me" answers, ranked — provides higher-level direction so decisions "make themselves."
- **Evidence procedure**: How a person knows an outcome is achieved — a load-bearing element of any strategy.
- **Rep system**: Visual/Auditory/Kinesthetic (etc.) channel; strategies are sequences across rep systems.
- **Eye-accessing cues**: Eye movements that reveal which rep system is active (Vr up-right, Vc up-left, etc.).

## Mental Models
- **Excellence has syntax**: Talent looks like magic only because its sequence is hidden; elicit the order and it becomes transferable.
- **Roles are resources, not identities**: Use Dreamer/Realist/Critic as needed — never *become* the chronic critic or perpetual dreamer.
- **Go meta, ignore content**: To work with strategies, meta-programs, and conflicts, rise above *what* is discussed to *how* it is structured.
- **Same strategy, wrong context**: A strategy that wins in one domain fails when blindly applied to another (literature strategy on math).

## Anti-patterns
- **Labeling people by a meta-program**: They are continua and context-bound; "away-from snakes" flips to "towards" near an alligator. Tagging is rigid and false.
- **Identifying with the Critic (or Dreamer)**: Collapsing a role into a permanent stance kills flexibility.
- **Modeling content instead of structure**: Copying *what* someone says, not the physiology/state/syntax behind it.
- **Generating a "part" too far from the person**: Installing a "socially popular" part in a socially anxious client breeds conflict; neutralize the feeling first (Swish/Anchoring).
- **Confusing skill-learning with behavior generation**: New Behavior Generator surfaces natural capacities; it won't teach tennis from scratch.

## Worked Example
The Walt Disney Strategy on launching a course. Four floor-spots are anchored: meta, Dreamer, Realist, Critic. Standing in **Dreamer**, dissociated, you free-wheel — "What if anyone could learn all of NLP for the price of one book?" No idea is censored. Stepping to **Realist**, you associate in and storyboard the actual sequence: write, edit, record, publish — experiencing each collaborator's view in order. Stepping to **Critic** (placed far from the others so its state doesn't bleed), you spot gaps and convert them into *questions*: "How will buyers trust an unknown author?" — not "this will fail." Back in **Dreamer**, you brainstorm answers to those questions. You cycle Dreamer→Realist→Critic until the plan feels congruent at every location, then break state on an unrelated topic you enjoy.

## Key Takeaways
1. A strategy is an ordered sequence of representations; capture the order (and the TOTE) and excellence becomes copyable.
2. Modeling is built on perceptual positions — 2nd position to feel the strategy, 3rd position to see its structure.
3. Meta-programs are flexible continua; pace them for rapport, challenge them for clarity, change them for range — never use them as fixed labels.
4. A ranked values hierarchy turns hard decisions into near-automatic choices; honored (felt) values give them energy.
5. Reframe stuck problems by changing the *definition* — verb the noun, expose presuppositions, run the as-if frame.
6. Separate creative roles (Dreamer/Realist/Critic) into distinct anchored spaces so each state stays clean.
