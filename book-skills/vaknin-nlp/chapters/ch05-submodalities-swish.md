# Chapter 5: Submodalities & the Swish Family

## Core Idea
Submodalities are the fine-grained "settings" of a mental image, sound, or feeling (brightness, size, distance, location, volume, temperature). Because emotional intensity is driven by these settings rather than content, you can rewire automatic reactions by finding the few "driver" submodalities and swapping or reversing them at speed — the engine behind the Swish and its relatives.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The Swish** (Bandler & Grinder): Replace an automatic, unwanted reaction with a resourceful self-image.
  - When to use: smoking, nail-biting, anger, nervousness, low confidence — any habit with a recognizable trigger.
  - How: (1) Build a dissociated, compelling replacement self-image; (2) find the associated (first-position) trigger image that fires the unwanted state; (3) put a small, dim, postage-stamp replacement in the corner of the big, bright trigger image; (4) "swish" — trigger shrinks/darkens and shoots away while replacement zooms big/bright with a whoosh; (5) clear the mind (break state) and repeat 5–7 times; (6) test by trying to recall the old image.

- **The Kinesthetic Swish Pattern** (Robert Dee McDonald): Same logic in feeling-space.
  - When to use: when the problem is a felt state, not a picture.
  - How: associate into the negative feeling (note location, movement, temperature); break state; build and amplify the desired feeling; map the negative feeling into the positive feeling's location and submodalities. Runs slower than visual Swish — give it time.

- **Pragmagraphic Swish Design** (Dilts & Epstein): Spatial-anchor Swish for compulsions.
  - When to use: addictions, blurting, comfort-eating — catching a compulsion before drive peaks.
  - How: lay out floor locations for "Must," "Desire," "?" (trigger), "Creative Choice." Step backward through them to find the trigger; elicit submodalities that strengthen "?" and those that boost "Creative Choice"; then reverse their intensities while walking from "?" to "Creative Choice." Repeat 5×, test.

- **Submodality Overlapping**: Strengthen a weak rep system by leaning on a strong one.
  - When to use: building submodality acuity, creativity, richer description.
  - How: describe a scene in your favored system, then deliberately switch to the weak system and re-render the same scene (e.g. visual park → its sounds: chirps, squeaks, echo).

- **External Stimulus Threshold**: Raise tolerance to a triggering external cue (companion drill in this family).

- **The Godiva Chocolate Pattern**: Transfer intense pleasure onto a desired behavior.
  - When to use: low motivation for a worthwhile activity.
  - How: vividly imagine an intense craving (image 1); make a dissociated image of the desired behavior (image 2); ecology check; hide image 1 behind image 2; open a hole to glimpse the pleasure, then shrink it; repeat 3+ times until excitement attaches to the behavior. Stay out of the client's way — talk little.

- **Decision Destroyer**: Swap an early limiting decision for a constructive one along the timeline.
  - When to use: pre-verbal/childhood decisions driving self-sabotage.
  - How: clarify the limiting decision; build and anchor a superior decision; float back the timeline to a point ~15 min before the original decision carrying the positive resource; re-experience that moment with new resources; zoom to present and future-pace.

- **Compulsion Blow-Out**: Desensitize a compulsion by overdriving its submodalities.
  - When to use: nail-biting, obsessing — compulsions with an external trigger.
  - How: identify trigger; find its two strongest submodalities; crank them to an extreme, return to normal, crank again, repeatedly — until it becomes boring/trivial. Refer out for clinical issues.

- **The Spinning Icons Pattern** (Zink & Munshaw): Blend a negative and antidote state into a meta-state.
  - When to use: escaping/preventing recurring negative states, creativity.
  - How: pick a weak negative state and its antidote; turn each into a simple icon; orbit the two icons around each other, accelerating to extreme speed; let them blend into one image; tell a story to break state; test.

- **Auditory Rep System Development** (Dilts): Sharpen auditory acuity — listen to a sound, reproduce it vocally, rate each submodality 0–10 with a partner, reproduce again.

- **Visual Rep System Development**: Same for vision — observe an unfamiliar object (an onion, not the over-familiar orange) in full detail to train fresh visualization.

## Key Concepts
- **Submodality**: the qualities/"modes" of a representation within a sense (e.g. visual: size, brightness, color, distance, location, movie-vs-still).
- **Driver (driving) submodality**: the one whose change produces the largest emotional shift; light intensity and size/location are common drivers. Find these first for elegant change-work.
- **Critical submodality**: a submodality selected as decisive for a given swap; an experienced practitioner may run a Swish on two critical submodalities at once.
- **Associated**: experiencing from inside your own eyes (first position) — high emotional charge; the Swish trigger image is associated.
- **Dissociated**: watching yourself from outside (third position, "in a movie") — lower charge; the resourceful replacement self-image is dissociated.
- **Analog submodality**: one that varies on a continuum (brightness, volume) versus digital/on-off ones.
- **Mapping across**: importing the submodalities of one image onto another to change its feeling.
- **Trigger**: the cue (often subtle, just before the state) that fires an unwanted reaction.

## Mental Models
- **The mixing board**: feelings are not fixed by content; they are knob settings you can turn. Find the loudest knob (driver) and the whole track changes.
- **Direction of self-image**: the Swish makes you "shoot toward" who you want to be — it sets a direction, not a scripted behavior; you won't know in advance exactly what you'll do, only that the old reaction won't auto-fire.
- **Speed beats analysis**: rapid repetition (swish, spin, blow-out) installs the change below conscious editing; slowing down lets the old pattern reassert.

## Anti-patterns
- **Swishing without a break state between reps**: the images bleed together; clear the mind every time.
- **Making the replacement a behavior, not a self-image**: Swish toward who you'd be, not a single action — otherwise it doesn't generalize.
- **Keeping the replacement associated**: an associated replacement loses the aspirational pull; keep it dissociated.
- **Over-talking in Godiva/trance work**: the more you narrate early, the less effective; let the subconscious do the work.
- **Ignoring driver submodalities**: tweaking weak submodalities wastes effort — test for the driver first.
- **Skipping the ecology check (Godiva) or clinical referral (Blow-Out)**: amplifying a compulsion's pleasure or intensity without checking consequences is reckless.

## Worked Example: Swish on nail-biting
1. **Replacement self-image (dissociated, compelling):** picture yourself across the room, calm and self-assured, hands relaxed and elegant — someone with composed, well-groomed hands. Enhance submodalities until it's vivid and magnetic.
2. **Trigger (associated, first position):** find what fires the bite — the tingling/pressure sensation of nails "existing" or reaching a certain length, seen through your own eyes. This big, bright image is the cue.
3. **Place replacement:** shrink the resourceful image to a dark, postage-stamp dot in the bottom corner of the big trigger image.
4. **Swish:** simultaneously — trigger image shrinks, darkens, and zooms off into the distance while the replacement explodes large and bright, "whoosh," filling the whole field.
5. **Break state:** open eyes, think of your favorite color or tonight's dinner. Breathe.
6. **Repeat 5–7×**, faster each pass, until you struggle to even hold the old trigger image.
7. **Test:** try to call up the nail-biting urge. Notice you now have to consciously reconstruct it — it no longer fires automatically. Re-run in a day, then a week, if relapse looms.

## Key Takeaways
1. Emotional intensity rides on submodalities, not content — change the settings, change the response.
2. Find the driver submodality first (often light/size/location); it gives the most leverage.
3. The Swish: big/bright associated trigger ↔ small/dim dissociated replacement, swap fast, repeat with break states, test.
4. Aim Swishes at a self-image (a direction) rather than a single behavior, so change generalizes.
5. The same submodality engine powers spatial Swishes (Pragmagraphic), pleasure transfer (Godiva), desensitization (Blow-Out), and state-blending (Spinning Icons).
6. Speed and repetition install the change; break state between reps to keep images clean.
7. Train rep-system acuity (Auditory/Visual Development) so you can detect and adjust submodalities reliably.
