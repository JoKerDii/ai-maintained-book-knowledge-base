# Chapter 6: Reframing & Parts Work

## Core Idea
Behavior you dislike is produced by a "part" of you acting on a positive intention; you change the behavior not by fighting the part but by honoring its intention while reframing the meaning, generating better behaviors, or integrating the part with its opposite. Reframing shifts the frame (content or context) around an experience; parts work resolves the internal conflicts that drive self-sabotage.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Six Step Reframe** (Grinder): elicit subconscious resources so a part swaps a habit for better behaviors.
  - When to use: a habit you "can't stop" (nail-biting, smoking, overeating).
  - How: (1) select the behavior; (2) establish a yes/no signal from the part that creates it; (3) elicit the part's positive intentions; (4) ask your creative part for three alternative behaviors that meet those intentions; (5) get the part's signal that it accepts the alternatives; (6) future-pace each alternative and run an ecology check, retrying if other parts object.

- **Content Reframing**: change what an experience *means* while facts stay constant.
  - When to use: a person feels defective/stuck because of a limiting interpretation.
  - How: select disliked behavior, name the part, find its positive intention, surface the frame's presuppositions, de-frame ("what else could this mean?"), reframe the intention, link to a context where it serves, integrate, test.

- **Context Reframing**: keep the behavior, change *where* it applies — find a context where it is useful.
  - How: pick a state/behavior; ask where it would express your higher values; vividly imagine it in an appropriate context; then in random contexts; test for new flexibility.

- **Reframing Beliefs and Opinions**: conversational reframing using shared *values* (not facts) to dissolve a rigid opinion (e.g., the China dialogue — each "disgusting" habit reframed as economical/hygienic via values George already holds).

- **Parts Negotiation**: broker a treaty between two conflicting parts.
  - How: select behavior; identify the part supporting it and the part distressed by it; elicit each part's outcomes and meta-outcomes; build mutual understanding of positive roles; negotiate "if it stops interfering, will you?"; secure a trial-period commitment with a dissatisfaction signal; test.

- **Conflicting Beliefs Integration** (Dilts, advanced): align two opposing beliefs along a timeline. State the irrational belief vs. rational alternative; anchor an ideal-future resource state; trace the obstacle's origin; treat each belief as a part; from a meta-position have parts face each other, find each other's positive intent and shared mission; share resources; mentor the past self; merge both parts into a single integrated identity walking forward in time; test.

- **Advanced Visual Squash**: integrate two parts held one in each hand.
  - How: identify the conflict, put a sensory image of each part in each hand, elicit each part's positive intention, map shared resources across (energy flowing hand to hand), form a combined image, turn palms to face each other, bring hands slowly together as the parts blend, draw the integrated part into your chest.

- **Resolving Internal Conflicts**: switch between parts to build mutual understanding. Recall the conflict from observer; step into one side, review the other; have the other side voice its positive intentions; switch and repeat until both understand; go meta and brainstorm solutions both accept; form a new part from the amalgam and bring it into your body; future-pace.

- **Basic Inner Conflict Integration** (Bandler/Grinder): resolve habitual negative states overriding resourceful ones across logical levels, so a desired behavior becomes consistent.

- **Dis-Identification**: loosen over-identification with limiting beliefs/roles. Adopt the supporting belief that a self exists beyond circumstances; use "I have X but I am not X"; deepen in trance; treat traits as functions; tune into a transcendent identity, name/symbolize it, amplify its submodalities and center your consciousness there.

- **Core Transformation** (Connirae Andreas): chain outcomes inward to a core state, then run it back out. Access a part; ask "what do you want?", then repeatedly "when you have that, what beyond that is important?" until you reach the **core state** (centered, expanded peace — "coming home"); name the core-state outcome; reverse the chain, treating the core state as a *source* not a result, letting it expand every prior outcome and the original context.

- **Perceptual Positions**: shift viewpoint. 1st = associated, own eyes; 2nd = inside the other person; 3rd = dissociated observer/movie; 4th = systems/collective view; 5th = cosmic/transcendent.

- **Aligning Perceptual Positions**: get all three rep systems (visual, auditory, kinesthetic) into the *same* position at once. Misalignment (e.g., hearing your own thoughts as the other's voice, feeling others' feelings as yours) often *is* the problem; alignment grounds you and prevents codependent over-empathy.

- **Conflict Resolution** (Bandler/Grinder, dev. Dilts): for conflict between two people. Clarify the polarity and its logical level; go meta; surface each party's positive intentions and have each acknowledge the other's; chunk up to a shared higher intention; generate alternatives (often better than compromise); secure a concrete plan; ecology check; test.

- **Co-Dependency Resolution**: imagine the metaphorical "ties" (ropes, apron strings) to an over-involved person; notice secondary gain and the positive intention behind keeping them; create an ideal self with healthy boundaries; reconnect the ties to that ideal self and experience the benefits from first position.

## Key Concepts
- **Frame**: the boundary on what you consider; meaning is frame-dependent.
- **Reframe**: changing the frame to get a better outcome — content (what it means) or context (where it fits).
- **Positive intention**: the beneficial purpose every part pursues, even via harmful behavior. Honor the intention, change the behavior.
- **Part**: a largely subconscious constellation of motives, attitudes, and an accessible state; may be irrational by conscious standards.
- **Secondary gain / meta-outcome**: the hidden higher benefit a "negative" behavior delivers (e.g., overeating soothes anxiety, weight feels protective).
- **Ecology check**: verify no other part or life area objects before locking in a change.
- **Meta-position**: a dissociated stance above/outside the conflict, used to negotiate.
- **1st / 2nd / 3rd position**: self (associated) / other's shoes / detached observer.
- **Logical levels**: conflicts at behavior level often dissolve when chunked up to identity/values level.

## Mental Models
- **The part is an ally, not an enemy.** Self-sabotage is misdirected protection; argue with the behavior and you lose, recruit the intention and you win.
- **Separate intention from behavior.** Same positive intention can be served by many behaviors — that gap is where change lives.
- **Integration over suppression.** Two warring parts share resources and merge into one richer part; nothing is amputated.
- **Move the viewpoint to change the feeling.** Dissociating to 3rd position cools emotion; aligning positions returns ownership of your own thoughts and feelings.

## Anti-patterns
- **Willpower against a part**: trying to override programming consciously — it wins back eventually because you keep thinking about it.
- **Assuming malice in a part**: treating a behavior's negative result as its goal; usually it's an unintended side effect of a positive intention.
- **Skipping the ecology check**: imposing a change other parts reject, producing relapse or a new symptom.
- **Reframing with facts not values**: arguing data rarely shifts a belief; shared values do.
- **Killing the part**: trying to eliminate rather than integrate leaves the need unmet.

## Worked Example
Six Step Reframe on nail-biting. **(1) Select:** "I want to stop biting my nails but can't." **(2) Signal:** I ask the part that bites to give a yes signal — a slight lift in my right index finger; after a moment it rises. **(3) Positive intention:** I ask what the biting does *for* me. The answer surfaces: it discharges tension when I'm anxious or focused. The intention is regulating stress, not ruining my nails. I thank the part. **(4) Alternatives:** I ask my creative part for three other ways to meet that same need — slow breathing, squeezing a small object, pressing fingertips together. **(5) Acceptance:** I check with the biting part: are these three acceptable substitutes? I wait for the yes signal — the finger lifts. **(6) Future-pace & ecology:** I rehearse each alternative in the situations that usually trigger biting (deadlines, boredom) and scan for objections from any other part. None object, so I try them in real life; if biting persists I assume a parts conflict and re-run the pattern.

## Key Takeaways
1. Every unwanted behavior carries a positive intention — find it before you try to change anything.
2. Change behavior, not intention: generate alternatives that satisfy the same need.
3. Reframe content (meaning) or context (where it applies); use shared values, not facts.
4. Resolve internal conflict by integration — share resources and merge parts, never amputate.
5. Always ecology-check and secure the part's agreement before sealing a change.
6. Shifting perceptual position changes emotional charge; aligning all three rep systems restores grounded ownership.
