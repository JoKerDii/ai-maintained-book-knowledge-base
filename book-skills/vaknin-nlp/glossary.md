# Glossary — The Big Book of NLP

Key terms with definitions, alphabetical. Chapter references in parentheses.

**Alternating bilateral stimulus** — Side-to-side eye movement, sound, or touch that triggers the reprocessing state shift (Ch 13).

**Anchor** — A stimulus conditioned to trigger a particular state; learned, not innate. *Firing* an anchor activates the state (Ch 4).

**Associated** — Experiencing from inside your own eyes (1st position) with full felt charge; opposite of dissociated (Ch 5, Ch 8).

**Calibration** — Linking an observed behavioral cue to a person's internal cognitive/emotional state (Ch 3).

**Characterological Adjective (CA)** — A single word naming a relationship role; implies its counterpart (victim↔victimizer) (Ch 13).

**Chunking up / down** — Moving to more abstract/general (up) or more specific/concrete (down) levels; up wins agreement, down enables solutions (Ch 9).

**Congruence** — Alignment of words, tone, body, and outcomes; root of charisma and effective change (Ch 1, Ch 12).

**Conversational (covert) hypnosis** — Producing trance through ordinary-seeming talk with no formal induction (Ch 10).

**Core state** — The centered, expanded peace reached by chaining outcomes inward (Ch 6).

**Criteria** — Values that drive decisions; ranked in a hierarchy where higher override lower (Ch 7, Ch 11).

**Deep structure** — The fuller internal experience that words derive from; what the Meta-Model recovers (Ch 9).

**Dissociation** — Observing yourself from outside (3rd/meta position) with little felt charge (Ch 5, Ch 8).

**Double bind** — A choice between two options that both fulfill the same presupposition/outcome (Ch 10).

**Double dissociation** — Two layers of separation (watching yourself watch a movie, from the projection booth); for highly triggering memories (Ch 8).

**Downtime** — A light internal trance with awareness turned fully inward; *uptime* is outward awareness informed by inner awareness (Ch 4, Ch 10).

**Driver (driving/critical) submodality** — The submodality whose change most shifts a state's intensity; the highest-leverage edit point. Find it first (Ch 5, Ch 8).

**Ecology / Ecology check** — The systemic impact of a change across the whole person/system; verifying no part or life area objects before locking in a change (Ch 1, Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Embedded command** — A directive inserted and marked out (via tone/tempo/gesture) inside a larger sentence or question (Ch 10).

**Evidence procedure** — How a person knows an outcome has been achieved (Ch 2, Ch 11).

**Extinction** — Decay of an anchor from overuse or staleness (Ch 4).

**Frame** — The boundary on what you consider; "the one who sets the frame controls the action." A *reframe* changes it (Ch 6, Ch 1).

**Imprint / Re-imprinting** — A belief installed during a developmental window (imprint); returning to that source to supply missing resources rewrites it (re-imprinting) (Ch 7).

**Law of Reversed Effect** — The harder one consciously tries, the harder the result becomes; demands indirect suggestion (Ch 10).

**Law of Requisite Variety** — "Only variety can destroy variety"; more options/positions weaken a rigid belief and increase influence (Ch 7, Ch 1).

**Limiting belief** — A non-resourceful generalization that blocks goals; treated as loosenable structure, not truth (Ch 7).

**Logical Levels** — Dilts's hierarchy: Environment, Behavior, Capability, Beliefs/Values, Identity, Spirit/Purpose; higher levels organize lower (Ch 12).

**Map vs. territory** — Your internal model is never the reality it represents; always improvable (Ch 1).

**Matrixing** — Strategic case planning across four categories: Meaning, Context, Behavior, Physiology (Ch 13).

**Meta-program** — A context-dependent, continuum-based tendency in how attention is sorted (toward/away, etc.); never a fixed label (Ch 11).

**Meta-position** — A dissociated observer stance above a conflict, used to negotiate (Ch 6, Ch 8).

**Meta-state** — A layered meaning/value stacked on top of another state (Ch 12).

**Milton-Model** — Artful, deliberately vague language (Meta-Model violations on purpose) to induce trance and bypass conscious resistance; inverse of the Meta-Model (Ch 10).

**Modal operator** — The word (must/can't/should/could) revealing a mode of necessity, possibility, or impossibility (Ch 9).

**Modeling** — Extracting another's strategy or state of excellence so it can be reproduced (Ch 11).

**Nominalization** — A process/verb frozen into a static noun (relationship, fear); fails the "put it in a wheelbarrow" test (Ch 9).

**Pacing and leading** — Match the person's verifiable experience first (pace), then shift your state to move theirs (lead) (Ch 3, Ch 10).

**Part** — A largely subconscious cluster of motives/attitudes acting like a sub-personality (Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Perceptual positions** — 1st (self/associated), 2nd (the other's shoes), 3rd (detached observer/meta) (Ch 6, Ch 8).

**Positive intention** — The constructive purpose underlying even a harmful behavior; respond to it, not the symptom (Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Presupposition** — A useful operating belief acted on without proof; the 21 NLP presuppositions are NLP's core beliefs (Ch 1).

**Priming** — Triggering a state conducive to a way of thinking or acting; broader and vaguer than a command (Ch 10, Ch 13).

**Reframe** — Changing the frame for a better outcome: *content* (what it means) or *context* (where it fits) (Ch 6).

**Rep systems (representational systems)** — Channels through which experience is coded: Visual, Auditory, Kinesthetic, plus auditory-digital/olfactory-gustatory (Ch 1, Ch 4).

**Reprocessing** — Re-encoding a poorly stored overwhelming experience so it integrates and symptoms resolve; shared mechanism behind EMDR, EFT, Time Line Therapy, and the NLP phobia cure (Ch 13).

**SCORE** — Symptom / Cause / Outcome / Resource / Effect; a framework for shaping any problem (Ch 8).

**Secondary gain** — A hidden benefit a problem behavior delivers that sabotages change until addressed (Ch 1, Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Self-sabotage** — A negative pattern sustained by a hidden payoff; addressed via its positive intention (Ch 12).

**Sensory acuity** — Trained ability to notice involuntary micro-changes (skin color, muscle tone, breathing, pupils) (Ch 1, Ch 3).

**Somatic / bottom-up** — Changing thought by working through body, state, and nervous system rather than cognition (Ch 13).

**State** — The combined mind-body condition at a given moment; functional or dysfunctional (Ch 4).

**Strategy** — An ordered sequence (syntax) of internal representations that produces a result; e.g. spelling = visual recall then kinesthetic check (Ch 11).

**Submodality** — A quality within a sense (visual: size, brightness, color, distance, location, movie-vs-still; auditory: volume, pitch; kinesthetic: location, temperature) (Ch 5).

**Surface structure** — The spoken words; a compressed, deleted/distorted/generalized output of the deep structure (Ch 9).

**Swish** — The signature submodality pattern: a big/bright associated trigger image is rapidly swapped for a small/dim dissociated desired-self image (Ch 5).

**Thought virus** — A toxic, self-propagating belief (Dilts); inoculated against and quarantined (Ch 7).

**Time Line Therapy** — Processing memory along the timeline via Source Event, Echo Events, and Future Success (Tad James) (Ch 13).

**TOTE** — Test–Operate–Test–Exit; the feedback loop inside every strategy (Ch 11).

**Transderivational search (TDS)** — The mind's fuzzy hunt for meaning in vague input; itself produces brief trance (Ch 10).

**Trance** — A focused, inward, suggestible state; can be light and momentary (Ch 10).

**Universal quantifier** — Totalizing words (never/always/everyone) signaling over-generalization (Ch 9).

**Utilization** — Accepting and turning whatever the person presents — even a symptom or resistance — into a resource (Ch 1, Ch 10).

**V-K dissociation** — Changing a memory's visual viewpoint to break its kinesthetic (felt) charge; engine of the Phobia Cure (Ch 8).

**Values hierarchy** — Your most important values, ranked, giving higher-level decision direction (Ch 11).

**Well-formed (well-defined) outcome** — A goal that is positive, specific, self-actionable, context-bound, sensory-rich, and ecological (Ch 2).
