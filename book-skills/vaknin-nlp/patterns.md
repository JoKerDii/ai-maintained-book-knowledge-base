# Patterns Catalog — The Big Book of NLP

Concrete techniques, when to use them, how, and trade-offs. Grouped by theme. For full steps and worked examples, open the linked chapter.

## Outcomes, Feedback & Ecology (Ch 2)

## Well-Defined Outcomes
**When**: Starting any goal or change, before spending resources. **How**: State it positive → ability-framed → contextualized → in the senses → chunked → resourced → ecology-checked → milestoned → written → tested. **Trade-off**: Over-specifying can cause analysis paralysis.

## Failure Into Feedback
**When**: A belief makes you feel defeated about a capability or goal. **How**: Sort the failure's VAK into eye positions, build the goal image up-right, normalize the memories, anchor a positive reference and fire it at the goal. **Trade-off**: Needs comfort with eye-position/submodality work.

## Ecology Check
**When**: Before committing to any change. **How**: Dissociate to 3rd position; ask who benefits/is hurt, short- and long-term; use Cartesian questions ("What would happen if you did / didn't?"). **Trade-off**: Skipping it invites self-sabotage.

## Finding Positive Intention / Secondary Gain
**When**: Self-sabotage or a stalled change. **How**: Address the part; ask what it wanted to accomplish; recycle answers up to the core value. **Trade-off**: Relies on accepting the "parts" frame; hidden gains rationalize away easily.

## Rapport, Pacing & Mirroring (Ch 3)

## Pacing and Matching / Mirroring
**When**: Building rapport, sales, coaching, modeling. **How**: Subtly match cadence, predicates, posture, breathing; then test by leading. **Trade-off**: Obvious imitation breaks rapport and offends.

## Satir Category Rapport-Building
**When**: Pacing a difficult communicator (Blamer, Placater, Computer, Distracter, Leveler). **How**: Pace each style without triggering its defense, then lead; default to Leveler (congruent, factual) if unsure. **Trade-off**: Styles are situational, not fixed labels.

## When NOT to Mirror
**When**: Aggression, suffering, or intense-need states. **How**: Use same-team posture; mirror only general comforting cues. **Trade-off**: Mirroring distress induces overwhelm.

## States & Anchoring (Ch 4)

## Anchoring / Self Anchoring
**When**: You want a resource state on demand. **How**: Pick a unique trigger, elicit the state strongly, set the anchor at the PEAK, test. **Trade-off**: Anchors decay if reused or unreinforced.

## Collapsing Anchors
**When**: A situation triggers an unwanted state. **How**: Anchor the negative and a stronger positive; fire both; release the negative first; test. **Trade-off**: Needs the positive clearly stronger.

## Chaining States / Circle of Excellence
**When**: A target state is too far to reach in one jump (chaining); or you want a peak state at will (Circle). **How**: Bridge through intermediate anchored steps; or load a stepped-into spatial circle with the state and future-pace. **Trade-off**: Tedious; the spatial cue must stay dedicated.

## Physiomental State Interruption
**When**: A stuck/dysfunctional state won't shift. **How**: Exaggerate its driving submodality to absurdity; test. **Trade-off**: Fails if you target the wrong submodality.

## Submodalities & the Swish (Ch 5)

## The Swish
**When**: An unwanted habit with a clear trigger. **How**: Big/bright associated trigger image ↔ small/dim dissociated desired-self image; swap fast with a whoosh; repeat 5–7× with break states; test. **Trade-off**: Needs a real trigger; generalizes only when aimed at a *self*-image, not a behavior.

## Godiva Chocolate
**When**: Low motivation for a worthwhile behavior. **How**: Hide a craving image behind the behavior image; open/shrink a hole 3+ times. **Trade-off**: Requires ecology check; over-talking kills it.

## Compulsion Blow-Out / Spinning Icons
**When**: A compulsion or recurring negative state. **How**: Overdrive its two strongest submodalities (or orbit negative + antidote icons) to extreme until boring/blended; break state. **Trade-off**: Refer clinical cases; start with weak states.

## Reframing & Parts Work (Ch 6)

## Six Step Reframe
**When**: A habit you "can't stop." **How**: Signal the part → elicit its positive intention → creative part makes 3 alternatives → part accepts → future-pace + ecology. **Trade-off**: Needs a cooperative unconscious signal.

## Content / Context Reframing
**When**: A limiting interpretation (content), or a behavior bad only in some settings (context). **How**: "What else could this mean?" / "Where would this serve you?" **Trade-off**: Can feel like denial if facts are ignored.

## Parts Negotiation / Advanced Visual Squash
**When**: Two parts at war. **How**: Elicit each part's outcome and meta-outcome; broker a non-interference treaty (negotiation), or merge a part in each hand after sharing resources (squash). **Trade-off**: Needs honest meta-outcomes; abstract if parts won't image.

## Beliefs & Belief Change (Ch 7)

## Re-Imprinting
**When**: A deeply imprinted childhood belief or symptom. **How**: Float to the source imprint; give the past self and role models the missing resources; rewrite forward. **Trade-off**: Heavyweight; overkill for small issues.

## Belief Chaining (Basic / Advanced) & Belief Out-Framing
**When**: Shift a belief from un-resourceful to resourceful. **How**: Walk spatial steps with reframes; or view the belief from a 3×3 grid (Past/Present/Future × Self/Other/Observer) and gather alternatives. **Trade-off**: Advanced versions need a partner/guide.

## Hierarchy of Criteria
**When**: Procrastination from a values conflict. **How**: Tie the stalled behavior to a higher Identity-level criterion. **Trade-off**: Requires a grasp of logical levels.

## Phobias, Trauma & Emotional Healing (Ch 8)

## Phobia Cure (Fast Phobia / V-K Dissociation)
**When**: An irrational fear or single-event trauma. **How**: Double dissociation; watch a black-and-white movie from the projection booth; then run it backward fast in color, associated; repeat; test. **Trade-off**: Can re-traumatize — refer clinical cases to professionals.

## Change Personal History
**When**: Recurring button-pushing memories. **How**: Set an exit cue, chain to the earliest instance, collapse adult-resource anchors onto the memories. **Trade-off**: Needs flexible real-time adaptation.

## SCORE / Dancing SCORE
**When**: A stuck multi-faceted problem. **How**: Gather Symptom/Cause/Outcome/Resource/Effect (statically, or spatially with movement); loop effects as feedback; add the missing resource. **Trade-off**: Analytical; needs space/privacy for the Dancing version.

## The Meta-Model: Precision Language (Ch 9)

## Meta-Model Challenging
**When**: Gathering accurate information or loosening a limiting belief. **How**: Map the words to a sensory representation; spot the deletion/distortion/generalization; ask the recovery question; test back. **Trade-off**: Over-use feels like interrogation and breaks rapport — use softeners.

## De-Nominalizing
**When**: Someone is stuck/disempowered by a "thing" (a nominalization). **How**: Find the frozen process; turn the noun back into a verb with an actor in the driver's seat. **Trade-off**: Dissolves the easy agreement that vague nouns create.

## Persuasion by Chunking Up / Down
**When**: Negotiating (up) vs problem-solving (down). **How**: Chunk up to vague generalities for agreement; chunk down to specifics to solve. **Trade-off**: Chunking up is near-hypnotic — wrong for solving.

## The Milton-Model & Conversational Hypnosis (Ch 10)

## Pace–Link–Lead
**When**: Inducing light trance in conversation. **How**: State 3 verifiable facts, join with "and/as," then lead with a suggestion. **Trade-off**: Needs genuine rapport or feels manipulative.

## Embedded Commands / Double Binds
**When**: Implanting a directive past resistance, or increasing cooperation. **How**: Mark a short command inside a quote/question with intonation; or offer two choices that both serve the same presupposition. **Trade-off**: Over-marking becomes obvious; sophisticated listeners spot the non-choice.

## Nested Loops
**When**: Delivering suggestions inside story. **How**: Open 5 stories, embed the suggestion in #5, close 5→1 in reverse. **Trade-off**: Requires skill and engaging stories; long.

## Strategies, Meta-Programs & Modeling (Ch 11)

## Spelling Strategy
**When**: Poor spelling or rote recall. **How**: View the word up-left from a calm anchored state; write it from the image; write it backward; loop. **Trade-off**: Visual-heavy; resists non-visual learners.

## Walt Disney Strategy
**When**: Developing a plan or idea. **How**: Anchor Dreamer, Realist, and Critic in three separate spots; cycle through them; the Critic asks questions, not attacks. **Trade-off**: Needs space and discipline to keep states clean.

## 2nd / 3rd Position Modeling
**When**: Copying someone's excellence. **How**: Take 2nd position for the *feel*, 3rd position for the *structure/syntax*; extract the strategy and state. **Trade-off**: Self-report can be inaccurate; needs practiced observation.

## Self, Identity & Logical Levels (Ch 12)

## Logical Levels Alignment / Co-Alignment
**When**: Reclaiming direction on a goal (self) or aligning a team. **How**: Walk the issue through all six levels; build bottom-up carrying each state upward; find overlaps and integrate down. **Trade-off**: Slow; needs group buy-in for teams.

## Loving Yourself / Inner Hero / Assertiveness Installation
**When**: Low self-worth, acting-out, or passivity. **How**: See yourself through a loving person's 2nd position and anchor the state; or pace the person's highest identity-level values, then lead; build a state + boundary "force-field." **Trade-off**: Fails if recognition is artificial; needs a genuinely safe model figure.

## Advanced & Newly Introduced Tools (Ch 13)

## Reprocessing
**When**: Trauma, anxiety, or intrusive symptoms. **How**: Target the memory → state shift via alternating bilateral stimulus → optional cognitive work → body scan / future pace → recovery. **Trade-off**: Needs training/supervision; refer severe trauma.

## Matrixing
**When**: A complex, multi-issue ("twilight"/subclinical) client. **How**: Mind-map across Meaning, Context, Behavior, Physiology into actionable items. **Trade-off**: Requires psychopathology knowledge; slower than single techniques.

## Vision Communication Protocol
**When**: One partner overwhelms the other in relationship talks. **How**: Single issue → vision → values → steps → one tiny ask, framed in the partner's language. **Trade-off**: Only for functional relationships; can suppress serious issues if misused.

## Smart Eating Pattern
**When**: Stress eating vs real hunger. **How**: Felt-sense hunger check → imagine eating → contrast → future-pace. **Trade-off**: Relies on honest interoception.
