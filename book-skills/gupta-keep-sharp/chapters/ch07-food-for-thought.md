# Chapter 7: Food for Thought (Pillar 4: NOURISH)

## Core Idea
There is no single brain "superfood" and no perfect branded diet — what matters is a *style of eating* (everything in moderation, mostly plants, real food). How you fuel your body meaningfully protects your brain, primarily by controlling blood sugar and inflammation. Remember: "Diets may seem confusing, but food isn't."

## Frameworks Introduced

### S.H.A.R.P. — Gupta's guide to good eating
- **S — Slash the Sugar and stick to your ABCs.** Reduce refined sugar (avg American: 163g/day); artificial sweeteners are *not* a good replacement (disrupt gut microbiome → insulin resistance). Best foods have no nutrition label — the whole foods around the store's perimeter.
- **H — Hydrate smartly.** "Drink instead of eat" (we mistake thirst for hunger). If you feel thirst, you've already waited too long. Water first; coffee/tea fine (caffeine-free after 2 p.m.). Alcohol: if you don't drink, don't start; if you do, don't overdo (≤2 drinks/day men, ≤1 women; prefer red wine for polyphenols).
- **A — Add more Omega-3s from *dietary* sources** (fatty fish — salmon, mackerel, sardines; flaxseed, walnuts, chia). Fix the skewed omega-6:omega-3 ratio (modern 12:1–25:1 vs. ancestral 1:1). **Food, not supplements** — fish-oil pills show mixed/negative results.
- **R — Reduce portions.** Cook at home, measure, no seconds, smaller plates. Prefer low-heat cooking (sauté/steam/bake) over frying. Intermittent fasting (12–16 hr) is an option — boosts BDNF and insulin sensitivity (check with doctor).
- **P — Plan ahead.** Don't get caught starving → junk food. Plan meals 1–2×/week; build in fiber (whole fruits/veg, beans, whole grains).

### The ABCs of food (from the Global Council on Brain Health)
- **A-list (consume regularly)**: fresh leafy greens, whole berries, fish/seafood, healthy fats (extra virgin olive oil, avocado, whole eggs), nuts and seeds.
- **B-list (include)**: beans/legumes, whole fruits, low-sugar/low-fat dairy, poultry, whole grains.
- **C-list (limit)**: fried food, pastries/sugary foods, processed foods, red meat and red-meat products, whole-fat/high-saturated dairy (cheese, butter), salt.

### The MIND diet (Morris)
Mediterranean–DASH Intervention for Neurodegenerative Delay. Highest adherence → 53% lower Alzheimer's risk and a cognitive-decline difference equal to ~7.5 years of aging. Thumbs up: vegetables (esp. greens), nuts, berries, beans, whole grains, fish, poultry, olive oil, (optional wine). Thumbs down: red meat, butter/margarine, cheese, sweets, fried/fast food.

## Key Concepts
- **Entourage effect**: micronutrients work best *in whole food* — the accompanying molecules aid absorption. Real food beats supplements. "That bottle with broccoli on the label doesn't have broccoli in it."
- **Type 3 diabetes** (recap): high blood sugar → faster cognitive decline; slashing sugar directly protects the brain.
- **Fiber**: changes a meal's chemistry (slows glucose absorption, lowers inflammation); soluble (oats, beans, apples) vs. insoluble (whole grains, nuts, veg).
- **Curcumin** (turmeric): a well-designed study showed 90mg 2×/day for 18 months improved memory/attention and reduced amyloid/tau on scans.
- **Chrono eating**: *when* you eat matters — "eat only when the sun is shining"; frontload calories ("breakfast like a king, lunch like a prince, dinner like a peasant").

## Mental Models
- Eating well is **a style, not a rigid protocol** — "avoiding entire food groups to game your way to health" can backfire and hasten death (Seidelmann, Lancet).
- **Focus on what to eat, not what to avoid** — fuel with good calories and you'll naturally crowd out the bad.
- **"Eat the rainbow"**: aim for **seven different-colored real foods per day** for macro/micronutrient coverage.

## Worked Example — Precision-medicine prevention (Isaacson)
At Weill Cornell's Alzheimer's Prevention Clinic, Dr. Richard Isaacson *prescribes* foods alongside exercise, sleep, and stress management. His patients (ages 25–86, all with family history) received an average of **21 personalized recommendations**; following ≥60% produced measurable cognitive *improvement* — even in those already diagnosed with MCI — in as little as 18 months. His ABCs of prevention: **A**nthropometrics (body fat, muscle), **B**iomarkers (cholesterol, inflammation, blood sugar, genes), **C**ognitive performance — reassessed every 6 months. Core message: "One in three cases of Alzheimer's may be preventable."

## Anti-patterns
- **Following strict/branded diets** that fight willpower and eliminate whole food groups.
- **Supplements as food substitutes** — the industry is woefully unregulated; no supplement (ginkgo, CoQ10, apoaequorin, fish oil) reliably prevents dementia. "Spend the money on walking shoes or a good pillow instead."
- **Artificial sweeteners** — disrupt gut bacteria; contribute to the metabolic problems they claim to solve.
- **Going gluten-free without celiac/sensitivity** — no evidence it helps; a low-gluten diet low in whole grains can *raise* heart-disease risk.

## Key Takeaways
1. Slash sugar first — it's the easiest lever and directly controls the blood-sugar/inflammation pathway.
2. Get omega-3s and micronutrients from food, not pills (entourage effect).
3. Eat mostly plants, seven colors a day; MIND/Mediterranean style; portion control.
4. When you eat matters — frontload calories, consider a gentle 12-hour overnight fast.
5. Bonus habit: **floss** — gum inflammation raises stroke/brain risk.
