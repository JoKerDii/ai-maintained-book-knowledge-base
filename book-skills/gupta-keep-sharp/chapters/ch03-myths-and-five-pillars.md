# Chapter 3: 12 Destructive Myths and the 5 Pillars That Will Build You

## Core Idea
This is the hinge chapter: it clears away the twelve myths that make people fatalistic about the aging brain, then introduces the **5 Pillars of brain health (Move, Discover, Relax, Nourish, Connect)** that structure the entire rest of the book. The brain remains plastic and can grow new cells throughout life — decline is not destiny.

## Frameworks Introduced

### The 5 Pillars of Brain Health (MOVE · DISCOVER · RELAX · NOURISH · CONNECT)
First described by AARP's Global Council on Brain Health; the backbone of the Keep Sharp plan.
- **Move** (Ch4): exercise is the *only* activity scientifically proven to improve brain health. "Break a sweat every day."
  - When to use: it's the #1 lever — start here if you do only one thing.
- **Discover** (Ch5): learn new, complex things; cultivate a sense of purpose (ikigai); get in flow.
- **Relax** (Ch6): restorative sleep + daily stress reduction. Multitasking and chronic stress impair the brain.
- **Nourish** (Ch7): eat to protect the brain (MIND diet / S.H.A.R.P.); mind the gut microbiome.
- **Connect** (Ch8): face-to-face social connection gets an "A"; crosswords get a "B-".

### The Dirty Dozen (12 Destructive Myths)
1. The brain remains a complete mystery — *false; huge recent strides.*
2. Older people are doomed to forget — *false; you gain vocabulary, judgment, emotional control with age.*
3. Dementia is inevitable with old age — *false; age-related change ≠ disease.*
4. Older people can't learn new things — *false; neurogenesis + plasticity persist; even Alzheimer's patients can learn.*
5. You must master one language before learning another — *false; bilingual kids gain language-structure knowledge.*
6. Memory training means you never forget — *false; "use it or lose it" applies.*
7. We use only 10% of our brains — *false; most of the brain is engaged even in simple tasks.*
8. Male/female brains dictate different learning ability/intelligence — *false; differences exist but not in capability.* (Note: 2/3 of Alzheimer's patients are women — reason still unknown.)
9. A crossword a day keeps the brain doctor away — *mostly false; crosswords flex only word-finding; real reserve needs broader challenge.*
10. You're either "right-brained" or "left-brained" — *false; hemispheres are intricately codependent.*
11. You have only five senses — *false; there are ~11 (add proprioception, equilibrioception, nociception, thermoception, chronoception, interoception).*
12. You're born with all your brain cells, the brain is hardwired, damage is always permanent — *false; neurogenesis + neuroplasticity disprove all three.*

## Key Concepts
- **Neurogenesis**: birth of new neurons; hippocampal neural stem cells continually replenished (Eriksson, 1998).
- **BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor)**: "Miracle-Gro for the brain" (Ratey) — nurtures neurogenesis, protects neurons, encourages synapse formation. Boosted by exercise, restorative sleep, stress reduction, sunlight, and fasting. Decreased in Alzheimer's.
- **SuperAgers**: 80+ people with memories as sharp as people 20–30 years younger; thick cortexes; not purely genetic — they keep sharp with good habits.
- **Negative plasticity**: bad habits and constant worry build neural maps that reinforce depression/anxiety.

## Mental Models
- The 10% myth reframed: the brain is like **a town** — homes and shops (10–20%) are in constant use, but the *roads* connecting them (the rest) are necessary even when not always busy.
- "Experience coupled with attention leads to physical changes... moment by moment we choose and sculpt how our ever-changing minds will work" (Merzenich). What you focus attention on physically rewires you.

## Anti-patterns
- **Fatalism about aging brains** — every myth above feeds it; the antidote is knowing the brain stays plastic.
- **Relying on crosswords/brain games alone** as a brain-health strategy (see Ch5 for why complexity + novelty + social matter more).

## Key Takeaways
1. Memorize the pillars: **Move, Discover, Relax, Nourish, Connect.**
2. Neurogenesis + neuroplasticity mean the brain can be built at any age.
3. Prevention is the most powerful antidote — and most risk factors besides age are modifiable.
4. What you repeatedly attend to becomes a structural neural trait — choose it deliberately.
