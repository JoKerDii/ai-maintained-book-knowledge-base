# Chapter 10: Diagnosing and Treating an Ailing Brain

## Core Idea
The key to treating dementia is prevention — and the same lifestyle strategies that reduce risk also improve quality of life once the disease is present. Early detection matters enormously (Alzheimer's starts 20–30 years before symptoms), and many "dementia" cases are actually treatable mimics. Aim for "asymptomatic Alzheimer's": even with plaques, no symptoms is a desirable outcome.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Stages of Alzheimer's** (Alzheimer's Association):
  - **Early / Mild**: functions independently; notices memory lapses (words, object locations); trouble with names, planning, work/social tasks.
  - **Middle / Moderate** (longest stage, years): confusing words, moodiness/withdrawal, forgetting personal history, incontinence, sleep changes, wandering, suspicion/delusions, needs more care.
  - **Late / Severe**: loses ability to converse, respond to environment, walk, sit, swallow; needs around-the-clock care; vulnerable to pneumonia.
- **The Top Ten Early Signs**: memory loss of recent events; mood/personality change; social withdrawal; misplacing things; difficulty with familiar tasks; confusion of time/place; poor judgment; struggling to communicate; vision changes; inability to plan/solve problems.
- **Isaacson's ABCs of prevention management**: **A**nthropometrics (body fat, muscle) · **B**iomarkers (cholesterol, inflammation, blood sugar, genes) · **C**ognitive performance — reassess every 6 months, adjust protocols.

## Key Concepts
- **Preclinical time**: the 20–30 year window between brain changes and symptoms — the prime opportunity to intervene.
- **APOE genotype**: E2 (rare, ~40% protective), E3 (neutral, most common), E4 (raises risk, lowers onset age). One E4 copy = 2–3x risk; two copies = ~12x. Testing is available but should be done with genetic counseling. "Lifestyle habits influence your brain's fate more than genetics."
- **No single diagnostic test** exists; MRI/CT/PET mostly *rule out* other causes. Pathologists disagree on how much plaque = Alzheimer's.
- **Dementia mimics (often treatable)**: **Normal Pressure Hydrocephalus (NPH)** — CSF buildup causing walking problems, incontinence, memory issues; treatable with a shunt (dramatic reversals possible). **Medications** — anticholinergics (Benadryl, some antidepressants, bladder/Parkinson's drugs) raise dementia risk ~50%+; opioids, benzodiazepines, steroids can mimic dementia. **Depression** (pseudodementia) — reversible when treated, but still raises later dementia risk.

## Mental Models
- **"Treat the patient, not the brain scan"** — the goal is a better patient, not a prettier image; asymptomatic-with-plaques is a win.
- **Research groupthink** is a hazard: once a theory (amyloid) gets funded, labs pile onto the same mechanism — need renegade approaches and shared data (the Dementia Discovery Fund).
- Prevention/early intervention as the highest-leverage investment — cure-hunting has siphoned resources from detection and coping strategies that also matter.

## Worked Example — The NPH reversal
A man carried an Alzheimer's diagnosis for two years before a third opinion found NPH (classic triad: gait/balance problems, urinary incontinence, memory difficulty). A lumbar drain test relieved symptoms within a day — he sat up, walked, felt "uncongested." A permanent shunt (diverting CSF to the abdomen) sustained the improvement. ~1 million people have NPH; fewer than 20% are properly diagnosed. Lesson: rule out treatable mimics before accepting an Alzheimer's verdict.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring symptoms** — 81% of those with worsening memory/confusion after 60 never consult a provider, losing time for intervention.
- **Accepting an Alzheimer's label without ruling out mimics** (NPH, medications, depression, thyroid, B12, etc.).
- **Long-term anticholinergic use** — 3+ years linked to 54% higher dementia risk; ask about alternatives.
- **Waiting for a miracle drug** — two phase-3 amyloid trials were halted in 2019; prevention is what we can control now.

## Key Takeaways
1. See a doctor about persistent memory/confusion changes — early detection buys intervention time.
2. Rule out treatable mimics (NPH, meds, depression) before accepting a dementia diagnosis.
3. Tell your doctor *every* drug and supplement you take; review anticholinergics.
4. Lifestyle beats genetics — even APOE4 carriers can shift their destiny.
5. Prevention doubles as treatment: the same habits reduce risk and improve life with the disease.
