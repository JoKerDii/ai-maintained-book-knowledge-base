# Patterns & Techniques — Keep Sharp

## The 5 Pillars (master framework)
**When to use**: any brain-health plan; the backbone of the whole book.
**How**: **M**ove · **D**iscover · **R**elax · **N**ourish · **C**onnect. One chapter each (Ch4–8), assembled in the 12-week program (Ch9).
**Trade-offs**: Move has the strongest evidence; the others are synergistic and reinforce each other.

## Exercise Prescription (Pillar: Move)
**When to use**: building or upgrading a fitness routine.
**How**: ≥150 min/week; 30 min cardio × 5 days (heart rate ≥50% above resting for ≥20 min); add interval + strength training; optimal-longevity dose ~450 min/week (much can be brisk walking). Sedentary start: 5–10 min burst (30 sec effort / 90 sec recovery) → 20 min × 3/week.
**Trade-offs**: weightlifting alone is insufficient — you must go aerobic. Break up sitting every hour (2-min moves).

## S.H.A.R.P. Eating (Pillar: Nourish)
**When to use**: designing brain-healthy meals.
**How**: **S**lash sugar (+ ABCs of food) · **H**ydrate smartly (drink instead of eat) · **A**dd omega-3s from food · **R**educe portions (cook at home, smaller plates) · **P**lan ahead (avoid starving → junk).
**Trade-offs**: a style, not a rigid diet; moderation beats elimination of whole food groups.

## The ABCs of Food
**When to use**: quick food triage at the store or on a menu.
**How**: **A-list** (regularly): leafy greens, berries, fish, healthy fats, nuts/seeds. **B-list** (include): beans, whole fruits, low-fat dairy, poultry, whole grains. **C-list** (limit): fried food, sweets, processed food, red meat, high-fat dairy, salt.

## Memory Diagnosis: Encode → Store → Retrieve
**When to use**: figuring out *why* memory fails and where to fix it.
**How**: Encoding needs attention (stop multitasking); storage needs sleep + no alcohol for short→long transfer; retrieval is trainable. "Bad memory" for names is usually attention or retrieval, not systemic failure.

## Analytical Meditation (Pillar: Relax)
**When to use**: solving a nagging problem; building focus; daily rest.
**How**: eyes closed, place the problem in a large clear bubble in front of you; rotate/flip it; let it disentangle from emotion until the answer illuminates. First 2 min are hardest, then flow.
**Trade-offs**: different from single-point meditation; better for problem-solvers/skeptics.

## Deep Breathing / Relaxation Response
**When to use**: fast stress relief anywhere.
**How**: sit relaxed, inhale through nose filling the diaphragm, take in a bit more at the top, exhale slowly to a count of 20; ≥5 rounds. Triggers parasympathetic response, lowers cortisol.

## Pomodoro Technique (productivity)
**When to use**: focused work, beating procrastination.
**How**: pick your most important task, 25 min no-distraction work, 5 min break (dopamine reward), repeat.

## Don't Multitask — "Tackle Your Day Like a Surgeon"
**When to use**: any cognitively demanding work.
**How**: the brain only *switches* attention, it doesn't parallel-process. Single-task in focused sequences; guard mornings ("golden time") for creative work; avoid email first thing.

## Marbles-and-Sand Planning
**When to use**: weekly/daily prioritization.
**How**: put the marbles (big commitments — exercise, sleep, key tasks) in the jar first; let sand (email, nonurgent items) fill gaps. Sunday-night check-in: "What must I accomplish this week to call it a success?"

## The Triple Threat (Pillar: Connect)
**When to use**: combining social + physical + emotional benefit in one act.
**How**: take a walk + with a friend/neighbor + talk through what worries you. Add appropriate touch (hand-holding lowers cortisol).

## Social Engagement + Challenge
**When to use**: maximizing the brain benefit of socializing.
**How**: socialize *around a challenging shared activity* (class, quilting, photography), not passive chit-chat; keep a large, cross-age network and ≥1 reliable confidante.

## Cognitive Reserve Building (Pillar: Discover)
**When to use**: choosing genuinely brain-building activities.
**How**: pick complex + novel + effortful + ideally social learning you *enjoy* (language, instrument, real class). Avoid crosswords-only and hyped "brain games." Define your ikigai; seek flow.

## Intermittent Fasting / Chrono Eating
**When to use**: reducing calories, boosting BDNF and insulin sensitivity (with doctor's OK).
**How**: gentle 12-hr overnight fast (stop 7–8 p.m., resume 9–10 a.m.); 16-hr version skips breakfast. Frontload calories: "breakfast like a king, dinner like a peasant."
**Trade-offs**: not for everyone (esp. diabetics — consult doctor).

## Sleep Hygiene (Top 10 Secrets, Pillar: Relax)
**When to use**: fixing poor sleep.
**How**: fixed wake time; sleep before midnight; morning light; no caffeine after 2 p.m.; no food 3 hrs pre-bed; cool/dark/quiet (60–67°F); no screens/blue light; 30–60 min calming ritual; know the warning signs → sleep study.

## Rule out Dementia Mimics
**When to use**: before accepting a dementia diagnosis.
**How**: evaluate NPH (treatable with shunt), medications (anticholinergics, opioids, benzos, steroids), and depression (pseudodementia). Tell the doctor every drug/supplement.

## Post-Diagnosis Planning Checklist (caregivers)
**When to use**: immediately after a dementia diagnosis.
**How**: line up support/education programs, early-stage engagement, clinical trials, home safety, legal plan (will/trust/POA/advance directive), financial plan, and a care team. Communicate with family early to avoid conservatorship.

## The Cardinal Caregiving Rule
**When to use**: interacting with someone who has dementia.
**How**: never correct them. Early phase: gently guide with questions. Later: don't deny their reality; redirect. Use music (longest-surviving faculty) and touch; break repetition with an activity. Put yourself on your own to-do list.
