# Glossary — Four Thousand Weeks

**Active patience** — Patience reframed not as passive resignation but as an "almost muscular state of alert presence." (Ch 11)

**Art of creative neglect** — The decision discipline of deliberately, calmly choosing what *not* to do; treating neglect as a skill, not a failure. (Ch 4)

**Atelic activity** — An activity with no end point or external goal, valuable in itself (a walk, listening to music). Contrast *telic*. (Ch 9, via Kieran Setiya)

**Attention economy** — The system in which companies profit by capturing your finite attention, incentivized to show you enraging or distracting material. (Ch 5)

**Being-toward-death** — Heidegger's idea that authentic living means facing mortality rather than fleeing it; finitude defines human existence. (Ch 3)

**Buffett's 25/5 rule** — List 25 ambitions, keep the top 5, and *actively avoid* the other 20 as traps. (Ch 4, Appendix tool 3)

**Causal catastrophe** — Valuing present activities only for what they cause/lead to, never for themselves — so the present is endlessly sacrificed to a future that never arrives. (Ch 8)

**Clock time** — Treating time as an abstract, measurable resource separate from life, to be spent efficiently; the modern default. Contrast *deep time*. (Ch 1)

**Closed / open lists** — A two-list system: an unbounded "open" list of everything, and a "closed" list capped (~10) you can only add to by completing an item. (Appendix tool 1)

**Consolidate your caring** — Deliberately narrowing the causes/worries you engage so your finite capacity for care makes a real difference. (Appendix tool 5)

**Conveyor belt of time** — Edward Hall's image: time as a belt delivering containers (hours/weeks) we must fill; becoming "productive" just speeds the belt up. (Ch 0–1)

**Cosmic insignificance therapy** — The relief of recognizing your life matters far less on a cosmic scale than your ego demands, freeing you to live a meaningful human-scale life. (Ch 13)

**Creative neglect** — See *art of creative neglect*. (Ch 4)

**Deep time** — The expansive, timeless absorption in reality that comes from forgetting the abstract yardstick of the clock ("living in deep time," Richard Rohr). (Ch 1)

**Decide what to fail at** — *Strategic underachievement*: pre-selecting domains where you'll deliberately not seek excellence. (Appendix tool 3)

**Done list** — A list that starts empty each day and fills with what you accomplished, countering "productivity debt." (Appendix tool 4)

**Efficiency trap** — The pattern where becoming more efficient surfaces more demands rather than freeing time. (Ch 2)

**Eigenzeit** — German for "the time inherent to a process itself"; letting things take the time they take. (Ch 1)

**Fixed-volume approach** — Designing your work around hard, predetermined limits (work hours, list caps) instead of trying to expand capacity. (Appendix tool 1)

**FOMO / JOMO** — Fear of missing out vs. *joy* of missing out; since missing out is guaranteed, it's what makes choices meaningful. (Ch 3–4)

**Helsinki Bus Station Theory** — Arno Minkkinen's parable: early work resembles others' until you "stay on the bus" long enough for your route to diverge into originality. (Ch 11)

**Human disease, the** — The compulsion to demand certainty and cosmic reassurance about the future. (Ch 14)

**Impatience spiral** — The self-reinforcing loop where demanding speed erodes our tolerance for slowness, making everything feel more frustrating. (Ch 10)

**Instantaneous generosity** — Acting on a generous impulse immediately rather than deferring it (the only donation that counts is the one you make). (Appendix tool 9)

**Instrumentalizing time** — Treating each moment merely as a means to a future end. (Ch 8)

**Intimate interrupter, the** — The inner source of distraction: we flee toward distraction to escape the discomfort of a finite, difficult task. (Ch 6)

**Joyless urgency** — Marilynne Robinson's phrase for the modern compulsion to always be getting more done. (Ch 0–1)

**Limit-embracing life** — Organizing your days knowing you definitely can't do everything, and making hard choices consciously. (Ch 1)

**Paradox of limitation** — The more you chase total control over time, the worse life gets; the more you confront finitude, the better it gets. (Ch 1, the book's thesis)

**Pay yourself first (with time)** — Do your most-valued activity today, first, instead of hoping for leftover time. (Ch 4, Appendix tool 1)

**Practice doing nothing** — Training the capacity to not-act, so you stop making poor choices just to feel like you're acting. (Appendix tool 10)

**Radical incrementalism** — Robert Boice's finding: sustained creative output comes from short, fixed daily sessions, stopping on time. (Ch 11)

**Researcher in relationships** — Adopting curiosity (not a desired outcome) toward other people and the future. (Appendix tool 8)

**Rocks-in-the-jar lie** — Covey's "big rocks first" parable is rigged: reality has more big rocks than any jar can hold. (Ch 4)

**Serialize** — Work one big project at a time, finishing before starting the next. (Appendix tool 2)

**Sisyphus's inbox** — The endlessly refilling task list; the work is never done by design. (Ch 2)

**Strategic underachievement** — See *decide what to fail at*. (Appendix tool 3)

**Task orientation** — Pre-clock way of living where rhythms emerge from tasks themselves, not an abstract timeline. (Ch 1)

**Telic activity** — An activity aimed at a goal/end point, valued for its completion. Contrast *atelic*. (Ch 9)

**Work in progress (WIP) limit** — Capping active projects (≈3) so new work waits until a slot frees. (Ch 4, *Personal Kanban*)

**You are time** — We don't *have* time as a possession; our existence *is* time, so "managing" it as a resource is a category error. (Ch 3)
