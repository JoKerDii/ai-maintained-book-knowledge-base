# Patterns & Techniques — Four Thousand Weeks

The book's concrete, repeatable practices. Most come from Chapter 4, Chapter 11, and the Appendix. These are *practices, not insights* — pick one or two and run them this week.

## The Two-List System (fixed-volume productivity)
**When to use**: your to-do list is bottomless and you never feel "on top of it."
**How**: Keep an **open list** (everything, however long) and a **closed list** capped at ~10. You may only move a task from open→closed when you complete one already on the closed list. Optionally add an "on hold" list for items awaiting others.
**Trade-offs**: forces conscious prioritization and a felt sense of completion; requires accepting the open list will never empty.

## Predetermined Time Boundaries
**When to use**: work expands to fill all available hours.
**How**: Decide *in advance* your start and stop times (e.g., start by 8:30, hard stop 5:30). Let the fixed container force wiser choices. (Newport: "you could fill any arbitrary number of hours with what feels to be productive work.")
**Trade-offs**: the constraint is the whole point; resist the urge to extend "just this once."

## Pay Yourself First (with time)
**When to use**: a high-value pursuit keeps getting deferred to "leftover" time.
**How**: Do an hour of your most-valued work *first*, before email/meetings. Block recurring calendar "meetings" with yourself. *Claim* time; don't try to *find* it — spare time never arrives.
**Trade-offs**: something else gets less of you, on purpose.

## Limit Work in Progress (WIP ≈ 3)
**When to use**: you keep starting things and bouncing between them when one gets hard.
**How**: Cap active projects at ~3. New demands wait in a queue until a slot frees by completion *or deliberate abandonment*. Name slots as next achievable steps ("draft ch4 outline"), not giants ("write book").
**Trade-offs**: from *Personal Kanban*; converts the false comfort of many open projects into real completion.

## Buffett's 25/5
**When to use**: too many moderately attractive options compete for your finite life.
**How**: List your top 25 ambitions, rank them, circle the top 5. The other 20 are **avoid-at-all-costs**, not a someday list — they're appealing enough to distract, not important enough to build a life on.
**Trade-offs**: the hardest "no" is to things you genuinely want.

## Serialize
**When to use**: anxiety tempts you to start everything at once.
**How**: One big project at a time (at most one work + one non-work). Consciously postpone everything else and tolerate the resulting anxiety. Only truly essential maintenance (bills, school run) is exempt.
**Trade-offs**: feels worse short-term, finishes more long-term.

## Decide in Advance What to Fail At (strategic underachievement)
**When to use**: "work-life balance" feels impossible because everything demands excellence.
**How**: Pre-select domains to deliberately neglect (lawn, tidy kitchen) with zero shame. In essential domains, fail *cyclically* — bare minimum at work for two months while you focus on kids, then switch.
**Trade-offs**: replaces impossible balance with deliberate, confident imbalance.

## Keep a "Done List"
**When to use**: you can never feel good because work is never finished ("productivity debt").
**How**: Start each day with an empty list; log what you actually accomplish. In a rut, lower the bar ("made coffee" counts). Leverages the motivating power of "small wins."
**Trade-offs**: a reframe, not more output — but small wins compound.

## Radical Incrementalism (Boice's method)
**When to use**: long-horizon creative/knowledge work prone to bingeing and burnout.
**How**: A fixed, modest daily block (Boice's academics: sometimes 10 min, never >4 hrs). Show up every day; **stop when the block ends, even mid-flow**, to preserve the appetite to return. Take weekends off.
**Trade-offs**: slower on any single day, far more sustainable over a career; beats all-day binges.

## Stay on the Bus (Helsinki Bus Station)
**When to use**: your work looks derivative and you're tempted to start over in a new direction.
**How**: Assume you simply haven't ridden far enough; keep committing to your route. Routes diverge into originality only past the shared early stretch of imitation and skill-building.
**Trade-offs**: requires enduring a long unoriginal phase; restarting resets you to the station.

## Consolidate Your Caring
**When to use**: social media makes you feel you must care about every crisis at once.
**How**: Pick your battles — devote spare capacity to a couple of causes for a couple of years. Not because others don't matter, but because real impact needs focus.
**Trade-offs**: deliberately ignoring worthy causes; the attention economy pressures every cause to claim uniqueness.

## Make Technology Boring
**When to use**: focused work keeps losing to one-swipe-away distraction.
**How**: Remove social apps; set the phone to grayscale (Settings > Accessibility > Color Filters); use single-purpose devices (Kindle). Strip the seductive escape from limitation out of your tools.
**Trade-offs**: less convenience, more reclaimed attention.

## Seek Novelty in the Mundane
**When to use**: years feel like they're accelerating and collapsing.
**How**: Don't cram in exotic experiences — pay *deeper attention* to ordinary life. New route to work, unplanned walks, photography, journaling, "I Spy" with a kid. Novel attention lays down memory.
**Trade-offs**: subtle; works through presence, not novelty-chasing.

## Be a Researcher in Relationships
**When to use**: you're trying to control how time with others (or the future) unfolds.
**How**: In a hard or boring moment, switch to curiosity — "who is this person?" Curiosity is satisfied whether they please you or not; a demand for outcomes is frustrated each time. Choose curiosity over worry about the future too.
**Trade-offs**: relinquishes control of outcomes.

## Instantaneous Generosity
**When to use**: a generous impulse arises (give, praise, check in).
**How**: Act *now*, imperfectly. The donation/message you actually send beats the perfect one you never do. We defer not from meanness but from control-seeking.
**Trade-offs**: none worth mentioning — it also makes you happier immediately.

## "Do Nothing" Meditation
**When to use**: you can't tolerate not-acting, so you make poor choices just to feel active.
**How**: Set a 5–10 min timer; sit; stop trying to do anything. Each time you notice you're "doing" something (even thinking, or focusing on the breath), stop doing it. Keep stopping until the timer ends.
**Trade-offs**: rebuilds autonomy from the urge to evade how reality feels right now.
