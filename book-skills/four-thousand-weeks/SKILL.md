## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The Four Thousand Weeks frame.** An 80-year life is ~4,000 weeks. Treat this not as motivation to cram more in, but as the fact that reframes every choice. Time is not a resource you *have* — you *are* your time.

**The Paradox of Limitation (the book's spine).** The more you try to master time to gain total control and freedom from human limits, the more stressful, empty, and frustrating life gets. The more you confront finitude and work *with* it, the more productive, meaningful, and joyful life becomes. Apply: when overwhelmed, stop optimizing and start accepting limits.

**The Efficiency Trap.** Becoming more efficient doesn't create free time — it surfaces more demands. Clearing the decks makes them refill faster (Inbox Zero → more email). Don't try to get on top of everything; you never will, by design. Decide which balls to *consciously* drop.

**Embrace finitude / "It's worth doing badly."** Since you can't do everything, the real skill is **choosing what to neglect** — and feeling fine about it. Every "yes" is the sacrifice of countless alternatives; that sacrifice is what makes a choice *mean* something. FOMO is structurally guaranteed, so trade it for JOMO (joy of missing out).

**The Art of Creative Neglect (Ch 4) — decide what to fail at, on purpose:**
1. **Pay yourself first (with time)** — do your most-valued work *today, first*, not with leftover time that never comes.
2. **Limit work in progress** — cap active projects at ~3; new work waits until a slot frees.
3. **Resist middling priorities (Buffett's 25/5)** — list 25 ambitions, keep the top 5, *actively avoid the other 20*. They're traps, not a backlog.

**The Three Principles of Patience (Ch 11) — patience as power, not passivity:**
1. **Develop a taste for having problems** — problems are the substance of a meaningful life, not interruptions to it.
2. **Embrace radical incrementalism** (Boice) — work in small, fixed daily stints; *stop on time even when energy remains* to keep the appetite to return.
3. **Originality lies on the far side of unoriginality** — endure the long derivative apprenticeship. "Stay on the bus" (Helsinki Bus Station Theory): your route only diverges from everyone else's if you stay on it.

**Attention is life (Ch 5–6).** What you pay attention to *is* your life. Distraction isn't stolen from you by tech alone; you flee toward it (the "intimate interrupter") to escape the discomfort of confronting a hard, finite task. Guarding attention is guarding life itself.

**You are here / stop instrumentalizing the present (Ch 7–8).** We treat every present moment as a means to a future payoff ("when I finally…"), so satisfaction never arrives — the future is a fantasy we never inhabit. The "causal catastrophe": valuing activities only for what they lead to. Hike the trail, not just the summit.

**Cosmic Insignificance Therapy (Ch 13).** Relief comes from realizing your life matters far less, on a cosmic scale, than your ego demands. Lower the bar from "world-historical impact" to a meaningful human-scale life; the grandiose standard is a trap.

**Beyond hope / the human disease (Ch 14, Afterword).** The "human disease" is the compulsion for certainty and reassurance about the future. Hope can be a covert demand that the future conform to our wishes. Freedom is acting wholeheartedly *without* guarantees — "I don't mind what happens" (Krishnamurti).

## Scope & Limits

This skill covers the ideas in *Four Thousand Weeks* — a philosophy of finitude, not a step-by-step productivity system. Burkeman deliberately rejects "fit it all in" optimization; apply these frameworks to *choose and accept* limits, not to maximize throughput. For implementing specific tactics in your own life or tools, combine with your own context.
