## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The thesis**: AI's economic impact comes not from how well it performs a task but from **how it restructures the system around that task**. Stop asking "How smart is it?" or "What can we automate?" Ask: *what does this system look like once it adopts the machine's logic?* Judging AI by how human it seems is the **intelligence distraction**; solving today's problem with yesterday's system model is the **framing error**.

**AI is a coordination technology, not (mainly) an automation technology.** Automation lowers the cost of executing tasks and collapses their economic value. **Coordination** restructures workflows and changes which tasks matter at all. Historically coordination technologies (the shipping container, the barcode) redrew whole economies. AI's distinctive addition: it can **coordinate without consensus**.

### The mechanism: unbundling → rebundling
When a technology removes an old constraint, the bundle (a job, a product, a workflow, a value chain) breaks into parts — **unbundling** — and is reassembled around whatever constraint now binds — **rebundling**. Track constraints, not tasks. The constraint never disappears; it *moves*.

### The Five Factors of Coordination Power (Ch 3) — use to find who really holds power
1. **Representation** — whose map of reality is authoritative? (Systems can only act on what they can see; *representation determines reward*.)
2. **Decision** — who chooses?
3. **Execution** — who acts, on whose trigger?
4. **Composition** — who defines how parts (and jobs) are bundled?
5. **Governance** — who sets metrics and rules of participation?

### The Three-Circle Model (Ch 2) — always analyze at three levels
The task-level **job** sits inside the **organizational system of work**, which sits inside the **competitive ecosystem**. A task can survive at circle 1 and the role still die at circle 2 — the **typist effect**: typing exploded after word processors; the typist's role vanished because the logic that justified it did.

### For individuals: the Three Types of Constraints (Ch 5)
Value flows to whoever resolves the binding constraint:
- **Scarcity-based** (rare knowledge or access) — AI dissolves these fastest. Do not build here.
- **Risk-based** (errors carry serious consequences) — value grows; own **judgment** and accountability.
- **Coordination-based** (many misaligned moving parts) — value grows most; own the seams.

**The Four Rebundling Questions**: (1) What is getting unbundled? (2) What constraints does that create that the new system doesn't manage well? (3) How can the role be reconfigured to manage them? (4) Which complementary capabilities should be rebundled around it?
Then check the **Four Conditions for Economic Visibility**: the work must be **visible**, **scarce**, **leveraged** (you can negotiate), and **close to the transaction**. You are not paid for being essential — you are paid for being *recognized* as essential.

**Prefer the constraint question over the skill question.** The **reskilling fallacy** is believing new skills protect you when the system no longer needs human execution. Don't ask what AI can't do; ask what it *breaks*.

**Three Forms of Value (Ch 4)**: **intrinsic** (meaning), **economic** (what someone pays — requires scarcity), **contextual** (leverage within the workflow). Automation attacks economic value; coordination attacks contextual value. Meaningful work can be worthless; check both **economic and contextual displacement**.

**Above-the-Algorithm vs. Below-the-Algorithm** is the real modern divide — do you set the rules and use the system for leverage, or does it allocate, price, and evaluate you? Beware the **human-in-the-loop illusion**: supervising AI is not the same as deciding.

### For organizations (Ch 6)
The binding constraint in knowledge work is **coordination, not execution**. The **coordination tax** — meetings, chasing context, search, rework — grows exponentially with scale and accrues across three layers: **encoding** knowledge, **organizing** it, **deploying** it into decisions. Use AI as **coordination infrastructure** and the **autonomy-coordination trade-off** stops being a seesaw. Deploy unevenly and you trigger the **coordination paradox**: local automation fragments the org and *raises* coordination cost.

### For firms and ecosystems
**AI as a tool vs. AI as an engine (Ch 8)**: a tool is swappable without redesigning anything — it helps you compete better at the old game. An **engine** determines system performance and the business is designed around it — it changes the terms of competition. Build-vs-buy test: *what happens if you lose access to this?* Inconvenienced → rent. Crippled → own.
**The integration trap**: tools commoditize execution → absorb expertise → become the center you reorganize around → **performance-based lock-in**, and you are a **wrapper**. Tool providers win structurally via **learning advantage**, **scope expansion** (horizontal rebundling + vertical encroachment), and **clockspeed**.

**Tool vs. Solution (Ch 9)**: A **solution** is accessible, usable, and **reliable** — it delivers predictable performance in unpredictable conditions by absorbing risk. Customers pay for **risk absorption**, not capability. When adoption stalls, the problem is a surrounding constraint (financing, workflow, missing complements), not the tool — improving the tool cannot fix it. Be the **system builder**. Escalate pricing deliberately: **work- → results- → outcomes-as-a-service**, only as far as you can measure and absorb liability.

**Control points (Ch 10, 11)**: Power comes from **dependence, not dominance** — from solving the coordination problem others can't. A real control point needs all three layers: **unified representation** (shared source of truth / digital twin) → **unified decision** → **unified execution**. Demand-side control points are earned by absorbing customers' **decision friction**; supply-side by helping partners coordinate.
**Coordination without consensus (Ch 1, 11)**: where actors will never agree on standards, don't standardize — *interpret*. AI reads each actor's native, unstructured inputs and generates shared meaning; standards become implicit rather than explicit. CCC coordinates through standardization; Tractable coordinates through interpretation. This breaks the ecosystem **cold start problem** by creating value before consensus.

### Strategy (Ch 12)
**You don't need an AI strategy — you need a strategy for the conditions AI creates.** Return to **where to play** and **how to win**: *coordination determines where to play* (it redraws viable positions), *control determines how to win* (identify emerging constraints and resolve them better than anyone). Winning comes from **coordinating what's stuck**, not owning what's scarce.
**Four strategic postures**: **reactive optimizer** (speed up today's tasks) → **anticipator** ("Gretzky": spot value migration, same game) → **logic shifter** ("Curry": change where and how decisions are made) → **field reshaper** ("Tiger": restructure the ecosystem). Deeper reimagining of coordination = more enduring advantage.
**Three traps**: chasing transient advantage (Chegg), building executional agility in the wrong direction (Yahoo), perpetual experimentation. All three optimize *within* the old system.
