# Chapter 5: Rebundling the Job — Why reskilling is a losing game in a system that's already changed

## Core Idea
Jobs exist to resolve constraints in a system of work, not to hold tasks; when AI dissolves a constraint, the constraint moves rather than disappears, and value moves with it. The winning move is not to chase skills AI cannot yet perform but to identify the new constraint and rebundle complementary capabilities around it.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The Three Types of Constraints (scarcity-based, risk-based, coordination-based)**: Scarcity-based constraints arise when something essential is limited or hard to access, and value accrues to whoever controls or allocates it (the ship's Morse radio operator). Risk-based constraints arise where actions carry serious consequences, and value accrues to whoever reliably manages uncertainty and owns the outcome (the anesthesiologist). Coordination-based constraints arise where many moving parts must align, and value flows to whoever holds them together (the film producer).
  - When to use: to work out where value is migrating in any system after a technology lands — for your own role, a team design, or a business model.
  - How: name the constraint the current role was built to resolve; ask whether AI has removed it; then ask what became riskier or harder to coordinate as activity accelerated. Structure your role around that.
  - Why it works: AI removes constraints on the availability and accessibility of skilled labor, and two new constraints predictably emerge in their place — risk and coordination. Removing a bottleneck exposes others; what got simpler is visible, what got riskier is not.

- **The Four Rebundling Questions**: (1) What is getting unbundled? (2) What constraints does that create that the new system of work doesn't effectively manage? (3) How can workers reconfigure their roles to manage those constraints? (4) Which complementary capabilities can they rebundle so the work holds value?
  - When to use: as the redesign sequence for any role or business model facing AI-driven commoditization.
  - How: start with the system, not the skill. Don't ask what AI can't do; ask what it *breaks*. New skills may or may not be required — skills only carry value in relation to the constraint they resolve.
  - Failure mode: chasing skills without locating the friction makes you very good at something that no longer matters.

- **The Four Conditions for Economic Visibility**: Contextual value only converts into pay when the work is (1) visible — the system can see and recognize it; (2) scarce — hard to substitute or replicate; (3) leveraged — the actors can negotiate their value; and (4) close to where pricing decisions or transactions are made.
  - When to use: after you have rebundled a role, to check whether it will actually be rewarded.
  - How: audit the redesigned role against all four; fix the weakest. You don't get paid for being essential, you get paid for being *recognized* as essential.

- **Curiosity and Curation**: When answers become abundant, value migrates upstream to framing the right inquiry (curiosity — the disciplined ability to identify performance bottlenecks and frame the question before chasing answers) and downstream to deciding which answers deserve action (curation — deciding what to elevate and what to exclude, exercising narrative control).

## Key Concepts
- **Constraint**: the limiting factor that determines how quickly or effectively the rest of the system can operate; the greater its limiting power, the higher the value of managing it.
- **Reskilling fallacy**: the belief that learning new skills is sufficient protection when the system no longer needs human execution of the task.
- **Rebundling**: rebuilding the role forward around a new constraint, recombining overlooked or undervalued tasks into a new bundle — not preserving the old bundle.
- **Judgment**: the capacity to act under uncertainty while fully accountable for the outcome; not intuition, and not scoreable — it is trusted because of past irreversible decisions made at real risk.
- **Process-level vs. system-level coordination**: process-level work stops things falling through the cracks; system-level work rearchitects the workflow so the cracks never exist, and commands the highest economic value.
- **Economic visibility**: the extent to which a role's contribution is recognized and rewarded by the system.
- **System-aware adaptation**: tracking how constraints move as systems evolve and aligning your role to the new frictions, rather than chasing skills or reacting to automation.
- **Narrative control**: curation that directs what people act on, not merely what they see — where algorithmic curation stops and human curation earns a premium.
- **Value migrates to complements**: when the core good is commoditized, economic value shifts to the capabilities that sit around it.

## Mental Models
- Think of your job as a response to a constraint, not a bundle of tasks — when the constraint collapses, the logic for the role no longer holds.
- Use "what does AI break in the system?" in place of "what can AI not do?" — the first points at new constraints, the second at a shrinking hiding place.
- Think of narrow AI as accelerating the parts and broad human intelligence as holding the whole together; siloed optimization manufactures coordination gaps.
- Use the trap test: you resist having your own job described as tasks, yet find it plausible for jobs you don't understand — because you can't see the constraints they resolve.
- Think of curation as proactive unbundling: a tool for shedding tasks that are losing value and claiming higher-value ones.

## Anti-patterns
- **The reskilling treadmill**: pivoting to the next skill AI hasn't mastered is a race without a finish line; magicians who try to outpace the trick-deconstructors lose the same way.
- **Assuming proximity to the customer equals power**: RevOps looked like back-office work and was more strategic than any front-office function. Power comes from resolving the system's constraints.
- **Looking for risk inside the technology**: model accuracy and cybersecurity matter, but the enduring risk roles emerged because refrigeration and elevators worked *too well* and transformed the systems around them.
- **Solving a constraint invisibly**: the Tuareg guides made the Sahara salt trade possible yet captured almost none of its value — fragmented, uncoordinated, and far from the point of value capture. Care work is on the same trajectory.
- **Treating AI predictions as truths**: optimizing narrowly on probabilistic output produces the U.S. hospital algorithm that ranked Black patients lower because it optimized historical spend rather than need, and Amazon pricing agents that drove a textbook to $23.7 million.

## Worked Example
Hospitals digitized care with electronic health records and billing automation. Individual tasks got faster and cleaner — doctors had richer data, administrators had better billing — but responsibility for the patient's journey fragmented across screens and departments. What had been coordinated informally through memory and conversation now fell through the gaps, especially in chronic care. Some nurses rebundled their role into nurse navigators: guiding patients through appointments and departments, translating complex care plans into plain language, resolving the logistical and insurance obstacles that block access. The role resolved three constraints — fragmented ownership of the journey, incoherent pathways across optimizing specialists, and wildly varying patient needs — and then layered on complementary capabilities in logistics, advisory, and patient advocacy. Results were measurable: better patient experience, fewer readmissions, improved treatment adherence. As AI enters radiology and monitoring, navigators now ensure AI-driven findings trigger the right follow-up.

## Key Takeaways
1. Don't chase the skill, follow the constraint — start with the system, find what is now scarce, risk-prone, or hard to coordinate, then evaluate your capabilities against it.
2. Run the four rebundling questions on your own role before the next tool lands, not after.
3. Rebundle complementary capabilities around the constraint in ways that increase differentiation, deepen trust, and tie the role closer to outcomes.
4. Position yourself where liability sits: roles will command value for the risk they assume or help manage, not the tasks they perform.
5. Prefer system-level coordination to process-level firefighting — one architectural decision creates leverage across thousands of future executions.
6. Make the value visible, scarce, leveraged, and close to the point of transaction, or you will be a Tuareg guide.
7. Invest in curiosity and curation: framing the right inquiry (the Wright brothers solving control before power) and exercising narrative control over which answers get acted on.
