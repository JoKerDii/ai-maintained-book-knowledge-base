# Introduction: Why Unintelligent AI Matters

## Core Idea
AI's economic impact comes not from how intelligent it is but from how it restructures the system it enters — changing how decisions are made, attention is allocated, and value is captured. Stop asking "how smart is the machine?" and start asking what your systems look like once they adopt the machine's logic.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Five Functions of AI (sense, model, reason, act, learn)**: Treat any AI — Google Maps or ChatGPT — as a narrow practical utility that senses its environment, builds a working model of it, reasons and acts on that model, and learns to update the model.
  - When to use: Evaluating whether an AI capability will actually change a workflow, and where it will break.
  - How: For the target system, name each of the five: What does it observe? What model of the world does it hold? What reasoning rules constrain it? What actions can it take? What feedback loop updates it? Gaps in any one predict the failure point.
  - Why it works: It replaces the unanswerable "is it intelligent?" with five answerable design questions, and it exposes constraints as deliberate performance choices — Google Maps is reliable *because* it senses limited inputs and holds a simple model.
- **The Intelligence Distraction**: The habit of judging AI by how human it seems rather than by what it does, which keeps you from seeing its systemic implications.
  - When to use: Whenever a debate stalls on consciousness, creativity, hallucinations, or AGI timelines.
  - How: Replace the question with "Is it effective at what it's supposed to do?" then move immediately to "What does this change about how we coordinate?"
  - Failure mode: Both hype and dismissal come from the same error; skeptics reject working applications because AGI hasn't arrived, optimists over-promise because it might.
- **System-first analysis (not technology-first)**: Start with the system's moving parts, constraints, and logic; then ask how the technology changes those foundations.
  - When to use: Before any AI adoption decision or investment case.
  - How: Map the system's current interactions and constraints, identify which constraint the tool removes, then trace second-order effects on structure, business model, and competition.

## Key Concepts
- **Intelligence distraction**: Searching for human-like traits in AI instead of assessing its actual economic capability.
- **Pattern prediction**: The single underlying mechanism — statistical pattern-matching over vast data — behind language, vision, and generation, unlike human reasoning.
- **Selective attention and memory**: What distinguishes modern AI from older software; it prioritizes what matters now using prior context.
- **Constraints as design choices**: Deliberate narrowing of inputs, model, and reasoning rules that makes a tool reliable rather than weak.
- **Tacit knowledge**: Valuable organizational knowledge buried in meetings, emails, and conversations that historically resisted scaling or transfer.
- **Institutional infrastructure**: AI's real role — shaping how organizations manage knowledge, allocate attention, and therefore decide.
- **Unbundling and rebundling**: Breaking systems into components and reassembling them under a new logic; the book's central framework.
- **Systems bottleneck**: The claim that institutions, governance models, and workflows — not model accuracy — are what hold value back.

## Mental Models
- Think of AI as a map, not a lens: maps don't just describe space, they transform it (colonial borders, redlining, Google Maps rerouting traffic through quiet streets).
- Use the "mundane task" test: judge a capability by what it restructures, not by how impressive it looks — AI note-taking converts tacit talk into structured decisions, which is more consequential than it appears.
- Think of today's AI as the least performant you will ever use; design systems for the capability curve, not the current snapshot.
- Use system framing when a technology is described as "just a tool bolted onto" existing processes — that's the signal advantage is being left on the table.

## Anti-patterns
- **Technological solutionism**: Deploying a precise tool into a system whose hidden fault lines, incentives, and non-compliance you never modeled.
- **Benchmark chasing**: Measuring progress on complex-task performance while mundane-task transformation quietly restructures the organization.
- **Bolting AI onto old assumptions**: Adopting features and hoping for advantage; sustainable advantage comes from restructuring, not layering.

## Worked Example
Singapore, spring 2021: the global model for pandemic control — sealed borders, obsessive contact tracing, a vaccine rollout underway. Then cases surged. The chain ran through KTV lounges, adult entertainment venues where hostesses moved room to room. Patient Zero had entered on the Familial Ties Lane, the one visa category still open, and was working illegally. The lounges protected their discreet clientele and ignored contact tracing; clients hopped between venues, then went home to families and offices. When the outbreak broke, reputational shame kept infected men from coming forward for testing. Nothing technical failed — the vaccine worked, the tracing app worked. The tools assumed compliance and visibility; the real system supplied neither. The failure was systemic: an invisible entry point, a compliance-avoidant transmission space, and a shame-driven reporting gap. Precision policy plus accurate technology lost to unmapped system interdependencies.

## Key Takeaways
1. Ask "is it effective at what it's supposed to do?" — never "does it think like us?"
2. Diagnose any AI deployment through sense, model, reason, act, learn.
3. Expect the largest organizational changes from the most mundane transformed tasks, not the most impressive ones.
4. Treat constraints in an AI tool as reliability features, not deficiencies.
5. Use AI first to convert tacit organizational knowledge into explicit, reusable form.
6. Assume small interventions produce large, unintended shifts in how work is coordinated — audit what your AI summarizes, since that shapes what gets decided.
7. Start every strategy conversation with the system, not the technology; the technology will change, the system logic endures.
