# Patterns — Reshuffle (Sangeet Paul Choudary)

## System-First Diagnosis
**When to use**: Before any AI initiative, roadmap, or "AI strategy" request.
**How**: Work outside-in, not inside-out. (1) Has the competitive landscape changed? (2) Has the basis of value creation moved? (3) Does the business model still make sense? (4) What capabilities does the new game require? (5) Which roles and workflows still matter? Only then ask what AI should do. Never start from "what can we automate?"
**Trade-offs**: Slower to first visible win; leadership impatient for demos. But task-first programs produce motion without direction — Yahoo was agile in the wrong direction.

## Three-Circle Analysis
**When to use**: Assessing exposure of a job, product, or firm to AI.
**How**: Analyze at three nested levels — the task-level job, the organizational system it sits in, the competitive ecosystem that system sits in. A task can survive at level 1 and still be eliminated at level 2 (typist effect) or level 3.
**Trade-offs**: Requires information about the ecosystem you may not own; but skipping it produces the framing error of defending tasks in a system that no longer needs the role.

## Locate the Moving Constraint
**When to use**: Repositioning a role, team, or firm when AI erodes existing value.
**How**: Classify what your value currently rests on — scarcity-based (rare access/knowledge), risk-based (high-consequence errors), or coordination-based (aligning many moving parts). AI collapses scarcity fastest. Ask where friction has *moved to* in the new system, then rebundle capabilities around that new constraint. Re-run periodically: constraints move.
**Trade-offs**: You may have to abandon the skill that earned your reputation. Reskilling into an adjacent scarce skill is the trap this pattern replaces.

## Unbundle-Then-Rebundle a Role
**When to use**: A job whose core task has been economically collapsed by AI.
**How**: (1) Decompose the role into tasks. (2) Mark each task by whether it lost *economic* value (cheap now) or *contextual* value (no longer matters in the workflow). (3) Identify the new constraint the workflow now has. (4) Assemble a new bundle of complements around that constraint. (5) Make the contribution economically visible so the system pays for it.
**Trade-offs**: Rebundled roles (RevOps manager, nurse navigator) often lack a title or budget line at first — visibility must be created, not assumed.

## Audit the Coordination Tax
**When to use**: When local AI deployments are delivering task speedups without organizational gains.
**How**: Measure the friction between teams — meetings, status chasing, document search, rework from handoffs — not the time inside tasks. Then apply AI to the seams. Watch for the coordination paradox: automating locally while leaving seams manual makes fragmentation worse.
**Trade-offs**: Coordination cost is invisible in most metrics, so the business case is harder to build than a task-automation ROI case.

## Unified Representation → Decision → Execution
**When to use**: Building coordination advantage across fragmented teams or ecosystem actors.
**How**: Layer 1 — build a shared, live representation (a digital twin / source of truth) all actors can align on. Layer 2 — use it to drive a unified decision the actors would otherwise negotiate over. Layer 3 — integrate workflows so the decision is executed reliably across tools. Own all three layers and you own the control point.
**Trade-offs**: Deep integration is slow and expensive; a UI layer is fast but earns no right to coordinate.

## Coordination Without Consensus
**When to use**: Ecosystems too fragmented to ever agree on shared standards (construction, healthcare, multi-vendor supply chains) — exactly where consensus-based platforms have repeatedly failed.
**How**: Don't force participants onto a shared vocabulary. Let each keep its own tools and formats, and use AI to interpret unstructured inputs into structured, shared meaning. Standards become implicit (learned from data) rather than explicit (agreed in advance).
**Trade-offs**: Consensus models (CCC) are more auditable and defensible once established; interpretation models (Tractable) scale into new geographies and insurers without new integrations but depend on data volume and stay probabilistic.

## Escape the Tool Integration Trap
**When to use**: Your differentiation increasingly comes from an AI engine you don't own.
**How**: Watch the three stages — tools commoditize execution, then absorb expertise, then become the gravitational center you reorganize around. Counter by keeping a proprietary layer the engine cannot see: field data, risk absorption, customer decision rights, or an integrated delivery system. Diversify engines before performance-based lock-in sets in.
**Trade-offs**: Refusing maximum engine performance costs short-term competitiveness. The alternative is becoming a wrapper.

## Climb the Solutioning Stack
**When to use**: Deciding how much outcome liability to take on as a tool or solution provider.
**How**: Move deliberately along work-as-a-service (paid for operational delivery) → results-as-a-service (paid for measurable results) → outcomes-as-a-service (paid for customer-specific strategic outcomes). Each step requires more visibility (representation), more decision integration, and more execution control. Only climb as far as you can absorb the risk.
**Trade-offs**: Higher rungs earn more pricing power but demand liability, monitoring, and domain depth; climbing without them turns a margin story into a loss story.

## Design for Indecision
**When to use**: Customers face abundant options and low confidence — where AI has made supply cheap and choice overwhelming.
**How**: Find the highest-friction decision in the customer journey, take responsibility for it, and rebundle the fragmented steps around it into a trusted path. Owning the decision moment is a demand-side control point; you can then charge a platform tax for access to it.
**Trade-offs**: Owning decisions means owning blame for bad ones; trust is slow to build and fast to lose.

## Choose Your Strategic Posture Deliberately
**When to use**: Setting the ambition level of an AI program.
**How**: Name which of the four you are: **reactive optimizer** (speed up today's tasks), **anticipator** (spot value migration early but keep the old game), **logic shifter** (change where and how decisions get made), **field reshaper** (restructure the ecosystem itself). Deeper reimagining of coordination = more enduring advantage.
**Trade-offs**: Reshaping requires ecosystem access and patience; optimizing is safe, cheap, and non-compounding.

## Set Positive Constraints
**When to use**: Designing an AI-native product or workflow where unbounded capability produces mush.
**How**: Choose a deliberate boundary that structures behavior and generates data — TikTok's 60-second limit forced fast signal for the behavior graph. Retire the constraint once it has done its job.
**Trade-offs**: Constraints look like self-imposed handicaps to competitors and internal stakeholders until the flywheel they enable becomes visible.
