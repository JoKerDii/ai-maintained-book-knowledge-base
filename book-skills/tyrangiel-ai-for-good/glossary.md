# Glossary — AI for Good

**Accelerationists** — camp arguing AI should be built as fast as possible to reap benefits and beat global competition (Ch 1)
**Administrative burden** — the 25–30% of US healthcare spending (~$5B at Cleveland Clinic scale) consumed by non-clinical work (Ch 8)
**AGI (artificial general intelligence)** — theoretical synthesis of AI capabilities surpassing human intellect across domains (Ch 1)
**Alert fatigue** — clinician desensitization from too many low-precision automated warnings (Ch 13)
**Ambient scribe** — AI that listens to a clinical visit and drafts the note, with doctor-in-the-loop review (Ch 11)
**Bayesian Health** — vendor whose sepsis model tiers alerts by probability rather than firing uniformly (Ch 13)
**Bonini's paradox** — a model detailed enough to be fully accurate becomes as hard to understand as the thing modeled (Ch 26)
**Bullshit umbrella** — senior cover that shields a team from bureaucratic interference during a crisis (Ch 16)
**Case mix index (CMI)** — severity-weighted measure of a hospital's patient population (Ch 12, 13)
**Character.ai** — companion chatbot platform built on an attention-based engagement model (Ch 22)
**Classifier** — AI that labels or sorts: images, email, tumors on an MRI (Ch 1)
**Context stuffing** — loading a model's context window with source material to constrain its answers (Ch 4)
**Context window** — the span of text a model can consider at once (Ch 3, 4)
**Digital twin** — a live data model mirroring a physical system, from a supply chain to a patient's anatomy (Ch 9, 15)
**DOGE** — initiative Tyrangiel treats as the cautionary counterexample: provocation over collaboration (Ch 21)
**Doomers** — camp arguing AI risks catastrophe or extinction and must be slowed (Ch 1)
**Ed** — the failed Los Angeles schools chatbot; shorthand for edtech deployed ahead of its competence (Ch 2)
**Eliza effect** — human tendency to attribute understanding to a system that merely reflects language back (Weizenbaum, 1966) (Ch 22)
**Epic** — dominant electronic health record system; its closed nature shapes what hospital AI can do (Ch 11, 13)
**FASA (Federal Acquisition Streamlining Act, 1994)** — statute requiring agencies to consider commercial products; the basis of Palantir's suit (Ch 17)
**Frozen middle** — the acquisition/program layer between leadership intent and execution, not incentivized to move (Ch 18, 20)
**Gall's Law** — every complicated working system evolved from a simple working system (Ch 18)
**God view** — a single synthesized real-time picture of a whole problem space (Ch 1, 15)
**Hallucination** — fluent, confident, false model output (Ch 2, 3)
**Human-in-the-loop** — required human review of AI output; also functions as legal and political cover (Ch 11, 20)
**IMS (Integrated Master Schedule)** — Operation Warp Speed's synchronized plan across all agencies and vendors (Ch 15)
**Inherently governmental** — legal category of work that may not be delegated to machines or contractors (Ch 20)
**Iron triangle** — the closed loop of agencies, incumbent contractors, and Congress that resists new entrants (Ch 17)
**Notional data** — placeholder data used to build and demo a system before real data arrives (Ch 16)
**Ontology** — Palantir's core abstraction: a shared model of an organization's entities and relationships (Ch 16)
**Operation Warp Speed** — the COVID vaccine distribution effort; the book's origin story (Ch 1, 14, 15)
**Optimizer** — AI making split-second decisions to improve logistics, pricing, or allocation (Ch 1)
**p(doom)** — self-reported probability of AI-caused human extinction (Ch 1)
**POSIWID** — "the purpose of a system is what it does" (Anil Dash) (Ch 21)
**Productive struggle** — the difficulty a learner must experience for learning to occur; the thing a tutor must not short-circuit (Ch 7)
**Prompt drift** — degradation of a tuned prompt when the underlying model is updated (Ch 3)
**Red teaming** — adversarially attacking your own system to find failures before users do (Ch 4)
**Sepsis** — fast-moving infection response; the canonical case for probabilistic clinical alerting (Ch 13)
**SERT (Sepsis Emergency Response Team)** — the human team an alert is designed to mobilize (Ch 13)
**Sycophancy** — a model's tendency to agree with the user under pressure, abandoning correct answers (Ch 2)
**SWAP Study** — Defense Innovation Board's Software Acquisition and Practices report; named the frozen middle (Ch 18)
**Team Human** — Tyrangiel's shorthand for AI that amplifies human judgment rather than replacing it (Ch 27)
**Vibes-based evaluation** — judging model output by impression instead of a repeatable test; the default failure (Ch 4)
**Zero-shot translation** — translating a language pair the model was never explicitly trained on (Ch 24, 26)
**Zone of proximal development** — the band just beyond current ability where learning happens; applied to device design (Ch 23)
