# Chapter 7: The Destiny Instinct

## Core Idea
The **destiny instinct** is the belief that innate characteristics fix the destiny of people, countries, religions, or cultures — that "things have always been this way and will never change." In reality, societies and cultures move constantly; slow change is still change.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Four ways to control the destiny instinct** (the toolkit):
  1. **Keep track of gradual improvements** — slow change is not no change. 1%/year doubles in 70 years; 2% in 35; 3% in 24.
  2. **Update your knowledge** — social-science facts go off fast, like milk; even Gapminder's own 1998 quiz answers expired in 13 years.
  3. **Talk to Grandpa** — compare your values to your grandparents'/grandchildren's to see how much culture shifts.
  4. **Collect examples of cultural change** — actively gather counterexamples to "it's their culture."
- **"Rocks move"**: The chapter's master metaphor — cultures, nations, religions are not unchanging rocks; they transform, often faster outside the West.

## Key Concepts
- **Income, not religion, drives family size**: Muslim women average 3.1 children, Christian 2.7 — no major difference. Within every religion, women on Level 1 have more babies. (In 1960 it *looked* like "Christian or Japanese," a generalization that didn't hold up.)
- **Feelings disguised as facts**: Grand claims about a culture's fixed destiny usually look like analysis but are feelings — e.g., "Africa will never make it. It's their culture."
- **Western progress is not guaranteed either**: The IMF kept forecasting 3% Level-4 growth for five years post-2008; the fast growth (>5%) was actually on Level 2 (Ghana, Nigeria, Ethiopia, Bangladesh) — misdirecting retirement-fund investment.

## Mental Models
- **Africa can catch up**: 50 years ago, China/India/South Korea were behind today's sub-Saharan Africa, and Asia's destiny was deemed hopeless ("they'll never feed 4 billion"). The poorest remaining ~200M are stuck by *soil and conflict*, not culture.
- **Values change**: Sweden's sex/gender values 60 years ago (grandfather Gustav refusing to discuss contraception; abortion illegal, students traveling to Catholic Poland) match the "Asian values" / "Muslim values" people now call unchangeable.

## Anti-patterns
- **"There's not a snowball's chance in hell Africa will make it — it's their culture"**: The Edinburgh investor; pure feeling that cost his clients the fastest-growing markets.
- **Treating "Asian/Muslim/African values" as innate**: They're patriarchal values like Sweden's of 60 years ago — and they vanish with social and economic progress.
- **Letting a free press blind you to progress**: Iran's record-fast fertility drop (>6 to <3 babies/woman in 15 years) went unreported in the Western media.

## Worked Example
At the African Union's "Agenda 2063" conference, Rosling gave "the lecture of his life," explaining how extreme poverty in Africa could end within 20 years, and closed by hoping his grandchildren would visit Africa as tourists on its new high-speed trains. Chairwoman Nkosazana Dlamini-Zuma thanked him — then said: "you don't have any vision." Ending *extreme* poverty just leaves *ordinary* poverty. "It is **my** grandchildren who are going to visit **your** continent and travel on **your** high-speed trains… my 50-year vision is that Africans will be welcome tourists in Europe and not unwanted refugees." Rosling — a lifelong Africa collaborator who thought he was the rare European who "got it" — realized he was *still* stuck in a static, colonial mind-set, unable to imagine "they" fully catching up with "us."

## Key Takeaways
1. Cultures, religions, and nations are not destinies — they change, often invisibly slowly.
2. Don't dismiss a 1%/year change; compounded, it transforms the world.
3. Refresh your knowledge constantly; social facts have a short shelf life.
4. "It's their culture" is usually a feeling — find counterexamples and talk across generations.
5. Watch your own destiny instinct: even experts smuggle in "they can never catch up."
