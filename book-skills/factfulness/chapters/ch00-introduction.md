# Chapter 0: Introduction — Why I Love the Circus

## Core Idea
Most people — including highly educated experts — score *worse than random* (worse than chimpanzees) on basic fact questions about the world, because they hold an outdated, **overdramatic worldview**. The fix is not memorizing data but learning a handful of thinking tools (Factfulness) to control the ten dramatic instincts.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 13-question fact test**: A short multiple-choice quiz (poverty, life expectancy, vaccination, girls' education, population, etc.) used to expose systematic ignorance. Correct answers to all 13: **1:C, 2:B, 3:C, 4:C, 5:C, 6:B, 7:C, 8:A, 9:C, 10:A, 11:C, 12:C, 13:A**.
  - When to use: Before claiming you understand a global trend — test your intuition against documented facts.
  - How: Answer honestly, then notice that wrong answers cluster on the *most dramatic/negative* option.
- **The Chimpanzee benchmark**: Random guessing scores 33% per 3-option question (~4/12). Humans average ~2/12 — *worse than random*.
  - When to use: As a humility check. If you'd lose to a banana-picking chimp, your "knowledge" is actively wrong, not merely absent.
- **Overdramatic worldview vs. fact-based worldview**: The instinctive picture (world divided, getting worse, rich-poor gap widening) vs. reality (most people in the middle, gradually improving).

## Key Concepts
- **Megamisconception**: A wrong belief so basic it distorts all other global proportions (the worst: "the world is divided in two").
- **Systematically wrong**: Errors that all lean one direction (more dramatic/negative), not randomly scattered — proof of an underlying instinct, not mere ignorance.
- **Dramatic instincts**: Evolved mental shortcuts (crave drama, gossip, quick conclusions) that helped hunter-gatherers but now mislead.
- **Data as therapy**: Understanding the world as it really is reduces stress and irrational fear.
- **Upgrade problem (rejected explanation)**: Rosling first thought ignorance was just outdated knowledge; Davos proved even up-to-date elites get it wrong, so the cause must be instinct.

## Mental Models
- Think of global misconceptions as **optical illusions**: knowing the lines are equal doesn't stop you seeing them as unequal. Illusions happen in the brain, not the eyes — so be curious, not embarrassed.
- Treat your worldview like a **car GPS**: decisions fail if the underlying map is wrong, no matter how smart the driver.
- **Drama intake** is like sugar/fat intake: needed in small doses, harmful uncontrolled. Don't remove the instinct — control it.

## Anti-patterns
- **Blaming the media / fake news**: Tempting but wrong. The overdramatic worldview persists because of how brains work, not because of an evil media villain.
- **Assuming education or intelligence fixes it**: Nobel laureates and medical researchers scored among the *worst*. It is not an IQ problem.
- **Treating ignorance as stupidity**: Rosling means "lack of correct knowledge," not lack of intelligence — stay humble.

## Worked Example
At Davos (January 2015), Rosling posed three fact questions (poverty, population growth, vaccination) to ~1,000 of the world's most powerful leaders, alongside Bill and Melinda Gates. On poverty, a strong 61% got it right — but on future population growth and basic health care, this elite audience *still did worse than chimps*, despite having advisers who could update them continuously. This was the moment that proved ignorance is not an "upgrade problem" but an instinct problem — and crystallized the book.

## Key Takeaways
1. Your intuition about global trends is probably *systematically* wrong toward the negative/dramatic.
2. The cure is thinking tools, not memorizing statistics.
3. Being wrong is not shameful — it's a shared illusion; respond with curiosity ("Wow, how is that possible?").
4. Don't blame media, education, or intelligence; blame (and control) the ten instincts.
5. A fact-based worldview is less stressful and leads to better decisions.
