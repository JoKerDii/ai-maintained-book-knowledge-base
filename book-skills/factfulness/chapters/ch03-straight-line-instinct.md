# Chapter 3: The Straight Line Instinct

## Core Idea
The **straight line instinct** is the assumption that a trend line will just continue straight. In reality, lines come in many shapes (S-bends, slides, humps, doublings) — and straight lines are far rarer than we think.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The five curve shapes** (the central tool): When you see a trend, ask which shape it really is.
  - **Straight line**: income ↔ health, education, marriage age, recreation spending.
  - **S-bend**: income ↔ primary education / vaccination — flat-low at Level 1, rises fast through Level 2, flat-high at Levels 3–4.
  - **Slide**: babies per woman vs. income — flat, then slopes down, then flat-low (~2).
  - **Hump**: lower at Levels 1 and 4, peak in the middle — dental decay, traffic accidents, child drownings.
  - **Doubling line**: Ebola cases, E. coli, transport distance & CO₂ with income.
  - When to use: Before extrapolating *any* trend into the future.
  - How: You need **three points** to tell a straight line (1,2,3) from a doubling line (1,2,4) — two points always look straight.
- **The fill-up effect / "peak child"**: Population will grow to ~11 billion *not* because of more children (already flat — "peak child") but because existing children grow into 3 billion more adults over three generations, then growth stops.
- **The doubling income scale**: Compare income by doublings ($1→$2→$4...→$64), because each extra dollar matters far more on Level 1 than Level 4. Six doublings move you across all four levels.

## Key Concepts
- **The third megamisconception**: "The world population is *just* increasing" — the word "just" (implying it grows forever unless drastic action is taken) is the error.
- **"Lived in balance" vs. "died in balance" with nature**: Pre-1800 flat population came from ~6 births with ~4 child deaths each — brutal balance, not harmony.
- **More survivors → fewer people**: The proven way to curb population growth is to *end* extreme poverty (lower child mortality + educate women + contraceptives), so parents *choose* fewer children.

## Mental Models
- **Mino's growth chart**: Extrapolating a baby's first-6-months growth makes a 13-foot ten-year-old — obviously absurd because we *know* bodies. The error is invisible only when we're unfamiliar with the topic.
- **The chessboard / rice-doubling legend**: Anything that keeps doubling grows far faster than intuition expects.

## Anti-patterns
- **Extrapolating a straight line from two points**: The Ebola failure — assuming linear growth when cases were doubling every three weeks delayed action.
- **"Saving poor children causes overpopulation"**: Sounds intuitive, is exactly backward. *Delaying* escape from poverty increases population.
- **Cherry-picking large religious families**: Media exceptions; religion correlates weakly with family size — extreme poverty is the real driver (debunked further in Ch 7).

## Worked Example
On Sept 23, 2014, Rosling saw a WHO graph showing Ebola cases were **doubling** (1, 2, 4, 8, 16) — each victim infecting ~2 others, cases doubling every three weeks — not rising on a straight line. He realized three doublings (nine weeks) would make the crisis 8× worse, that Ebola could fly across borders untreated, and that every three-week delay doubled the resources needed. He canceled three months of work and flew to Liberia. His own initial slowness ("I had assumed the increase was a straight line") is the chapter's confession: misreading the curve shape costs lives.

## Key Takeaways
1. Don't assume straight lines — most trends aren't.
2. Identify the curve shape (straight, S-bend, slide, hump, doubling) before extrapolating.
3. Three data points beat two: only then can you distinguish linear from doubling.
4. The part of a curve you see on Level 4 may not apply on Levels 1–3.
5. Population growth is self-limiting via prosperity; "peak child" has already arrived.
