## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The central thesis**: People — including experts — are *systematically* wrong about the world (worse than random/chimpanzees), always in the negative/dramatic direction. The cause isn't outdated facts or evil media; it's ten hardwired **dramatic instincts**. The fix is **Factfulness**: recognizing each instinct and applying a rule of thumb to control it. Result: a worldview that's both more accurate and less stressful.

### The Four Income Levels (master framework — replaces "developed/developing")
Sort the world by daily income, not into two boxes:
- **Level 1** (~$1/day, ~1bn): extreme poverty — barefoot, walk hours for dirty water, ~5 children.
- **Level 2** (~$4/day, ~3bn): bike, sandals, gas stove, kids in school, unstable electricity.
- **Level 3** (~$16/day, ~2bn): cold tap, fridge, motorcycle, multiple jobs, high school.
- **Level 4** (>$64/day, ~1bn): car, 12+ years education, hot water, air travel — *where book readers live.*

Each step ≈ 4× income; moving up usually takes generations. Most of humanity is in the **middle** (Levels 2–3) — there is no gap. **PIN code of the world: 1-1-1-4** (Americas, Europe, Africa, Asia in billions) → 1-1-4-5 by 2100.

### The Ten Instincts → Control Rules
1. **Gap** (world split in two) → **look for the majority**. Beware comparisons of averages, of extremes, and the view from up here.
2. **Negativity** (world getting worse) → **expect bad news**. Separate *level* (bad) from *direction* (better) — both can be true.
3. **Straight Line** (trends continue straight) → **lines come in 5 shapes** (straight, S-bend, slide, hump, doubling); get a 3rd data point.
4. **Fear** (frightening ≠ dangerous) → **calculate the risk = danger × exposure**; get calm before deciding.
5. **Size** (lonely numbers look big) → **get things in proportion**: compare & divide (per person); use 80/20.
6. **Generalization** (one example → whole group) → **question your categories**: differences within, similarities across.
7. **Destiny** ("it'll never change") → **slow change is still change**; update your knowledge; talk to Grandpa.
8. **Single Perspective** (one cause/solution) → **get a toolbox, not a hammer**; it's "both, case-by-case."
9. **Blame** (find a villain) → **look for causes not villains, systems not heroes**; look in the mirror.
10. **Urgency** ("now or never") → **take small steps**; insist on data; beware fortune-tellers and drastic action.

### Key reasoning heuristics
- **The safe bet**: when unsure about a global trend, the **most positive** answer is usually correct.
- **Compare & divide**: never trust a lonely number — compare it (over time/place) and turn it into a per-person rate.
- **Possibilist** stance: neither optimist nor pessimist — "neither hope without reason, nor fear without reason."
- **Foundation**: humility ("I don't know"; change your mind) + curiosity ("how interesting that I was wrong").
- **The 5 risks worth real urgency**: pandemic, financial collapse, world war, climate change, extreme poverty.

## Scope & Limits

This skill covers the book's content and Hans Rosling's frameworks. Specific statistics reflect data as of ~2017 (the book's publication) — Rosling's own rule applies: **update your knowledge**, since social-science facts expire. For current figures, consult Gapminder (gapminder.org), the UN, or the World Bank. The frameworks for thinking, however, remain durable.
