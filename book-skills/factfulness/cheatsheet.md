# Factfulness — Cheatsheet

## The 10 Instincts → Warning Sign → Control Rule

| # | Instinct | Warning sign (the "tell") | Control rule |
|---|----------|---------------------------|--------------|
| 1 | **Gap** | "the gap," two opposed groups, us/them, developed/developing | **Look for the majority** |
| 2 | **Negativity** | "things are getting worse," bad news, nostalgia | **Expect bad news** |
| 3 | **Straight line** | a trend assumed to continue straight | **Lines might bend** (5 shapes) |
| 4 | **Fear** | frightening images, "in times like these" | **Calculate the risk** = danger × exposure |
| 5 | **Size** | a lonely big number, one vivid victim | **Compare & divide** (+ 80/20) |
| 6 | **Generalization** | "they," one example → whole group, "their culture" | **Question your categories** |
| 7 | **Destiny** | "it will never change," "it's their culture" | **Slow change is still change** |
| 8 | **Single perspective** | one cause/solution for everything | **Toolbox, not a hammer** |
| 9 | **Blame** | a clear villain (or hero) | **Causes, not villains; systems, not heroes** |
| 10 | **Urgency** | "now or never," "act today" | **Take small steps** |

## The Four Income Levels (memorize this first)
| Level | Income/day | People | Life looks like |
|-------|-----------|--------|-----------------|
| 1 | ~$1 | ~1bn | barefoot, walk for dirty water, ~5 kids, extreme poverty |
| 2 | ~$4 | ~3bn | bike, sandals, gas stove, kids in school, unstable power |
| 3 | ~$16 | ~2bn | cold tap, fridge, motorcycle, multiple jobs, high school |
| 4 | >$64 | ~1bn | car, 12+ yrs education, hot water, air travel (**you**) |
*Each step up ≈ 4× income. PIN code of the world: **1-1-1-4** (Americas-Europe-Africa-Asia) → 1-1-4-5 by 2100.*

## Decision rules
- **See a lonely number?** → Never trust it alone. Compare (vs. last year / a comparable place) AND divide (per person). Then judge.
- **See a doubling/tripling number?** → Look closer (Ebola, terrorism), but get a 3rd data point before extrapolating.
- **Scared by a news event?** → Ask: what's the actual death toll / proportion? Is this frightening or dangerous? Get calm before acting.
- **Tempted to extrapolate a line?** → Which curve shape? Need 3 points. Does my Level-4 view apply to Levels 1–3?
- **Hear "majority"?** → Ask 51% or 99%?
- **Hear "it's their culture / they'll never change"?** → Feeling disguised as fact. Find a counterexample; talk across generations.
- **Offered one cause/solution for everything?** → Get a toolbox. Test it against someone who disagrees.
- **Reaching for a villain?** → You've stopped thinking. Find the system. Look in the mirror.
- **Told "act NOW or never"?** → Almost always manipulation. Ask for time + data. Demand a range of scenarios, not one.
- **Forecasting?** → Show mid + best + worst; round to your own disadvantage to protect credibility.

## The "safe bet" heuristic
On Rosling's fact questions, **the most positive answer is usually correct.** When unsure about a global trend, guess that things are better than your instinct says.

## Proportions people get wrong (reality is ~80–90%, guessed ~20%)
Vaccinated 1-yr-olds **88%** · electricity access **80%** · girls in primary school **90%** · world life expectancy **~72** · extreme poverty (last 20 yrs) **halved** (29%→9%).

## Thresholds & rules of thumb
- **80/20**: ~20% of a list's items = ~80% of the total — handle those first.
- **Rule of 70**: years to double ≈ 70 ÷ growth%. (1%→70yr, 2%→35yr, 3%→24yr.)
- **Statistics caution**: ignore differences smaller than ~10% — likely noise.
- **Forecast accuracy** tracks system simplicity: demographics (decades) ≫ economics ≫ weather (a week).

## The 5 global risks worth real urgency
Global **pandemic** · **financial collapse** · **World War III** · **climate change** · **extreme poverty** (+ the unknown risk). Approach all with cool heads, robust independent data, baby steps — never cry wolf.

## Two foundational values
**Humility** (it's hard to get facts right; say "I don't know"; change your mind) + **Curiosity** (seek disconfirming facts; "how interesting that I was wrong"). Be a **possibilist**, not an optimist or pessimist.
