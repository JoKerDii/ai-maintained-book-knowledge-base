# Factfulness — Glossary

**80/20 rule** — Find the few largest items in any list; usually ~20% of items make up ~80% of the total. Deal with those first. (Ch 5)

**Attention filter** — A mental shield with ten instinct-shaped holes; only drama-fitting information passes, so we believe the unusual is usual. (Ch 4)

**Bad and better** — Holding two thoughts at once: a *level* can be bad while its *direction* is improving. (Ch 2)

**Blame instinct** — The urge to find a simple, guilty individual or group for a bad event; it stops thought and blocks systemic understanding. Control: look for causes, not villains. (Ch 9)

**Chimpanzee benchmark** — Random guessing scores 33% per 3-option question (~4/12); humans average ~2/12, i.e., worse than random. (Intro)

**Chemophobia** — Exaggerated fear of invisible chemical contamination, generalized from a few vivid examples; blocks data-driven argument. (Ch 4, Ch 6)

**Compare and divide** — The two "magic tools" for proportion: never trust a lonely number — compare it (over time/place) and divide it into a rate (per person). (Ch 5)

**Cool chains** — Refrigerated logistics keeping vaccines cold from factory to child; require the same infrastructure as new factories. (Ch 6)

**Curve shapes** — Trends come in five shapes: straight line, S-bend, slide, hump, doubling line. Three points are needed to tell them apart. (Ch 3)

**Destiny instinct** — Belief that innate traits fix the unchanging destiny of people/cultures/nations. Control: remember slow change is still change. (Ch 7)

**Dollar Street** — Project photographing ~300 families across 50+ countries by income, showing that income (not culture/religion/country) is the main factor in how people live. (Ch 1, Ch 6)

**Dramatic instincts** — The ten evolved mental shortcuts (gap, negativity, straight line, fear, size, generalization, destiny, single perspective, blame, urgency) that crave drama and distort the worldview. (Intro)

**Doubling income scale** — Comparing income by doublings ($1→$2→…→$64) because each extra dollar matters far more at low levels; six doublings span the four levels. (Ch 3)

**Fear instinct** — Attention to frightening things, which aren't necessarily the most dangerous (harm, captivity, contamination). Control: calculate the risks. (Ch 4)

**Fill-up effect** — Population grows ~3 billion more not from more births but from already-born children growing into adults over three generations, then stops. (Ch 3)

**Four Income Levels** — The book's master framework replacing developed/developing: Level 1 (~$1/day, ~1bn), Level 2 (~$4, ~3bn), Level 3 (~$16, ~2bn), Level 4 (>$64/day, ~1bn). Each step ≈ 4× income. (Ch 1)

**Frightening vs. dangerous** — Frightening = perceived risk; dangerous = real risk. Direct fear at the dangerous. (Ch 4)

**Gap instinct** — The urge to split things into two conflicting groups with a gap between; reality is usually a continuum with the majority in the middle. Control: look for the majority. (Ch 1)

**Generalization instinct** — Lumping together different things/people, or judging a category from one example. Control: question your categories. (Ch 6)

**Hammer and nails** — Overusing your favorite tool/expertise where it doesn't fit. Get a toolbox, not a hammer. (Ch 8)

**Konzo** — Incurable paralytic disease from improperly processed cassava that Rosling researched; central to the Nacala and Makanga stories. (Ch 10, Ch 11)

**Mega misconception** — A wrong belief so basic it distorts all global proportions. The three: world divided in two (Ch 1), world getting worse (Ch 2), population just increasing (Ch 3).

**Negativity instinct** — Noticing the bad more than the good, from misremembered pasts + selective reporting + feeling. Control: expect bad news. (Ch 2)

**Overdramatic worldview** — The instinctive, too-negative picture: world divided, worsening, gap widening. Opposite of the fact-based worldview. (Intro)

**Peak child** — The point (now reached) where the number of children worldwide stops increasing. (Ch 3)

**PIN code of the world (1-1-1-4)** — Population in billions: Americas 1, Europe 1, Africa 1, Asia 4. By 2100 → 1-1-4-5. (Ch 5)

**Possibilist** — Someone who "neither hopes without reason, nor fears without reason," resisting both optimism and pessimism. (Ch 2)

**Recovery position** — Laying an unconscious person prone so vomit drains; correct for soldiers but wrongly generalized to sleeping babies. (Ch 6)

**Risk = danger × exposure** — Real risk equals how dangerous a thing is times how exposed you are — not how scared it makes you feel. (Ch 4)

**Single perspective instinct** — Attraction to one cause/solution that explains everything; blinds you to disconfirming information. Control: get a toolbox, not a hammer. (Ch 8)

**Size instinct** — Misjudging importance: a lonely number looks big, a visible victim outweighs statistics. Control: get things in proportion. (Ch 5)

**Slow change is not no change** — 1%/yr doubles in 70 years; small annual changes compound into transformation. (Ch 7)

**Statistics as therapy** — Plain official data, shown to people with a too-negative worldview, makes them feel better ("a new kind of happy pill"). (Ch 2)

**Straight line instinct** — Assuming a trend continues straight; most trends don't. Control: remember curves come in different shapes. (Ch 3)

**Urgency instinct** — A decision feeling "now or never" when it rarely is; drives drastic, untested action. Control: take small steps. (Ch 10)

**View from up here** — From Level 4, Levels 1–3 all look equally poor — like looking down from a tall building. (Ch 1)
