# Factfulness — Techniques & Patterns

The ten instinct-control techniques, each with how to apply it. Use these whenever you read news, evaluate a claim, make a forecast, or prioritize resources.

## 1. Look for the Majority (Gap Instinct)
**When to use**: A claim splits the world into two opposed groups (rich/poor, us/them, developed/developing), or cites "the gap."
**How**:
- Replace the binary with the **Four Income Levels**.
- Beware **comparisons of averages** (check the spreads — they usually overlap).
- Beware **comparisons of extremes** (the majority is in the middle).
- Beware **the view from up here** (from the top, everything below looks equally small).
**Trade-off**: Real gaps occasionally exist (apartheid South Africa) — verify before dismissing.

## 2. Expect Bad News (Negativity Instinct)
**When to use**: You feel the world is getting worse, or a story implies decline.
**How**: Separate *level* (bad) from *direction* (better). Ask: "If an equally large improvement had happened, would I have heard about it?" Distrust rosy memories of the past.
**Trade-off**: Don't over-correct into rosy positivity — aim for accuracy, not a feel-good counterweight.

## 3. Don't Assume Straight Lines (Straight Line Instinct)
**When to use**: Extrapolating any trend into the future.
**How**: Ask which of five shapes it is — straight, S-bend, slide, hump, doubling. Get a **third data point** (1,2,3 is linear; 1,2,4 is doubling). Remember the part of a curve you see on Level 4 may not apply on Levels 1–3.
**Trade-off**: Some lines really are straight (income↔health) — identify, don't assume.

## 4. Calculate the Risks (Fear Instinct)
**When to use**: Fear is driving attention or a decision.
**How**: Compute **Risk = danger × exposure**, not the feeling. Distinguish *frightening* (perceived) from *dangerous* (real). When afraid, **get calm before deciding** — make as few decisions as possible until panic subsides.
**Trade-off**: Fear is useful when aimed at real risks; don't suppress it entirely.

## 5. Get Things in Proportion (Size Instinct)
**When to use**: A single number looks impressive, or one victim dominates attention.
**How**: **Compare** (vs. last year, vs. a comparable region) and **Divide** (into a per-person rate). Use the **80/20 rule** on lists/budgets — handle the biggest items first.
**Trade-off**: Numbers, but not only numbers — keep the human reality in view.

## 6. Question Your Categories (Generalization Instinct)
**When to use**: A claim treats a group (nation, religion, "Africa") as uniform, or generalizes from one example.
**How**:
- Look for **differences within** groups and **similarities across** groups.
- Beware **"the majority"** (51% or 99%? ask for the number).
- Beware **vivid/exceptional examples**; ask for more, or flip the example.
- **Assume you are not "normal"** and others are not idiots ("In what way is this smart?").
- Don't generalize from one group to another (soldiers ≠ babies).
**Trade-off**: We must categorize to function — aim for better categories (the Four Levels), not none.

## 7. Remember Slow Change Is Still Change (Destiny Instinct)
**When to use**: Someone says a culture/country/religion "will never change" or "it's their culture."
**How**: Track gradual improvements (1%/yr doubles in 70 yrs). **Update your knowledge** (social facts expire). **Talk to Grandpa** (compare generations). **Collect counterexamples** of cultural change.
**Trade-off**: Slow ≠ instant — change is real but takes decades; stay patient and possibilistic.

## 8. Get a Toolbox, Not a Hammer (Single Perspective Instinct)
**When to use**: One idea (free market, equality, one metric, one technology) is offered as the answer to everything.
**How**: **Test your ideas** against people who disagree. Respect **limited expertise** (experts only within their field). Watch **hammers and nails** (tool overuse). Use **numbers but not only numbers**. Beware simple solutions — answer is usually "both, case-by-case."
**Trade-off**: Simple ideas are seductive and time-saving; trade speed for accuracy when it matters.

## 9. Resist Finding a Scapegoat (Blame Instinct)
**When to use**: Something went wrong (or right) and you're reaching for a villain (or hero).
**How**: **Look for causes, not villains** — understand the interacting system. **Look for systems, not heroes** — ask whether the good outcome would have happened anyway. Look in the mirror. Credit institutions and technology.
**Trade-off**: Accountability still matters; the point is to find the *system*, not to excuse everyone.

## 10. Take Small Steps (Urgency Instinct)
**When to use**: A decision feels "now or never," or you're being pushed to act immediately.
**How**: **Take a breath** (ask for time and information). **Insist on the data** (relevant *and* accurate). **Beware fortune-tellers** (demand a range of scenarios; round to your disadvantage). **Be wary of drastic action** (ask the side effects; prefer step-by-step + evaluation).
**Trade-off**: A few risks are genuinely urgent — the five global risks — but even those need cool heads, not panic.

---

## Meta-pattern: Test for ignorance
**When to use**: In your own organization, field, or community.
**How**: Ask "What are the most important facts here, and how many people know them?" Pose a few multiple-choice fact questions humbly. Expect worse-than-random results — and an avalanche of curiosity once revealed.
