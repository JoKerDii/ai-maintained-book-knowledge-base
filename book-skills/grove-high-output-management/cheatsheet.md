# Cheatsheet — Grove's Decision Rules

## Thresholds & Defaults
| Quantity | Grove's number |
|---|---|
| Direct reports (supervisory manager) | **6–8**; 3–4 too few, 10 too many |
| Time budget per subordinate | **~½ day/week** (2 days → meddling; 1 hour → can't monitor) |
| Coordinating-group membership | ½ day/week = **one subordinate-equivalent** |
| One-on-one length | **1 hour minimum** |
| One-on-one frequency | Weekly (low TRM) → every few weeks (veteran) |
| Decision-meeting size | ≤6–7; **8 absolute cutoff** |
| Ad hoc mission-oriented meetings | **>25% of your time = malorganization** |
| Problems absorbed by regular meetings | Target **80%** |
| Work simplification, round one | **30–50% of steps eliminated** |
| MBO objective difficulty | **50/50 odds** of hitting it |
| MBO cycle if planning annually | **Quarterly or monthly** |
| Pipeline loss factor | ~20% (semiconductor benchmark) |
| Visual aid pacing | **4 min** presentation + discussion per aid |
| Interview talk ratio | Candidate **80%** |
| Performance bonus | **~50%** senior / **10–25%** middle manager |
| Training time | **2–4%** of employee hours (Intel) |
| Training leverage | 12 hrs prep → **200 hrs** returned at 1% improvement |
| Long-range plan horizon vs. commitment | Look 5 years, **commit 1** |

## Decision Rules
- **If a step isn't the limiting step, don't optimize it.** Find the longest/hardest/costliest step and schedule backward from delivery.
- **If a defect can be caught earlier, catch it there** — cost compounds with every stage it survives. *Exception:* reliability defects admit no economic trade-off at all.
- **If big problems are unlikely, monitor rather than gate.** Same money buys far more coverage.
- **If you have only one indicator, you have none.** Pair every quantity with a quality, every effect with its counter-effect.
- **If you wouldn't act on a leading indicator, don't track it** — you've bought anxiety, not control.
- **If you're measuring calls instead of orders, you're measuring activity, not output.**
- **If value can be priced → market. If not, and CUA is low → contract. If CUA is high and interests are shared → culture. If CUA is high and interest is self → chaos** (lower the CUA or build group-interest; trying harder won't work).
- **If you say yes to something, name what you just said no to.** Time is the only truly finite resource.
- **If you're free and have no discretionary project inventory, you will meddle.** Keep one stocked.
- **If two people are debating something that affects only them, break it off** and move the staff meeting on.
- **If you don't know what decision the meeting must produce, don't call it.**
- **If a decision is controversial, be *more* explicit, not less** — obscuring only postpones the argument and deepens the anger.
- **If no consensus has formed and the deadline has arrived, the senior person decides.** Using position power *before* that point is destructive; refusing to use it *after* is equally so.
- **If peers are circling, insert a peer-plus-one** — or let whoever has most at stake take charge.
- **If you're forecasting demand, don't discount it by what you think you can deliver** — you'll never tool up for the real demand.
- **If your subordinate's task changed, their TRM just reset.** Revert to structured direction immediately, without embarrassment.
- **If you're delegating, monitor at the rough-draft stage, sample partially, and set frequency by TRM.** No monitoring = abdication. Full checking = meddling.
- **If a review has more messages than the person can remember, cut some.** What you drop keeps until next time.
- **If you uncover a surprise while preparing a review, deliver it anyway.** Swallow hard.
- **If the subordinate is still denying or blaming, do not propose the solution** — nothing can happen until they assume responsibility.
- **If you get commitment without agreement, take it.** Don't confuse emotional comfort with operational need.
- **If a manager's organization failed, his rating cannot exceed theirs** — no matter how well he presents.
- **If your best review effort goes to your worst performers, you have it backwards.** Stars carry disproportionate output; improving them is the high-leverage move.
- **If someone valuable says "I quit," drop everything now.** Deferring confirms exactly the feeling that produced it.
- **If a promotion went too far, recycle — don't fire.** It was management's misjudgment.
- **If the training contradicts how you actually operate, it does damage, not nothing.**

## Choosing a Management Style (TRM)
| Subordinate's TRM *on this task* | Style | Always |
|---|---|---|
| **Low** | Structured; tell what, when, how | **Monitor** |
| **Medium** | Two-way communication, support, mutual reasoning | **Monitor** |
| **High** | Minimal: set objectives, monitor | **Monitor** |
*Trap: managers skip the medium row, choosing full immersion or full absence. And 90% rate their own style as more delegating than their subordinates do.*

## Tells & Smells
| What you see | What it means |
|---|---|
| Output great, but turnover up and people grumbling | **Light from distant stars** — you're seeing last year's work. Trust the internal measures. |
| A unit that hits monthly goals only in the final week | Poor manpower/equipment use; one breakdown from missing entirely |
| People waiting to hear the boss's view before speaking | Free discussion has already failed; decisions will be worse than your information allows |
| "I think *our* position seems to be…" | Peer-group syndrome — consensus-hunting, not thinking |
| A queue choking a process that passes 98% of cases | You're inspecting 100% where sampling would do. Sorting the queue is overhead; shrinking inspection is throughput. |
| Salespeople chasing commissions you designed | You built a market inside your org. Audit incentives before blaming people for responding to them. |
| A manager who waffles | Negative leverage: no green light is a red light, and a whole org can stop |
| A visibly depressed manager | Pervasive, elusive, and very hard to counter — worse than a bad training session |
| Someone quits saying money and perks | Possibly fine. Someone quits feeling unappreciated → **you failed as their manager** |
| A candidate who impresses you enormously | No signal. Grove hired one such person who "failed badly on the job." |
| Interview answers you can't evaluate | You're not in shared vocabulary — steer to subjects familiar to both |
| A "meets requirements" performer | Two kinds: **noncompetitor** (settled, unchallenged) vs. **competitor** (just promoted, will exceed again). Same rating, opposite meanings. |
| A high performer given no new challenge | They will atrophy back to "meets" and stay there |
| Objectives you're confident of hitting | Set too low — you're leaving output on the table |
| Review comments that are general, avoid negatives, or contradict the raise | Four of the seven things managers hate about their own reviews |

## Fast Diagnostics
- **Underperformance**: *If their life depended on it, could they do it?* Yes → motivation. No → training.
- **Where someone sits on Maslow**: *Does the absolute raise matter, or the relative one?* Absolute → physiological/safety. Relative → esteem/self-actualization (money as measure).
- **Whether to befriend a subordinate**: *Imagine delivering a tough review to them. Do you cringe?* Yes → don't.
- **Whether a meeting is justified**: *Necessary? Desirable? Justifiable?* Anything but yes on all three → don't call it.
- **Whether you're delegating or abdicating**: *Are you monitoring?*
- **Whether it's monitoring or meddling**: *Are you sampling, or checking everything?*

## Grove's Named Laws
- **The output equation** — A manager's output = the output of his organization + the output of the neighboring organizations under his influence.
- **Grove's Law** — All large organizations with a common business purpose end up in a hybrid organizational form.
- **Grove's Principle of Didactic Management** — "Ask one more question!"
- **Grove's malorganization test** — >25% of time in *ad hoc* meetings (not meetings generally).
- **The motto** — "Let chaos reign, then rein in chaos."
