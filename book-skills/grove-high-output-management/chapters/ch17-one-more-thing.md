# "One More Thing…" — Grove's 100-Point Assignment List

## Core Idea
Grove closes with a scored self-assignment list and one instruction: **"if you do at least 100 points worth of what you find here, you'll be a distinctly better manager for it."** This is the book converted into an action backlog.

## The Framing
> "Please! You invested the price of this book plus perhaps eight hours of your time. At the risk of sounding like the author of a diet book, I would ask you to do something specific… **Choose what you like — but choose some — and perform them honestly.**"

Note the point weighting: **20-point items are the ones that change how the organization runs** (installing indicators, holding real one-on-ones, setting objectives with key results, giving people a racetrack, auditing your reviews). **10-point items are analysis and diagnosis.** Hitting 100 points on 10-pointers alone means ten analyses and zero installed changes — the weighting is telling you to do the heavy ones.

## Section 1 — Production

| Pts | Assignment |
|---|---|
| 10 | Identify the operations in your work most like **process, assembly, and test** production. |
| 10 | For a project you are working on, **identify the limiting step** and map out the flow of work around it. |
| 10 | Define the proper places for the equivalents of **receiving inspection, in-process inspection, and final inspection** in your work. Decide whether these should be **monitoring steps or gate-like**. Identify the conditions under which you can relax things and move to a **variable inspection** scheme. |
| 10 | Identify **half a dozen new indicators** for your group's output. They should measure **both the quantity and quality** of the output. |
| **20** | **Install these new indicators as a routine in your work area, and establish their regular review in your staff meetings.** |
| **20** | **What is the most important strategy (plan of action) you are pursuing now?** Describe the **environmental demand** that prompted it and your **current status or momentum**. Is your strategy likely to result in a satisfactory state of affairs if successfully implemented? |

## Section 2 — Leverage

**Simplifying and defining your work**
| Pts | Assignment |
|---|---|
| 10 | Conduct **work simplification** on your most tedious, time-consuming task. **Eliminate at least 30 percent of the total number of steps.** |
| 10 | **Define your output**: what are the output elements of the organization you manage *and the organizations you can influence*? List them **in order of importance**. |
| 10 | Analyze your **information- and knowledge-gathering system**. Is it properly balanced among "headlines," "newspaper articles," and "weekly news magazines"? **Is redundancy built in?** |

**Getting out of your office**
| Pts | Assignment |
|---|---|
| 10 | **Take a "tour."** Afterward, list the transactions you got involved in during its course. |
| 10 | **Create a once-a-month "excuse" for a tour.** |

**Delegation and discretionary time**
| Pts | Assignment |
|---|---|
| 10 | Describe **how you will monitor the next project you delegate** to a subordinate. What will you look for? How? **How frequently?** |
| 10 | Generate an **inventory of projects** on which you can work at discretionary times. |

**Meetings and the calendar**
| Pts | Assignment |
|---|---|
| **20** | **Hold a scheduled one-on-one with each of your subordinates.** (Explain to them in advance what a one-on-one is about. **Have them prepare for it.**) |
| 10 | Look at your calendar for the last week. **Classify your activities as low-/medium-/high-leverage.** Generate a plan of action to do more of the high-leverage category. **(What activities will you reduce?)** |
| 10 | **Forecast the demand on your time for the next week.** What portion is likely to be spent in meetings? Which are **process-oriented**? **Mission-oriented**? **If the latter are over 25 percent of your total time, what should you do to reduce them?** |

**Objectives and decisions**
| Pts | Assignment |
|---|---|
| **20** | Define the **three most important objectives** for your organization for the next three months. **Support them with key results.** |
| **20** | **Have your subordinates do the same for themselves**, after a thorough discussion of the set generated above. |
| 10 | Generate an **inventory of pending decisions** you are responsible for. Take three and **structure the decision-making process using the six-question approach**. |

**People**
| Pts | Assignment |
|---|---|
| 10 | **Evaluate your own motivational state** in terms of the Maslow hierarchy. **Do the same for each of your subordinates.** |
| **20** | **Give your subordinates a racetrack**: define a set of performance indicators for each. |
| 10 | List the **various forms of task-relevant feedback** your subordinates receive. **How well can they gauge their progress through them?** |
| 10 | **Classify the task-relevant maturity of each of your subordinates as low, medium, or high.** Evaluate the management style that would be most appropriate for each. **Compare what your own style is with what it should be.** |
| **20** | **Evaluate the last performance review you received and also the last set of reviews you gave**, as a means of delivering task-relevant feedback. How well did they improve performance? **What was the nature of the communication process during the delivery of each?** |
| 10 | **Redo one of these reviews as it should have been done.** |

## Suggested 100-Point Paths
Not in the book — a way to use it. Total available is 340 points across 25 assignments.

- **The installer's 100** (things that change the operating system): install indicators (20) + hold real one-on-ones with everyone (20) + set three objectives with key results (20) + cascade to subordinates (20) + give each subordinate a racetrack (20). *All five are 20-pointers; nothing here is merely analysis.*
- **The diagnostic 100** (if you're new to a group): define your output in priority order (10) + classify last week's calendar by leverage (10) + forecast next week's meeting mix (10) + inventory pending decisions and structure three (10) + classify each subordinate's TRM against your actual style (10) + list existing task-relevant feedback (10) + Maslow-evaluate yourself and your team (10) + limiting step for a live project (10) + six new paired indicators (10) + audit your information hierarchy for redundancy (10).
- **The feedback-repair 100**: audit reviews given and received (20) + redo one properly (10) + one-on-ones with everyone (20) + racetracks for each subordinate (20) + TRM classification vs. actual style (10) + list task-relevant feedback forms (10) + Maslow evaluation (10).

## Key Takeaways
1. Grove's own closing measure of the book is behavioral: 100 points of action, not comprehension.
2. The 20-point items are all *installations* — recurring routines and structures — while 10-pointers are one-time analyses.
3. The list doubles as a chapter index by practice: production (Ch 1–2, 6), leverage and time (Ch 3–4), planning and decisions (Ch 5–6), motivation and feedback (Ch 11–13).
4. Two assignments explicitly ask you to compare intent with reality: your actual style vs. the TRM-appropriate style, and your actual calendar vs. your leverage claims.
5. The meeting assignment encodes Grove's malorganization test as a personal audit: over 25% in *mission-oriented* meetings means fix something.
