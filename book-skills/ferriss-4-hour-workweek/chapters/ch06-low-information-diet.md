# Chapter 6: The Low-Information Diet — Cultivating Selective Ignorance

## Core Idea
Information is like calories: modern knowledge workers consume too much, from the wrong sources, most of it irrelevant, unimportant, or unactionable. Deliberately ignore it. **Selective ignorance** protects the time and attention that 80/20 just freed.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Low-Information Diet**: consume only information that is immediately and importantly actionable. Start with a **one-week media fast** (go cold turkey), then rebuild intake minimally.
  - When to use: when you feel overwhelmed, anxious from news, or perpetually "researching."
  - How: see the Worked Example.
- **Just-in-time vs. just-in-case information**: pull information only when a decision or task needs it (JIT), never hoard it "in case" it's useful someday.
  - How: before consuming anything ask, *"Will I definitely use this information for something immediate and important?"* If not, skip it.
- **The art of nonfinishing**: quitting a boring/unproductive book, article, movie, or task mid-stream is a skill. "Stopping something is often 10× better than finishing it" (absent a boss requirement).

## Key Concepts
- **Selective ignorance**: the trained ability to ignore or redirect irrelevant information and interruptions.
- **Media fast**: an abrupt one-week total cutoff of news, most reading, and non-essential web — the Band-Aid approach ("information is too much like ice cream").
- **Read-to-act**: read only what you'll apply within 24 hours (with rare, deliberate exceptions).

## Mental Models
- **"Ignorance may be bliss, but it is also practical."**
- **"Information is useless if it is not applied to something important, or if you'll forget it before you act."**
- **Being well-informed ≠ being effective.** Most news changes nothing you can act on.

## Anti-patterns
- **Just-in-case learning** — stockpiling knowledge for hypothetical future needs.
- **Compulsive news/feed checking** — high anxiety, near-zero actionability.
- **Finishing things out of guilt** — sunk-cost reading and watching.
- **"I'll just jump online for a minute"** — as realistic as "half a spoonful of ice cream."

## Worked Example — Running the Diet
1. **Go on an immediate one-week media fast.** No newspapers, magazines, non-fiction books (except this one), news websites, TV (except ~1 hr/day leisure), or non-work web browsing. Delete the bookmarks. The world "doesn't even hiccup."
2. **Fill the void with action.** When you reflexively reach for information, do something toward a dreamline milestone instead.
3. **Rebuild minimally, on demand.** After the week, add back only sources tied to a *current* decision or task. Ferriss reads front-page headlines through the newsstand glass on the walk to lunch — nothing more — and in 5 years had "not a single problem."
4. **Practice nonfinishing.** Abandon anything boring or unproductive without guilt.
5. **Outsource remaining reading**: have others summarize what you truly need (a preview of ch08 outsourcing).

## Key Takeaways
1. Cut information like calories — most is excess from bad sources.
2. Start with a one-week total media fast; go cold turkey.
3. Ask "will I use this immediately and importantly?" before consuming anything.
4. Prefer just-in-time to just-in-case; pull, don't hoard.
5. Learn to quit boring things mid-stream — nonfinishing is a superpower.
