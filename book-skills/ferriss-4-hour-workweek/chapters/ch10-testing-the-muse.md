# Chapter 10: Income Autopilot II — Testing the Muse

## Core Idea
Never manufacture on faith. **Micro-test** real customer demand with cheap advertising *before* investing in inventory — measure whether people will actually click and try to buy, for ≤ $500 in ~5 days.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Step 3 — Micro-Testing**: run a small paid-traffic test against a bare-bones offer to measure real purchase intent before spending on product.
  - When to use: after brainstorming products (ch09), before any manufacturing commitment.
  - How: the Worked Example below (test page + PPC + "temporarily out of stock").
- **Google AdWords / PPC test**: buy targeted search clicks ($0.05–$1 each) for terms your niche searches; drive them to a simple 1–3 page test site; measure click-through to "purchase."
  - Rule of thumb: **100 specific terms at $0.10/click beat 10 broad terms at $1.00/click** — more, cheaper, targeted traffic = more statistically valid results. Budget ~$500 (up to $1,000 for validity).
- **The "temporarily sold out" test**: collect purchase intent without charging. When a visitor clicks to buy, route them to a page that says the product is temporarily out of stock and captures their email — you get conversion data with zero fulfillment and no legal risk of charging pre-shipment.

## Key Concepts
- **Drop-shipping** (from ch09's Doug case): the manufacturer ships directly to the customer, so you hold no inventory and pay as little as ~45% of retail up to 90 days later (net-90). Near-zero financial risk.
- **eBay as a demand probe**: auction candidate products to test demand and the *highest possible* price before buying inventory (Doug ordered only after a customer ordered).
- **Test, don't guess**: real click-and-buy behavior beats surveys and opinions.

## Mental Models
- **Sell it before you build it.** Advertising and checkout behavior are the only honest votes.
- **Cheap, fast, reversible tests** — $500 and 5 days answer what months of deliberation can't.
- **Traffic quality > traffic quantity**: narrow, intent-rich keywords convert.

## Anti-patterns
- **Manufacturing/buying inventory before validated demand** — the classic muse-killer.
- **Broad, expensive keywords** that burn budget on low-intent clicks.
- **Charging customers before shipment** — often illegal; use the out-of-stock capture instead.
- **Trusting opinions over behavior** — "would you buy this?" ≠ a click and a credit-card attempt.

## Worked Example — The Basic Micro-Test (Doug's muse, then the general method)
Doug's 4-stage muse: **1) Market Selection** (a niche he knew) → **2) Product Brainstorm** (popular resale sound libraries) → **3) Micro-Testing** — he *auctioned products on eBay* to confirm demand and pricing, then built a Yahoo Store and tested Google AdWords → **4) Rollout & Automation** — added trade-magazine print ads and outsourced operations down to ~2 hrs/week.

General method:
1. Build a simple **1–3 page test site**: a landing/benefit page, a pricing/features page, and a checkout page.
2. Drive **AdWords PPC traffic** on many narrow keywords for ~5 days, ~$500 budget.
3. When a visitor clicks "buy," show **"temporarily out of stock"** and capture their email.
4. Read the **click-through-to-purchase-attempt rate**. Strong conversion → proceed to manufacture and roll out; weak → tweak positioning/price or kill the idea cheaply and try the next.

## Key Takeaways
1. Micro-test demand before manufacturing — always.
2. AdWords + a 1–3 page test site validates a product in ~5 days for ≤ $500.
3. Use "temporarily out of stock" to measure real purchase intent without charging.
4. Many narrow keywords beat a few broad ones for cost and validity.
5. Drop-shipping and net-90 terms let you sell with near-zero inventory risk.
