# Chapter 13: Mini-Retirements, Filling the Void, and the Top 13 Mistakes

## Core Idea
Don't defer life to a lump-sum retirement — distribute recovery and adventure as **mini-retirements** throughout life. But freedom brings its own challenge: once work is subtracted, you must deliberately *add* meaning through continual learning and service, or existential dread creeps in.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Mini-Retirements**: recurring 1–6+ month relocations/sabbaticals interspersed with work, rather than one retirement at the end. Not cheap travel — a rejection of location-lock.
  - When to use: as the standing rhythm of the New Rich life (Ferriss took 3–4/year).
  - How: relocate somewhere and *live* there (a month in a nice apartment costs ~the same as a few nights in a hotel); cancel duplicate stateside bills; use a countdown checklist (which shrinks to a few afternoons after the first trip).
  - Why it works: geoarbitrage makes extended slow travel *cheaper* than staying home.
- **Filling the Void — the two fundamentals**: freedom must be filled with **(1) continual learning** (e.g., become conversationally fluent in a language in ~3–6 months) and **(2) service** — "doing something that improves life besides your own."
  - How: use "life hubs" (a language, a place, a cause) as starting points that generate further experiences.
- **The Top 13 New Rich Mistakes**: the recurring failure modes of newly-free people (see Worked Example) — mostly relapses into W4W and micromanagement.

## Key Concepts
- **Existential panic**: the predictable slump ("can't drink another piña colada") when novelty fades — a signal to practice freedom as a skill, not a sign of failure.
- **Service as an attitude**: improving quality of life (as a musician, mentor, etc.) counts as much as saving lives; skip "my cause beats your cause" debates.
- **W4W relapse**: the master trap — inventing urgency and busywork to avoid the void.

## Mental Models
- **"Doubts are no more than a signal for action of some type."** When overwhelmed, take a break and 80/20 both business and personal life.
- **Freedom is a new sport** — exciting at first, then it takes deliberate practice to play well.
- **Whatever you're doing now is a stepping-stone**, not the end-all — no rut is permanent.

## Anti-patterns (the Top 13, condensed)
- **Perfectionism** — "great for a few things, good enough for the rest"; 95% right takes 6 months, 98% takes 20–30 years.
- **Micromanaging / emailing to fill time**; handling problems your outsourcers can; solving the same problem twice instead of writing an if-then rule.
- **Chasing customers** when cash flow already funds your dreams.
- **Working where you live/sleep/relax** — separate environments.
- **Skipping the 80/20 review** every 2–4 weeks.
- **Making non-urgent issues urgent** to justify work.
- **Treating one product/job as your whole identity.**
- **Expecting work to fill a void** that relationships and non-work life should — and **ignoring the social rewards** of life.

## Worked Example — The Top 13 New Rich Mistakes (verbatim intent)
1. Losing sight of dreams; falling into **W4W**.
2. **Micromanaging** and emailing to fill time.
3. Handling problems outsourcers/co-workers can handle.
4. Helping with the same problem more than once — give **if-then rules** and autonomy (Ferriss: fix anything < $400 without asking).
5. Chasing customers when cash flow suffices.
6. Answering email that won't produce a sale or that a FAQ/autoresponder could handle.
7. Working where you live, sleep, or should relax.
8. Not doing a thorough **80/20 analysis every 2–4 weeks**.
9. Striving for endless perfection instead of great-or-good-enough.
10. Blowing minutiae out of proportion as an excuse to work.
11. Making non-time-sensitive issues urgent to justify work.
12. Viewing one product/job/project as the be-all of existence.
13. Ignoring the social rewards of life — don't live it alone.

## Key Takeaways
1. Distribute life as recurring mini-retirements, not a deferred lump sum.
2. Extended slow travel is *cheaper* than staying put (geoarbitrage).
3. Fill the freed time with continual learning and service, or dread fills it.
4. Existential panic is normal — practice freedom like a sport.
5. Guard against the Top 13 relapses, especially W4W and micromanagement.
