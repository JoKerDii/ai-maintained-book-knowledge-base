# Chapter 4: System Reset — Dreamlining and Your Target Monthly Income

## Core Idea
Unrealistic goals are *easier* to achieve than realistic ones (less competition, more adrenaline). **Dreamlining** converts vague dreams into dated, priced targets and collapses them into a single number — your **Target Monthly Income (TMI)** — so "freedom" becomes a concrete cash-flow goal.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Dreamlining**: applying timelines to dreams. Fill 6-month and 12-month timelines with what you want to **have, be, and do**, then price them into a monthly cash-flow target.
  - When to use: right after fear-setting, to define what you're building toward.
  - How: the 4-step Worked Example below.
- **Being → Doing conversion**: every "being" goal is a temporary holding spot; convert it into a concrete *action* that would prove you'd achieved it (e.g., "be a great cook" → "make Thanksgiving dinner without help").
- **Target Monthly Income (TMI) & Target Daily Income (TDI)**: the dollar figure that funds your four chosen dreams as monthly cash flow — the target for the rest of the book.

## Key Concepts
- **Unreasonable and unambiguous**: big, specific goals beat modest vague ones. "10 and a 2" — a Greek-island trip (difficulty 10) is more motivating and more likely done than a dull Columbus, Ohio weekend (difficulty 2).
- **Monthly cash flow > grand totals**: reframe cost and income as dollars in/out per month. A $260K Lamborghini is ~$2,898/month; things cost far less than the sticker implies.
- **Financeable vs. one-time costs**: use per-month installment cost for financeable items.

## Mental Models
- **"Doing the unrealistic is easier than doing the realistic."** The realistic middle is the most crowded and least inspiring.
- **Adrenaline of a big goal** is the endurance you'll need for the inevitable obstacles.
- **Reframe every dream as a monthly number** — freedom is a cash-flow problem, not a lump-sum problem.

## Anti-patterns
- **Setting "realistic" goals** that don't inspire enough effort to clear the first hurdle.
- **Thinking in lump sums** ($1M in the bank) instead of monthly cash flow.
- **Leaving "being" goals abstract** instead of converting them into testable actions.

## Worked Example — The Dreamlining Worksheet (4 steps)
1. **Two timelines, three columns.** Create **6-month** and **12-month** timelines. In each, list up to **5 things you want to Have, Be, and Do**. Stuck on "doing"? Ask: "What would I do day-to-day if I had $100M?" and "What would excite me / make me feel alive?"
2. **Convert "Being" → "Doing."** Turn each state of being into a defining action (e.g., "be fluent in Spanish" → "hold a 5-minute conversation with a native speaker").
3. **Select the top 4.** Star the four most exciting/important dreams across all columns for the 6-month timeline (repeat for 12 months if desired).
4. **Compute TMI, then TDI.**
   - Total columns A (Have), B (Be→Do), C (Do) counting only the 4 selected dreams (any can be $0).
   - **TMI = (sum of the 4 monthly dream costs + your total monthly expenses) × 1.3** — the 1.3 adds a 30% buffer for safety/savings.
   - **TDI = TMI ÷ 30** — a daily income target (often easier to work toward).

Example: if the four dreams cost ~$1,000/month combined and living expenses are ~$3,000/month → TMI = (1,000 + 3,000) × 1.3 = **$5,200/month** → TDI ≈ **$173/day**.

## Key Takeaways
1. Pick unreasonably exciting goals — they're easier to finish than tepid ones.
2. Fill Have/Be/Do across 6- and 12-month timelines; convert "being" to "doing."
3. Price the top 4 dreams as *monthly* cash flow, not lump sums.
4. TMI = (dream costs + expenses) × 1.3; TDI = TMI ÷ 30.
5. This number is the finish line the Automation muse is engineered to hit.
