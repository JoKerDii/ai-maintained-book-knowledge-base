# Chapter 12: Disappearing Act & Beyond Repair — Escaping (or Killing) the Job

## Core Idea
For employees, Liberation means location freedom *before* hour reduction (the "L" of DELA). Engineer it with the **hourglass approach**: use a long proof-of-concept to win a short remote trial, then negotiate back up to full-time remote — creating leverage so refusal becomes too expensive.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The two levers of remote work**: (1) **demonstrate the business benefit** of remote working, and (2) **make it too expensive or excruciating to refuse** (raise your value; time the ask when firing you would be disruptive).
- **The Hourglass Approach (5-step escape)** — Sherwood's playbook:
  1. **Increase your value & investment in you.** Boost measurable output (but *hold some in reserve*); get the company to invest in you (e.g., paid training) so losing you costs more.
  2. **Prove remote productivity covertly.** "Call in sick" Tue–Thu (looks less like a long weekend, tests social isolation), double your output, leave an email trail, keep quantifiable records; use remote-access tools (e.g., GoToMyPC).
  3. **Prepare a business case.** A bullet-point list of what you achieved *more* of remotely, framed as a good business decision — not a personal perk.
  4. **Propose a small, revocable trial.** Ask for 1–2 remote days for two weeks, casually (no serious PowerPoint). **Bracket**: ask for two so you can fall back to one; the boss retains the right to cancel anytime.
  5. **Expand remote time.** After each successful trial, present results and up the ante (2→4→5 days, full-time), each time prepared to concede one step.
- **Bracketing**: ask for more than you need so your fallback is still your target.

## Key Concepts
- **Hold back productivity in reserve** so you can "suddenly" jump it during the trial.
- **When to time the ask**: during a period when firing you would be too disruptive (mid-project, your expertise needed).
- **Quitting leverage**: being genuinely willing to quit (funded by your muse) is the ultimate bargaining chip — *how* you ask matters as much as *when*.
- **Some jobs are Beyond Repair**: if the boss refuses remote work, the job will never give time freedom — get a new boss or become an entrepreneur.

## Mental Models
- **Ask for an inch, turn it into a foot** — without setting off panic alarms.
- **Frame remote work as *their* win**: lower office costs, higher productivity, happier (retained) employee.
- **Get fired tactfully rather than quit**, when severance/unemployment can fund a long trip.

## Anti-patterns
- **Asking for full-time remote up front** — too much to accept; triggers "are you quitting?" alarms.
- **Presenting the request as serious/irreversible** — a formal deck reads as a threat; keep it a casual trial.
- **Cutting hours before securing location freedom** (violating DELA) — gets you noticed and shut down.
- **Reducing in-office performance visibly** during the transition — maintain your lower baseline, spike your remote output.

## Worked Example — Sherwood's Escape (July → Oktoberfest)
Over ~2 months in slow season: he (1) got the company to pay for a design course and quietly raised output; (2) "called in sick" Tue–Thu, doubled his work via GoToMyPC, and logged results; (3) built a bullet-point business case; (4) proposed **two** remote days for two weeks (fallback: one) via a casual script, boss keeps veto; (5) expanded to four days, then requested full-time remote during a project needing his expertise — **prepared to quit** (his shirt muse funded his dreamlines). The boss acquiesced without the threat being used. Sherwood bought a $524 round-trip to Munich — less than one week of shirt sales.

## Key Takeaways
1. Employees: win location freedom first (DELA), via the hourglass approach.
2. Raise your value and get invested-in so losing you is costly.
3. Prove remote productivity quietly, with hard records, before you ask.
4. Propose small, casual, revocable trials; use bracketing; expand stepwise.
5. If refused, the job is beyond repair — leave, ideally on funded, tactful terms.
