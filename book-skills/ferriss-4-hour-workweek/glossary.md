# Glossary — The 4-Hour Work Week

**80/20 Principle (Pareto's Law)** — 80% of outputs come from 20% of inputs; often 90/10 or 95/5. Apply via two questions: which 20% causes 80% of problems (cut) and which produces 80% of results (multiply). (Ch 5)

**Absentee management** — see Management by Absence. (Ch 11)

**Absolute income** — income measured in raw dollars per year, ignoring hours worked. Contrast with relative income. (Ch 2)

**Batching** — grouping similar recurring tasks (email, calls, errands) and doing them on a set schedule to avoid repeated setup/switching cost (up to 45 min to resume interrupted work). (Ch 7)

**Bracketing** — asking for more than you need in a negotiation so your fallback position is still your real target. (Ch 12)

**Comfort challenge** — deliberate practice at social discomfort to build the action habit and dissolve fear. (Ch 3)

**Cost of inaction** — the financial, emotional, and physical price of *not* acting, measured over 1/5/10 years; usually far larger than the risk of acting. (Ch 3)

**Dealmaker** — one who treats rules outside science and law as negotiable; "Reality is negotiable" without being unethical. (Ch 1)

**DEAL** — Definition → Elimination → Automation → Liberation; the New Rich process and the book's structure. (Ch 1)

**DELA** — the employee's implementation order: read in DEAL order but win Liberation (location freedom) before cutting hours. (Ch 1)

**Deferred-life plan** — sacrificing your most capable years for freedom "someday"; the book's central target. (Ch 1)

**Distress** — harmful stress that weakens you (abusive bosses, destructive criticism); to be removed. (Ch 2)

**Dreamlining** — applying 6- and 12-month timelines to dreams (Have/Be/Do), then pricing them into a Target Monthly Income. (Ch 4)

**Drop-shipping** — the manufacturer ships directly to your customer, so you hold no inventory and pay a fraction of retail on delayed terms. (Ch 10)

**Effectiveness** — doing the things that move you toward your goals. Beats efficiency. (Ch 5)

**Efficiency** — doing a task (important or not) economically; useless unless applied to the right things. (Ch 5)

**Eliminate before you delegate** — never automate what can be eliminated; never delegate what can be automated. (Ch 8)

**Empowerment failure** — an interruption caused by someone needing your approval for something small; fixed with written if-then rules. (Ch 7)

**Eustress** — healthy, growth-producing stress (role models, training, comfort-expanding risk); to be sought. (Ch 2)

**Fear-setting** — a written exercise defining the worst case, its repairability, and the cost of inaction, to escape paralysis. (Ch 3)

**Freedom multiplier** — money's practical value multiplied by control of the four W's (what/when/where/whom). (Ch 1)

**Geoarbitrage** — exploiting global price/wage differences: earn strong currency, spend or hire in weak. (Ch 8, 13)

**Hourglass approach** — long proof-of-concept → short remote trial → negotiate back up to full-time remote. (Ch 12)

**Information product** — how-to content (book, DVD, course); the ideal muse: fast to make, high markup, FAQ-able. (Ch 9)

**Interruption** — anything preventing start-to-finish completion of a critical task; three types: time consumers, empowerment failures, self-generated. (Ch 7)

**Just-in-time vs. just-in-case information** — pull information only when a task needs it; never hoard "in case." (Ch 6)

**License (licensee/licensor)** — pay an inventor a royalty (~3–10% wholesale) to sell their product, or invent and let others sell yours. (Ch 9)

**Lifestyle Design (LD)** — deliberately engineering how and where you spend time rather than accepting the default track. (Ch 1)

**Low-information diet** — consuming only immediately actionable information; begins with a one-week media fast. (Ch 6)

**Management by Absence (MBA)** — a self-correcting business architecture that runs without you; be the roadside cop, not the tollbooth. (Ch 11)

**Media fast** — an abrupt one-week total cutoff of news and non-essential information. (Ch 6)

**Mini-retirement** — a recurring 1–6+ month relocation/sabbatical distributed throughout life instead of one end-of-life retirement. (Ch 2, 13)

**Minimum effective load** — doing the least necessary for maximum effect. (Ch 1)

**Muse** — an automated, low-maintenance business built solely to fund your dreamlines with minimal time. (Ch 9)

**New Rich (NR)** — those who design a free lifestyle now using time and mobility as currency. (Ch 1)

**Nonfinishing (art of)** — the skill of quitting boring/unproductive books, tasks, or media mid-stream. (Ch 6)

**Parkinson's Law** — a task swells in perceived importance/complexity to fill the time allotted; short deadlines force focus. (Ch 5)

**Premium positioning** — pricing above competitors ($50–200 range) to raise margin, perceived value, and customer quality. (Ch 9)

**Puppy Dog Close** — winning permanent change by proposing a small, reversible "just try it once" trial. (Ch 7)

**Relative income** — income per hour of life ($/hr); the true New Rich measure of wealth once absolute income clears your dream threshold. (Ch 2)

**Reselling** — buying wholesale and reselling; easiest to start, least profitable, fastest to die to price competition. (Ch 9)

**Selective ignorance** — the trained ability to ignore or redirect irrelevant information and interruptions. (Ch 6)

**Target Daily Income (TDI)** — TMI ÷ 30; a daily income target. (Ch 4)

**Target Monthly Income (TMI)** — (four dream costs + monthly expenses) × 1.3; the cash-flow goal your muse must hit. (Ch 4)

**Virtual architecture** — the interchangeable chain of outsourcers (fulfillment, call center, processor) that runs the muse. (Ch 11)

**Virtual assistant (VA)** — a remote assistant used as low-risk management training and life outsourcing. (Ch 8)

**W4W (work for work's sake)** — staying busy to feel productive or avoid hard questions; the book's most-hated acronym. (Ch 1, 5, 13)
