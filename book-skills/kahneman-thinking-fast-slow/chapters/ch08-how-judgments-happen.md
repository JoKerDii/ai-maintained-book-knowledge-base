# Chapter 8: How Judgments Happen

## Core Idea
System 1 continuously and effortlessly generates a set of "basic assessments" of any situation and can translate intensities across unrelated dimensions — but it computes more than asked (the mental shotgun), which makes these automatic evaluations available for substitution into harder questions.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Basic Assessments**: Continuous, automatic evaluations System 1 makes of the environment (threat, normality, approach/avoid, similarity, causality, availability, dominance, trustworthiness).
  - When to use: Recognizing the raw intuitive inputs that feed judgment before deliberation.
  - How: Note these run without intention and are used opportunistically to answer whatever question arises.
- **Intensity Matching**: The ability to translate a value on one dimension onto any other intensity scale.
  - When to use: Explaining how we produce numeric or cross-domain answers to vague questions.
  - How: Map a felt magnitude (e.g., Julie's precocity) onto another scale (height, income, GPA, crime severity) — feelings, dollars, loudness, and brightness are all intensity scales.
- **The Mental Shotgun**: System 1's tendency to perform excess, unrequested computations alongside the intended one.
  - When to use: Explaining intrusive irrelevant judgments that disrupt or bias a task.
  - How: Note that aiming at one computation scatters into others (spelling intrudes on a rhyme task; metaphorical truth intrudes on a literal-truth task).

## Key Concepts
- **Basic assessment**: An automatic, continuous System 1 evaluation (e.g., good/bad, safe/threatening) requiring no intention.
- **Dominance and trustworthiness**: Two facial dimensions read at a glance (square chin = dominance cue; expression = intention cue) — Todorov's work.
- **Judgment heuristic**: Falling back on an easy assessment (facial competence) when System 2 must judge something harder (who will govern well).
- **Averages vs. sums**: System 1 represents categories by prototypes, so it computes averages effortlessly but neglects sums and quantity.
- **Sum-like variables**: Quantities (total length, number of birds) that require adding up instances — ignored by System 1.
- **Prototype**: A typical exemplar standing for a whole category; drives average-like but not sum-like judgments.
- **Scope/quantity neglect**: Insensitivity to the number of instances, especially in emotional contexts.
- **Translating values across dimensions**: The competence behind "If Sam were as tall as he is intelligent, how tall would he be?"

## Mental Models
- Use "basic assessment" awareness when a snap impression of a face or situation is influencing a consequential judgment.
- Use "prototype vs. sum" when a decision requires quantity: System 1 will feel the average and ignore scale — force System 2 to count.
- Use "intensity matching" to notice you're converting a feeling into a number that may not be statistically appropriate.
- Use "mental shotgun" to explain why irrelevant information (you like the product) contaminates a distinct judgment (is the firm sound).

## Anti-patterns
- **Scope neglect**: Giving nearly identical responses whether saving 2,000, 20,000, or 200,000 birds — reacting to a prototype image, not quantity.
- **Voting the face**: Letting a rapid competence assessment from a photo drive a choice about governing ability (strongest for uninformed, TV-heavy voters).
- **Prediction by matching**: Translating one scale to another (reading age → GPA) as if it were a valid prediction — statistically wrong.
- **Shotgun contamination**: Letting an irrelevant automatic judgment (spelling, liking) intrude on the intended one.

## Worked Example
The Exxon Valdez willingness-to-pay study shows quantity neglect from prototype-based judgment. Separate groups of participants were asked how much they would pay for nets to keep migratory birds from drowning in oil ponds — the groups were told 2,000, 20,000, or 200,000 birds could be saved. If saving birds were treated as an economic (sum-like) good, saving 100 times more birds should command far more money. Instead average contributions were $80, $78, and $88 — essentially flat. Participants were not integrating over the number of birds; all three groups reacted to the same prototype: the vivid, awful image of a single helpless bird soaked in thick oil. Because System 1 represents the category "drowning birds" by a typical exemplar and computes averages rather than sums, the quantity barely moved the answer. Kahneman notes this near-complete neglect of quantity in emotional contexts has been confirmed many times.

## Key Takeaways
1. System 1 runs continuous basic assessments of the world with no intention and little effort.
2. We read dominance and trustworthiness from faces instantly; competence judgments from photos predict ~70% of election winners.
3. System 1 handles averages and prototypes well but neglects sums and quantity (scope neglect).
4. Intensity matching lets us translate a magnitude on one scale onto any other, generating cross-domain and numeric answers.
5. The mental shotgun makes System 1 compute more than requested, letting irrelevant assessments intrude and bias judgment.
6. These capabilities — basic assessments, intensity matching, the mental shotgun — jointly enable substitution, the subject of the next chapter.
