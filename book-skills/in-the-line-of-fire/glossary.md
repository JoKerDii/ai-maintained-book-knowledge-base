# Glossary — In the Line of Fire

**Active Listening** — Concentrating fully on the questioner to extract the Roman Column; the martial art of Concentration (Ch 4).

**Agility counters Force** — Core principle: beat a stronger/larger opponent (one-against-many) with skill and leverage, not brute force (Intro, Ch 9).

**Anticipate** — Pre-compile the worst questions you might face (questions only, not canned answers) (Ch 6, 8).

**Back reference ("As I said…")** — Citing earlier material; banned in Q&A as condescending. Use forward references only (Ch 5).

**Buffer** — A neutral restatement of a question that deflects negativity and tees up the answer; first line of self-defense. Three forms: paraphrase, "you"+paraphrase, Key Words (Ch 5).

**Contentious** — Combative reaction to a question (e.g., Perot's "You're lying!"); most damaging negative behavior (Ch 1, 9).

**Control** — The one-word thesis of the book; managing the question, answer, questioner, audience, time, and yourself (Intro).

**Cool Zone** — The neutral center where the Roman Column belongs; left zone = "Guilty!" (repeat), right zone = "Contentious!" (reverse) (Ch 5).

**Defensive** — Self-protective reaction (squirming, stammering) under a tough question (Ch 1).

**Double Buffer** — A buffer in front of the Buffer ("The question is…", "Glad you asked"); mostly useless fillers — fix by inserting "you" (Ch 5).

**Effective Management (Perceived)** — The positive subliminal impression created by well-handled questions; the goal of all techniques (Intro).

**Empty your cup** — Zen lesson: clear your own thoughts to receive the questioner's (Ch 4).

**Evasive** — Answering a different question than the one asked (Lott; Pedro Martinez) (Ch 1).

**Eye Connect** — Locking eyes with the questioner until they look back ("the click of engagement"); stronger than "eye contact" (Ch 4, 5).

**Fight or Flight** — The instinctive stress reaction underlying defensive/evasive (flight) and contentious (fight) behavior (Ch 1, 7).

**Forward reference** — Answering presented material fresh, without "as I said"; the only reference allowed in Q&A (Ch 5).

**Guilty as Charged** — A question whose charge is true; handled by the 5-step sequence: Buffer → Agree → "But…" → Evidence → Call to action + benefit (Ch 6).

**Head nod (involuntary)** — The questioner's automatic assent to a correct Buffer; your accuracy meter (a frown = wrong) (Ch 5).

**Key Words (Buffer)** — The most advanced Buffer: embed the key word and roll straight into the answer; zero thinking time, maximum sharpness (Ch 5).

**Manage the Answer** — Meet the issue only head-on; no tangents; ~60-second max (Ch 6).

**Paraphrase** — Restating the question "alongside" (para) in neutral form — NOT re-phrasing (re = again, carries the negative). Interrogative + Roman Column, voice drops at end (Ch 5).

**Point A → Point B** — A = audience uninformed/unconvinced; B = convinced and ready to act. Point B is your call to action (Ch 6).

**Quid Pro Quo** — "If they ask it, you must answer it"; address the Roman Column directly (Ch 6).

**Red Flag Issues (Universal)** — The handful of recurring concern areas: Management, Competition, Growth, Price/Cost, Contingencies, Timing, Problems, Intellectual Property (Ch 6).

**Return to Sender** — When you don't understand 100%, hand the floor back: "I'm sorry, I didn't follow; would you mind restating the question?" (Ch 4, 5).

**Roman Column** — The one or two words at the heart of a question (named for orators' memory-trigger columns); find it before answering. Questions can have a *double* Roman Column (Ch 4).

**Sub-vocalization** — Silently naming the key issue under your breath to crystallize the Roman Column (Ch 4).

**Topspin** — Ending an answer with Point B/WIIFY for a strong, offensive close (tennis metaphor); must be *earned* by answering first (Ch 6, 7).

**Triple Fail-Safe** — Three checkpoints before answering: ① grasp the Roman Column (else Return to Sender) ② Buffer + wait for head nod ③ frown → Return to Sender (Ch 5).

**Verbalize** — Rehearse answers *aloud* (Buffers, answers, Topspin) like a tennis volley (Ch 5, 8).

**Visual Listening** — Physical signals of attentiveness: balanced stance, Eye Connect, head nods, voice assent, steady fingers (Ch 4).

**WIIFY** — "What's In It For You" (pronounced "whiffy"); the audience's reason to act — why they should move to Point B (Ch 6).

**Winning Sequence** — Question → Buffer → Answer (with evidence) → Topspin (Ch 10).

**Worst-Case Scenario** — Prep assumption: every question maximally hostile, every group large (Ch 2).

**"Wrong Q&A"** — Weissman's rename of "Rude Q&A": pre-canning answers before understanding the question (Ch 3).

**"You're not listening!"** — The fatal audience perception (cousins: "That's not what I asked," "What I'm really asking…") (Ch 3).

**"Ready, Fire, Aim!"** — Answering before the target (key issue) is in your sights (Ch 3).
