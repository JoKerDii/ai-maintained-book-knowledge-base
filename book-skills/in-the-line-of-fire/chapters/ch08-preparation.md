# Chapter 8: Preparation (Martial Art: Discipline)

## Core Idea
None of the techniques work without disciplined preparation — "the most important part begins even before you put your hand on the sword." The two disciplines: **Prepare** (anticipate and position) and **Verbalize** (rehearse aloud, repeatedly).

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Prepare**: anticipate the worst case; list the questions you *don't want to hear*; find the Roman Columns in tough *and* easy questions; develop positions on every major (especially negative) issue; gather supporting evidence; define an overall strategy — all *well in advance*.
- **Verbalize**: speak your words *aloud* in practice exactly as you will in the real session (introduced in Ch 5). Enlist colleagues to fire tough questions; verbalize your **Buffers**, your **answers** (until succinct), and a **Topspin to every answer**. Rehearse "like a tennis volley" until "your returns of serve snap like whips." Treat every Q&A like an IPO road show.

## Key Concepts
- **1% inspiration, 99% perspiration** (Edison): mastery is disciplined practice (karate belt progression analogy).
- **Verbalization ≠ silent review**: politicians have spoken their messages countless times by debate day; you haven't that advantage, so manufacture it with mock sessions.

## Worked Examples
- **Kennedy vs. Nixon, 1960** (first televised debate): Kennedy arrived 3 days early, rested, tanned, wore a dark suit against the light-blue set; Nixon campaigned to exhaustion, underweight, ill-fitting clothes, "Lazy Shave" that he sweated through, a light suit that washed out on B&W TV. **TV viewers thought Kennedy won; radio listeners thought Nixon won** — preparation and image, not just words. Preparation became an imperative for every debate since.
- **Gore vs. Perot, NAFTA (deeper look)**: Gore spent two weeks studying Perot's character and tapes, assembling a team at the Naval Observatory. Key strategy (advisor Greg Simon): *"If you're dealing with a hothead, you make him mad… if you start interrupting him, he'll lose it."* Gore interrupted Perot repeatedly; Perot complained 8 times, then unraveled ("Give me your whole mind," "Work on it!"). Perot, notoriously *unprepared*, "cruised toward the discussion" and had earlier fired consultant Ed Rollins for ignoring advice. **Disciplined prep beat raw force.**

## Key Takeaways
1. Prepare the worst-case question list + positions + evidence + strategy, in advance.
2. Verbalize out loud, with colleagues, including a Topspin on every answer.
3. Image and rest are part of preparation (Kennedy/Nixon).
4. Studying your opponent's temperament can hand you the strategy (Gore/Perot).
