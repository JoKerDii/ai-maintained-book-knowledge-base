# Chapter 6: Provide the Answer (Martial Art: Balance)

## Core Idea
Only after Listening (Ch 4) and Buffering (Ch 5) do you answer — and the answer must hit the Roman Column **head-on, honestly, and succinctly**, then end with **Topspin** (a confident push to your Point B / WIIFY). Provide the positive Yang for the question's negative Yin.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Quid Pro Quo**: *If they ask it, you must answer it* (except things you genuinely don't know). Address the Roman Column directly; ducking → "That's not what I asked!"
- **Manage the Answer**: meet the issue *only* head-on — resist tangents and "a presentation after the presentation." Q&A exists only to *clarify*. Keep answers to a **~60-second maximum**.
- **Anticipate**: compile a list of the worst questions (questions only, not answers — see Ch 3's "Wrong Q&A") from colleagues, customers, partners, even competitors. 100 tough questions collapse into a handful of **Universal Red Flag Issues**: Management, Competition, Growth, Price/Cost, Contingencies, Timing, Problems ("what keeps you up at night?"), Intellectual Property. Write a **press-release-style position statement** for each, in advance — "do all your thinking offline, not on your feet."
- **Point B & WIIFY** (from *Presenting to Win*): **Point B** = where you want the audience to move (from Point A: uninformed → Point B: convinced and ready to act) = your call to action. **WIIFY** ("What's In It For *You*," pronounced "whiffy") = the audience's reason to act. *Point B = what you want them to do; WIIFY = why they should.*
- **Topspin**: ending an answer with Point B/WIIFY for a strong, confident close (tennis: a stroke that bounces sharply and is hard to return). You *earn* the right to Topspin by first answering. Add multiple upward swirls (coach gesture: forefinger skyward, twirl).
- **The Guilty-as-Charged sequence** (5 steps when the charge is *true*): **Buffer → Agree** ("You're absolutely right…", using their words) **→ "But…"** (don't agree too long; the loud "but" invalidates the admission — the 998th-word-"but" story) **→ Evidence → Call to action + benefit** (Point B + WIIFY).

## Key Concepts
- **Special question types**: *Tangential* (answer or take offline — no irrelevant questions); *Inaccurate* (refute: "No!"); *Unknown* (admit it, promise follow-up, ask for a card — unless it's central, then you can't duck); *Confidential* (give a positive *reason* — policy/security/legality — never "I can't reveal that"); *Speculative* (don't forecast; attribute restraint to policy).
- **Truth, again**: every answer must be honest and straightforward.

## Anti-patterns
- **Pleading guilty and surrendering** to a true charge — you don't have to; use the 5-step sequence.
- **Media Sound Bites trap**: a reporter can clip "You're absolutely right; it is a jungle out there" out of context. In *media* exchanges, do NOT agree with guilty-as-charged questions — counter immediately, or bury the admission in a subordinate clause before a Topspin: *"While the arena is a jungle, I'm fully confident we can succeed."*
- **G.W. Bush's "biggest mistake" evasion** (Apr 13, 2004): "I wish you'd given me this question ahead of time…" — read as evasive; the press chased it for months. Lesson: answer tough questions directly — lightly, frankly, or by standing your ground, but *answer*.

## Worked Example
**How G.H. Bush *should* have answered Marisa Hall:**
- **Buffer**: "How can a person of means find a cure for those who are less fortunate?"
- **Agree**: "You're absolutely right; I don't make mortgage payments or car payments…"
- **"But…"**: "…but that doesn't mean that I don't care."
- **Evidence**: "I care so much that I initiated X, Y, and Z programs to help people less fortunate than I am."
- **Call to action + WIIFY**: "So if you'll only elect me to a second term, I'll initiate even more such programs."

Weissman: had Bush done this, "the world might never have heard of Monica Lewinsky." The same skeleton works for a startup CEO facing "it's a jungle out there."

## Key Takeaways
1. Answer the Roman Column head-on, honestly, in ≤60 seconds — then Topspin.
2. Anticipate: prep questions (not canned answers) → position statements for ~8 universal red-flag issues.
3. Point B = what to do; WIIFY = why; Topspin = ending on them.
4. For true charges: Buffer → Agree → "But" → Evidence → Call to action.
5. In media, never agree with a guilty-as-charged frame — counter or subordinate it.
