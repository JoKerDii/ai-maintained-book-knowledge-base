# Chapter 7: Topspin in Action (Martial Art: Agility)

## Core Idea
Topspin moves you from defense (deflecting energy) to offense (asserting influence) — but it requires overriding the Fight-or-Flight instinct. The cardinal rule: **earn the right to Topspin by first answering the question.** Topspin before a real answer reads as evasive.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Earn the right to Topspin**: answer the Roman Column directly (quid pro quo), *then* add Point B / WIIFY. Topspin then flows naturally and counters the challenge.
- **Topspin to Point B vs. Topspin to a WIIFY**: end with the call to action ("we'll succeed in this market" — Point B) or the audience benefit ("in the long run you'll pay less" — WIIFY), depending on what counters the question.
- **Topspin as Yang to the question's Yin**: any "too expensive / too small / too late / too anything" charge can be countered with Topspin — *after* you neutralize with the Buffer and give a substantive answer (with evidence if possible).

## Key Concepts
- **Fight vs. Flight in answering**: Fight = terse defensive/evasive answer; Flight = short answer then rush to the next question. Both skip Topspin. Standing your ground to add Topspin "requires an act of extreme will."
- **Stay on message**: G.W. Bush's 2004 mantras — "Freedom is on the march," "A safer, stronger America," "We will prevail," "We have a moral responsibility" — repeated relentlessly as Topspin.

## Worked Examples
- **Dukakis misses a free kick (1988)**: With no opponent rebuttal possible, asked about his ability to lead, he *ended negatively*: "…it's very difficult to convey that." He should have ended with Topspin: "…they would vote for me for the presidency." Lesson: never end on a down-note; finish with Point B.
- **G.W. Bush's evolution** — the cautionary middle and the fix:
  - *2000, Lisa Kee* asks how his tax plan affects "a 34-year-old single person with no dependents." Bush rambles into elderly-care, Medicare, world peace, education — racing *past* the Roman Column (his tax plan), reaching a limp WIIFY ("…a good helping hand") with no conviction. **Topspin before answering = evasive, and it fizzled.**
  - *2001 Crawford press conf.*: answers the U.S./Russia question directly, then links forward with the key word "relationship" → "the more likely the world will be at peace" (WIIFY) → "defeat the evil ones" (Point B). Clean answer-then-Topspin.
- **Lloyd Bentsen (1988 VP debate)**: after Quayle compared his experience to JFK's, Bentsen's Topspin for the ages: *"Senator, I served with Jack Kennedy… you are no Jack Kennedy."*
- **Ronald Reagan (1984)**: asked about his age, *"Not at all,"* then immediate offensive Topspin: *"I am not going to exploit for political purposes my opponent's youth and inexperience."* Even Mondale laughed.

## Key Takeaways
1. Answer first — *then* Topspin; never lead with it.
2. Never end an answer on a negative; close with Point B or WIIFY.
3. Topspin overrides Fight (terse defense) and Flight (rush onward) — both skip it.
4. Repetition of a few key messages = campaign-grade Topspin.
