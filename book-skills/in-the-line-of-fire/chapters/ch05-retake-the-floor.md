# Chapter 5: Retake the Floor (Martial Art: Self-Defense)

## Core Idea
When you retake the floor after a hostile question, **don't answer and don't repeat — paraphrase** (the Buffer). Reframe the question around the bare Roman Column, stripped of charged words, so you start your answer from neutral ground. This is self-defense: become one with the opponent, level the playing field.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Buffer** — the first line of self-defense; a shock absorber that deflects negativity by restating the question neutrally. Benefits: signals "I heard you," condenses the ramble, buys thinking time, lets you verbalize/clarify the Roman Column, tees up the answer, makes the question audible to all. **Buffer every question, even friendly ones.**
- **Paraphrase** (not re-phrase): "para" = alongside; "re" = again (and repetition carries forward the negative). Build it: start with an interrogative (What/Why/How/Does/Can/Is) + the Roman Column + drop your voice at the end (statement, not question). *Use as few words as possible — less is more.*
- **The Cool Zone (Figure 5.1)**: Position the Roman Column in the neutral center. Repeating the charged question lands you in the left danger zone (**Guilty!**); reversing/invalidating it lands you in the right zone (**Contentious!**). Aim for the middle.
- **Key Words Buffer**: the most advanced Buffer — no paraphrase, just embed the key word and roll straight into the answer ("Our pricing rationale is…", "The way we compete is…"). Allows *zero* thinking time, so the Roman Column must already be crystal clear — but makes you look razor-sharp.
- **The Power of "You"**: insert "you" into the Buffer ("**Your** question is…" not "**The** question is…"). "You" is the most persuasive word in English (ahead of "love" and "money"); it keeps **Eye Connect**, makes the questioner feel attended, and lets you read their head-nod.
- **The Triple Fail-Safe** (checkpoints against rushing to the wrong answer):
  1. Retake the floor *only* after you fully grasp the Roman Column; else Return to Sender.
  2. Use the key word in your Buffer; hold Eye Connect until you see the **head nod**; move forward only then.
  3. If you get a **frown** instead of a nod, do NOT proceed — Return to Sender.
- **Three Buffer options (Figure 5.3)**: ① Paraphrase ② "You" phrase + paraphrase ③ Key Words. (①② buy thinking time but sound stilted if overused — "like a hoot owl"; ③ buys none but looks sharpest.)

## Key Concepts
- **The involuntary head nod**: a correct Buffer always produces an automatic nod (the equivalent of Marisa Hall's "Well, yeah, uh-huh"); a wrong one produces a frown/head-shake — your built-in accuracy meter.
- **Handling question types via Buffer**: Negative ("Why don't you…?") → turn positive, address only what you *are* doing. Irrelevant → *there is no irrelevant question; if they ask it, you must answer it.* Multiple disparate → pick ONE, answer it, then "You had another question." Statement (non-question) → turn into a question. Presented material → answer fresh, never "As I said…"; use **forward references only**.

## Anti-patterns
- **Repeating the question** ("Where do *we* get off charging so much?") → validates guilt.
- **Answering a question with a question** ("Why do you ask?") → evasive, irritating.
- **The Double Buffers to delete**: "The question is…", "That was a good question / I'm glad you asked" (judging questioners), "What you're really asking…" (insulting), "If I understand your question…" (signals you weren't listening), "The issue/concern is…" (confirms a problem exists). *Fix: insert "you."*
- **Back references ("As I said…")** during Q&A → impatient/condescending; rules differ from a presentation.

## Worked Example
**Three hostile questions → one-word Roman Columns + neutral paraphrase:**
- *"…sticker shock price twice your competition! Where do you get off charging so much?"* → Roman Column = **price** → "Why have we chosen this price point?" (Key Word Buffer: "Our pricing rationale is…")
- *"It's a jungle out there and you're just getting off the ground! What makes you think you can survive?"* → **compete** (NOT "survive" — that's the danger zone) → "How do we compete?"
- *"What makes you, a woman, think you can penetrate that old boy network?"* → **capability** (NOT "sexism") → "What are my capabilities to reach decision makers?"

**Colin Powell, Foreign Press Center, Apr 15, 2003** — the role model for Key Word Buffering. Across 11 questions he *never* paraphrased or used a Double Buffer; he opened each answer with the key word. To a question ending in "reformed," he didn't take the bait — *"The UN remains an important organization."* To one ending in "the settlements," he answered *"With respect to the roadmap…"* (the larger, favorable frame). And to the accusation "spread values at gunpoint" / "police of the world," he skipped the Buffer entirely and refuted: *"No, of course not."* (Inaccurate charges get immediate rebuttal — the "I never started beating my wife" answer.)

## Key Takeaways
1. Paraphrase, never repeat; strip charged words and land the Roman Column in the cool zone.
2. Buffer *everything*; three options — paraphrase, "you"+paraphrase, Key Words.
3. Wait for the involuntary head nod before answering; a frown = Return to Sender.
4. Inaccurate accusations get *no* Buffer — refute immediately ("No.").
5. There is no irrelevant question; pick one of multiple questions; forward references only.
