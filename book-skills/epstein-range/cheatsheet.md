# Range — Decision Cheatsheet

## Decision Tree: Is this a KIND or WICKED environment?

```
1. Do the same patterns recur reliably?        no -> WICKED
2. Are the rules stable, complete, and known?  no -> WICKED
3. Is feedback fast (within the decision)?     no -> WICKED
4. Is feedback accurate and unambiguous?       no -> WICKED  <- most decisive
   all four yes -> KIND
```
Then:
- **KIND** → repetition builds real intuition. Specialize, drill, trust the veteran, trust your gut, automate.
- **WICKED** → experience builds *confidence* faster than accuracy. Prefer breadth, outside views, explicit probability, aggressive belief updating. Distrust the confident veteran — including yourself.
- **MIXED / SHIFTING** → the common case. Re-score whenever rules change; expertise built under old rules becomes cognitive entrenchment under new ones.

## Core Decision Rules

- **When feedback is delayed or ambiguous, do not trust pattern-matching intuition** — because the environment never taught you the pattern, only that you had one.
- **When you're early in anything, sample rather than commit** — because match quality information is the scarce resource, and head-start advantages fade out.
- **When deciding whether to quit, ask "what have I learned about myself since I chose this?"** — because informed switching beats persistence, and grit research was measured on already-selected populations (restriction of range).
- **When practice feels smooth and fast, you are probably learning less** — because desirable difficulties depress in-session performance and raise retention.
- **When estimating a project, build a reference class first** — because your case's vivid details are exactly what corrupts the estimate.
- **When specialists are stuck, broadcast outward** — because winners come from the margins, and the answer may already be published in a literature they don't read.
- **When you can't win on frontier tech, win on application of withered tech** — because reach and reliability beat specs in most markets.
- **When the situation stops matching your training, drop the tool, don't grip it harder** — because overlearned routines are lethal once conditions change.
- **When a forecaster is famous and certain, discount them** — because fame correlates inversely with accuracy (hedgehogs make better television).
- **When your culture feels perfectly aligned, add cross-pressure** — because congruence suppresses the dissent that prevents catastrophe.

## Specialize vs. Sample

| Signal | Specialize now | Keep sampling |
|---|---|---|
| Environment | Kind, stable rules | Wicked or shifting |
| Skill ceiling | Age-limited (elite sport, music virtuosity) | No hard age ceiling |
| Self-knowledge | You've tested the fit against alternatives | Fit untested or inherited |
| Feedback | Fast and unambiguous | Delayed or noisy |
| Cost of switching | Rising steeply | Still low |
| Your evidence | "I tried others; this fits" | "I never tried anything else" |

## Breadth vs. Depth

| | Depth (I-shaped) | Breadth (foxes, T/π-shaped) |
|---|---|---|
| Wins at | Repeating problems, tactics, execution | Novel problems, strategy, transfer |
| Fails at | Rule changes, novel structure | Routine technical mastery |
| Learning | Blocked practice, fast gains | Interleaving, slow gains, far transfer |
| Best use | Kind domains, near-term output | Wicked domains, breakthroughs |
| Ideal | Not either — breadth *plus* at least one depth |

## Thresholds & Defaults Epstein Commits To

- **Default to delayed specialization.** Early specialization is the exception, justified only by a kind environment with a hard age ceiling.
- **The sampling period is integral, not a delay.** Late specializers catch up fast and match better; they trade a head start for fit.
- **Judge learning on delayed tests and novel problems**, never on in-session fluency or current test scores.
- **Use multiple, distant analogies** — one near analogy is worse than none.
- **Belief updating frequency is the single best predictor of forecast accuracy** — more than intelligence or subject expertise.
- **Plan experiments, not plans.** Short-term steps from present interests; revise on results.
- **Breakthroughs are high variance** — judge the portfolio and the process, not any single attempt.
- **Don't feel behind.** There is no universal timeline; comparison to the Tiger path is the error.

## Tells & Smells

**A wicked environment is masquerading as kind when**: success stories are told retrospectively as if the path were plannable; confident experts disagree with each other; feedback arrives long after the decision, or only via outcomes you can't attribute; "best practice" is defined by what current elites do *now*.

**Specialization is premature when**: the choice was made before any alternative was tried; the credential was committed to before the fit was known; interests are described in inherited language ("I've always been a X person"); the main argument for staying is time already invested.

**The match is bad when**: you're optimizing to survive the role rather than to do it; persistence is motivated by sunk cost, identity, or others' expectations; the "grit" you're exercising is consistency of interests you never chose.

**You're stuck in local search when**: everyone consulted shares your training; the problem is stated in terms that presuppose the solution's form; confidentiality reflex blocks asking outsiders; the first familiar approach keeps recurring (Einstellung).

**You're gripping a familiar tool when**: you demand quantification for a concern that can't be quantified; you defend the process rather than the outcome; dissent went to an offline caucus and came back unanimous; silence is being read as consent.
