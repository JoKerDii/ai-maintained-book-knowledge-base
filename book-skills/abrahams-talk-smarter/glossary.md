# Glossary — Think Faster, Talk Smarter

**AAA** — the apology structure: Acknowledge (the specific offense, owned) → Appreciate (its impact, esp. emotional) → Amends (what you'll do/change) (Ch 12).

**ADD** — the Q&A answer structure: Answer (one declarative sentence) → Detail an example (concrete evidence) → Describe the value (relevance to the asker). Order is flexible; all three should appear (Ch 10).

**Accessibility** — one of the four Focus filters: make ideas easy to grasp — plain language, relatable framing, no jargon (Ch 6).

**Back-pocket question** — a ready-made question you pose yourself ("A question I often get is…") to break the ice when no one asks in Q&A (Ch 10).

**The 4 I's** — the feedback structure: Information (specific, unemotional observation) → Impact ("I feel/think…") → Invitation (a request, ideally phrased as a question) → Implications (consequences of changing or not) (Ch 11).

**Anxiety Beast** — Abrahams' name for speaking anxiety; managed (not eliminated) by addressing both symptoms and sources (Ch 1).

**Buffering** — in Q&A, briefly restating or bridging from a question to buy thinking time and ensure you answer what was actually asked (Ch 10).

**Concision** — one of the four Focus filters: say it in as few words as needed; less is more (Ch 6).

**Dare to be Dull** — the Unlock mantra: give yourself permission to be merely adequate; lowering the bar frees you to speak fluently (Ch 2).

**Focus (the F-word)** — the sixth step: streamline what you say through Precision, Relevance, Accessibility, Concision (Ch 6).

**Greeting your anxiety** — acknowledging nervousness ("hello, anxiety") instead of fighting it, which reduces its grip (Ch 1).

**Growth mindset** — seeing spontaneous-speaking ability as improvable through practice; part of Redefining the situation (Ch 3).

**Inner critic** — the self-monitoring voice that drives perfectionism and freezing; quieted in the Unlock step (Ch 2).

**Maximize Mediocrity** — the aim of the Unlock step: accept "good enough" in the moment so you can actually perform (Ch 2).

**Opportunity, not threat** — the core Redefine reframe: treat being put on the spot as a chance, changing your physiology and performance (Ch 3).

**Pace and space** — giving yourself time and room (pausing, listening) before responding, rather than rushing (Ch 4).

**PREP** — a spontaneous structure: Point, Reason, Example, Point; for clear opinions and answers (Ch 5).

**Precision** — one of the four Focus filters: choose exact, concrete words that convey your meaning (Ch 6).

**Problem–Solution–Benefit** — the persuasion/pitch structure: name the problem, present the solution, spell out the benefit (Ch 5, 9).

**Past–Present–Future** — a structure for narrating change, updates, and transitions (Ch 5).

**Relevance** — one of the four Focus filters: include only what matters to *this* audience (Ch 6).

**Shift vs. Support response** — in conversation, a "shift" turns attention to yourself; a "support" response keeps focus on the other person — favor support (Ch 7).

**Six-Step Method** — Calm, Unlock, Redefine, Listen, Structure, Focus: the book's core methodology for spontaneous speaking (Part I).

**Spontaneous speaking** — unplanned, in-the-moment communication (questions, small talk, toasts, feedback); the book's whole subject.

**Structure** — a simple reusable story spine (not a script) that organizes thoughts on the fly; the fifth step (Ch 5).

**WHAT formula** — for toasts/tributes/introductions: Why are we here → How are you connected → Anecdotes/learnings → Thank (Ch 8).

**What? So What? Now What?** — the most versatile spontaneous structure: what it is → why it matters → what to do next (Ch 5).
