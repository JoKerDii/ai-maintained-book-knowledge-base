## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Big Idea
Spontaneous speaking (being put on the spot) fails not from lack of ideas but from **anxiety + the pursuit of perfection**. The cure is to manage your state, lower the bar, and lean on **pre-learned structures** so that in the moment you only have to *fill in*, not *invent*. Aim to **be great in the moment, not perfect.**

### The Six-Step Method (Part I) — mnemonic order: Calm · Unlock · Redefine · Listen · Structure · Focus
1. **Calm — Tame the Anxiety Beast (Ch 1).** Manage symptoms and sources of anxiety: reframe (it's a *conversation*, not a performance), greet your anxiety, breathe (long exhales / belly breathing), stay present-oriented, use a mantra, work with anxiety rather than against it.
2. **Unlock — Maximize Mediocrity (Ch 2).** Get out of your own way. Silence the inner critic and **"dare to be dull"** — permission to be merely okay unlocks fluency. Perfectionism causes freezing; good-enough gets you speaking.
3. **Redefine — Mind Your Mindset (Ch 3).** See spontaneous speaking as an **opportunity, not a threat**; a conversation, not a test; adopt a growth/abundance mindset. How you *label* the situation changes your physiology and performance.
4. **Listen — Don't Just Do Something… Stand There! (Ch 4).** Slow down and truly listen; give yourself **pace and space**. Deep listening (to others *and* your own intuition) reveals what the moment actually needs before you respond.
5. **Structure — Structure Your Spontaneity (Ch 5).** Use a simple **structure** to organize thoughts on the fly. Structure = a story spine, not a script. Core reusable structures below.
6. **Focus — The F-Word of Spontaneous Speaking (Ch 6).** Streamline to what matters using four filters: **Precision, Relevance, Accessibility, Concision**. Say less, land more.

### Reusable Spontaneous Structures (Ch 5 — the workhorses)
- **What? / So What? / Now What?** — describe it → why it matters → what to do next. The most versatile all-purpose structure.
- **Problem / Solution / Benefit** — the persuasion/pitch spine (Ch 9).
- **PREP** — Point, Reason, Example, Point. For opinions and clear answers.
- **Past / Present / Future** — for narrating change, updates, and transitions.
> Rule: pick the structure *first*, then fill it. In the moment you assemble; the structure did the heavy lifting.

### Situation Structures (Part II)
- **Small talk (Ch 7)** — reframe as a shared game; seek comparable self-disclosure; be a "support" responder, not a "shift" responder; don't put your partner on the spot.
- **Toasts, tributes, introductions (Ch 8) — the "WHAT" idea**: Why are we here → How are you connected → Anecdotes/learnings → Thank. It's about *them*, not you.
- **Pitch / persuasion (Ch 9)** — Problem → Solution → Benefit; use analogies, frame benefits positively, address obstacles, tone down perfectionism.
- **Q&A (Ch 10) — "ADD"**: **A**nswer (one clear declarative sentence) → **D**etail an example (concrete evidence) → **D**escribe the value (relevance to the asker). Prepare likely questions; keep a back-pocket question; wait five seconds before closing.
- **Feedback (Ch 11) — "the 4 I's"**: **I**nformation (specific, unemotional observation) → **I**mpact ("I feel/think…") → **I**nvitation (a request, ideally a question) → **I**mplications (consequences of changing or not).
- **Apologies (Ch 12) — "AAA"**: **A**cknowledge (the specific offense, own it) → **A**ppreciate (the impact on others, especially emotional) → **A**mends (what you'll do/change). A conditional apology deepens the offense.
