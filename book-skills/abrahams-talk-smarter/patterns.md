# Patterns & Structures — Think Faster, Talk Smarter

Structures are the heart of the method: rehearse them cold, and in the moment you only *fill in the blanks*.

## What? / So What? / Now What?
**When to use**: the universal default — answers, updates, reactions, feedback, most on-the-spot moments.
**How**: **What** = describe the thing/idea/situation. **So What** = why it matters to this audience. **Now What** = the implication, next step, or ask.
**Trade-offs**: so flexible it can feel generic; sharpen with a concrete example in the "What."

## PREP (Point, Reason, Example, Point)
**When to use**: stating an opinion or giving a crisp answer under pressure.
**How**: **Point** (your position) → **Reason** (why) → **Example** (evidence/story) → **Point** (restate).
**Trade-offs**: best for single clear claims; less suited to complex multi-part answers.

## Problem / Solution / Benefit
**When to use**: persuading, pitching, proposing, selling an idea (Ch 9).
**How**: **Problem** (the pain, made concrete) → **Solution** (your proposal) → **Benefit** (what the listener gains).
**Trade-offs**: weak if the "problem" isn't one the audience feels — establish the problem first.

## Past / Present / Future
**When to use**: status updates, narrating change, transitions, career/story questions.
**How**: where we were → where we are → where we're going.
**Trade-offs**: chronological, so it can drag — compress past/present, dwell on future.

## The WHAT formula (toasts, tributes, introductions — Ch 8)
**When to use**: weddings, launches, panels, farewells, introducing a speaker.
**How**: **Why** are we here (the occasion) → **How** are you connected to the honoree → **A**necdotes or learnings (one vivid story) → **T**hank (close with gratitude). Keep it about *them*.
**Trade-offs**: brevity matters — one anecdote, not three.

## ADD — the Q&A answer structure (Ch 10)
**When to use**: fielding questions after a talk, in interviews, in meetings, tough questions from a boss.
**How**: **A — Answer** the actual question in one clear declarative sentence (no preamble) → **D — Detail an example** (a few sentences of concrete evidence) → **D — Describe the value** (relevance/benefit to the asker; for a negative question, use this step to state your remedy/plan). Order is flexible but keep all three. Prep likely + toughest questions in advance; keep a **back-pocket question**; wait five seconds before closing; answer honestly ("I don't know — I'll follow up by Friday").
**Trade-offs**: don't over-elaborate the example; don't bluff.

## Anxiety-Management Toolkit (Calm — Ch 1)
**When to use**: before/at the moment of being put on the spot.
**How**: reframe as a *conversation*, not a performance; greet the anxiety; take slow exhale-heavy breaths (belly breathing) to lower arousal; use a grounding **mantra**; hold objects / cool your hands to manage physical symptoms; stay present-focused.
**Trade-offs**: aim to *channel* moderate anxiety (it sharpens you), not erase it.

## Dare to be Dull (Unlock — Ch 2)
**When to use**: when perfectionism is making you freeze or over-prepare.
**How**: deliberately lower the bar — permit a merely-okay response; quiet the inner critic; get the first words out. Fluency beats flawlessness.
**Trade-offs**: not license to be careless — it's a starter to break the freeze.

## Redefine the Situation (Redefine — Ch 3)
**When to use**: the instant you feel "put on the spot."
**How**: relabel threat → opportunity, test → conversation, verdict → practice. The label you assign changes your physiology and behavior.
**Trade-offs**: takes rehearsal to make the reframe automatic under stress.

## Listen with Pace and Space (Listen — Ch 4)
**When to use**: before responding to any question or comment.
**How**: pause; listen to what's said *and* unsaid and to your own intuition; give yourself space rather than rushing to fill silence. Then respond to what's actually needed.
**Trade-offs**: silence feels longer to you than to them — tolerate it.

## Focus Filters (Focus — Ch 6)
**When to use**: to tighten any spontaneous response.
**How**: run it through **Precision** (exact words) → **Relevance** (only what this audience needs) → **Accessibility** (plain, relatable) → **Concision** (fewest words). Cut the rest.
**Trade-offs**: over-editing in real time can stall you — apply lightly, favor concision.

## Support (not Shift) responses (Small talk — Ch 7)
**When to use**: conversations, networking, small talk.
**How**: when someone shares, respond with a **support** move (ask more, build on it) rather than a **shift** move (redirect to your own story). Seek comparable self-disclosure; don't put your partner on the spot.
**Trade-offs**: too much support with no sharing feels like an interview — balance.

## The 4 I's — the feedback structure (Ch 11)
**When to use**: giving feedback in the moment — to a report, peer, boss, student, or child.
**How**: **Information** (specific, verifiable observation, emotion stripped out; scope what you're *not* discussing) → **Impact** (the effect on you, first-person: "I feel/think…", which lowers defensiveness) → **Invitation** (a specific request, ideally a question — "How can we…?" — or a declarative for urgency) → **Implications** (positive/negative consequences of adopting or rejecting the change).
**Trade-offs**: skipping Impact makes it feel like an attack; skipping Invitation makes it a lecture, not a collaboration.

## AAA — the apology structure (Ch 12)
**When to use**: after you've caused offense or dropped the ball ("emergency roadside assistance" for a relationship).
**How**: **Acknowledge** the specific offending behavior and own it (no justifying/minimizing) → **Appreciate** the impact on others, especially the emotional consequences (don't "put it in perspective") → **Amends** — state specifically what you'll do, stop doing, or how your thinking will change.
**Trade-offs**: a defensive or conditional apology ("sorry you felt that way," "I'm sorry, but…") worsens the breach.
