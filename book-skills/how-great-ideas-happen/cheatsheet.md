# Cheatsheet — *How Great Ideas Happen* (Newman)

## Which stage am I in? (diagnose, then act)
| Tell | Stage | Do this | Mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| No idea where to look; scanning randomly | **Surveying** | Expand influences; low-res survey many domains; find the problem | Deep → surface |
| Have a direction, no map | **Gridding** | Write the guiding question; build the What/Why Grid | Deep |
| Have a mapped cell, few ideas | **Digging** | Volume; evaluation-free; double your target | Surface |
| Too many ideas, can't choose | **Sifting** | Distance + subtraction; others pick | Deep |

## Decision rules
- **When your idea feels strikingly original, cut the novelty** — audiences penalize excess originality; hits cluster at ~90–95% conventional / 5–10% novel.
- **When you feel tapped out mid-session, run another round** — the creative cliff is an illusion; late ideas rate higher.
- **When you must judge your own ideas, don't** — the creative endowment effect is immune to warnings and examples; only elapsed time or another person's eyes fix it.
- **When the brief feels impossibly constrained, stop negotiating the constraints** — the most-constrained groups produce the highest-rated work.
- **When an idea makes you uncomfortable, develop it 10–15 min before discarding** — discomfort marks an information gap, which is where curiosity (and value) peaks.
- **When editing, ask what to remove first** — people default to adding; subtraction has to be prompted explicitly.
- **When the work underperforms, tweak and ship again — don't restart** — Wright's law: ~10–15% improvement per doubling of cumulative output.
- **When everyone around you agrees, go to weak ties** — strong ties know what you already know.
- **When you notice a cross-domain analogy, don't stop at Recognition** — map Relevance element-by-element, or it's decoration.
- **When you're stuck and working harder, change inputs instead of effort** — read others' work, adjacent fields, new places.
- **Never say "be creative"** — the instruction itself cuts idea productivity ~20%. Say "as many ideas as possible."

## Guiding question — four-criteria gate
Keep it only if it is **specific** · **open-ended** · **motivating** · **measurable**.
Template: *"What is a [project type] that [unique value/meaning] for [audience] in [context], despite [obstacle]?"*
- Bored delivering your own one-minute pitch? → the **idea** is wrong, not the pitch.
- Starting from what you want to say? → flip **inside-out → outside-in**: what will they think about on the drive home?

## Three Roadblocks (Conclusion) — pick the diagnosis
- **Stuck with nothing** → *not enough work up front*. Missing why/guiding question or grid. Go back to Gridding.
- **Stuck with a mediocre idea** → *settled too early*. "Ideas are cheap" is false; breakthrough ideas are rare. Go back to Digging.
- **Stuck defending an idea** → *in love with the idea, not the process*. Also applies to being attached to *finishing*. Go to Sifting-with-others.

## Transplanting: the Three R's — where it fails
| R | Question | Failure mode |
|---|---|---|
| Recognition | What in another domain resembles this? | Stopping here (most common) |
| Relevance | Which elements map, exactly? | Metaphor mistaken for mapping |
| Refinement | Does it survive prototyping here? | Skipping domain experts |
Different people are good at different R's — staff each stage separately.

## Tower vs. Raft (Sifting posture)
| | Building a Tower | Floating a Raft |
|---|---|---|
| Stance | Cram everything in, defend | Audience hunts for holes |
| Move | Add | Subtract |
| Test | "Look what I made" | "What does this element contribute to my guiding question?" |
Default is Tower. Force the Raft frame deliberately.

## Feedback triage
- **Weight the tails**, not the agreeable middle — outlier comments carry the signal.
- **Then apply the frequency rule** to the middle: many people tripping on the same thing is real, however badly they explain it.
- Ask *"What specific aspects are strong / could be improved?"* and *"Does this remind you of things you've seen?"* — never *"Do you like it?"*
- State your guiding question as context **first**, and pre-declare what you will not change.
