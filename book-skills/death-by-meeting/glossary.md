# Glossary — Death by Meeting

**Ad Hoc Strategic Meeting** — a separately convened strategic meeting for a critical
issue that can't wait for the next Monthly Strategic; kept distinct from the Weekly
Tactical so minds reset and enough time is allotted (Ch 5).

**Boondoggle** — a self-indulgent, socially-driven off-site that wastes the
opportunity for critical conversations; an anti-pattern for the Quarterly Off-Site (Ch 5).

**Cascading Messages** — agreeing at meeting's end on what to communicate downward to
the rest of the organization; a field on the Weekly Tactical guide (Ch 5).

**Contextual Structure** — clearly distinguishing the purpose, format, and timing of
each meeting type so participants know what mode they're in; the cure for Problem #2 (Ch 4).

**Daily Check-in** — ~5-minute, stand-up daily meeting to share schedules/activities
and keep priorities aligned; genre: Headline News (Ch 5).

**Disciplined Spontaneity** — planning the meeting's structure while letting the
*agenda* emerge live from real activity and progress; basis of the Real-Time Agenda (Ch 5).

**Drama** — legitimate, constructive ideological conflict that keeps people engaged;
its absence makes meetings boring (Problem #1) (Ch 2–3).

**Fear of Conflict** — reluctance to engage in unfiltered ideological debate; a named
challenge that undermines Monthly Strategic and Off-Site meetings (Ch 3, 5).

**The Four Meetings** — Daily Check-in, Weekly Tactical, Monthly Strategic, Quarterly
Off-Site Review; the structural solution to meeting stew (Ch 5).

**The Hook** — jolting participants in the first ten minutes by making the stakes
vivid, so they have a reason to care (Ch 3).

**Huddle** — Verne Harnish's term (from *Mastering the Rockefeller Habits*) for the
short daily meeting Lencioni adapted into the Daily Check-in (Ch 5).

**Ideological Conflict** — disagreement about ideas/issues (productive), as opposed to
personal or political conflict (destructive) (Ch 3).

**Lightning Round** — opening segment of the Weekly Tactical where each person states
their 2–3 weekly priorities in ≤60 seconds; sets the tone and surfaces issues (Ch 5).

**Lack of Contextual Structure** — Problem #2: cramming all issue-types into one
meeting, leaving no clear context for any discussion → ineffective meetings (Ch 4).

**Lack of Drama** — Problem #1: removing conflict, the ingredient that makes meetings
engaging → boring meetings (Ch 3).

**Magnitude Mismatch** — the difficulty of shifting between decisions of very
different weight (e.g., travel policy vs. a merger) in one meeting (Ch 4).

**Meeting Paradox** — meetings are simultaneously critical and painful; resolvable by
fixing attitude and design, not by avoiding meetings (Ch 2).

**Meeting Stew** — the structural error of throwing every issue-type into one
undifferentiated staff meeting "like a bad stew" (Ch 4).

**Meetings vs. Movies** — meetings *should* beat movies for engagement because they're
interactive and directly relevant, while movies are passive and irrelevant (Ch 2–3).

**Mining for Conflict** — the leader's deliberate act of seeking out and surfacing
buried disagreements, even at the cost of being temporarily unpopular (Ch 3).

**Monthly Strategic** — 2+ hours/topic meeting (monthly or biweekly) to debate and
decide a few critical long-term issues; genre: feature film (Ch 5).

**Parking Lot** — capturing an out-of-context (usually strategic) issue raised in a
tactical meeting so it gets the right forum later; the Monthly Strategic is the
standing parking lot (Ch 4–5).

**Progress Review** — Weekly Tactical segment reviewing a handful (4–6) of key metrics
quickly, without deep discussion (Ch 5).

**Quarterly Off-Site Review** — 1–2 day quarterly meeting to review strategy, team,
personnel, and competition holistically; genre: mini-series (Ch 5).

**Real-Time Agenda** — setting the Weekly Tactical's agenda only *after* the lightning
round and progress review, based on actual current activity (Ch 5).

**Real-Time Permission** — the leader interrupting a first tense exchange to affirm the
conflict is good and wanted, separating ideological passion from fear of rejection (Ch 3).

**Sneaker Time** — uncounted hours spent after bad meetings clarifying via email,
voicemail, and hallway visits; the hidden cost good meetings eliminate (Ch 6).

**TV/Film Genre Analogy** — mapping each meeting type to a media format (Headline News,
sitcom, feature film, mini-series) so context sets expectations (Ch 1).

**Weekly Tactical** — 45–90 minute weekly meeting to review activities/metrics and
resolve tactical obstacles; goals: resolution of issues + reinforcement of clarity (Ch 5).

**Will's White Board** — the fable's artifact where Will maps the four meetings to TV/
film genres and diagnoses the missing elements of meetings (Ch 1).
