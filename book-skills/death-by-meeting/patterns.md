# Patterns & Techniques — Death by Meeting

## The Hook (First Ten Minutes)
**When to use**: opening any important meeting, especially a dry-sounding one.
**How**: in the first ~10 minutes, make the stakes vivid — name a competitive threat,
the cost of a bad decision, or the mission's impact on customers/employees/families.
Give people "a reason to care."
**Trade-offs**: requires the leader to prepare a genuine stake; overuse or manufactured
urgency reads as manipulation. The conflict/stakes must be legitimate.

## Mining for Conflict
**When to use**: real disagreement exists but is being politely buried.
**How**: as leader, prioritize uncovering unresolved disagreements; when people dodge,
force the issue onto the table. Both leaders and participants should act as "miners."
**Trade-offs**: temporarily unpopular; risks tipping into personal conflict if not kept
ideological. The cost of *not* doing it is worse — buried conflict returns as politics.

## Real-Time Permission
**When to use**: the tense moment a team unaccustomed to conflict first debates openly.
**How**: interrupt, name that the conflict is exactly what's wanted, encourage both
parties to continue, remind them questioning each other improves the outcome.
**Trade-offs**: feels "paternal," but is "remarkably effective" at separating
ideological passion from interpersonal fear so debate can continue.

## The Lightning Round
**When to use**: opening of every Weekly Tactical.
**How**: go around the table; each person names 2–3 priorities for the week in ≤60
seconds. ~10 minutes total even for a large team.
**Trade-offs**: strict 60-second cap can feel rushed, but over-detail loses the room
and obscures the real issues. Sixty seconds is "a lot longer than it seems."

## Progress Review
**When to use**: right after the lightning round in the Weekly Tactical.
**How**: quickly review a *handful* (4–6) of key metrics; clarifying questions only.
~5 minutes. Avoid deep discussion of underlying issues here.
**Trade-offs**: reviewing too many metrics drowns the signal; pick the few that define
success.

## Real-Time Agenda (Disciplined Spontaneity)
**When to use**: setting the Weekly Tactical agenda.
**How**: do *not* pre-set the agenda. After the lightning round + progress review
(~15 min in), let the agenda emerge from what people are actually working on and how the
company is actually performing. Plan the structure; improvise the content.
**Trade-offs**: sacrifices some control and predictability; requires leader discipline
to resist preparing an agenda 48 hours early. Payoff: the meeting addresses what
actually matters this week.

## Parking Lot (Park Strategy in Tactical Meetings)
**When to use**: a strategic/long-term issue surfaces during a Weekly Tactical.
**How**: take it off the table and add it to a list for the next Monthly (or Ad Hoc)
Strategic. Don't discuss it now.
**Trade-offs**: feels like deferring something important, but mixing magnitudes wrecks
both topics; a trusted parking lot gives people confidence to table issues.

## Ad Hoc Strategic Meeting
**When to use**: a critical strategic issue genuinely can't wait for the next Monthly
Strategic.
**How**: convene a *separate*, clearly delineated meeting (even later the same day);
allot enough time for real analysis. Keep it distinct from the Weekly Tactical.
**Trade-offs**: disrupts schedules — justified only for truly critical issues, but
those are exactly when great teams outmaneuver competitors.

## The Trial-Period Commitment
**When to use**: introducing a new meeting type (especially the Daily Check-in).
**How**: commit to running it consistently for a set period (~2 months) before judging
whether it works; keep time/place consistent and never cancel.
**Trade-offs**: forces persistence through early awkwardness when abandonment is
tempting.

## Outside Facilitator (Off-Site Exception)
**When to use**: a Quarterly Off-Site where the leader wants to participate fully.
**How**: bring in a trusted facilitator who knows the business and serves the team's
objectives — not an outsider who changes team dynamics.
**Trade-offs**: the *only* sanctioned exception to "no outsiders at the off-site";
choose someone driven by the team's goals, not their own.

## Genre-Tagging Meetings (TV/Film Analogy)
**When to use**: teaching/enforcing the four-meeting structure.
**How**: label each meeting by its genre so attendees bring the right expectations —
Headline News (Daily), sitcom (Weekly), feature film (Monthly), mini-series (Quarterly).
**Trade-offs**: an explanatory device, not a rule; useful for buy-in and for keeping
meetings in their lane.
