## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Central Diagnosis — The Two Problems
Meetings are not inherently bad. They fail for exactly two reasons:
1. **Lack of Drama** → meetings are **boring** because leaders strip out conflict.
2. **Lack of Contextual Structure** → meetings are **ineffective** because every
   issue-type is crammed into one undifferentiated meeting ("meeting stew").

**Diagnose first**: *boring* = drama problem; *nothing decided* = structure problem.

### Fixing Drama — Be a Miner of Conflict
- **The Hook**: in the **first 10 minutes**, make the stakes vivid so people have a
  reason to care (name the competitive threat, the cost of a bad call, the mission).
- **Mining for Conflict**: deliberately seek out and surface buried disagreements —
  even when it makes you temporarily unpopular. Avoided conflict returns later as
  politics and hallway whispering.
- **Real-Time Permission**: when first conflict gets tense, interrupt to affirm it's
  good and wanted — this separates ideological passion from fear of personal rejection.
- Pursue only **constructive, ideological** conflict (about ideas), never personal.
- Mental model: *conflict is to a meeting what it is to a movie* — the source of
  engagement. Meetings should beat movies: they're interactive and relevant.

### Fixing Structure — The Four Meetings
Replace the single "staff meeting" stew with four purpose-built meetings:

| Meeting | Time | Cadence | Purpose | Genre |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Daily Check-in** | 5 min, standing | Daily | Share schedules/activities, stay aligned | Headline News |
| **Weekly Tactical** | 45–90 min | Weekly | Review activity/metrics, resolve tactics | Sitcom |
| **Monthly Strategic** | ~2 hrs/topic | Monthly / Ad Hoc | Debate & decide a few big issues | Feature film |
| **Quarterly Off-Site** | 1–2 days | Quarterly | Review strategy, team, talent, competition | Mini-series |

**Weekly Tactical runs in a fixed order**: Lightning Round (each person, 2–3
priorities, ≤60 sec) → Progress Review (4–6 key metrics) → **Real-Time Agenda** (set
*after* reporting, from what's actually happening — "disciplined spontaneity").
Strategic topics that surface get **parked** for the Monthly Strategic, never discussed
in the tactical meeting (don't mix magnitudes).

### The Myth of Too Many Meetings & Sneaker Time
The fix for bad meetings is *better* meetings, not fewer. Good meetings are net
**time-savers** because they eliminate **sneaker time** — the uncounted hours spent
after bad meetings clarifying by email, voicemail, and hallway visits. Get clarity,
closure, and buy-in *in the room* or pay for it repeatedly outside it.

## Scope & Limits

This skill covers the book's content only — Lencioni's meeting theory and the fable
that illustrates it. It's a leadership/management model, not a project-management tool;
adapt the cadences to your team's reality (e.g., remote teams may run the Daily
Check-in by phone or skip it). For facilitation of specific meetings in your context,
combine with your own agenda artifacts and the [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md).
