# Glossary — Radical Candor

**Abrasive Trap** — Gender bias whereby women who challenge directly get penalized as "abrasive/shrill/bossy"; a small rating bias compounds into far fewer female leaders (Ch 6).

**Absentee Management** — Hands-off, ears-off, mouth-off: no curiosity, no goals, unaware of problems. One failure mode opposite micromanagement (Ch 7).

**All-Hands Meeting** — Company/team-wide meeting to persuade people of decisions and hear/address dissent via Q&A (e.g., Google TGIF, Apple Town Hall) (Ch 8).

**Big Debate Meeting** — Meeting reserved for debating major issues, explicitly *not* deciding; lowers tension and builds a debate culture (Ch 8).

**Big Decision Meeting** — Meeting to make (final) decisions, usually following a big debate; led by the appointed "decider" (Ch 8).

**Care Personally** — First dimension of Radical Candor: giving a damn about people as whole human beings, not just their work (Ch 1).

**Career Conversations** — Russ Laraway's three 45-min conversations (life story → dreams → 18-month plan) to understand motivation and help people grow (Ch 7).

**Challenge Directly** — Second dimension of Radical Candor: telling people clearly when work isn't good enough (and when it is); often the best way to show you care (Ch 1).

**Collaboration Tax vs. Investment** — When GSD steps drag, collaboration feels like a tax; run well, it's an investment. The boss absorbs much of the tax (Ch 4).

**Fundamental Attribution Error** — Explaining behavior by personality traits ("you're lazy") instead of situation/behavior; inaccurate and unfixable (Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Free at Work** — The state people reach when the boss relinquishes unilateral authority and builds trust, so they bring their best selves (Ch 5).

**Get Stuff Done (GSD) Wheel** — Seven-step results process: Listen → Clarify → Debate → Decide → Persuade → Execute → Learn (Ch 4).

**"Get it right" vs. "be right"** — Great bosses care about reaching the right answer, not being right; they insist people tell them when they're wrong (Ch 4).

**Gauge (guidance)** — Having your team tag where your guidance lands on the quadrant, since Radical Candor is measured at the listener's ear (Ch 6).

**Growth Management** — Reframing "talent management/potential" as helping each person move along the growth trajectory they want (Scott Forstall's "growth" not "potential") (Ch 3, Ch 7).

**Growth Trajectory** — Whether someone is in a steep (rapid-change) or gradual (stability/mastery) growth phase right now; not permanent (Ch 3).

**HHIPP** — Principles for giving impromptu guidance: Humble, Helpful, Immediate, In person (Praise in public, criticize in private), don't Personalize (Ch 6).

**Kanban Board** — To Do / In Progress / Done board (Toyota/Ohno) making workflows and bottlenecks visible; measures activities, not just results (Ch 8).

**Left-Hand Column** — Argyris/Schön exercise: write what you *said* (right) vs. what you *thought* (left) to surface arrogant snap judgments (Ch 6).

**Listen, Challenge, Commit** — Andy Grove's mantra: humility to listen, confidence to challenge, wisdom to stop arguing and get on board (Ch 6).

**Loud Listening** — Stating a strong point of view and insisting people challenge it back ("strong opinions, weakly held") — Steve Jobs's style (Ch 4).

**Manipulative Insincerity** — Worst quadrant: neither caring nor challenging; fake praise/criticism for advantage or ease (Ch 2).

**Micromanagement** — Hands-on, ears-off, mouth-on: tells "how" without understanding, gets lost in details, hoards information (Ch 7).

**Obnoxious Aggression** — Challenging without caring ("front-stabbing"); the second-best quadrant — at least people know where they stand (Ch 2).

**Partnership** — The management target: hands-on, ears-on, mouth-off — curious, listens, asks why, removes obstacles (Ch 7).

**Pathos / Ethos / Logos** — Aristotle's persuasion elements: the *listener's* emotion, credibility (expertise + humility), and logic ("show your work") (Ch 4).

**Personalizing** — Making guidance about character/essence instead of a specific behavior; corrodes accuracy and the relationship (Ch 2, Ch 6).

**Platinum Rule** — Do unto others as *they* would have done to them (figure out their comfort), vs. the Golden Rule's own-preference default (Ch 5).

**Radical Candor** — Caring personally *and* challenging directly at the same time; also the top-right quadrant (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Rock Star** — Person on a gradual growth trajectory; solid, has mastered and loves the craft, doesn't want the next job; reward, don't promote out (Ch 3).

**Ruinous Empathy** — Caring without challenging; withholding criticism to avoid discomfort; responsible for most management mistakes (Ch 2).

**Situation–Behavior–Impact (SBI)** — Feedback technique: describe the situation, the behavior, and the impact — keeps guidance humble and specific (Ch 6).

**Skip-Level Meeting** — Annual meeting with your reports' reports (without those managers) to help managers improve; "not for attribution" (Ch 6).

**Snippets** — Short written weekly updates in a shared doc, read in a staff-meeting "study hall" to avoid interminable verbal updates (Ch 8).

**Spelunking** — A boss occasionally diving deep into details / going straight to the source of facts to stay grounded (Ch 4).

**Superstar** — Person on a steep growth trajectory; needs constant new challenge; the source of a team's growth (Ch 3).

**Think Time** — Sacred blocked calendar time to think that no one may schedule over (Ch 8).

**Whoops the Monkey** — Dan Woods's stuffed-animal ritual: people self-report mistakes at all-hands for automatic forgiveness and shared learning (with a "Killer Whale" for peer praise) (Ch 6).

**Work-Life Integration** — Reframing "work-life balance" (zero-sum) so work and life give each other a "double bounce" (Ch 5).
