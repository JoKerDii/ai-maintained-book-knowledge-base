# Chapter 5: Relationships — An approach to establishing trust with your direct reports

## Core Idea
Radically Candid relationships are built from the inside out: stay centered yourself first, relinquish unilateral authority so people feel "free at work," and negotiate personal boundaries individually (the "Platinum Rule"). Shareholder value is the *result*, not the core.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Stay Centered → Work-Life Integration**: You can't give a damn about others if you don't take care of yourself. Reject "work-life balance" (a zero-sum framing) for **integration** — work and life give each other a "double bounce."
  - How: Find your **"recipe"** to stay centered and protect it *most* when times are hardest. (Kim's: 8h sleep, 45 min exercise, breakfast + dinner with family; plus a novel/week, a romantic weekend quarterly, a family vacation yearly.) Put it on your calendar; "show up for yourself" — don't let others schedule over it.
- **Free at Work → Relinquish Unilateral Authority**: The first rule of building trust that makes people feel free is to lay down unilateral control. "Authority derives naturally from merit, not the other way around" (Jack Dorsey). Google replaced managerial fiat with committee-based hiring, promotion, and 360-calibrated reviews — not to control employees but to *replace* control with process.
- **The Platinum Rule** (for physical space / boundaries): Golden Rule = do unto others as you'd have done to you. **Platinum Rule** = figure out what makes the *other* person comfortable, and do that. (Bill Campbell's warm bear-hug-then-candor with Stacy Brown-Philpot; Russ asking *not* to be hugged.)

## Key Concepts
- **Tyranny vs. anarchy**: The only thing worse than a dictator (unilateral authority) is a warlord (anarchy, "nasty, brutish, and short"). Good process avoids both.
- **Openness (not shared values)**: You don't need the same values to build good relationships — you need to *respect* others' values when shared. Insisting everyone share your worldview is the "fastest path to artificial relationships" and organizational mediocrity.
- **Boundaries are individual**: There's no single right place for them. Build trust through a consistent pattern of good faith, mainly via regular 1:1s. Don't assume too much trust too fast.

## Mental Models
- **"Coin-operated" incentives backfire**: The steel-mill foreman — "guys who can't write their names will learn calculus to figure out how to screw me." Treat people like cogs and you get the minimum, plus an incentive to break the machine.
- **You can't hide your mood; own it instead.** "Everybody notices what kind of mood the boss is in — it's adaptive" (Russ). Don't repress or take it out on the team; say, "I'm having a shitty day; it's not you or your work." Take a mental-health day for real upsets.
- **You are responsible for your reactions, not their emotions.** Trying to *manage* others' emotions is a fast path to Manipulative Insincerity. Acknowledge emotions, ask questions, react compassionately — but don't own them.

## Anti-patterns
- **Forced "values" exercises**: Writing/sharing personal values in a 45-min HR exercise can force people to lie (the closeted gay employee) or drive wedges. Live your values; don't list them.
- **Mandatory "fun"**: Even non-mandatory events feel mandatory (Marissa Mayer's whale-watching seasickness; the 9pm work-life-balance session after go-carts). "Sometimes the greatest gift you can give your team is to let them go home."
- **Booze at work**: A long catalog of disasters (jail, ruined couches, harassment, worse). A social lubricant that badly backfires.
- **Telling people how to feel**: "Don't be sad," "Don't take it personally," "don't cry" — guarantees the very reaction you fear (the white-bear effect; the boss who forbade crying, so Kim "cried all the damn time").

## Worked Example
**Mastering reactions to others' emotions (the compassion toolkit).** Kim's practical moves when emotions run high in a 1:1:
- **Acknowledge**: "I can see you're mad/frustrated/____" — don't pretend it isn't happening.
- **Ask questions** until you understand the real issue; don't over-direct.
- **Don't add your guilt**: Someone crying doesn't mean you did wrong; guilt makes you defensive, then cold. Focus on them, not yourself.
- **Keep tissues a short walk away** and **closed water bottles at your desk**: Offering a tissue at the first tear can *turn on* the waterworks; stepping out to fetch one (or the pause of unscrewing a water bottle) lets the person regain composure.
- **Walk, don't sit**: For hard conversations — emotions are less on display, and looking in the same direction feels collaborative.
- **Forgive yourself** if you can't handle an outburst: "I'm sorry you're upset. I'll step out and get you some water… let's come back to this later. I can see it's important."

## Key Takeaways
1. Care for yourself first — protect your centering "recipe" *hardest* when things are worst.
2. Choose **work-life integration** over balance; put personal needs on the calendar and honor them.
3. **Lay down unilateral authority** so people feel free at work — merit-based, not power-based, trust.
4. Respect differing values through **openness**; don't require a shared worldview.
5. Use the **Platinum Rule** for boundaries and physical space — the other person's comfort, not yours.
6. **Acknowledge emotions, master your reactions, own your moods** — but don't try to manage others' feelings.
