# Chapter 7: Team — Techniques for avoiding boredom and burnout

## Core Idea
The concrete practices for putting the right people in the right roles: three-part **Career Conversations** to understand motivation and dreams, annual **growth-management plans**, and disciplined **hiring, firing, and promotion** processes — all aimed at partnership over absentee management or micromanagement.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Career Conversations** (Russ Laraway's three 45-minute conversations, spread over 3–6 weeks):
  1. **Life Story**: "Starting with kindergarten, tell me about your life." Focus on *changes* the person made and *why* — values reveal themselves at moments of change. Write down motivators (e.g., "financial independence," "part of a team") and check them back.
  2. **Dreams**: "What do you want the pinnacle of your career to look like?" Ask for 3–5 dreams (not "career goals" — that elicits professional, guarded answers). Map dreams × skills-needed × current-competence to reveal what to develop. Includes a "CAD — crazy-ass dream" (the spirulina farm).
  3. **18-Month Plan**: "What do I need to learn to move toward my dreams? How do I prioritize it? Who can I learn from? How can I change my role to learn it?" End with action items — who does what by when.
  - When to use: First thing to roll out on a team; once a year per direct report, in 1:1 slots.
- **Growth-Management Plan** (annual): Put names in the growth-management 2×2 boxes *temporarily* (spend ≤20 min, think fast), get an outside perspective to check bias, then write a 3–5 bullet plan per person. Don't be an "easy grader" or "hard grader"; calibrate with peers; ensure fairness by level (don't let senior people hog the "superstar" box).
- **Absentee Management ↔ Partnership ↔ Micromanagement** (the management chart):
  - **Absentee**: hands-off, ears-off, mouth-off — no curiosity, no goals, unaware of problems.
  - **Partnership** (the target): hands-on, **ears-on, mouth-off** — displays curiosity, listens and asks "why," asks about *relevant* details, leads collaborative goal-setting, removes obstacles.
  - **Micromanagement**: hands-on, ears-off, mouth-on — tells "how" without understanding, gets lost in details, hoards information.

## Key Concepts
- **Dreams, not "career aspirations"**: The word choice matters — "aspirations/five-year plan" invites the answer the boss wants to hear; "dreams" invites the real one.
- **Motivators emerge from stories, not abstractions**: "Financial independence" could mean materialism *or* a childhood memory that reframes it entirely — you only learn which from the life story.
- **Bias toward "no" in hiring**: "If you're not *dying* to hire the person, don't make an offer." Let strong "no" votes overrule.
- **Firing: don't wait too long, don't decide unilaterally, give a damn, follow up.** Four reasons to identify underperformance early: fair to the person, to the company (legal risk), to yourself (trust), and to your top performers.

## Mental Models
- **Educo — "to lead out"**: A boss's job in career conversations is not to assign dreams but to help people articulate and pursue their own.
- **"There's a lid for every pot"** (and "it's not the person who sucks, it's the job that sucks for them"): Reframe firing — you're freeing someone to find work they can excel at. Recall your own worst job.
- **Blind skills assessments minimize bias**: Like orchestras' blind auditions (5× more women hired). Define team "fit" as rigorously as skills; the *hiring manager*, not a recruiter, writes the job description.
- **Casual moments reveal team fit**: Walk candidates to the car, ask the receptionist. The "No Assholes" rule (Bob Sutton) — one candidate made the scheduler cry; not hired.

## Anti-patterns
- **Boundary-violation fear in career conversations**: Most people welcome the conversation in an environment of trust; but respect discomfort and don't press (the woman with childhood trauma).
- **Announcing promotions publicly**: Breeds unhealthy competition for *status* rather than skill. Praise the *work* you want more of, not the promotion.
- **Desperation hiring**: A "hole" on the team makes people ignore warning signs; include an interviewer from another team who doesn't feel the pain.
- **Advising women to "navigate around" bias**: Your job is to help the whole team *recognize and eliminate* bias, not to coach the target to bake cookies (Kim's boss who offered her a *demotion* to be more "likeable" — she quit within weeks).

## Worked Example
**Russ, Sarah, and the spirulina farm (career conversations that retain).** Asked her long-term vision, Sarah first said "I want to be like you, Russ." Pushed for a "crazy-ass dream," she confessed she wanted to run a spirulina (superfood) farm. Russ then did the *life story* conversation, learned her real motivators (financial independence, environmentalism, hard work, *stewardship* — she rejected his word "leadership"), and mapped her dreams against needed skills. The key skill for a spirulina *rancher* turned out to be **management experience**, not the analytics/presentation skills she'd assumed — and she could get it *faster by staying at Google* under a great manager. She stayed several years, became an effective leader, and saved for her ranch. Retention was the by-product; meaningful work was the goal.

**Firing with humility (following your gut over legal advice).** At Juice, a lawyer kept advising Kim to hire a security guard to walk a fired employee out. Knowing that would shame him and make him *more* likely to blow up, she asked, "What's the risk of *not* walking him out?" — the same answer. She did the opposite, let him say goodbye on his own terms; he thanked her later, and it likely avoided a lawsuit. Then she followed up a month later — a practice that once earned thanks from a man *and his wife* because leaving the job was good for his career, marriage, and kids.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run **career conversations** (life story → dreams → 18-month plan) as the first thing you roll out; repeat yearly.
2. Use the word **"dreams"** and pull motivators from *stories*, not abstractions.
3. Write an annual **growth-management plan** per person; check your boxes against an outside perspective and peer calibration.
4. Aim for **partnership** (ears-on, mouth-off) — avoid both absentee management and micromanagement.
5. Hire with rigor and a **bias toward "no"**; define fit as carefully as skills; use blind assessments.
6. **Fire well**: don't wait too long, don't decide alone, give a damn, follow up — reframe it as freeing the person.
