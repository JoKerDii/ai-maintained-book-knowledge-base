# Chapter 8: Results — Things you can do to get stuff done together, faster

## Core Idea
The meeting-and-rhythm mechanics that operationalize the GSD wheel: 1:1s (the must-do), staff meetings, separate debate/decision meetings, all-hands, plus think time, Kanban boards, and walking around — all while staying conscious that, as boss, everything you do shapes culture.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The meeting system** (decide who communicates with whom, how often; minimize duration/frequency/attendees):
  1. **1:1 Conversations** — the must-do.
  2. **Staff Meetings** — learn, listen, clarify.
  3. **Think Time** — sacred, blocked.
  4. **"Big Debate" Meetings** — debate, don't decide.
  5. **"Big Decision" Meetings** — decide.
  6. **All-Hands** — persuade + hear dissent.
  7. **Meeting-Free Zones / fight meeting proliferation.**
  8. **Kanban Boards** — make workflows visible.
  9. **Walk Around** — learn small problems early.
  10. **Be Conscious of Culture.**
- **1:1 mechanics**: *Direct report sets the agenda*; you listen and clarify — **not** a place to dump saved-up criticism. Frequency scales inversely with headcount (≈50 min/week for 5 reports; 25 min/week or biweekly for 10–20). **Show up — never cancel.** Warning signs you're failing: cancellations, mere updates, good news only, no criticism, no agenda.
- **Staff-meeting agenda** (three goals, no decisions/debates here):
  - **Learn**: review key metrics / dashboard (~20 min).
  - **Listen**: "study hall" — everyone writes & reads updates ("snippets") in a shared doc (~15 min), no side conversations.
  - **Clarify**: identify (don't make) the week's key decisions & debates (~30 min); assign owners to separate "big debate"/"big decision" meetings.
- **Separate Debate and Decision meetings**: Splitting them lowers tension (half the room wants to decide, half to debate), lets you slow key decisions, and builds a culture of debate. Both: check egos at the door, no winners/losers, notes shared, anyone may attend but only named people must.

## Key Concepts
- **Think Time is sacred**: Block it (e.g., 2 hours/day) and let no one — not even a president — schedule over it. Get "seriously angry" if they try.
- **Kanban boards** (Taiichi Ohno/Toyota): To Do / In Progress / Done with color-coded Post-its. Makes bottlenecks visible so resources flow *without* management intervention; measures *activities*, not just results (the AdSense cold-calls vs. inbound-orders story; Jack Rivkin's equity-research team went #15→#1 and hired more women once activities were measured — fairness follows visibility).
- **Management by Walking Around** (Lincoln → HP): Schedule ~1 hour/week. Find small problems and treat them as "the universe through a grain of sand" — a boss is often last to know when something's wrong. Fixing small things kills the "it's beneath me" mentality (Costolo moving the dirty dishes himself).
- **"Facts, not recommendations"** and **snippets**: Keep updates lightweight and shared; do them *in* the meeting if people resist doing them alone.

## Mental Models
- **"Culture eats strategy for lunch."** A leader's personality has an outsized effect on team culture — sometimes a "fun-house mirror" of your flaws. You can't change who you are, but you *can* focus on behavior and consistently run Radical Candor + the GSD wheel.
- **"Everyone is watching you, but that doesn't mean it's all about you."** Offhand remarks become orders (Bob Rubin's "I like gold"; Steve's car color → the bus interiors; Kim's "I like a man in a white oxford"). You may move far out on "challenge directly" without meaning to — so **clarify relentlessly**, even when you think you're saying nothing.
- **Meeting proliferation is fire; fight it with fire**: Block execution time on your calendar and encourage the team to do the same (no-chairs and no-meeting-days rarely stick).
- **Culture is self-replicating when strong** — you'll know you succeeded when it's "no longer about you" (the AdSense China team's video that captured the irreverent culture with zero direction from Kim).

## Anti-patterns
- **Canceling 1:1s**: The single worst meeting habit — signals the partnership doesn't matter.
- **Using 1:1s to dump criticism**: That belongs in 2–3 minute impromptu conversations; 1:1s are for *listening*.
- **Interminable update meetings**: Reading updates aloud while everyone else watches; use silent study-hall snippets instead.
- **Delegating culture-shaping decisions to HR/legal**: Punting on "holiday vs. Christmas party," alcohol, dress, conflicts pushes culture in a "the law is an ass" or *Lord of the Flies* direction. Decide explicitly with your humanizing influence.

## Worked Example
**1:1 follow-up questions (the listening toolkit).** Kim's questions to surface the gap between what people *are* doing, *think they ought* to do, and *want* to do:
- "Why?" · "How can I help?" · "What can I do or stop doing that would make this easier?"
- "What wakes you up at night?"
- "What are you working on that you don't want to work on? — because it's not interesting, or not important? What can you do to stop?"
- "What are you *not* working on that you *do* want to work on? Why not? What can you do to start?"
- On dependencies: "How do you feel about the priorities of teams you depend on? Have you talked to them directly? If not, why not?" (Goal: get people to raise issues with each other, not have you solve it.)

**"On the AdSense team, we move the couches!"** A couch was moved into a spot that forced people to take extra steps. Kim, wanting a culture obsessed with efficiency, shoved it back herself. A teammate joked, "Looks like Kim's got a new job." She replied, "If something's in your way, it's *always* your job to fix it!" Two years after she left, that slogan was painted on the wall — a small action that self-replicated into culture.

## Key Takeaways
1. **1:1s are the must-do** — direct report owns the agenda, you listen, never cancel.
2. Run a tight **staff meeting** (learn/listen/clarify) and split **debate** from **decision** meetings.
3. Protect **think time** and **execution time**; actively fight meeting proliferation.
4. Make work **visible** with Kanban and measure **activities**, not just results — visibility breeds fairness.
5. **Walk around** to catch small problems early and kill the "beneath me" mentality.
6. You're under the microscope: **clarify constantly**, shape culture consciously, and know you've won when it's no longer about you.
