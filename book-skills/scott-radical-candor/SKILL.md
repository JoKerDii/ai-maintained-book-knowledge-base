## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### 1. The two dimensions (the foundation)
Radical Candor rests on two **independent** axes that combine, not trade off:
- **Care Personally** — give a damn about people as whole humans, not just their work. "Bring your whole self to work." Not birthdays/chitchat — real conversations about what people want from life.
- **Challenge Directly** — tell people clearly when work isn't good enough (and when it is); make hard calls; hold a high bar. **Challenging directly is a *form* of caring, not its opposite.**

The hardest, most important move: **solicit challenge from your team** — invite them to challenge you so hard that *you* might be the one upset.

### 2. The Radical Candor quadrant (the signature framework)
Care Personally (vertical) × Challenge Directly (horizontal):
- **Radical Candor** (care + challenge) — the goal. "It's not mean, it's clear!"
- **Ruinous Empathy** (care, no challenge) — withholding criticism to avoid discomfort. **The most common management mistake.**
- **Obnoxious Aggression** (challenge, no care) — "front-stabbing." Second-best: at least people know where they stand.
- **Manipulative Insincerity** (neither) — fake praise/criticism. **The worst quadrant.**

Rules: the quadrants describe **guidance in the moment, never a person**. Radical Candor is **measured at the listener's ear, not the speaker's mouth**, and is culturally relative. Prefer Obnoxious Aggression to silence; aim top-right.

### 3. Giving guidance — HHIPP
**H**umble · **H**elpful · **I**mmediate · **I**n person (**P**raise in public / criticize in private) · don't **P**ersonalize.
- Solicit criticism **before** giving it — prove you can take it. Go-to question: *"Is there anything I could do or stop doing that would make it easier to work with me?"* Then embrace the discomfort (count to six of silence).
- Give it in **2–3 minutes**, immediately; don't save it for reviews.
- Use **Situation–Behavior–Impact**. Say **"that's wrong," not "you're wrong."** Avoid the **fundamental attribution error** (attacking character, not behavior).
- **Reward candor** — never critique the criticism; act visibly on it.

### 4. Understand motivation — Rock Stars & Superstars
Every strong team needs both:
- **Rock star** (gradual growth) — solid, loves and has mastered the craft, doesn't want the next job. **Reward, don't promote out.**
- **Superstar** (steep growth) — needs constant challenge; the source of growth. **Keep challenged, don't squash.**

Manage to the trajectory a person **wants right now** (it changes with life). Use **"growth," not "potential."** No permanent labels. There's no such thing as a B-player, but tolerating mediocrity is Ruinous Empathy toward the whole team.

### 5. Drive results — the GSD wheel
**Listen → Clarify → Debate → Decide → Persuade → Execute → Learn.** Cycle quickly; skipping *or* stalling on a step wastes time. **Telling people what to do doesn't work** — even for geniuses. You're usually *not* the decider: push decisions to whoever's closest to the facts; collect **facts, not recommendations**. **Persuade** with Aristotle's pathos (the *listener's* emotions), ethos (expertise + humility), logos (show your work).

### 6. Build it in practice
- **Stay centered first** (work-life *integration*); **relinquish unilateral authority** so people feel *free at work*; use the **Platinum Rule** for boundaries (Ch 5).
- **Career conversations** (life story → dreams → 18-month plan) are the first thing to roll out (Ch 7).
- **1:1s are the must-do** — report owns the agenda, you listen, never cancel (Ch 8).
- **Core self-check when lost**: "Am I showing I care personally?" and "Am I challenging each person directly?"
