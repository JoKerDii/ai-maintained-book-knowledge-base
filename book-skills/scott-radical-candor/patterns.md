# Patterns & Techniques — Radical Candor

Concrete, reusable techniques from the book, with when/how/trade-offs.

## Solicit criticism with a go-to question
**When**: Building a guidance culture; you get too little criticism.
**How**: Ask "Is there anything I could do or stop doing that would make it easier to work with me?" Then embrace the discomfort — count to six of silence, don't fill it, don't reassure. Ask again if needed. Reward whatever you get; never critique the criticism.
**Trade-offs**: Feels awkward at first; requires you to genuinely act on what you hear or trust evaporates.

## Situation–Behavior–Impact (SBI)
**When**: Giving any praise or criticism.
**How**: State the situation you saw, the behavior, and the impact. ("In this morning's meeting *(S)*, the way you talked through the objection *(B)* was persuasive because it showed you'd heard the other side *(I)*.")
**Trade-offs**: Requires you to have observed specifics — a reason to give guidance immediately, before details fade.

## Left-hand column
**When**: A frustrating conversation where judgment leaked out.
**How**: Draw a line down a page; right = what you said, left = what you thought. Question the left column's assumptions.
**Trade-offs**: Reflective, not in-the-moment; best as prep or debrief.

## Gauge your guidance
**When**: You want to know how your guidance actually lands.
**How**: Explain the quadrant to the team; have them tag your praise/criticism by quadrant (stickers, or the Candor Gauge app). Track over time.
**Trade-offs**: Stickers cause pile-on/anonymity issues; anonymous tags signal low trust you must rebuild.

## Reward candor visibly
**When**: Someone gives you hard, valid criticism.
**How**: Make a visible change (Kim's "radical bander" rubber band others could snap when she interrupted). If you can't change fast, show effort; if you disagree, agree with something, check understanding, then explain why.
**Trade-offs**: Gimmicks lose novelty; the point is the signal, not the prop.

## Career conversations (3 conversations)
**When**: First thing to roll out on a team; yearly per report, in 1:1 slots.
**How**: (1) Life story — "from kindergarten, tell me about your life," note motivators at moments of change; (2) Dreams — ask for 3–5 dreams incl. a "crazy-ass dream," map dreams × skills × competence; (3) 18-month plan — what to learn, from whom, how the role can change, with action items.
**Trade-offs**: ~2.25 hrs/person over 3–6 weeks; respect discomfort and don't press personal topics.

## Growth-management plan
**When**: Annually, to balance the team.
**How**: Put names in the growth 2×2 boxes fast (≤20 min), get an outside/peer check for bias, write 3–5 bullets per person. Calibrate with peers; ensure fairness by level.
**Trade-offs**: Boxes are temporary snapshots — never permanent labels.

## Hiring process
**When**: Any hire.
**How**: Hiring *manager* (not recruiter) writes the job description defining fit as rigorously as skills; use blind skills assessments; a consistent 4-person diverse committee incl. someone off-team; casual moments reveal fit; write feedback within 15 min; bias toward "no" — if not *dying* to hire, don't.
**Trade-offs**: Slower; the cost of a bad hire (and firing) is far higher.

## Firing well
**When**: Repeated Radically Candid guidance hasn't produced improvement.
**How**: Don't wait too long; don't decide unilaterally (boss/peers/HR, get documents *edited*); give a damn (follow gut over rigid legal advice when it reduces harm); follow up ~a month later.
**Trade-offs**: Emotionally and legally hard; reframe as freeing the person ("the job sucks for them").

## GSD wheel
**When**: Any significant team decision/initiative.
**How**: Listen → Clarify → Debate → Decide → Persuade → Execute → Learn. Ensure each step happens; don't skip or stall. Push decisions to the person closest to the facts; collect facts, not recommendations.
**Trade-offs**: Feels slow, but skipping steps costs more later; you needn't be in every step.

## Two listening styles
**When**: You want everyone heard.
**How**: **Quiet** (silence, neutral face — reassure sometimes) or **Loud** (state strong opinions weakly held, insist people push back). Pick the one natural to you.
**Trade-offs**: Quiet → people guess your view; loud → intimidates the marginal, so build their confidence.

## Persuade with pathos/ethos/logos
**When**: Bringing along people who weren't in the decision.
**How**: Address the *listener's* emotions, establish credibility (expertise + humility, "we" not "I"), and show your logic ("show your work").
**Trade-offs**: The neglected one is usually the listener's emotion.

## 1:1 meetings
**When**: Weekly (5 reports) → biweekly (10–20); never cancel.
**How**: Direct report sets the agenda; you listen and clarify (not dump criticism). Use follow-up questions ("Why?", "How can I help?", "What wakes you up at night?", "What are you working on that you don't want to?").
**Trade-offs**: 1:1 time is the natural bottleneck on how many reports you can have.

## Staff meeting (learn/listen/clarify)
**When**: Weekly.
**How**: Review key metrics (~20 min) → silent study-hall snippets in a shared doc (~15 min, no side chatter) → identify (don't make) key decisions/debates (~30 min), assigning owners to separate big-debate/big-decision meetings.
**Trade-offs**: Not the place for debates/decisions — split those out.

## Split debate and decision meetings
**When**: Team > ~20; contentious topics.
**How**: Big Debate = wrestle, no decision, set decide-by date, switch roles halfway. Big Decision = decider (appointed) makes final call. Both: egos at the door, notes shared, anyone may attend.
**Trade-offs**: Looks like meeting proliferation but actually reduces it via self-selection.

## Kanban board / measure activities
**When**: Making work visible; ensuring fair recognition.
**How**: To Do / In Progress / Done with color-coded Post-its; measure activities not just results.
**Trade-offs**: Visibility itself reduces bias and routes help to bottlenecks.

## Walk around
**When**: ~1 hr/week, esp. as a manager of managers.
**How**: Talk to people you haven't seen lately; find and sometimes fix small problems yourself.
**Trade-offs**: Office hours attract the same cranks; walking reaches the quiet ones.

## Prevent backstabbing / clean escalation
**When**: One person wants to complain to you about another.
**How**: Refuse; insist they talk directly. If unresolved, host a live three-way conversation (not email) and help them find a workable solution.
**Trade-offs**: Adds occasional mediation meetings but prevents political blowups.

## Skip-level meetings
**When**: Once a year per direct report (manager of managers).
**How**: Meet reports' reports without the manager; explain-show-explain; get consent; do it for everyone; not-for-attribution; take & project notes yourself; share with the manager within ~8 min; ensure 1–2 concrete changes.
**Trade-offs**: Energy-intensive; guard against proliferation and gripe sessions.

## Peer guidance: "Whoops the Monkey"
**When**: Building peer-to-peer candor.
**How**: At all-hands, people self-report mistakes (auto-forgiveness, shared learning) via a stuffed monkey; peers award a "Killer Whale" for great work.
**Trade-offs**: Culturally specific — inappropriate where mistakes are safety-critical or the design ethos is formal.
