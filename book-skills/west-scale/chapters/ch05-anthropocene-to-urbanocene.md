# Chapter 5: From the Anthropocene to the Urbanocene — A Planet Dominated by Cities

## Core Idea
Biology's sublinear scaling yields bounded growth, but the socioeconomic world runs on open-ended, faster-than-exponential expansion driven by cities and fossil fuels — a shift that converted Earth from an open, solar-powered system into a closed one where the Second Law of Thermodynamics bites.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **Anthropocene vs. Urbanocene**: West would reserve *Anthropocene* for the ~10,000 years since agriculture, when human effective metabolic rate began rising. He coins **Urbanocene** for the intense post-Industrial-Revolution epoch dominated by cities — the era in which the fate of cities is the fate of the planet.

- **Exponential growth and doubling time**.
  - When to use: any constant-percentage growth process — population, compound interest, resource consumption.
  - Key property: under pure exponential growth, doubling time is *constant*. Under **faster-than-exponential (superexponential)** growth, the doubling time itself *shrinks*. Watch the doubling time, not the level.
  - Diagnostic property: on a chessboard doubling grains per square, **any square exceeds the sum of all previous squares combined**. Exponential growth is invisible until it is overwhelming.

- **Open vs. closed system** — the chapter's core thermodynamic reframing. Life on Earth ran for billions of years as an *open* system powered by an external sun, so entropy could be exported. Burning fossil fuels turned the human enterprise into a *closed* system supplied from internal stock, where entropy strictly accumulates.

- **Effective / social metabolic rate**: the energy throughput per person including all technology and infrastructure. Biological baseline ~2,000 kcal/day ≈ **100 watts**. Global average human today: **>3,000 watts** (~30× biological). United States: ~**11,000 watts** (~100× biological). Multiply through and the effective global population is >200 billion, not 7.3 billion.

- **Malthus vs. the innovation optimists**: Malthus (*An Essay on the Principle of Population*, 1798) argued population grows geometrically while food grows arithmetically. Ehrlich (*The Population Bomb*, 1968) and the Club of Rome (*The Limits to Growth*, 1972) restated it; Julian Simon (*The Ultimate Resource*, 1981) and Paul Romer's endogenous growth theory answered that more people means more ingenuity — "Possibilities do not add up. They multiply."
  - West's verdict: the Malthusians ignored innovation and were wrong on timing; the optimists ignore energy and entropy, and ideas are not free. *Ex nihilo nihil fit.*

## Key Concepts
- **Doubling time** — years to double at the current rate; shrinking doubling time is the signature of superexponential growth.
- **Cornucopian view** — the position that innovation always outruns scarcity.
- **Carrying capacity** — the level a system's resource base can sustain; absent from most economics textbooks, along with energy, entropy, and metabolism.
- **Compound interest** — the everyday face of exponential growth.

## Reference Table — The Shrinking Doubling Time of Human Population

| Milestone | Approx. date | Interval since previous |
|---|---|---|
| ~10 million (pre-agriculture) | — | — |
| 500 million | ~1500 | — |
| 1 billion | ~1800 | ~300 years |
| 2 billion | ~1920s | ~120 years |
| 4 billion | 1974 | ~45 years |
| >7.3 billion | present | adding ~80 million/year |

The pattern — 300 years, then 120, then 45 — *is* the point: this is faster than exponential.

## Mental Models
- **Think of "exponential" as a technical term, not an intensifier.** It means constant percentage growth, which looks flat for most of its history and then runs away.
- **Use the doubling time as your diagnostic.** Constant doubling time = exponential. Shrinking = superexponential = a finite-time singularity is coming (Ch 10).
- **Think of energy as the missing variable in every economic model you read.**
- **Ask "open or closed?"** A system that exports its entropy can run indefinitely; one that accumulates it cannot.

## Anti-patterns
- **Using "exponential" to mean "fast."** It obscures the actual danger, which is the deceptive early flatness.
- **Assuming the container is half full halfway through the process.** It is half full one step before the end.
- **Assuming ideas are free.** Innovation itself consumes energy and produces entropy.
- **Reading economics that omits energy, entropy, metabolism, and carrying capacity.** As Kenneth Boulding told Congress: anyone who believes exponential growth can go on forever in a finite world is either a madman or an economist.
- **Championing innovation while denying its entropic consequences** (Simon's climate skepticism).
- **Nostalgia for pre-industrial village life.** Child labour, filth, and twelve-hour days predate industry; what changed was the *scale*.
- **Asymmetric risk perception**: tolerating continuous diffuse deaths (>1.25 million road deaths a year worldwide) while fearing discrete dramatic ones (fewer than 100 direct deaths across the entire history of nuclear power plants).

## Worked Example
**The bacterial test tube — why you notice too late.**

Put one bacterium in a nutrient container at 8:00 a.m. It splits every minute. The nutrients are calibrated so that the container fills exactly at noon. When is it half full?

Not 10:30. Not 11:15. It is half full at **11:59** — one minute before the end. Run it backwards: 11:58 is a quarter full; 11:57 an eighth. At **11:55**, with five minutes still to go, it is 1/32 ≈ 3% full and barely visible. At **11:50** — a sixth of the entire run still ahead — it is 0.1% full and looks empty.

So for essentially the whole life of this universe, nothing appears to be happening, even though growth was exponential the entire time. Seen from inside: at 100 generations (≈2,000 human years at 20 years per generation) food is abundant and the boundaries are unimaginable. At around 235 generations a few bacteria first notice scarcity. At 239 things look dire. One generation later it is over.

West flags the caveats — real bacteria double in ~30 minutes, and the fable ignores toxic waste and mortality — but insists the moral holds, because by depending on fossil fuels rather than the sun we have engineered ourselves into exactly this kind of **closed** container.

*Counterpoint worth holding alongside it:* the sun delivers roughly 10^18 kWh/year to Earth against our ~1.5 × 10^14 kWh/year consumption. **More solar energy arrives in one hour than the world uses in a year.** Frank Shuman built a working 50 kW solar thermal plant on the Nile in 1913, pumping over 5,000 gallons a minute; cheap oil killed it in the 1930s.

## Key Takeaways
1. Watch doubling times, not absolute levels. A shrinking doubling time means the system is heading for a singularity, not a plateau.
2. Human population growth has been *faster* than exponential — the intervals between billions are collapsing.
3. Effective population is what matters: at ~30× biological metabolic rate globally, the planet already supports the energetic equivalent of 200+ billion people.
4. The Industrial Revolution's deep significance is thermodynamic — it closed the system.
5. Both Malthusians and cornucopians are half right. Innovation is real; so is entropy. Neither camp has a mechanistic theory.
6. Roughly a third of global energy is wasted; only ~20% of gasoline energy moves the car. Efficiency is a large, unexploited reservoir.
