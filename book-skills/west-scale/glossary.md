# Glossary — *Scale* (Geoffrey West)

**Allometric scaling** — scaling in which shape changes with size, so exponents are not simple multiples of ⅓; term coined by Julian Huxley. Contrast isometric. (Ch 2, 3)

**Anthropocene** — the ~10,000 years since agriculture, when human effective metabolic rate began rising. (Ch 5)

**Area-preserving branching** — network geometry in which the summed cross-section of daughter branches equals the parent's, eliminating wave reflections; noted by Leonardo da Vinci in trees. (Ch 3)

**Central place theory** — Christaller's static hexagonal lattice of nested settlements; space filling and self-similar, but untested against data. (Ch 7)

**Coarse-grained theory** — a zeroth-order model predicting system averages (why human life span is ~100 years) rather than individual outcomes. (Ch 1, 3)

**Coastline paradox** — measured length depends on ruler size; a length quoted without its resolution is meaningless. (Ch 3)

**Compustat** — S&P database of US publicly traded companies since 1950; 28,853 firms over 1950–2009 underpin the mortality analysis. (Ch 9)

**Censored companies** — firms alive at the end of the data window; must be corrected for (Kaplan–Meier) or life expectancy is biased downward. (Ch 9)

**Dimensionality (of a company)** — the breadth of its product and idea space; contracts with age, unlike a city's, which expands. (Ch 9)

**Dimensionless number** — a unit-free ratio identical for model and original when they are dynamically similar; e.g. the Froude number. (Ch 2)

**Doubling time** — time to double at the current rate. Constant under exponential growth; **shrinking** under superexponential growth. (Ch 5, 10)

**Dunbar's number** — ~150 stable social relationships, part of a nested hierarchy 5 / 15 / 50 / 150 / 500 / 1,500 with branching ratio ~3. (Ch 7)

**Economy of scale** — sublinear scaling: the bigger you are, the less you need per capita. (Ch 1, 3)

**Effective / social metabolic rate** — energy throughput per person including technology. Biological ~100 W; global average >3,000 W; US ~11,000 W. (Ch 1, 5)

**Emergent behavior** — collective properties not present in, nor predictable from, the individual components. (Ch 1)

**Entropy** — the inevitable disorder produced whenever energy is transformed (Clausius, 1855). "Entropy kills." (Ch 1, 4, 5)

**Essential singularity** — the accumulated limit of infinitely many increasingly frequent finite-time singularities. (Ch 10)

**Finite time singularity** — growth reaching infinity at a *finite* time; the signature of superexponential growth and the core of West's sustainability argument. (Ch 1, 10)

**Fourth dimension of life** — fractal, space-filling networks make organisms behave as if four-dimensional, turning Euclidean ⅓ exponents into ¼. (Ch 4)

**Fractal dimension** — a measure of crinkliness; equals the log-log slope plus 1. Cities cluster near ~1.2, up to ~1.8. (Ch 3, 7)

**Froude number** — v²/(Lg); matching it between model and full scale makes motion dynamically similar, so speed scales as √length. (Ch 2)

**Increasing returns to scale** — the economists' term for superlinear scaling. (Ch 1, 7)

**Invariance of terminal units** — network endpoints (capillaries, alveoli, leaves, electrical outlets, people and houses) stay the same size across all system sizes. Second network postulate. (Ch 3, 7)

**Isometric scaling** — shape preserved; areas ∝ length², volumes ∝ length³. Galileo's case. (Ch 2)

**Kaplan–Meier estimator** — survival analysis method (1958) correcting for censored observations. (Ch 9)

**Kleiber's Law** — metabolic rate ∝ mass^(3/4); holds across ~27 orders of magnitude of mass. (Ch 1, 3)

**Krogh radius** — maximum oxygen diffusion distance from capillary to cell; sets an upper bound on body size. (Ch 4)

**Liability of adolescence** — the acute vulnerability of a young firm once seed capital is spent without profit. (Ch 9)

**Log-log plot** — logarithmic axes on which a power law appears as a straight line whose **slope is the exponent**. (Ch 1, 2)

**Marchetti's constant** — ~1 hour per day of total travel, ~half an hour each way commuting; invariant across city size, mode, country, and era. (Ch 8)

**Metabolic theory of ecology (MTE)** — mass scaling combined with Arrhenius temperature dependence (ATP activation energy ~0.65 eV). (Ch 4)

**Mortality rate (constant)** — the age-independent death probability that produces exponential survivorship and a well-defined half-life. (Ch 4, 9)

**Ontogenetic growth model** — derivation of bounded, sigmoidal growth from sublinear supply (M^¾) against linear maintenance (M¹). (Ch 4)

**Optimization (minimization of energy dissipation)** — third network postulate: evolution selects the space-filling network that minimizes cardiac output. (Ch 3)

**Pace of life** — decreases with size in organisms (quarter-power laws); **increases** with size in cities (15% rule). (Ch 4, 8)

**Power law** — y ∝ x^α; the mathematical form of every scaling relation in the book. (Ch 1, 2)

**Preferential attachment (Yule–Simon)** — new items added in proportion to existing abundance; "the rich get richer." Generates Zipf-like distributions. (Ch 8)

**Quarter-power scaling** — the family of biological exponents built on ¼: life span +¼, heart rate −¼, metabolic rate ¾. (Ch 1, 3, 4)

**Scale-Adjusted Metropolitan Indicator (SAMI)** — a unit's residual deviation from its size-predicted value on the scaling curve; the correct replacement for per-capita ranking. (Ch 8)

**Self-similarity / scale invariance** — the same structure or ratio repeating at every magnification. (Ch 3)

**Sigmoidal (bounded) growth** — the S-curve produced when sublinear supply meets linear maintenance. (Ch 4, 9)

**Space filling** — first network postulate: the network must reach every locally active subunit. (Ch 3, 7)

**Square-cube law** — Galileo's argument that area ∝ length² while volume ∝ length³, so strength ∝ weight^(2/3). (Ch 2)

**Sublinear scaling** — exponent < 1; economies of scale. Organisms 0.75, city infrastructure ~0.85, companies ~0.9. (Ch 1, 7, 9)

**Superexponential growth** — faster than exponential; doubling time shrinks; leads to a finite-time singularity. (Ch 5, 8, 10)

**Superlinear scaling** — exponent > 1; increasing returns. Socioeconomic city output ~1.15. No biological analog. (Ch 1, 7)

**Terminal units** — see *invariance of terminal units*. (Ch 3, 4, 7)

**The 15 percent rule** — per doubling of city population, ~15% more socioeconomic output per capita and ~15% less infrastructure per capita. (Ch 1, 7, 8)

**Urbanocene** — West's coinage for the post-Industrial-Revolution epoch dominated by cities. (Ch 5, 6)

**Zipf's law** — a city's rank is inversely proportional to its size; also word frequencies. West treats it as phenomenological, without fundamental significance or an agreed mechanism. (Ch 7)
