# Patterns & Techniques — Executive Presence

Concrete, repeatable moves drawn from the book. Grouped by situation.

## Deepening Gravitas
**When to use**: You're not yet perceived as leadership material despite the credentials.
**How**: Surround yourself with people better than you; be generous with credit; stick to what you know (back claims with firsthand examples); show humility (admit failings); smile more; empower others' presence to build your own; drive change rather than be changed.
**Trade-offs**: Humility disarms detractors but requires security; over-deference reads as weakness.

## Grace Under Fire (Crisis Composure)
**When to use**: A crisis, a mistake (yours or others'), a hostile grilling.
**How**: Reach for your "iron core"; own mistakes publicly and fast; lay out concrete steps; never break eye contact; speak with empathy. Handling catastrophe > avoiding it.
**Trade-offs**: Deflecting blame to look clean (Hayward) is catastrophic; owning it (Dimon) bolsters gravitas.

## Show Teeth Discriminately
**When to use**: A decision everyone is avoiding.
**How**: Listen, gather info, weigh options, wait for the opening (when others writhe in indecision), *then* demand action. Hide your teeth more often than you bare them; pair every hard call with EQ.
**Trade-offs**: For women, decisiveness risks the "bitch"/"not a team player" penalty — frame around the team.

## Establish Connection in 5 Seconds
**When to use**: Opening any talk or presentation.
**How**: Unveil just enough of your inner core (a personal story) that the audience starts pulling for you — without oversharing. Give the impression you don't *need* to be liked.
**Trade-offs**: Over-revelation reads as neediness; too withheld reads as cold (especially penalizing for women).

## Deliver Words as a Musician Delivers Notes
**When to use**: High-stakes speaking; when you want a point to land.
**How**: Vary phrasing, inflection, pace. "Phrase off" the ends of sentences for closure (kills uptalk). Slow down. Surround key words with silence — "a musician's impact lies in the rests."
**Trade-offs**: None if practiced; silence feels risky but reads as confidence.

## Lose the Props → Make Eye Contact
**When to use**: Any presentation or important meeting.
**How**: Know your material cold so you can ditch notes, slides, and glasses. Prepare until the arc is "muscle memory." Move the podium; use a lavaliere mic to roam.
**Trade-offs**: Requires heavy prep; but reliance on props signals a lack of command.

## Be a Sniper, Not a Machine Gun
**When to use**: A meeting where you want your voice to carry weight.
**How**: Don't speak unless you have a strong point; wait to make a counterpoint or ask an insightful question. When *leading*, invert: open immediately with a bold, agenda-setting statement.
**Trade-offs**: Silence early can be mistaken for having nothing to say — so land a strong first contribution.

## Assert with the Company's Interest (Take Out the "I")
**When to use**: Pushing back, negotiating, or disagreeing upward.
**How**: Frame the demand around what's best for the company/team, not what you want. Mind tone and body language. Ask, "How will this be heard by those in power?" Follow a confrontation privately to reframe intent.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than blunt assertion, but avoids the "not a team player" brand.

## Achieve Polish (Golden Rule: Minimize Distractions)
**When to use**: Always; especially first impressions and client meetings.
**How**: Regular nails/hair; well-cut attire for your body type; accessorize without billboarding; never let clothing advertise your body; look "appropriate for your environment, authentic to you."
**Trade-offs**: Provocative dressing distracts and costs credibility; disheveled reads as poor judgment.

## Dress for the Job You Want
**When to use**: Angling for a promotion or stretch role.
**How**: Study the leaders above you; upgrade to their register; build a signature look with one distinctive style piece. Get "over the bar" first, then earn latitude to set your own code.
**Trade-offs**: Inauthentic clothing saps confidence — the look must still be *you*.

## Great Feedback (Give)
**When to use**: Developing talent; a core leadership competency.
**How**: Timely (right before/after the blunder) + specific (one behavior) + prescriptive (what to do) + framed in business outcome. Frequent discrete pointers, not semi-annual downloads. Never while angry (wait 24h). Good things first. Embed correctives. Catch people getting it right. Preface: "I'm telling you this because I want you to be successful." For appearance, route through personal brand. Enlist a third party if litigation is a risk.
**Trade-offs**: Uncomfortable, but withholding stalls the marzipan layer and questions your own leadership.

## Get the Feedback You Need (Receive)
**When to use**: You suspect an EP gap but keep hearing "you're doing great."
**How**: Recognize you need it; develop a thick skin; request *specific, timely, prescriptive* feedback on a recent high-EP encounter; if superiors won't, hire a coach (even on your own dime); build a peer feedback circle; cultivate a sponsor; listen for the "ring of truth"; demonstrate you'll act; don't cry or burn bridges.
**Trade-offs**: Vulnerable and painful — but far less painful than learning your blunders too late.

## Widen Your Latitude (Tightrope Strategies)
**When to use**: You face the narrow band of acceptability (women/minorities).
**How**: Show teeth for the team; speak truth with humor; snipe don't spray; build a latitude-granting personal brand (Branson); wield credentials consciously ("Dr."); show you care (public good works).
**Trade-offs**: Doesn't erase the bias — navigates it while preserving authenticity.

## Own Your Authenticity (Resolve the Tension)
**When to use**: At the "fork in the road" where conformity costs your identity.
**How**: Know your non-negotiables and walk away from cultures that don't deserve compliance; never fake a style that isn't you; play the long game (traditional role, untraditional execution); read slights as ignorance not bigotry; leverage and differentiate by your difference; secure air cover (a sponsor) before stepping out.
**Trade-offs**: Asserting difference invites bias — but bleaching out costs you personally *and* professionally, and difference is now a diversity dividend.
