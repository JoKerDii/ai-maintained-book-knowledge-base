# Appendix A — Executive Presence Self-Diagnostic

Hewlett's 10-item self-assessment. For each scenario, pick the response closest to what you'd do, then score. Scenarios are reconstructed compactly and each is tagged with the EP pillar/behavior it tests — use them as reflection prompts, not a scientific instrument.

## The Ten Scenarios

**1. Gravitas — grace under fire.** You must present to 20 important people immediately after an infuriating phone call from home.
- a. Excuse yourself, delaying the meeting 10+ minutes to compose yourself. *(2)*
- b. Take a breath, walk in, and present with composure — "graceful gravitas." *(3)*
- c. Cancel because you're not in the mood. *(1)*

**2. Communication / EQ — reading the room.** In a workshop, one or two under-prepared participants keep interrupting.
- a. Ignore their questions, hoping they catch up. *(2)*
- b. Get annoyed; project a "what do you want now" attitude. *(1)*
- c. Offer a short break to find where the understanding broke down. *(3)*

**3. Gravitas — speaking truth to power.** In a strategy meeting, the curt, old-fashioned CEO singles you out for your view on why business is slipping.
- a. Gulp water and wipe your brow before speaking. *(2)*
- b. Stand and speak with integrity, clarity, and confidence — truth to power. *(3)*
- c. Defer, saying you agree with the CEO. *(1)*

**4. Gravitas — emotional intelligence / integrity.** Partners nudge you to promote the Caucasian male over the higher-performing woman of color (with a speech impairment), assuming his "communication must be better."
- a. Advocate for the woman of color — she measures twice the new business. *(3)*
- b. Promote the man; tell her she "just didn't cut it." *(2)*
- c. Ask the partners to decide for you. *(1)*

**5. Communication — body language.** Asked unexpectedly to speak after boring speakers; you're tired.
- a. Present yawning, shoulders slumped, fidgeting. *(1)*
- b. Ask for a chair and sit comfortably to present. *(2)*
- c. Approach with a welcoming smile, standing tall, presenting with energy and engagement. *(3)*

**6. Communication — judgment / avoiding blunders.** You must tell your team it's being split; you say, "The reason is because most of you are women."
- a. You handled it professionally — truthful and direct. *(3 — see note)*
- b. It's management's call, so no feedback is warranted. *(2)*
- c. You committed one of the worst EP-detracting violations — poor judgment / gender bias. *(1 — see note)*

**7. Communication — banter / small talk.** At a dinner honoring your leader, seated with executives you don't know.
- a. Initiate a casual, off-the-record conversation to break the ice. *(3)*
- b. Cough loudly to draw attention to yourself. *(1)*
- c. Sit quietly and wait to be engaged. *(2)*

**8. Communication / gravitas — assertiveness under nerves.** Shy Janice, up for VP, must present to a panel; her voice is low and quivers.
- a. Not worry — her performance speaks for itself. *(2)*
- b. Be assertive; the panel will value her grace and substance. *(3)*
- c. Decline the nomination. *(1)*

**9. Appearance — appropriate + authentic.** First time at the golf outing; you're the only woman among the heavy hitters.
- a. The attractive, short flirty golf skirt. *(1)*
- b. The authentic, appropriate golf shirt that keeps attention on what you say/your score. *(3)*
- c. Option b, but with trendy wedge-heeled sneakers. *(2)*

**10. Mindset — what EP really is.** Which is true of EP?
- a. To look like a leader you must be perfect in all areas and slim. *(1)*
- b. Appearance is superficial nonsense only superficial folks tune into. *(2)*
- c. More than anything, how we look translates into respect — for ourselves, others, and the work. *(3)*

## Scoring Key (from the book, verbatim)
1. a-2, b-3, c-1 · 2. a-2, b-1, c-3 · 3. a-2, b-3, c-1 · 4. a-3, b-2, c-1 · 5. a-1, b-2, c-3 · 6. a-3, b-2, c-1 · 7. a-3, b-1, c-2 · 8. a-2, b-3, c-1 · 9. a-1, b-3, c-2 · 10. a-1, b-2, c-3

**Interpreting your total (out of 30):**
- **25–30** — Strong EP instincts across gravitas, communication, and appearance.
- **18–24** — Solid foundation; target the pillar(s) where you lost points (see the matching chapter).
- **10–17** — EP is likely holding you back; work through the pillars and get a sponsor's honest feedback (Ch 5).

> **Note on Question 6**: The published key scores answer **a** highest (3) and answer **c** lowest (1), but this appears inverted relative to the book's own teaching — option **a** endorses making a gender-biased statement, while option **c** is the one that *recognizes* it as "one of the worst communication violations." By the logic of Ch 3 (avoiding communication blunders) and Ch 6 (bias), **c is the EP-aware response**. Reproduced verbatim above for fidelity; treat Q6 as a reflection prompt, not a literal score.
