# Chapter 2: Gravitas

## Core Idea
Gravitas is the "weightiness or heft that marks you as worth following into the fire" — the core, decisive pillar of EP (62–67% of leaders call it the essence). It can't be faked, but it can be cultivated. CTI research breaks it into **six key behaviors**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Six Components of Gravitas**:
  1. **Grace / Confidence under fire** (the #1 pick — 79% women, 76% men). Composure in a crisis.
  2. **Decisiveness / "Showing teeth"** (70%). Making the hard call when others waver.
  3. **Integrity / Speaking truth to power** (60%+). The spine to voice inconvenient truths.
  4. **Emotional intelligence (EQ)** (~60%). Reading a room; tempering toughness with empathy.
  5. **Reputation / Personal brand** (56–57%). What precedes you into the room.
  6. **Vision** (50–54%). Telegraphing that you know where you're going.
  - When to use: As a self-audit. Nail ~3 of these to your natural strengths; avoid catastrophic failure on the rest.
  - How: Each is a learnable behavior, not an innate trait — detailed below.

- **Handling catastrophe confers gravitas** (grace under fire): "Avoiding catastrophe demonstrates competence; *handling* catastrophe confers gravitas."
  - How: In a crisis, reach for your "iron core" of self-confidence, own mistakes publicly, and speak from a place of calm. Owning up (Jamie Dimon, Sully Sullenberger, Tim Melville-Ross) can *bolster* gravitas; deflecting blame (Tony Hayward's "I'd like my life back") destroys it.

- **Show teeth discriminately** (decisiveness): "Real leaders listen, gather information, weigh options, look for a timely opening (typically when everyone else is writhing in indecision), and *then* demand action."
  - How: Hide your teeth more often than you bare them. It's not rendering the *right* decision that confers gravitas, but rendering *a* decision when no one else dares. "Sometimes it's just as important to know when being decisive is *not* the thing to do." (Bob Dudley)

## Key Concepts
- **Grace under fire**: Calm, confident, steady composure at the "white-hot center of a crisis."
- **Showing teeth**: Being (and appearing) decisive; imposing direction and owning it.
- **Speaking truth to power**: Sharing convictions upward from a "core of unshakable values" (not insubordination or arrogance).
- **Emotional intelligence (EQ)**: Reading and acting on others' feelings — a muscle built through experience, not an inborn trait.
- **Right-sizing your reputation**: Proactively managing your personal brand before others manage it for you.
- **Career-killer blunders**: Lack of integrity and sexual impropriety — they call values into question so fundamentally that gravitas evaporates.

## Mental Models
- **Iron core**: "Self-confidence is your iron core. To lean into the wind when your heart is pounding, you have to believe in yourself deep down." (Anne Erni)
- **Reading the room over commanding it**: "It may be more important to comfort a room than command it." (Kent Gardiner) EQ = self-awareness + situational awareness.
- **Brutal optimism**: "Great leaders are *brutally* optimistic." Leadership isn't a popularity contest — "Who do you take into the foxhole?" (Mellody Hobson)
- **Decisiveness is a double bind for women**: rendering tough decisions risks being seen as "unfeminine/unlikable" (see Ch 6).

## Anti-patterns
- **Deflecting blame in a crisis**: Tony Hayward's arrogance ("What did we do to deserve this?") cost him his job. Petulance reads as arrogance, not confidence.
- **Showing teeth without EQ**: Toughness untempered by empathy comes off as egotism (Mitt Romney's "47 percent"). Decisiveness *plus* insensitivity destroys buy-in.
- **Shooting from the hip**: Claiming to know more than you do. Back assertions with firsthand, concrete examples (Gadsden-Williams).
- **Hogging credit**: Nothing undermines followership faster than a boss who takes all the credit.

## Worked Example
*Speaking truth to power costs — and pays.* Sallie Krawcheck built a brand on "telling it like it is." As a research analyst she downgraded Travelers, enraging Sandy Weill — who was impressed enough to later hire her and promote her to CFO of Citigroup. At the height of the 2008 crisis she urged Citi to partially refund clients on mis-sold "low-risk" products; CEO Vikram Pandit fired her. In 2011 that same integrity won her the top job at Merrill Lynch. Her verdict: "Speaking truth has not always stood me in good stead in terms of my career progression. But it always, always, always stood me in good stead in terms of managing businesses… Had I to do it over again, I wouldn't do it any differently." **Lesson: gravitas from truth-telling is durable brand equity even when a given boss punishes it — but come from a core of values, not personal grievance.**

## Key Takeaways
1. Gravitas is the core of EP; without it, no title or polish makes you a leader.
2. Six components: confidence under fire, decisiveness, integrity, EQ, reputation, vision. Play to ~3 strengths.
3. Handling (not avoiding) crises and owning mistakes builds gravitas fastest.
4. Decisiveness must be discriminate and paired with EQ, or it reads as arrogance.
5. Two blunders are career-enders: lack of integrity and sexual impropriety.
6. To deepen gravitas: surround yourself with better people, be generous with credit, stick to what you know, show humility, smile more, empower others' presence, drive change rather than be changed.
