# Glossary — Executive Presence

**250-millisecond judgment** — The speed at which colleagues assess your competence, likability, and trustworthiness from appearance alone; these flash judgments stick (Ch 1, Ch 4).

**Air cover** — Sponsor protection secured *before* you step out to assert authenticity or take the spotlight; without it, visibility invites attack (Ch 7).

**Always on** — The principle that every verbal encounter (email, hallway chat, pitch) is a chance to build or erode your EP; you are never *not* presenting (Ch 3).

**Appearance** — The third EP pillar ("how you look"); rated least important long-term (5%) but the *filter* through which the other pillars are evaluated (Ch 1, Ch 4).

**Bleached-out professional** — Someone who scrubs away ethnic, religious, socioeconomic, or educational identifiers to be seen as "professional"; a lose-lose costing authenticity and visibility (Ch 7).

**Command a room** — Grabbing and holding an audience's attention; a core communication trait — "never be boring" (Ch 3).

**Cracking the EP code** — Learning and delivering the (often unwritten) rules of what bosses and coworkers look for (Ch 1).

**Diversity dividend** — The measurable bottom-line impact when leaders harness gender, ethnicity, culture, and nationality; your difference as a business asset (Ch 7).

**Emotional intelligence (EQ)** — Reading and acting on others' feelings (self- + situational awareness); a gravitas component and a muscle built by experience, not inborn (Ch 2).

**Executive presence (EP)** — The amalgam of confidence, poise, and authenticity that telegraphs you're in charge or deserve to be; the missing link between merit and success (Ch 1).

**First language / second language** — Trevor Phillips's model: your authentic identity (first) vs. the conventions of the dominant world you learn to survive (second) (Ch 7).

**Fork in the road** — The career rung where suppressing your difference no longer feels worth it (Ch 7).

**Goldilocks syndrome** — Being perpetually "too this, too that," never "just right" — the signature of hidden bias against those who don't fit the leader stereotype (Ch 6).

**Grace under fire** — Composure and confidence at the center of a crisis; the #1 gravitas behavior (79% women / 76% men) (Ch 2).

**Gravitas** — The first and core EP pillar ("how you act"); the "heft that marks you as worth following into the fire" (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Great Feedback Formula** — Feedback that is timely + specific + prescriptive + framed in business outcome (Ch 5).

**Invisible knapsack** — The kit of inborn privileges the dominant group can't perceive it carries (Ch 6).

**Iron core** — The deep self-confidence you reach for to "lean into the wind" in a crisis (Ch 2).

**Likability vs. competence trade-off** — The most tenacious double bind: successful women (unlike men) suffer social rejection and lost trust (Ch 6).

**Marzipan layer** — The rich, sticky talent layer just below top management where qualified women and minorities stall for lack of EP feedback (Ch 5).

**Narrow band of acceptability** — The constrained range of behavior/appearance women (and minorities) are allowed before being penalized in one direction or the other (Ch 5, Ch 6).

**Non-negotiables** — The values or cultures that don't deserve your compliance; know them and walk away (Ch 7).

**Personal brand / right-sizing your reputation** — Proactively managing how you're perceived before others do it for you; a gravitas component (Ch 2).

**Playing the game vs. selling out** — Distinguishing benign conformity from a compromise of your soul; only you can draw the line (Ch 7).

**Props** — Notes, slides, lists, glasses, podiums — anything that blocks eye contact and signals you don't command your material (Ch 3).

**Reading a room** — Sensing mood and cultural cues and course-correcting on the spot; EQ applied to communication (Ch 3).

**Shrillness** — A high-pitched, emotion-raised voice; the most-cited destroyer of a woman's EP — the "hint of hysteria" (Ch 3).

**Showing teeth** — Being (and appearing) decisive; imposing direction and owning it — but do it discriminately (Ch 2).

**Signature look** — A consciously built, personalized appearance/brand that telegraphs you embrace the code yet channel individuality (Ch 4).

**Speaking truth to power** — Voicing convictions upward from a core of unshakable values; a gravitas component (Ch 2).

**Sponsor** — A powerful leader vested in your success who advocates, steers assignments, protects you, and gives the blunt EP feedback mentors won't (distinct from a mentor) (Ch 5, Ch 7).

**Telegraph** — To signal (vs. perform); EP is a measure of image you telegraph, not of performance (Ch 1).

**The three pillars** — Gravitas (act), communication (speak), appearance (look); the universal structure of EP (Ch 1).

**Uptalk** — Ending declarative statements on a rising note as if questioning; denies closure and undermines the message (Ch 3).
