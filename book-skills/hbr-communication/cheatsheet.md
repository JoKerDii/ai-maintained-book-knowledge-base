# Cheatsheet — Decision Rules

## First branch: what kind of communication problem is this?

- **You need one person to say yes** → Ch 1 (classify their decision style) + Ch 4 (audit your credibility first)
- **You need many people to change behavior** → Ch 2 (six principles) + Ch 7 (springboard story)
- **People are not saying what they think** → Ch 5 (spiral of silence) + Ch 3 (linguistic style, not competence)
- **You have to stand up and deliver** → Ch 6 (rehearse intent) + Ch 9 (the five messages)
- **The conversation itself is the hard part** → Ch 10 (three stressful types)
- **You are selling a creative idea** → Ch 8 (pitcher types)

## Decision styles: how to argue (Ch 1)

| Style | % of execs | Lead with | Never | Latency |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Follower | 36% | Precedent, proven-elsewhere cases | Novelty framed as untested | Days |
| Charismatic | 25% | The exciting result, then risks you name yourself | Overselling | Fast, then verifies |
| Skeptic | 19% | Credibility borrowed from someone they trust | Contradicting them publicly | Days or on the spot |
| Thinker | 11% | Methodology and alternatives considered | Jumping to conclusions | Weeks–months, 2 meetings |
| Controller | 9% | Structured linear memos, problem data | Requesting the meeting yourself | Months; wait for a trigger |

- **If you cannot classify them, they are a follower** — the other four announce themselves.
- ~80% of presentations are pitched at skeptics and controllers, who are 28% of executives. Assume your default is wrong.
- Sequence to a thinker is roughly the reverse of the sequence to a charismatic.

## Cialdini: which principle fits (Ch 2)

| Situation | Principle | Move |
|---|---|---|
| New relationship, no history | Liking | Surface a genuine similarity; give real praise |
| You want future help | Reciprocity | Give first, visibly and without a ledger |
| Peers resist a change | Social proof | Let a respected *peer* speak, not the boss |
| Behavior must persist unsupervised | Consistency | Active + public + voluntary written commitment |
| Audience does not know you | Authority | Make real credentials visible *before* influencing |
| Information favors acting now | Scarcity | Exclusive and loss-framed, only if true |

- **Never on a false premise.** Detection kills the offer and reciprocity returns the dishonesty.
- Combine principles in one interaction; do not deploy them serially.

## Conger: order of operations (Ch 4)

1. Credibility — if absent, stop and buy it. Steps 2–4 are futile without it.
2. Common ground — if no shared advantage exists, change the position, not the framing.
3. Vivid evidence — data alone does not move people; supplement with story, metaphor, analogy.
4. Emotional connection — match their state, not yours. End on hope, not doom.

**Four ways not to persuade**: hard sell up front; refuse to compromise; rely on argument alone; treat it as an event rather than a process. Enter with concessions already prepared.

## Story selection (Ch 7)

| Goal | Detail | Tone | Form |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spark action | Minimal — seconds | Positive + evidenced | Springboard |
| Communicate who you are | Rich | Either | Full narrative, told in person |
| Transmit values | Rich, believable | Either | Only works if you live it |
| Foster collaboration | Moving, shared | Either | Then open the floor |
| Tame the grapevine | Enough to expose incongruity | Humor | Only if the rumor is false |
| Share knowledge | Detailed on the fix | **Negative** | Problem/fix, no hero |
| Lead into the future | Vague on specifics | Positive | Never predict particulars |

- Goal is action → subtract detail. Goal is trust → add detail.
- Rumor true or plausible? Do not ridicule — admit, reframe, move on. Denying gives it credibility.

## Pitching: is your type working for you? (Ch 8)

- Categorization happens in under 150 ms; character judgment inside 30 minutes.
- ~1% of ideas survive the initial pitch; ~25% of pitchers avoid fast elimination.
- **Negative cues outweigh positive ones** → eliminate pushover / robot / used-car salesman / charity case *before* optimizing anything else.
- Have a track record? Lead with it and skip type-play entirely. Do not fake the artist — genuineness is the credibility.
- Catcher's rule: if you feel swept away by a positive type match, start testing — showrunners are the favorite and the most dangerous.

## Silence: speak or stay quiet? (Ch 5)

Invert the default. Ask "is this one of the rare times to stay quiet?" Stay quiet only if:
- the issue is forgettable within a week or a month, **or**
- the relationship is short-lived and unimportant.

Defer (with a stated time) when emotions are hot or a deadline is imminent — indefinite postponement guarantees the difference returns. Never resolve conflict by dividing the work. **Tell**: chronic gossip about someone you have never confronted means you are already in a spiral.

## Stressful conversations (Ch 10)

| Type | Gambit | Opening line shape |
|---|---|---|
| "I have bad news for you" | Honor thy partner | Acknowledge your own share, then state the news plainly |
| "What's going on here?" | Disarm by restating intentions | "I can see how you took it that way. That wasn't what I meant." |
| "You are attacking me!" | Fight tactics, not people | Name the behavior's effect, not their character |

- **The opening determines the conversation.** Never ease into bad news with small talk.
- Clarity is not brutality: content is not brutal, delivery is. Default tone is neutral.
- If it is not an emergency, split it — raise in meeting one, discuss in meeting two.
- Improvement is unilateral: prepare against *your* predictable vulnerability, not their unpredictable behavior.

## Leader's defaults (Ch 9)

- Announce reorgs whole and fast; never preview (HP's preview cost ~a full quarter).
- Cap each direct report at **3 initiatives per quarter**. The enemy is starting the 85th thing.
- Withhold your own theory in problem-solving meetings — the boss's answer stifles the room.
- Define winning numerically, not adjectivally.
- Treat missed results as a diagnostic, never as a blame trigger.

## Cross-cutting tells

- "Poor communication" → assume differing **linguistic styles** until shown otherwise (Ch 3).
- Confidence judged from speech is unreliable — public claims diverge from private ones (Ch 3).
- Rehearsed gestures lagging the words is what audiences read as fake (Ch 6).
- **There is no one best way to communicate** — outcome depends on situation, culture, relative rank, and the interaction of two styles.
