# Glossary

**84 Great Things** — 7 direct reports x 3 doable initiatives per quarter x 4 quarters; the yearly output cap that keeps a team from over-starting (Ch 9)
**Answer man trap** — a leader whose identity rests on having the answer, which suppresses everyone else's thinking (Ch 9)
**Antistory** — the cynical counter-narrative an audience invents when a story sounds implausibly rosy (Ch 7)
**Artist** — pitcher type who inverts the power differential by commanding the catcher's imagination with thought experiments (Ch 8)
**Authority** — Cialdini principle: people defer to visible, genuine expertise as a shortcut through complexity (Ch 2)
**Buffered feedback** — praise-then-criticism ritual; literal listeners hear only the praise (Ch 3)
**Buzzwords** — style-specific vocabulary that holds a particular decision maker's attention (Ch 1)
**Catcher** — the executive who judges a pitch; judges the pitcher's creativity, not only the idea (Ch 8)
**Charismatic** — decision style: exuberant, visual, risk-seeking, short attention span; decides on balanced information (Ch 1)
**Clarification technique** — grant the other person's perception, own the words/intent mismatch, resume the substance (Ch 10)
**Coalition-building** — testing a concern with one savvy peer before voicing it upward (Ch 5)
**Commitment (active, public, voluntary)** — the three-part test a commitment must pass to bind future behavior (Ch 2)
**Common ground framing** — stating a position in terms of tangible benefit to the audience; if none exists, change the position (Ch 4)
**Consistency** — Cialdini principle: people align with commitments they have gone on record for (Ch 2)
**Controller** — decision style: abhors ambiguity, implements only his own ideas, avoids accountability (Ch 1)
**Conversational rituals** — conventionalized exchanges (ritual apology, ritual opposition, compliment exchange) that break when taken literally (Ch 3)
**Creative collaborator** — the catcher recast as co-author of the idea, which sharply lowers rejection odds (Ch 8)
**Credibility (expertise + relationships)** — Conger's two sources; the prerequisite to any persuasion attempt (Ch 4)
**Credibility transfer** — borrowing trust by having someone the audience already trusts deliver the message (Ch 1, Ch 4)
**Danger zone** — a conversation or counterpart you already know you handle badly (Ch 10)
**Default mode of decision making** — the style a person reverts to under complexity and consequence (Ch 1)
**Definitional control** — claiming a loaded term's meaning before rumor supplies one (Ch 9)
**Deviant act** — deliberately violating a silencing norm, e.g. asking the hard question nobody asks (Ch 5)
**Emotional matchmaking** — tuning emotional intensity to the audience's current state, not your own (Ch 4)
**Five Messages Leaders Must Manage** — hierarchy, financial results, the leader's job, time management, culture (Ch 9)
**Follower** — decision style: largest group, risk-averse, decides by precedent; wants innovative-yet-proven (Ch 1)
**Four Aims / intents** — Morgan's rehearsal targets: be open, connect, be passionate, listen (Ch 6)
**Four Essential Steps of Persuasion** — establish credibility, frame on common ground, reinforce with vivid evidence, connect emotionally (Ch 4)
**Grapevine** — the informal river of organizational communication; steer it rather than dam it (Ch 7)
**Hip-pocket phrases** — pre-rehearsed lines you can recall under pressure (Ch 10)
**Honor thy partner** — opening a bad-news conversation by acknowledging your own share of the problem (Ch 10)
**Indirectness** — meaning without spelling it out; used by the powerful as softened orders as much as by subordinates (Ch 3)
**Knowledge duet** — the showrunner's give-and-take of shared references before landing the idea (Ch 8)
**Knowledge-sharing narrative** — plotless, heroless, negative-toned problem/fix account; the workhorse story (Ch 7)
**Liking** — Cialdini principle: people comply with those similar to them and who like them (Ch 2)
**Linguistic style** — a person's characteristic speaking pattern: directness, pacing, pausing, jokes, questions, apologies (Ch 3)
**Loss language** — framing around what is forfeited by inaction rather than gained by action (Ch 2)
**Maximalist account** — the fully rendered story; engrossing but action-suppressing (Ch 7)
**Mental opt-out** — comply while contributing nothing; the terminal state of a silence spiral (Ch 5)
**Neophyte** — pitcher type who exploits the power differential by asking boldly for help (Ch 8)
**Person prototypes** — stereotype sets used to categorize a stranger in under a second (Ch 8)
**Persuasion as learning and negotiating** — an iterative move toward a shared solution, not a one-shot convincing (Ch 4)
**Practical intelligence** — feel for which ideas will actually work commercially; the showrunner's edge (Ch 8)
**Reciprocity** — Cialdini principle: people repay in kind; give first what you want to receive (Ch 2)
**Red zone** — a thwarting tactic paired with your own known vulnerability (Ch 10)
**Results as diagnostic** — treating a missed number as a root-cause investigation, not a punishment trigger (Ch 9)
**Ritual apology** — "I'm sorry" as expressed concern rather than admitted fault (Ch 3)
**Ritual opposition** — testing ideas through verbal combat; over when it's over (Ch 3)
**Scarcity** — Cialdini principle: people want more of what is less available; exclusive information outperforms common information (Ch 2)
**Second conversation** — the nonverbal channel, which fires before words and wins whenever the two conflict (Ch 6)
**Showrunner** — pitcher type who levels the power differential with a knowledge duet; the catcher's favorite and most dangerous type (Ch 8)
**Silencing** — declining to confront a difference, in yourself or in others (Ch 5)
**Skeptic** — decision style: combative, suspicious of anything challenging his worldview; decides fast on gut (Ch 1)
**Social proof** — Cialdini principle: people follow similar others, not distant strangers (Ch 2)
**Speed trap** — deadline pressure produces silencing, which produces worse decisions and more pressure (Ch 5)
**Spiral of silence** — silence, resentment, disconnection, self-protection, more fear, more silence (Ch 5)
**Splitting the conversation** — raising an issue in one meeting and discussing it in a later one (Ch 10)
**Springboard story** — minimal-detail, true, recent story with one protagonist that makes listeners leap to "why not here?" (Ch 7)
**Status/rapport dynamic** — every utterance both conveys ideas and negotiates relative position (Ch 3)
**Substitute credibility** — credibility borrowed from consultants, independent research, pilots, or allies (Ch 4)
**Thinker** — decision style: cerebral, risk-averse, data-hungry; slowest and toughest to persuade (Ch 1)
**Thought experiment** — the artist's invitation into an imagined world (Ch 6, Ch 8)
**Thwarting tactics** — shouting, accusation, distortion, digression, manipulation used to derail a conversation (Ch 10)
**Vivid evidence** — stories, metaphors, and analogies that make data land emotionally (Ch 4)
