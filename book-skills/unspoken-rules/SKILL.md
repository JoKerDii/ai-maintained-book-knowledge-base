## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The book's thesis**: career success isn't just hard work — it's knowing the *unspoken rules*: things managers expect but don't explain, and top performers do but don't realize. They're passed down privately, so they create an unlevel field between insiders and outsiders. This skill makes them explicit.

### The Three Cs — the master lens (Ch 1)
Every workplace judgment reduces to three things, needed **all at once**:
- **Competence** — do your job fully, accurately, promptly, without micromanagement.
- **Commitment** — be present and eager to help the team hit its goals.
- **Compatibility** — make others comfortable and glad to work with you, authentically.

Each C fails in **two** directions — undershoot (clueless/apathetic/cold) and overshoot (know-it-all/doormat/poser). Aim for the middle. And remember: managers judge the **perceived** version of your work — make progress visible.

### "Let's Give This a Shot" + the entitlement test (Ch 2)
Try things you might fail at but might succeed at. The worst case is almost always a "no," not danger — the fear is of judgment, not safety. Before asking for something, imagine the "no": if your honest reaction is *"just thought I'd ask,"* it's curiosity → proceed. If it's outrage, it's entitlement → don't.

### See the big picture (Ch 3) + Take Ownership (Ch 8)
Be a **thought partner, not a workhorse**: understand how your role fits the whole. On any task, clarify **What / How / By When** immediately, map **RACI** (Responsible/Accountable/Consulted/Informed), and work backward from the deadline. Ownership = treating the task as if your reputation rides on it — *including* knowing when to ask for help. When stuck, give people **something to react to** (option A/B + a recommendation), not an open "what do you think?"

### Ask good questions (Ch 3) + the two modes (Ch 4, Ch 12)
Sort every question into three circles: *what you know* / *what's figure-out-able* (don't ask) / *what's only learnable by asking* (ask — and **show your homework**). You're in **learner mode** when new (expected to ask) or **leader mode** when established (expected to know). Calibrate accordingly.

### Shape perception (Ch 5–7)
- **Tell your story**: lead with the **external narrative** (audience-tailored, proves the Three Cs), not the internal "me, me, me" one. Keep a **mental fridge** of talking points for the unexpected.
- **Manage appearance**: **mirror** coworkers at your level; sort identity elements into **sacred / negotiable / indifferent**; **bend** the rules — conform first, reveal authentic self after earning trust.
- **Send the right signals**: you're judged on **impact, not intent**. Five checks before send (typos / caught up / right time / tone / forward-safe). Read the team's email-first vs. IM-first and monochronic vs. polychronic rhythm.

### Manage workload (Ch 9)
Triage by **urgency** (proximity + scrutiny + anxiety + time-sensitivity) × **importance** (centrality + scrutiny + criticality) on the Eisenhower matrix. Chase **promotable** work over office housework. Preempt predictable fire drills; report problems at **root cause** via the "Why?" exercise. Under-promise, over-deliver, **no surprises**.

### Office politics (Ch 10–11)
**Read between the people**: map chain of command → influencers (Gatekeepers/Veterans/Experts/Socialites/Advisers) → swimlanes → loyalties → comfort zones, by behavior not titles. Friendly ≠ loyal. **Spark relationships** by answering **bids**, using **EAR** (Engage, Ask, Repeat) and "Yes, and…"; relationships **compound**, so start early and be an energy giver.

### Get ahead (Ch 12–15)
- **Meetings**: know your role (seen-and-heard / seen-not-heard / neither); bring one thoughtful comment + one thoughtful question.
- **Feedback**: decode it on two axes (verbal↔nonverbal, direct↔indirect); separate substance from delivery; apply fast and report back — coachability signals commitment.
- **Conflicts**: **fix it / live with it / leave it** — diagnose **People/Position/Place**, strip emotion with **10-10-10**, take the path of least regret.
- **Show potential**: aim top-right of the **nine-box** (do today's job well *and* show you can do the next); claim an **unoccupied swimlane**; make contributions **noticed and reported** and **better, not just different**.
