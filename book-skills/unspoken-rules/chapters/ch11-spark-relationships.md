# Chapter 11: Spark Relationships

## Core Idea
Careers advance through people, not just performance — those who get the biggest bonuses and fastest promotions are often not the hardest workers but the ones the most people know and praise. Building relationships comes down to finding excuses to spark a first interaction, then small ways to sustain the momentum.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Steps of Relationship Building**: Spark the connection → Play the game → Sustain the momentum.
  - When to use: Any time you want to build a professional (or personal) network, especially when new.
  - How: Notice/offer "bids," hold a flowing conversation, then keep the relationship alive over weeks and months.
  - Why it works: Relationships aren't built from single conversations but from many small interactions; the first time is always uncomfortable, the second always easier.

- **Bids (Dr. John Gottman)**: Subtle requests for human connection — a nod, smile, "How's it going?", an IM.
  - When to use: Constantly — to spark and to respond.
  - How: Notice others' bids and respond positively; offer your own. Saying "thank you" or replying to an IM is sometimes all it takes.
  - Failure mode: People who miss or reject bids weaken relationships (Gottman: couples who divorce miss bids; those who stay married notice and respond).

- **Three Audiences for the First Move**: (1) People you know/work near → find an excuse to chat (weather, agenda, "Happy Friday!"), then ask a question. (2) People you don't know but could be introduced to → request a **double opt-in intro**. (3) People you don't know and can't be introduced to → send a customized **cold email**.
  - When to use: Reaching out first instead of waiting.
  - How (cold email test): Ask "Would my email still make sense if I sent it to the wrong person?" If yes, it isn't customized enough — present yourself as a younger version of the recipient with a recipient-specific reason to connect.

- **EAR — Engage, Ask, Repeat**: A fallback for keeping any conversation going.
  - When to use: When panicking mid-conversation or with little in common.
  - How: Engage with what they say (listen, absorb, think) → Ask a question of your own → Repeat until you run out of things or must return to work. If you can't relate, default to asking questions.

## Key Concepts
- **Bid**: A request for human connection (Gottman) — the building block of every relationship.
- **Double opt-in intro**: An introduction where both sides agree before being connected; the broker forwards the request, both opt in, then is moved to BCC.
- **Second-degree connection**: Someone you can reach through a person you already know — the engine of networking ("who they know").
- **Cold email**: An outreach message to a stranger, customized enough that it couldn't be sent to anyone else.
- **"Yes, and …"**: An improv move — accept what the other person said, then build on it (embrace over reject).
- **Energy givers vs. energy vampires**: Be the former; people avoid complainers who suck energy out of the room.
- **Office housework trap**: Low-value "nice-to-do" tasks that disproportionately fall to women and people of color — volunteer mindfully.
- **Virtuous circle of people and opportunities**: The earlier you start building relationships, the more relationships and opportunities compound.

## Mental Models
- Think of conversation as a tennis match: your job is to hit the ball smoothly back over the net so the other side can return it (Anand's good reply vs. dead-end reply).
- Use "It's not only who you know but who they know" — every direct connection multiplies your indirect reach.
- Treat the first interaction as the hardest rep; it gets easier the second, third, and fourth times — so just start.
- "Your job isn't simply to do your job — it's also to build relationships" (the media account manager's lesson on bridging different backgrounds).

## Anti-patterns
- **Being a conversational wall**: One-word answers ("It was good") that give nothing to latch onto and kill the exchange.
- **Spray-and-pray cold emails**: Generic messages that read as spam and get ignored.
- **Skipping social events early on**: A pattern of absence signals disinterest and stops future invitations.
- **Failing to say hi again / not sending a thank-you**: Effectively communicates "I don't remember you" or "I'm not grateful."
- **Aggressive networking**: Burdening too many people with meeting requests at once.

## Worked Example
**Sustaining momentum — Donovan.** A lab technician new to Toronto built his network by answering bids at the dog park: when his dog tugged him toward another owner, he'd spark a chat — "What's your dog's name? How long have you had her?" — and follow up the next day about their week. Soon he was in a 7 a.m. dog-walker texting group. Three years later, wanting to switch into real estate and move to Houston, he told his dog-owning friends. One introduced him to Karis (real estate, NYC); Karis passed his résumé to Vicky (construction, Houston); over a Houston lunch with Vicky's coworkers, a passing superintendent joined the group and Donovan introduced himself; that coworker forwarded his résumé to another company's superintendent. A week later he was hired as a project manager — a fifth-degree connection, set in motion by responding to a simple bid and keeping the mindset *Let's give this a shot*.

## Key Takeaways
1. People decide hiring, assignments, and invitations — the well-known often beat the merely competent.
2. Notice and respond to bids, and offer your own; small acknowledgments build relationships.
3. Make the first move with the right tool per audience — casual chat, double opt-in intro, or a deeply customized cold email.
4. Keep conversations flowing by contributing details, highlighting commonalities, "Yes, and …", asking questions, listening, and mirroring — or fall back on EAR.
5. Be present and seen: stay near your team, attend socials early, and use cross-team projects to expand your network.
6. Sustain momentum with seven escalating tactics — say hi again, ask how things went, share relevant news, offer intros, show gratitude, mention wanting to work together, and ask for coffee.
7. You never know where a relationship leads, so start now and set the virtuous circle in motion.
