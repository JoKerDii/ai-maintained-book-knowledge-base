# Glossary — The Unspoken Rules

**Accountable** — the person whose reputation is on the line for a deliverable; only one per task (Ch 8).
**Ask-for-permission vs. beg-for-forgiveness spectrum** — how rigid a team's swimlanes are; rigid → clear it first, loose → act then inform (Ch 10).
**Authentic** — true to who you are, as opposed to the average of your coworkers (Ch 6).
**Bare-minimum rule** — outcomes scale with the initiative you put in; doing only what's asked caps your results (Ch 2).
**Bid** — a small request for connection (a comment, question, gesture); relationships are built by noticing and answering bids (Gottman) (Ch 11).
**Big picture / the eight blanks** — understanding how your org and role fit the whole; the senior-leader mindset that turns a workhorse into a thought partner (Ch 3).
**Blunt actions** — direct + nonverbal feedback: reassigning, redoing, or rejecting your work (Ch 13).
**Behavior changes** — indirect + nonverbal feedback: avoidance, micromanaging, going quiet (Ch 13).
**Boomerang employee** — someone who leaves an organization and later returns, often at the same level (Ch 14).
**Call to action** — the specific response a message asks for; every message should make the next step obvious (Ch 8).
**Coachability** — visibly applying feedback; reading as coachable signals commitment (Ch 13).
**Cold email** — outreach to a stranger, customized enough that it can't be mistaken for spam (Ch 11).
**Comfort zones** — the topics and behaviors a given person treats as appropriate vs. taboo (Ch 10).
**Commitment** — being fully present and eager to help the team hit its goals; one of the Three Cs (Ch 1).
**Compatibility** — making others comfortable and glad to be around you, without seeming inauthentic; one of the Three Cs (Ch 1).
**Competence** — doing your job fully, accurately, and promptly without micromanagement or making others look bad; one of the Three Cs (Ch 1).
**Curiosity vs. entitlement test** — imagine getting a "no"; if your honest reaction is "just thought I'd ask," the ask is curiosity, not entitlement (Ch 2).
**CYA ("cover yourself")** — because digital messages are permanent and forwardable, communicate so a forwarded copy never hurts you (Ch 7).
**Double opt-in intro** — connecting two people only after both have agreed to be connected (Ch 11).
**EAR (Engage, Ask, Repeat)** — conversation fallback: engage, ask a question, repeat; default to questions when you can't relate (Ch 11).
**Eight blanks** — see *Big picture*: the gaps you fill to understand how everything fits (Ch 3).
**Eisenhower Matrix (adapted)** — triage by urgency × importance: do / schedule / timebox-or-delegate / delete (Ch 9).
**Energy givers vs. energy vampires** — people who add energy to a room vs. those who drain it with complaints; be the former (Ch 11).
**External narrative** — the audience-tailored version of your story that proves the Three Cs; lead with this, not the internal "me, me, me" version (Ch 5).
**Feedback ranges** — feedback mapped on two axes, verbal↔nonverbal and direct↔indirect (Ch 13).
**Fire drill** — a sudden, urgent demand that disrupts planned work (Ch 9).
**First loyalties** — who someone is loyal to first; friendly toward you ≠ on your side (Ch 10).
**Fix it / live with it / leave it** — the three responses to any bad situation (Ch 14).
**Gatekeepers / Veterans / Experts / Socialites / Advisers** — the five informal-influencer types who hold power beyond their title (Ch 10).
**Good question** — one you genuinely can't answer on your own; only learnable by asking (Ch 3).
**Hero of your own journey** — others are too busy with themselves to scrutinize you, so act (Ch 2).
**Hero's journey (Past, Present, Future)** — frame your story as origin → present → goal in about two minutes (Ch 5).
**High-context vs. low-context** — cultures/people who communicate through the unspoken vs. at face value (Ch 13).
**IC (individual contributor)** — someone who does the work directly rather than managing others (Ch 15).
**Importance (three factors)** — centrality, scrutiny, criticality: how much a task matters (Ch 9).
**Indifferent zone** — appearance/identity elements you can abandon without any felt loss (Ch 6).
**Influencer** — someone with leverage to sway decisions without formal authority (Ch 10).
**Intent vs. impact** — what you mean vs. how you come across; close the gap, especially in writing/remote work (Ch 7, Ch 13).
**Internal narrative** — your private "why I do this" story; build it first, but rarely lead with it (Ch 5).
**Learner mode vs. leader mode** — the two expectation states: new → expected to ask questions; established → expected to know and contribute (Ch 4, Ch 12).
**"Let's give this a shot"** — the mindset of trying things you might fail at but might succeed at (Ch 2).
**Mental fridge** — a stocked set of talking-point "ingredients" you can pull from to answer unexpected questions (Ch 5).
**Mirroring** — inferring the unspoken norm from coworkers at your level and matching it (Ch 6).
**Monochronic vs. polychronic** — cultures that treat time as fixed blocks vs. fluid and flexible (Ch 7).
**Nine-box matrix** — performance × potential grid; aim for the top-right box to position for promotion (Ch 15).
**Negativity bias** — criticism registers more strongly than praise; weight feedback accordingly (Ch 13).
**Nonpromotable tasks / office housework** — note-taking, lunch orders, event-planning: work that rarely advances a career and disproportionately falls on women and people of color (Ch 9, Ch 11).
**Ownership** — treating a task as if your reputation depends on it, including knowing when to ask for help (Ch 8).
**Path of least regret** — choose the option best for the long term, not the one that relieves discomfort fastest (Ch 14).
**Pattern of behavior** — your "whenever… always…" reputation; managed by being predictable (Ch 9).
**People / Position / Place** — the three root sources of a conflict; diagnose which before acting (Ch 14).
**Perceived vs. actual** — managers judge the *perceived* version of your work (visible progress, confidence), not just the actual output (Ch 1).
**Performance vs. potential** — effectiveness in your current job vs. ability to do the next one / lead (Ch 15).
**Pre-day-one research (15 strategies)** — a ~45-minute prep routine to "see the big picture" before any role, call, or meeting (Ch 3).
**Preread / prework** — materials circulated before a meeting; mining them yields your one comment + one question (Ch 12).
**Promotable tasks** — visible, valued work that advances your career; seek these over office housework (Ch 9).
**Puddle (locality)** — the scope of a problem: small, medium, or large; sets how far you must go to escape it (Ch 14).
**RACI** — Responsible, Accountable, Consulted, Informed: clarify roles on any task (Ch 8).
**Reject / embrace / bend the rules** — three stances when your appearance doesn't "fit" the norm (Ch 6).
**Sacred / negotiable / indifferent zones** — three concentric circles for sorting which identity elements to protect, weigh, or give up (Ch 6).
**Seen and heard / seen but not heard / neither** — the three roles you can hold in a meeting; calibrate by size, attendees, topic (Ch 12).
**Skip manager** — your boss's boss (Ch 10).
**Swimlane** — what a person owns and when, regardless of their title (Ch 10, Ch 15).
**Subtle hints** — indirect + verbal feedback: softened, diluted messages (Ch 13).
**Ten-ten-ten (10-10-10)** — strip emotion from a decision by asking how each option feels in 10 minutes / 10 months / 10 years (Ch 14).
**Three Cs** — competence, commitment, compatibility; the book's central lens, needed all at once (Ch 1).
**Three circles of question quality** — what you know / what's figure-out-able / what's only learnable by asking; ask only from the outer circle (Ch 3).
**Thought partner vs. workhorse** — perceived as someone who grasps the big picture vs. someone who just executes (Ch 3).
**Transferable skills** — capabilities from a past role bridged explicitly to the new one (Ch 5).
**Trade-off triangle (people/position/place)** — you rarely get all three good at once; know which you're trading (Ch 14).
**Uncensored comments** — direct + verbal feedback: blunt, unsoftened criticism (Ch 13).
**Undershooting vs. overshooting** — each C fails in two directions: too little (clueless/apathetic) or too much (overbearing/poser) (Ch 1, Ch 5).
**Unoccupied swimlane** — an unclaimed but valuable need you can own to show potential (Ch 15).
**Uptalking** — rising intonation at the end of statements that undercuts perceived confidence (Ch 5).
**Voluntelling** — a higher-up framing an assigned task as if it were optional (Ch 4).
**Virtuous circle** — early relationships and opportunities compound into more of both (Ch 11).
**What / how / by when** — the three things to clarify the moment you receive any task (Ch 8).
**"Why?" exercise** — repeatedly asking "why is this happening?" to drill from symptom to root cause (Ch 9, Ch 14).
**Zone of commitment** — the scope you're authorized to act within; overshooting it reads as threatening (Ch 10).
