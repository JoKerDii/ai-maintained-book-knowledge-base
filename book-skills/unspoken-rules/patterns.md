# Patterns & Techniques — The Unspoken Rules

Concrete, repeatable methods from the book. Each: when to use, how, trade-offs.

## The Three Cs
**When**: As a diagnostic lens on any workplace move, message, or decision.
**How**: Before acting, check that you're showing all three at once — Competence (do the job fully/accurately/promptly), Commitment (be present and eager for the team's goals), Compatibility (make others comfortable). For each, avoid undershooting (too little → clueless/apathetic) and overshooting (too much → overbearing/poser).
**Trade-offs**: Optimizing one C can dent another (e.g. over-asserting competence can hurt compatibility); balance, don't maximize.

## "Let's Give This a Shot" + Curiosity vs. Entitlement Test
**When**: Facing an opportunity you might fail at, or unsure whether to ask for something.
**How**: Ask "what's the worst that can happen?" (usually a "no"). Then run the entitlement test: imagine the "no" — proceed only if your honest reaction is "just thought I'd ask," not outrage. Then act and let others say no.
**Trade-offs**: Some people need to be *less* entitled (got an unearned edge), some *more* (habitually wait for permission). Calibrate to which you are.

## Pre-Day-One Research (15 strategies)
**When**: Before any new role, first call, big meeting, or interview.
**How**: ~45 min of higher-priority moves — skim Wikipedia/company About + News, recent Google news, memorize key faces, LinkedIn the people CC'd, confirm dress code + logistics (arrive 15 min early), set a Google Alert, accept intro chats — plus lower-priority depth (competitors, Glassdoor/Reddit, "anyone else I should speak with?"). Goal: see the big picture and fill "the eight blanks."
**Trade-offs**: Time-box it; research is prep, not a substitute for showing up and asking good questions.

## Three Circles of Question Quality + "Do — and Show — Your Homework"
**When**: Before asking anything as a newcomer.
**How**: Sort the question — inner circle (you already know) / middle (figure-out-able → don't ask) / outer (only learnable by asking → ask). When you ask, narrate your work: "Here's what I know, here's what I don't" or "Here's my question and why I'm asking."
**Trade-offs**: Asking good questions early earns credit that buys leeway for naive questions later.

## Twelve "Yes!" Onboarding Questions + Find Your Job
**When**: Starting a role, especially with weak onboarding.
**How**: Self-check you can answer "Yes!" to all twelve (paperwork, reporting lines, role expectations, check-in cadence, intros, priorities, tools/access, routine…). Where the answer is "no," ask the scripted question. If given tasks but no real role: observe & take notes, ask & learn, volunteer for work.
**Trade-offs**: Think like an owner — the gaps are yours to close even when no one hands you a map.

## Tell Your Story: Internal vs. External Narrative (5 steps) + Mental Fridge
**When**: Any "tell me about yourself / why are you here" moment.
**How**: Build the internal narrative (your real why), then the external one (audience-tailored, proves the Three Cs), add structure (Past/Present/Future hero's journey), finesse style (no uptalking/overshooting), and practice. Stock a "mental fridge" of talking-point ingredients to answer the unexpected.
**Trade-offs**: Lead external, not internal — the "me, me, me" version reads as self-absorbed.

## Manage Appearance: Mirror, then Reject/Embrace/Bend + Sacred/Negotiable/Indifferent
**When**: Deciding how much to conform your look/identity to a workplace.
**How**: Infer the unspoken norm by mirroring coworkers at your level (default more formal when unsure). Choose to reject, embrace, or bend (conform first, reveal authentic self after building trust). Sort each element into sacred (protect), negotiable (weigh), indifferent (give away freely).
**Trade-offs**: Over-adapting wastes energy on things coworkers never noticed; protect what's sacred.

## Send the Right Signals: Five Questions Before Send + Intent vs. Impact
**When**: Before sending any message; calibrating remote/written presence.
**How**: Before send, check — typos? caught up on the thread? right time? tone matches intent? OK if forwarded? Match the team's email-first vs. IM-first culture, and its monochronic (time = blocks) vs. polychronic (time = fluid) rhythm.
**Trade-offs**: You're judged on impact, not intent; permanent digital signals mean over-clarify rather than assume.

## Take Ownership: What/How/By When + RACI + Give Others Something to React To
**When**: The moment you receive any task.
**How**: Clarify *what*, *how*, *by when* immediately; map RACI (who's Responsible/Accountable/Consulted/Informed); work backward from the deadline with interim check-ins. For non-urgent questions: search first, bundle them, escalate one rung with context. When stuck, propose option A/B with a recommendation instead of asking an open "what do you think?"
**Trade-offs**: Repeating the task back feels redundant but catches costly misunderstandings early.

## Manage Workload: Urgency × Importance Triage
**When**: Too much to do; deciding order.
**How**: Score urgency by four factors (proximity, scrutiny, anxiety, time-sensitivity) and importance by three (centrality, scrutiny, criticality). Triage on the adapted Eisenhower Matrix: do / schedule / timebox-or-delegate / delete. Preempt predictable patterns (Friday updates, post-holiday clearing, templates) and report problems at root cause via the "Why?" exercise.
**Trade-offs**: Seek promotable work over office housework; under-promise and over-deliver to avoid surprises.

## Read between the People (5 steps) + Five Influencer Types
**When**: Joining any new team.
**How**: Map (1) the chain of command, (2) the influencers, (3) swimlanes, (4) loyalties, (5) comfort zones — by observing behavior and language, not titles. Spot the five influencer types (Gatekeepers, Veterans, Experts, Socialites, Advisers) by who decisions route through. Gauge swimlane rigidity on the ask-permission ↔ beg-forgiveness spectrum.
**Trade-offs**: Friendly ≠ loyal; don't mistake an early ally's warmth for first loyalty.

## Spark Relationships: Spark → Play → Sustain + Bids + EAR
**When**: Building a network from zero.
**How**: Respond to others' bids for connection; keep conversation flowing with EAR (Engage, Ask, Repeat) and "Yes, and…"; tailor the first move to the audience (casual chat / double opt-in intro / customized cold email); sustain ties over time. Be an energy giver.
**Trade-offs**: Relationships compound (virtuous circle) — small early investment pays outsized later returns.

## Master Meetings: Seven Questions + Three Roles + One Comment + One Question
**When**: Before, during, and after any meeting.
**How**: Run the seven prep/participation/follow-up questions, scaling depth to the meeting's importance. Calibrate your role — seen and heard / seen but not heard / neither — by size, attendees, and topic. With prep time, mine the preread for one thoughtful comment + one thoughtful question (what's missing/confusing/wrong/unexpected).
**Trade-offs**: Every meeting has a hidden goal and two unspoken duties — to learn and to represent your team.

## Manage Feedback: The Feedback Ranges + Intent Behind Impact + Feedback Meeting
**When**: Receiving or soliciting feedback.
**How**: Locate the feedback on two axes (verbal↔nonverbal, direct↔indirect) → subtle hints / uncensored comments / behavior changes / blunt actions. Separate substance (what they mean) from delivery (how they said it). In a feedback meeting, ask "what should I start / stop / keep doing?", apply fast, and report back.
**Trade-offs**: Negativity bias makes criticism sting; coachability (visibly applying it) is itself a signal of commitment.

## Resolve Conflicts: Fix it / Live with it / Leave it
**When**: Any persistently bad work situation.
**How**: Diagnose root cause via People / Position / Place (and the "Why?" exercise). Strip emotion with 10-10-10 (how does each option feel in 10 min / 10 months / 10 years). Then fix it tactfully, live with it, or leave graciously — choosing the path of least regret. Use the leave-it ten-question test when weighing quitting.
**Trade-offs**: The people/position/place trade-off triangle means you rarely fix all three; your last impression matters as much as your first.

## Show Your Potential: Nine-Box + Unoccupied Swimlane + Ask-for-It
**When**: Positioning for a raise or promotion.
**How**: Aim for the top-right of the performance × potential nine-box (do your job well *and* show you can do the next one). Claim an unoccupied swimlane via the five questions (what can I do / fix / bridge / know / share?). Prioritize contributions that are noticed *and* reported, and that are better (not just different). To ask for a raise: bring data, a one-pager, a specific number, and appreciation.
**Trade-offs**: Different-but-unnoticed work doesn't count; aim for better-and-visible on a mission-critical problem.
