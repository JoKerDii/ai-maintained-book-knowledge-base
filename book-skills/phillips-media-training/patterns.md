# Patterns & Techniques — The Media Training Bible

## The ATMs (answering any tough question)
**When to use**: Off-topic, hostile, or trap questions where you must answer *and* stay on message.
**How**: **A**nswer (often one word) → **T**ransition (bridge phrase) → **M**essage (+ supports) → **s**ell (optional call to action). Spend 10–20% on A+T, 80–90% on M+s. Drop the "A" when the transition can serve as the answer (false premise, guarantee).
**Trade-offs**: Don't use on every answer — overuse reads as canned/evasive.

## CUBE A (building a message)
**When to use**: Drafting or stress-testing your three core messages.
**How**: Make each message **C**onsistent (repeatable theme), **U**nburdened (≤2 commas, ≤30 words, no jargon), **B**rief (one sentence), **E**ar-worthy (speak it aloud), **A**udience-focused (their needs/values).
**Trade-offs**: Frustrating self-editing; the discipline is the point.

## The Message-Support Stool (staying on message without repeating)
**When to use**: Every interview — to answer many questions on-message without sounding repetitive.
**How**: For each of 3 messages, prepare ≥2 **stories**, ≥2 **statistics**, ≥2 **sound bites** (~21 answers). Pair a support with a message or use it alone.
**Trade-offs**: Front-loaded prep work; pays off in flexibility.

## Why + What
**When to use**: Open-ended questions ("What does your company do?").
**How**: Lead with *why your work matters* (a striking context/stat), then *what* you do.
**Trade-offs**: Slightly less direct than a normal lead — reserved for open-enders only.

## Start at the End (don't bury the lead)
**When to use**: Most answers, especially live interviews.
**How**: Open with your conclusion/most important point → add context → restate the lead.
**Trade-offs**: Cuts against the chronological storytelling instinct; requires practice.

## The Three-Second Pause
**When to use**: Print and most edited interviews.
**How**: After a question, stay quiet a few seconds, form the answer, then speak. Kills filler, sharpens focus.
**Trade-offs**: Skip in live/hostile interviews (long silence reads as evasive) — instead just let the question finish before speaking.

## The Peter Jennings Rule (unknown answers)
**When to use**: Asked something you don't know or shouldn't guess.
**How**: "That's unclear, but here's what we know…" — give value from what you *do* know.
**Trade-offs**: If pressed for the specific fact afterward, it's fine to say you don't know.

## Rewrite the Hostile Question
**When to use**: Aggressive, loaded, or insulting questions.
**How**: Silently restate the question in neutral terms; answer that. "I disagree with the premise, and am happy to share our accomplishments…"
**Trade-offs**: Don't repeat the loaded language while doing it.

## Trap-Question Playbook
**When to use**: The ten recurring trap types.
**How**: False premise → reject immediately, drop the "A." False choice → "that's like choosing between air and water." Yes/No → reframe with context, don't concede the damaging word. Guarantee → "Here's what I *can* guarantee…" Speculation → "I can't speculate, but here's what I can tell you…" Competition → "I'll let them speak for themselves, but here's why ours is a hit…" Indirect attribution → dismiss the anonymous claim, return to message.
**Trade-offs**: Zoom-out/general answers can invite more probing follow-ups — practice in advance.

## Comment Without Commenting
**When to use**: Legitimately can't answer (confidential, legal, layoffs, safety).
**How**: Explain *why* you can't, then offer what you can. Never say "no comment."
**Trade-offs**: Comes "perilously close" to no-comment — transition smoothly.

## Crisis Response Protocol
**When to use**: Any breaking crisis.
**How**: Communicate within the hour ("present in your own coverage"); focus on victims with genuine humanity; get *all* bad facts out at once (don't drip); apologize fully the first time (no "if/then"); choose the right-level spokesperson (err high); use social media within ~30 min; weigh legal vs. reputational cost.
**Trade-offs**: Speaking early with incomplete facts feels risky — state what you know, what you're doing, and when you'll update.

## Crisis Preparation Drill
**When to use**: Before any crisis, ideally yearly.
**How**: Assemble a cross-department team → brainstorm via the 8 categories → reduce to the 5 most likely-and-damaging → write a response per crisis → run staged "real-time" on-camera drills (breaking news → reputation-forming → finger-pointing).
**Trade-offs**: Time-intensive; "once you hear the thunder, it's too late to build the ark."

## Kill Verbal Filler (the 30-second object drill)
**When to use**: Habitual "uhhh," "ummm," "like," "you know."
**How**: Shout a random object, talk about it for 30 seconds with pauses but zero filler; record to verify.
**Trade-offs**: Filler only matters when it distracts — don't over-police the occasional "uh."

## The Seven-Step Prep Workflow
**When to use**: Before every interview (do a "shortcut" version under deadline).
**How**: (1) Message worksheets → (2) File cards → (3) Interview the reporter → (4) Research both sides → (5) Small Q&A (≤12 Qs) → (6) Recorded practice interview → (7) Pick 3 improvement areas.
**Trade-offs**: Over-preparation paralyzes — keep the Q&A small; reduce to trigger words.
