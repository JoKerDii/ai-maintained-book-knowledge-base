# Cheatsheet — The Media Training Bible (Brad Phillips)

## Decision rules
- **Should I do the interview?** If the story is about you and runs with or without you → **yes** (1-for-3 beats 0-for-3). Decline only if irrelevant, off-brand, or zero upside.
- **Reporter calls now?** Don't interview on the spot. Get the topic, promise a fast call-back, prep, then call **before they've written** — not just by deadline.
- **Can't answer this?** Never "no comment." Explain *why* you can't, then give what you can.
- **Tempted to say "off the record"?** Don't. Assume everything is on the record, always.
- **Asked a negative/loaded question?** Never repeat the loaded word. Reframe positively, then transition to a message.
- **Open-ended "what" question?** Use **Why + What** (lead with why it matters).
- **Any other answer?** Lead with the conclusion (start at the end), keep to ≤30 seconds, make each sentence stand alone and on-message.
- **Don't know the answer?** Peter Jennings Rule — say what you *do* know.
- **Print/edited interview?** Pause ~3 seconds before answering. **Live/hostile?** Don't pause long — just let the question finish.

## The ATMs (tough-question formula)
`Answer (1 word) → Transition → Message (+ supports) → sell (optional)`
Time split: **10–20% A+T, 80–90% M+s.** Don't use on every answer. Drop the "A" for false-premise and guarantee questions.

## Trap-question quick reference
| Trap | Move |
|---|---|
| False premise | Reject at once: "I don't agree with the premise…" then M |
| False choice (this/that) | "That's like choosing between air and water." |
| Yes/No (damaging) | Reframe with context; don't concede the bad word |
| Demands a guarantee | "Here's what I *can* guarantee…" |
| Speculation / hypothetical | "I can't speculate, but here's what I can tell you…" |
| About a competitor | "I'll let them speak for themselves, but here's why ours is a hit…" |
| Indirect attribution ("people say") | Dismiss the anonymous claim; return to message |
| Left field | Pledge to learn more / talk about what you know / zoom out |
| Don't know | Peter Jennings Rule |
| Paraphrase | Reject unless 100% accurate; don't repeat their word |

## Message thresholds & defaults
- **3 messages**, one sentence each. **≥2 stories + ≥2 statistics + ≥2 sound bites** per message (~21 answers).
- Message ≤ **30 words**, ≤ **2 commas**, concrete images, **spoken aloud** to test.
- Audiences need **7–15 exposures** to act. Avg TV quote = **7.3 sec / ~18 words**; print quote = 8–20 words.
- Answers ≤ **30 seconds**. Speak **10–15% louder** than feels natural.

## CUBE A (message checklist)
**C**onsistent · **U**nburdened (no jargon/abstraction) · **B**rief (1 sentence) · **E**ar-worthy (speak it aloud) · **A**udience-focused (their needs/values)

## Statistics: make numbers land
Give context ("122 Fenway Parks") · make it personal ($1,250/family, not $100B) · use ratios ("1 in 3") · avoid bare percentages · give relative distance ("Maine to Miami").

## Body language & voice tells
- **Energy**: you feel an 8, the room sees a 4 → go bigger. **Eye contact**: aim ~100% (not the conversational 40–60%).
- **Posture**: lean slightly forward, off the chair-back; no crossed legs/arms, no swivel chairs.
- **Voice levers**: volume, pace (slow for complex/key points), pitch (**avoid upticks**), tone, silences, diaphragmatic breathing.
- **Smell test for "off message"**: if you say "Oh, by the way…" / "Incidentally…" you've strayed.

## Attire defaults
Solids in gray/blue/earth tones · high contrast · **both sexes wear makeup** · shined shoes, long dark socks · **avoid** stripes/checks, pastels (hard fields), lapel pins, gloss/shimmer, day-of haircut. HD → subtle, smooth, spotless.

## The 10 Truths of a Crisis (if you see X, do Y)
1. You'll suffer first — contain it, don't let it "jump the fire line."
2. Cast as good/bad guy — show you "get it."
3. **Communicate within the hour** — be present in your own coverage.
4. Media side with victims — right facts + wrong tone = clueless.
5. Spokesperson choice signals scale — err high, don't always lead with the CEO.
6. Train receptionists, guards, even spouses — anyone can be the confirming source.
7. Don't bury bad facts — tear the Band-Aid off; no drip-drip.
8. Social media first — correct misinformation within ~30 min.
9. Apologize fully the first time — no "if/then" half-apology; pledge action.
10. Lawyers can deepen the crisis — weigh reputation against legal payout.

## Format fast rules
- **Phone**: stand, headset, landline, gesture, listen for typing (slow down/repeat).
- **Radio**: prepare for abrupt start, match/exceed host energy, make your own plug.
- **TV**: phone off, monitor away, you're always "on" (split screen), stay seated till clear; in a crisis **no logos** in your background.
- **Edited**: never say "as I mentioned earlier," "in conclusion," a date, or the reporter's name.
- **Skype**: the one format where you **limit** gestures (jumpy on low bandwidth).
- **Social media**: treat as a nonstop, permanent, on-the-record press conference.

## Prep ritual (7 steps)
Worksheets → file cards (trigger words) → interview the reporter → research both sides → small Q&A (≤12) → recorded practice → **pick 3 improvement areas**. Remember less to perform better.
