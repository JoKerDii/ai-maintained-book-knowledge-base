## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The ATMs** — the book's central technique for answering *any* tough question while staying on message:
- **A**nswer (often one word) → **T**ransition (bridge phrase) → **M**essage (+ supports) → **s**ell (optional call to action).
- Spend **10–20% on A+T, 80–90% on M+s**. Don't use it on every answer (looks canned). Drop the "A" for false-premise and guarantee questions. *(Ch 4)*

**CUBE A** — the five elements of every effective message: **C**onsistent · **U**nburdened (no jargon/abstraction, ≤30 words, ≤2 commas) · **B**rief (one sentence) · **E**ar-worthy (write for the ear; speak it aloud) · **A**udience-focused (their needs and values). Build exactly **3 messages**. *(Ch 2)*

**The Message-Support Stool** — for each message prepare **≥2 stories, ≥2 statistics, ≥2 sound bites** (~21 on-message answers), so you "repeat, repeat, repeat" the *themes* without ever repeating words. Give statistics context, make numbers personal, use ratios. *(Ch 2)*

**The Rule of Thirds** — a story has three voices (you, your opponent, the reporter). Stay silent and you go "0-for-3"; participate and go at least 1-for-3. Corollary: **never make your opponent's case** (don't volunteer "balance"). *(Ch 1)*

**Never say "no comment"** — it reads as "Guilty!" Instead **comment without commenting**: explain *why* you can't answer, then offer what you can. Treat *everything* — before, during, after, email, hallway, social media — as on the record. *(Ch 1, Ch 6)*

**The Seven-Second Stray** — one off-message moment in an otherwise perfect interview becomes the only quote that airs, on repeat (BP's "I'd like my life back"). Your message is your **lifeboat**; keep swimming back to it. *(Ch 4)*

**Delivery beats words** when they conflict — avoid the **message disconnect** (exciting words, flat delivery). You under-read your own energy (you feel 8, the room sees 4) → go **10–15% bigger**; aim for **~100% eye contact**; lean slightly forward; gesture naturally. *(Ch 5)*

**Answer-craft rules**: lead with your conclusion (**start at the end**); use **Why + What** for open-ended questions; replace hedges with **strong, declarative language**; never repeat **negative/loaded language** in a denial ("I'm not a crook" became the quote); **pause ~3 seconds** before answering (except live/hostile); speak to one visualized **target person**, not the reporter. *(Ch 3)*

**The 10 Truths of a Crisis** — the handling, not the event, does the most damage. Most crises are predictable, so plan and drill them. Key truths: **communicate within the hour** ("be present in your own coverage"); the **media side with victims** (right facts + wrong tone = clueless); **don't bury bad facts** (tear the Band-Aid off); **apologize fully the first time** (no "if/then" half-apology); choose the right-level spokesperson; social media first; weigh legal vs. reputational cost. *(Ch 7)*

**Prep is a ritual, not a vibe** — seven steps: message worksheets → file cards (trigger words) → interview the reporter → research both sides → small Q&A (≤12) → recorded practice → **pick 3 improvement areas**. Over-preparation paralyzes; remember less to perform better. *(Ch 8)*
