# Section 6: The Different Media Formats (Lessons 61–77)

## Core Idea
Each medium has its own rules. The core techniques (messages, ATMs, energy) carry over, but email, phone, radio, TV, press conferences, social media, and newer formats each demand specific adjustments — and each carries its own traps.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Email interviews**: Useful for existing relationships, technical detail, quick clarifications, or a legal paper trail — but don't default to them out of fear. They preclude useful follow-up, prevent relationships, and can look obstructionist ("In a written statement, Company X said…"). Watch for unintended meanings; have others read your replies.
- **Phone interviews ("phoners") — 8 tips**: Get out of your office; bring notes (as triggers, don't read); use a headset (frees hands to gesture); stand (think faster); smile (warmth carries); use a landline (audio quality + perception); listen for typing (slow down/repeat); ask the reporter to read back a quote if you fear you mangled it.
- **Radio — 7 ways to rock it**: Prepare for an abrupt/no-notice start (turn your radio off); express passion (stand, gesture, match or exceed host's energy); sit a fist's-length from the mic; connect with the host; make your own plug (don't depend on them); treat crazy callers with respect; listen to the tape afterward.
- **Television — 10 logistics**: Arrive early; bring your own makeup; mirror check; test mic/earpiece (IFB); phone fully off; turn the monitor away (delay is distracting); beware split-screen/reaction shots (you're always "on"); restrict nodding (reads as agreement); avoid/pre-plan props; stay seated until told you're clear.
- **Social Media — Six Best Practices**: Know each network's culture; Listen before talking; Participate (two-way, not a press release); use a more casual tone; Respond to negative feedback; Monitor daily/hourly.

## Key Concepts
- **Radio — 5 things bad guests do**: long answers, complex answers, being boring, being alarmist (no solutions), leaving humor behind.
- **Know Your Background (TV)**: A background can reinforce or destroy your message. In a crisis, **never display your logo** (Sarah Palin's turkey-slaughter backdrop; "Nobody heard what you said" — Reagan aide on powerful visuals drowning out negative audio).
- **Live vs. Live-to-Tape vs. Edited**: Live = no restarts but unedited; Live-to-tape = treat as live, ask if you can restart; Edited = you can restart, but they may use your worse take. In edited interviews, never say "as I mentioned earlier," "in conclusion," "today," or the reporter's name.
- **Social media is a nonstop, always-on-the-record media interview**: Treat all public communication as a press conference. "Acela Bob" (loud train phone call → leaked layoffs); "once it's out there, it's out there forever"; no real line between personal and professional accounts; 80%+ of hiring managers check social profiles.
- **Press conferences**: Test logistics; state & spell your name; coordinate co-presenters (relay-race hand-offs); maintain eye contact with the questioner. Decide statement-only (early crisis) vs. statement + Q&A (announce a time limit up front so ending isn't seen as fleeing).
- **Five additional formats**: editorial-board meetings, deskside briefings, walk-and-talks, demos, comedy shows (play it straight unless you're a comedian).

## Mental Models
- Match or slightly exceed the host's energy in audio formats — flatness reads as boredom.
- On Skype, *limit* movement — it's the one format where gesturing can hurt (low frame rate looks "jumpy").
- In a crisis, ask "How will this look if a stranger — or my critic — sees it and wants to start trouble?"

## Worked Example
**Domino's disgusting video (2009)** — Employees filmed a coworker defiling food and posted it to YouTube; it hit a million views and customers fled. President Patrick Doyle eventually issued a heartfelt 2-minute YouTube apology — credited as the first major company president to use YouTube as the *primary* crisis-response channel — but he waited **48 hours**, and crisis pros agreed the delay caused unnecessary damage. The format lesson: social media is where the crisis breaks *and* where you must respond, fast (ideally within 30 minutes), in the medium's own native form.

## Anti-patterns
- **Defaulting to email** to avoid the phone — counterproductive and can look evasive.
- **Speaker/cell phone for a phoner** — poor audio; reporter thinks "too important to pick up the phone?"
- **Long, complex, alarmist radio answers** — listeners (and hosts) tune out.
- **Ignoring your TV background** — logos in a crisis, gruesome or off-message visuals.
- **Saying "as I mentioned earlier" / "in conclusion" / a date / the reporter's name** in edited interviews.

## Key Takeaways
1. Carry the core techniques into every format, then layer the format-specific rules.
2. Phone/radio: stand, gesture, use a headset and landline, match the host's energy.
3. TV: control logistics and your background; assume you're always "on."
4. Treat all social media (and public speech) as on-the-record and permanent.
5. In edited interviews, make every answer a stand-alone unit — no back-references, dates, or names.
