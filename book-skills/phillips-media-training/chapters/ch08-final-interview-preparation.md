# Section 8: Final Interview Preparation (Lessons 93–101)

## Core Idea
A seven-step pre-interview workflow that turns the book's techniques into a repeatable prep ritual — from drafting messages to drilling on camera — and then narrows everything down to three things to focus on, because you can't hold 100 techniques in your head at once.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Seven Steps of Final Preparation**:
  1. **Complete your message worksheets** — one per message (3 total), each with ≥2 stories, ≥2 statistics, ≥2 sound bites beneath it.
  2. **Create file cards** — distill each worksheet to a single index card (key phrases, not paragraphs). Think of words like golf strokes: fewer is better. Cards keep you on point without sounding scripted.
  3. **Interview the reporter** — before hanging up on the first call, ask (selectively) the eight questions: Who are you? What's the story? Any particular angle? Who else are you interviewing? What's the format? What do you need from me? Who's doing the interview? When does it run? Also state how you want to be identified (spell your name + title).
  4. **Do your research** — research the reporter and outlet (read their past stories, note their sources and leanings), *and* research yourself/your company using the reporter's own tools (Google your name, company, and any controversies).
  5. **Develop a Q&A document** — 30 min brainstorming most-likely questions + 30 min most-damaging; cut each to ≤6; spend up to 1 hour drafting answers (use your message worksheets as the guide). Don't try to be comprehensive — over-preparation paralyzes.
  6. **Conduct a practice interview** — record it; have someone ask your Q&A plus follow-ups, open-enders, and trap questions; match the real format; don't break character; then rate yourself (praise first, then improvements; be kind to yourself).
  7. **Select three areas for improvement** — from the 20-point checklist, pick the three with the biggest impact for your next interview; add a fourth only once you've improved the first three.

## Key Concepts
- **Message worksheet**: The structured template (Message → Stories → Statistics → Sound Bites) that captures your prep from Ch 2.
- **File cards**: The on-air memory aid — radically trimmed so you sound extemporaneous, not read.
- **The 20-point self-evaluation checklist**: 11 items on *message* (e.g. "each answer contains a message or support," "I use the ATMs," "I begin with the headline," "I use 'why + what'") and 9 on *voice/body language* (vocal variety, warmth, confidence, energy, eye contact, gestures, posture, no filler, TV-friendly attire). Use it to prep *and* to grade yourself afterward.
- **The over-preparation trap**: Too much information is "a curse as damaging as too little."

## Mental Models
- "Good communication requires a process of exclusion" — keep cutting (the boss who made him halve an article three times).
- When overwhelmed, **remember less to perform better**: pick three sentences, then one trigger word per sentence (the frozen executive who transformed once she stopped trying to recall a 10-page Q&A).
- Skills atrophy without use — rehearse in daily life: try "why + what" with a seatmate, the 12-year-old-nephew rule with cousins, pausing in meetings, avoiding seven-second strays with your spouse.

## Worked Example
**The over-prepared executive** — A top executive's team handed her a 10-page Q&A document before a major launch. She studied it thoroughly, then *froze* on camera, drowning in detail. Phillips asked her to identify the three most important sentences, then choose one trigger word in each. When the camera came back on she was "a different person — confident, well versed, and in control." The principle that closes the book: reduce 100+ techniques to three focus areas (Step 7), then build up one at a time. And do *every* interview you can, no matter how small ("Internet radio show with 200 listeners? Excellent") — small stages are low-risk practice.

## Anti-patterns
- **Interviewing on the spot when the reporter first calls** — buy time to prep (echoes Ch 1, Lesson 2).
- **Building a giant Q&A and memorizing it** — paralysis; reporters ask what you didn't anticipate anyway.
- **Reading file cards verbatim** — sounds scripted; use them as triggers only.
- **Trying to fix all 20 checklist items at once** — focus on three.
- **Skipping practice because of a tight deadline** — at least do the "shortcut" version.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run the seven steps before every interview; do a "shortcut" version even under deadline.
2. Distill messages → worksheets → file cards → trigger words. Less is more.
3. "Interview the reporter" and research both sides before you talk.
4. Build a *small* Q&A (≤12 questions) and rehearse on camera in the real format.
5. Pick three improvement areas at a time; practice skills in everyday life so they don't atrophy.
