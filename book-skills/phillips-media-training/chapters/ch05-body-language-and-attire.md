# Section 5: Body Language and Attire Guide (Lessons 48–60)

## Core Idea
The audience judges *how* you look and sound before they process *what* you say. Energy, eye contact, gestures, posture, and voice can amplify a great message — or a "message disconnect" can sink it. Dress and groom to disappear, so nothing distracts from your eyes and words.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Why Body Language Matters / Message Disconnect**: When words and body contradict, the audience believes the body. Saying "this is exciting" in a monotone tells the audience it isn't.
  - Mehrabian's study (emotional 1-on-1s): words 7%, tone 38%, body 55% — *not* literally applicable to media, but emblematic: delivery can override content.
- **Energy**: We badly misjudge our own energy (you feel an 8, the room sees a 4). TV mutes you. Speak **10–15% louder**; "passionate need not be loud," but go bigger than feels natural.
- **Eye Contact**: Aim for ~**100%** (everyday conversation is only 40–60%, which on camera reads as nervous/untrustworthy). Where to look depends on format.
- **Gestures**: Gesture naturally — it grabs attention, aids retention, and *improves your words*. Keep hands "unlocked"; avoid closed positions (crossed arms, clasped hands, hands in pockets).
- **Posture**: Lean slightly forward, behind off the chair-back, feet planted. Small shifts produce higher energy, more volume, more natural gestures. Beware swivel chairs and leg-crossing.
- **Voice (6 levers)**: Volume, Pace (~150–160 wpm; slow down for complex/key points), Pitch (avoid "upticks" that sound like seeking permission), Tone, Silences (a 1-second pause adds drama), Diaphragmatic breathing (stomach out on inhale → fuller, less nasal voice).

## Key Concepts
- **Three TV interview eye-contact formats**: (1) *Bites* — look at the interviewer, ignore the camera; (2) *On-set* — lock eyes with whoever's speaking; (3) *Remote* — stare into the lens the whole time (it's your "target person"). If unsure, ask the producer.
- **Verbal filler ("uhhh," "ummm," "like," "you know")**: Only a problem when it distracts. Kill it by pausing before answering, and by the "shout an object, talk 30 seconds with no filler" drill (record yourself to verify).
- **Be true to your brand / look like yourself**: Dress congruent with your brand, with only minor adjustments, so you can forget your appearance and radiate confidence.

## Mental Models
- Think of hair as a *frame* around your face — flatter without drawing attention (¾-inch clearance around the eyes).
- "If something outshines you, take it off" (Lillian Brown's mirror test, eyes half-closed).
- Overcoming fear: "It's not about you, it's about them" — serve the audience; you're a bad judge of your own nervousness (they don't see your pounding heart).

## Reference Tables
**Attire quick rules**
| Do | Avoid |
|---|---|
| Solid colors; grays, blues, earth tones | Stripes, polka dots, checks (shimmer on camera) |
| High color contrast (sharper, more authoritative) | Monochromatic looks; pastels in hard-edged fields |
| Long dark socks; shined shoes; well-fitted collar | Noisy/shiny accessories; lapel pins; red/black/white on non-HD |
| Bring & apply makeup (both sexes); powder for shine | Moisturizer/gloss/shimmer; smoky eyes; fresh haircut day-of |
| In HD: subtle, smooth, spotless | Bright colors & textures (exaggerated in HD) |

## Worked Example
**Overcoming fear — seven techniques** Phillips gives clients: (1) Practice — the #1 fear-reducer; do mock interviews and start with small outlets. (2) You don't have to be perfect — focus on doing the big things well. (3) Just because you feel it doesn't mean they see it. (4) It's not about you — focus on the audience's needs. (5) Stay in the moment — self-flagellate *after*, never during. (6) Long, deep breaths — exhale fully, inhale through the nose, hold, release; repeat 10–12×. (7) Progressive muscle relaxation — flex then release each muscle group head-to-toe. Caroline Kennedy's failed Senate bid ("you know" 168 times in 30 minutes) is the cautionary tale for ignoring filler.

## Anti-patterns
- **Message disconnect**: Exciting words, flat delivery.
- **Slumping back / crossed legs / swivel chairs**: Reads as arrogant, uninterested; kills energy.
- **Closed body language**: Crossed arms, clasped/hidden hands.
- **Refusing makeup** (Nixon, 1960 debate): TV viewers thought he lost; radio listeners thought he won.
- **Vocal upticks**: Make statements sound like requests, eroding credibility.

## Key Takeaways
1. Believe the body over the words — eliminate message disconnects.
2. Go 10–15% bigger on energy and volume; you're under-reading yourself.
3. Aim for ~100% eye contact, matched to the interview format.
4. Gesture naturally and lean slightly forward; both improve your words.
5. Dress and groom to be invisible — solids, subtle, spotless, "look like yourself."
