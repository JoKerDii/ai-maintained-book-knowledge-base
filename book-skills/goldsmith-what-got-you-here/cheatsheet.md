# Cheatsheet — Decision Rules & Diagnostics

## Decision Rules
- **When you're about to correct someone's idea, do nothing, because** you'll improve the idea 5% and cut their commitment 50% (fallacy of added value, #2).
- **When you're about to speak, run the four gates first — will this help our customers / our company / the person I'm talking to / the person I'm talking about? — because** if it fails all four, being right is worthless.
- **When the question is "is it true?", switch to "is it worth it?", because** roughly half of what you were going to say is accurate and still not worth the damage.
- **When you're diagnosing a problem in yourself, don't — get feedback, because** self-diagnosers see cancer where a professional sees a muscle pull.
- **When a behavior seems tied to your success, ask "because of or in spite of?", because** the superstition trap is what keeps successful people from changing.
- **When you want to change more than one thing, pick one, because** one gun, one bullet; multiple simultaneous changes is a personality makeover, not improvement.
- **When you can't decide whether to be nicer or stop being a jerk, stop being a jerk, because** omission is cheap and commission requires filling an entire box.
- **When you apologize, stop talking, because** every added word converts the apology into a defense.
- **When you've improved but no one has noticed, advertise harder, because** you must get 100% better to earn 10% credit; you're in the dumb phase.
- **When someone gives you feedback or a suggestion, say only "thank you", because** it's the one response that cannot be argued with or escalated.
- **When you want ideas without defensiveness, ask for two future suggestions (feedforward), not a critique, because** people accept ideas about the future and reject verdicts on the past.
- **When you're deciding whether to keep coaching someone, stop if** they don't think they have a problem, they're in the wrong job, or they think everyone else is the problem. Test: "if we shut down today — surprised, sad, or relieved?"
- **When you're 70 and being corrected for the ten-thousandth time, let it go** — and grant the same to others.

## Diagnostic: Symptom → Habit → Fix
| What you observe | Likely habit | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Debates trivia; must be right about restaurant choice | #1 Winning too much | Ask "is winning this worth it?" Concede on the trivial. |
| "Great idea, but here's how to make it better" | #2 Adding too much value | Say "thank you" and stop. Let them own it. |
| Rates every contribution good/bad | #3 Passing judgment | Receive input with a neutral "thank you." |
| Sarcasm that "makes you sound sharp" | #4 Destructive comments | Monetize it: $10 a shot. |
| Sentences opening "No…" "But…" "However…" | #5 No/But/However | Fine yourself per instance; substitute a pause. |
| Name-drops, corrects others' facts, fakes prior knowledge | #6 Telling the world how smart we are | Say "I didn't know that. Thank you." |
| Uses temper as a lever | #7 Speaking when angry | Silence is the only reliable strategy; "no one is in the other boat." |
| Unrequested "let me explain why that won't work" | #8 Negativity | Wait to be asked. |
| Hoards data; people learn things last | #9 Withholding information | Default to over-sharing; audit who wasn't copied. |
| Never praises; "I expected great work" | #10 Failing to give recognition | Twice-weekly name-list review; recognize or do nothing. |
| Retells shared wins in the first person | #11 Claiming credit | List a win's contributors; publicly reassign credit. |
| "That's just how I am" / "I'm impatient" | #12 Making excuses | Ban the phrase; treat the trait as behavior, not fate. |
| Blames upbringing, prior boss, history | #13 Clinging to the past | Redirect every past sentence to a future one. |
| Rewards flattery; has an inner circle | #14 Playing favorites | Rank reports by contribution vs. by adoration; compare. |
| Never says sorry | #15 Refusing to express regret | "I'm sorry. I'll try to do better." Then nothing. |
| Checks phone, finishes sentences, interrupts | #16 Not listening | Count to fifty; make them the most important person in the room. |
| Treats help as expected | #17 Failing to express gratitude | The 25-names note-writing drill. |
| Snaps at whoever brings bad news | #18 Punishing the messenger | Thank the messenger before responding to the message. |
| "Marketing dropped the ball" | #19 Passing the buck | Own it first, publicly, then fix it. |
| "That's just who I am" as a virtue | #20 Excessive need to be "me" | Ask whether the trait is a value or a habit wearing a value's costume. |
| Hits every target while the team burns out or bends rules | #21 Goal obsession | Ask what the goal was *for*. Check for time pressure, hurry, "it's important," dependents. |

## Thresholds & Defaults Goldsmith Commits To
| Item | Default |
|---|---|
| Behaviors to change at once | **1** |
| Feedback raters | ~8–15 (avg 15), all passing the Four Commitments |
| Follow-up cadence | **every month or so** |
| Total change horizon | **12–18 months** |
| Feedforward ask | exactly **2** suggestions, future only |
| Fine per offense when monetizing | **$10–$20** |
| Expected non-actors among sincere intenders | **~30%** |
| Credit earned for improvement | 100% better → ~10% credit |

## Trade-offs
| Choice | Take the left when | Take the right when |
|---|---|---|
| Feedback vs. Feedforward | You need to know *what* is wrong | You need ideas for *how* to fix it |
| Being right vs. being effective | Facts materially change the outcome | It's a preference, a detail, or someone's idea |
| Coaching vs. letting them go | They own the problem and want to change | They deny it, are in the wrong job, or blame everyone else |
| Skill training vs. behavioral change | The gap is competence | The gap is how people experience you |
| Doing something vs. doing nothing | The act helps someone besides you | The act mainly proves you're smart |

## Tells & Smells (a habit is firing)
- You start a sentence with "No," "But," or "However" — you've already said "I'm right, you're wrong."
- "Great idea — and you could also…" — #2 in progress.
- "I already knew that." — #6.
- "That's just the way I am." — #12/#20.
- "I don't suffer fools gladly." — the classic defense for #16.
- You're re-explaining an apology — you've undone it.
- You feel you're "too busy" to follow up — you're in the 30%.
- You're winning an argument with your own direct report — it isn't a fair fight.
- You hit the number and the team is quietly leaving — #21.
