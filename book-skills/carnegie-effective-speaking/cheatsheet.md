# Cheatsheet: Decision Rules & Heuristics

## Stage fright
- When your pulse races before speaking, **don't be alarmed** — a limited amount of fear makes you think faster and speak more intensely.
- When you feel unready, **prepare more** — "only the prepared speaker deserves to be confident." Preparation, not willpower, dissolves fear.
- When nerves peak just before your turn, **put attention on the other speakers**, not yourself.
- Right before speaking: **breathe deeply 30 seconds, stand to full height, meet eyes** "as if every one of them owed you money."
- When fear persists, **act brave until you become brave** (James/Roosevelt) — feeling follows action.

## Preparation
- If tempted to memorize word-for-word, **don't** — the mind goes "not just blank but black" (Churchill, Bushnell). Think in ideas, not words.
- If tempted to grab a magazine article, **don't** — give "ore from your own mine." Speak only on what you've earned the right to say.
- Choose your topic **early** so the subconscious incubates it.
- Gather **10–100x** more material than you'll use; assemble 100 thoughts, discard 90.
- Test topic fitness: **if someone opposed you and you'd leap to defend it, it's the right subject.**

## Choosing a topic (six fertile areas)
Early years/upbringing · early struggles to get ahead · hobbies/recreation · special knowledge · unusual experiences · beliefs & convictions (only with specific instances). **Vividness test**: if it still stands out in memory after years, it will interest an audience.

## Limiting scope
| Talk length | Max main points |
|---|---|
| < 5 min (short) | 1–2 |
| up to 30 min (longer) | 4–5 |
- When you're covering ground fast, stop — if listeners can't say "I know where he is now," you've lost them.

## Opening
- Open in the **first sentence**, not the second or third — leap into the incident.
- **Never** open with an apology, a canned joke, or a stunt.
- Prefer: incident · suspense · arresting fact · show-of-hands · self-interest promise · exhibit.
- Test it across the **dinner table** — if it's not conversational there, it won't be for an audience.

## Delivery
- If you sound stilted, tell yourself **"Wake up! Be human,"** then pick one person (the least attentive) and converse with only them.
- **Don't imitate** — your individuality is "your only real claim to importance."
- Work on voice/pace/pitch **only in practice** — fussing with technique before an audience is "fatal."
- Force beats polish: "a tallow candle with powder behind it" outperforms a "steel-jacketed talk with no force."

## Matching purpose to occasion
| Purpose | Structure |
|---|---|
| Get action | Magic Formula: Example → Point → Reason |
| Inform | Restrict · sequence · enumerate · compare · visualize |
| Convince/impress | Yes-response · common ground · enthusiasm · begin friendly |
| Entertain | (lightest touch; humor on yourself) |
- **Analyze audience and occasion first**, then choose one purpose (the congressman who informed at a political rally was hooted off stage).

## The Magic Formula (short action talk, ~2 min)
- Example ≈ **75%** of time; Point ≈ **20 seconds**; Reason ≈ a sentence.
- Lead with the story, **not** the demand ("I'm here to collect five dollars" → scramble for exits).
- Point must be **specific, overt, easy**: "Visit your grandparents this weekend," not "think of your grandparents."
- Give **one** reason, relevant to the example — like an ad, never sell two ideas.

## Persuading / convincing
- **Never** open with "I'm going to prove..." — it invites "Let's see you do it."
- Open on **common ground**; stack **yes-responses** (a "no" mobilizes the whole body into rejection).
- Enter beliefs "arm in arm with the owner" — frontal attack "always ends in a draw."
- Liken your proposal to **what they already believe** (Paley, Paul on Mars Hill).
- Stir **emotion over cold ideas**; "if you would impress an audience, be impressed yourself."

## Clarity (informative talk)
- Aim at the **least intelligent-looking person** or a child (Beveridge).
- **Define keystone words before using them**; go simple → complex.
- Turn facts into **pictures** (Alaska = 18 states; "white as coconut meat").
- Eye beats ear ~**25 to 1** — use visual aids; keep exhibits hidden till needed, big enough for the back row, never passed around.

## Closing
- **Plan the ending in advance.** Summarize, then ask for specific, achievable, easy action.
- **Never** trail off ("that's about all I have to say") or circle endlessly.

## Impromptu
- **Don't apologize** for being unprepared — it's expected. Launch in at once.
- **Get into an example immediately** — kills jitters, buys thinking time, grabs attention.
- Draw on the **here-and-now**: audience · occasion · prior speakers.
- Group around **one central point**, speak with animation, then **sit down**.

## Introductions & awards
- Introduction = "sell the topic and sell the speaker" in under a minute (T-I-S).
- Deliver the name **Pause–Part–Punch**, facing the audience till the last syllable.
- Awards: **be brief and sincere; avoid all exaggeration** ("greatest day of my life" takes in too much territory).

## Persist
- Expect **plateaus** — the weak quit, the gritty break through "almost overnight."
- "Never undertake anything thinking defeat" — belief drives the actions that produce success.
