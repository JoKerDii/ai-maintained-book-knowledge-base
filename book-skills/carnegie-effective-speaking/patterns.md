# Patterns: Techniques & Methods

## The Four Guideposts (start the journey right)
**When to use**: beginning speaking training and whenever motivation flags.
**How**: Take heart from others' experience; keep sight of your goal (vividly picture success and its rewards); predetermine your mind to success (burn mental escape routes); seize every opportunity to practice.
**Trade-offs**: attitude-setting, not technique — must be paired with actual speaking.

## The Four Rules for Confidence
**When to use**: to conquer stage fright.
**How**: get the facts about fear (it's normal and partly useful); prepare properly (assemble/arrange ideas, never memorize); predetermine your mind to success (lose yourself in the subject, avoid negative stimuli, give yourself a pep talk); act confident (deep breath, stand tall, meet eyes).
**Trade-offs**: acting confident fails without real preparation behind it.

## Proper Preparation Method
**When to use**: preparing any talk.
**How**: search your background for significant experience; brood over the topic until "mellow and expansive"; jot ideas on scraps of paper; arrange them; rehearse by "talking it out" in real conversations.
**Trade-offs**: slower than grabbing an article, but produces authentic material; never write out word-for-word (freezes thought, invites memorization).

## The Three Cardinal Rules (quick & easy speaking)
**When to use**: choosing a topic and setting attitude.
**How**: speak about what you've earned the right to talk about; be excited about your subject (counter-argument test); be eager to share it with listeners.
**Trade-offs**: limits topics to your genuine experience — but that's the point.

## T-I-S Formula (introducing a speaker)
**When to use**: introducing any speaker, under ~1 minute.
**How**: Topic (exact title) → Importance (bridge to the group's interests) → Speaker (relevant qualifications, then the name). Deliver the name with Pause–Part–Punch, facing the audience until the last syllable.
**Trade-offs**: keep conversational; never memorize or predict the speaker's content.

## Presentation & Acceptance Formulas (awards)
**When to use**: giving or receiving an award.
**How (present)**: why the award is made → group's interest in the recipient → how deserved and warmly felt → congratulate. **How (accept)**: sincere thanks → credit others → what it means to you → thanks again.
**Trade-offs**: brevity and sincerity essential; avoid all exaggeration.

## Six Ways to Open a Talk
**When to use**: capturing favorable attention in the first sentence.
**How**: begin with an incident/story; arouse suspense; state an arresting fact; ask for a show of hands; promise how to get what they want; use an exhibit. Combine as needed; test across the dinner table.
**Trade-offs**: avoid apology openings, "funny" story openings, and over-dramatic stunts.

## Five Ways to Support Main Ideas
**When to use**: giving substance to the few points of a longer talk.
**How**: statistics (made vivid by familiar comparison); expert testimony (vet with the four-part test); analogies; demonstrations; incidents/examples.
**Trade-offs**: keep points to three or four; support material — not the points — creates interest.

## Ways to Close a Talk
**When to use**: ending a longer talk to get action.
**How**: summarize the main points; ask for specific, achievable, easy action (pass a pre-written letter and pen). Plan the ending in advance.
**Trade-offs**: never trail off ("that's about all...") or circle endlessly.

## Five Ways to Use Illustration (Humanize, Personalize, Specify, Dramatize, Visualize)
**When to use**: filling any talk with attention-holding material.
**How**: tell human-interest stories; use names; apply the 5-W formula for detail; use dialogue; demonstrate physically.
**Trade-offs**: over-cluttering with irrelevant detail invites the "blue pencil of inattentiveness."

## Reserve Power
**When to use**: preparing to speak with authority.
**How**: choose the topic early; gather 10–100x more material than you'll use; ask probing questions ("Why do I believe this? When did I see it?"); discard 90 of 100 thoughts.
**Trade-offs**: labor-intensive, but yields authority and emergency-readiness.

## Five Keys to Delivery
**When to use**: standing up to speak.
**How**: crash through self-consciousness; be yourself (don't imitate); converse with the audience (pick one person); put your heart into it; practice voice/pace only away from the audience.
**Trade-offs**: technique before an audience is "fatal"; naturalness beats polish.

## Five Ways to Share the Talk (build rapport)
**When to use**: with every audience.
**How**: talk in terms of their interests; give sincere, specific appreciation; identify with them (names, "you"/"we"); make them partners (participation); play yourself down.
**Trade-offs**: hollow flattery and any hint of superiority backfire.

## The Magic Formula (Example–Point–Reason)
**When to use**: short talk (or letter/request) to get action.
**How**: Example (vivid personal incident, ~75% of time) → Point (specific, easy, overt action, ~20 sec) → Reason (one relevant benefit). Reverses the newspaper technique for suspense.
**Trade-offs**: only for action talks; would bore if used merely to inform.

## Five Rules for the Talk to Inform
**When to use**: explaining, instructing, reporting.
**How**: restrict the subject to fit the time; sequence ideas (time/space/topical); enumerate points; compare strange with familiar (turn facts into pictures); use visual aids; define keystone words.
**Trade-offs**: covering less ground more clearly beats covering everything sketchily.

## Five Rules for the Talk to Convince
**When to use**: winning agreement.
**How**: win confidence by deserving it (character); get yes-responses from common ground; speak with contagious enthusiasm; show respect/affection; begin friendly (liken your proposal to what they already believe).
**Trade-offs**: slower than frontal argument, but frontal attack "always ends in a draw."

## Six Rules for Impromptu Speaking
**When to use**: called on without warning.
**How**: practice (slip game, linkage story); be mentally ready (rehearse "what would I say now?"); get into an example immediately; speak with animation and force; use the here-and-now (audience/occasion/prior speakers); give a real talk grouped around one central point.
**Trade-offs**: don't apologize or ramble; keep it brief, then sit down.
