## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The big shift**: negotiation is emotional and psychological, not rational. People are "crazy, irrational, emotionally driven animals." Influence the emotional mind (System 1) and the rational mind (System 2) follows. The goal is *information-gathering + behavior-influencing*, done through listening — not winning arguments. The classic *Getting to Yes* / BATNA / "rational actor" model is treated as the thing to beat.

**Tactical Empathy** (the centerpiece) — recognize the counterpart's perspective *and* what's behind it, then vocalize that recognition. Empathy ≠ agreement. It's "listening as a martial art." All the tactics below serve it.

**The voice** — default to the *positive/playful* voice (smile, audible on the phone). Use the *late-night FM DJ voice* (deep, slow, downward inflection) selectively for calm authority. Avoid the *direct/assertive* voice — it triggers pushback.

**Mirroring** — repeat the last 1–3 words with an inquisitive tone, then go silent. Keeps them talking, builds rapport, lets you disagree without being disagreeable.

**Labeling** — name an emotion: "It seems like… / It sounds like… / It looks like…" (never start with "I"). Labeling negatives defuses them; labeling positives reinforces them. Always follow with silence.

**Accusation Audit** — say the worst things they could think about you *first*, to take the sting out.

**Master "No," beware "Yes"** — "No" is the start of the negotiation; it gives safety, autonomy, and control. Trigger it ("Is now a bad time to talk?"). Distinguish counterfeit / confirmation / commitment "Yes." Use the "No"-oriented email ("Have you given up on this project?") to revive ignored threads.

**"That's right" > "Yes"** — trigger it with a **summary** (paraphrase + label) that reflects "the world according to them." "You're right" is a brush-off meaning nothing changes.

**Calibrated Questions** — open-ended "How"/"What" questions (rarely "Why") that give the *illusion of control* while you frame the conversation. The greatest: "How am I supposed to do that?" Avoid closed verbs (can/is/are/do/does). Useless without emotional self-control — bite your tongue when attacked and ask a question instead.

**Bend their reality** — never compromise ("no deal is better than a bad deal"; splitting = mismatched shoes). Anchor emotions low with an accusation audit, then trigger **loss aversion** (make them feel they'll *lose* something). Let them anchor price first when you lack market info; otherwise use a **bolstering range**. Use **odd, non-round numbers**. Pivot to **nonmonetary terms**. Handle the word "Fair" deliberately. Deadlines are usually imaginary and cut both ways.

**Guarantee execution** — "Yes is nothing without How." Make them articulate implementation in their own words. Watch for **behind-the-table deal-killers**. Verify commitment with the **Rule of Three**. Read the **7-38-55 rule** (words/tone/body) and **pronouns** (lots of "I" = low power).

**Bargain hard** — identify the counterpart's type (**Analyst / Accommodator / Assertive**) and treat them as *they* need (the Black Swan rule, not the Golden Rule). Run the **Ackerman model**: target → 65% → 85% → 95% → 100%, decreasing raises, non-round final number, + a token non-cash item. The adversary is the situation, not the person.

**Find the Black Swan** — assume 3–5 hidden, game-changing facts (*unknown unknowns*) per negotiation. They multiply your **positive / negative / normative leverage**. Find them via face time, unguarded moments, the similarity principle, and understanding the counterpart's "religion" (worldview). When someone seems "crazy," they're ill-informed, constrained, or have hidden interests.

**Prepare a One Sheet** (Appendix) — Goal · Summary · Labels/Accusation Audit · Calibrated Questions · Non-cash offers. "You fall to your highest level of preparation."

## Scope & Limits

For domain-specific deals (legal, M&A, hostage situations) combine with professional expertise. The book's research citations (Kahneman, Mehrabian's 7-38-55, etc.) are summarized as Voss presents them.
