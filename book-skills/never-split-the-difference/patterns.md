# Patterns & Techniques — Never Split the Difference

## Mirroring
**When to use**: build rapport, keep them talking, buy time, disagree without being disagreeable.
**How**: repeat the last 1–3 (or critical) words with an inquisitive upward inflection → then stay silent. Sequence: FM DJ voice → "I'm sorry…" → mirror → silence (≥4s) → repeat.
**Trade-offs**: feels awkward at first; stepping on it with a follow-up question kills its power.

## Labeling
**When to use**: defuse a negative emotion or reinforce a positive one; whenever the counterpart is tense.
**How**: detect the emotion, then "It seems like… / It sounds like… / It looks like…" (never "I…"), then go silent. If they reject it: "I didn't say that's what it was. I just said it seems like that."
**Trade-offs**: must be neutral; "I"-framing raises guard.

## Accusation Audit
**When to use**: before a confrontation likely to surface accusations against you.
**How**: list the worst things they could say → convert to anticipatory labels → role-play pacing/pauses → voice them first in the meeting.
**Trade-offs**: feels self-loathing; said aloud, accusations sound exaggerated and the counterpart often denies them.

## Triggering "No"
**When to use**: openings, stalls, unresponsive counterparts.
**How**: "Is now a bad time to talk?"; ask what they *don't* want; mislabel to provoke correction ("It seems like you want this project to fail"); the "No"-oriented email "Have you given up on this project?"
**Trade-offs**: counterintuitive; goes against the "be nice / get to yes" instinct.

## Summary → "That's right"
**When to use**: break deadlock; confirm genuine buy-in.
**How**: paraphrase + label = summary. Reflect "the world according to them" fully enough that the only reply is "That's right." (If you get "You're right," you've failed — no buy-in.)
**Trade-offs**: requires real listening; slow but durable.

## Calibrated "How"/"What" Questions
**When to use**: nudge without pushiness, gather info, defuse confrontation, force empathy, guarantee execution.
**How**: avoid closed verbs (can/is/are/do/does). Start with What/How (rarely Why). Go-tos: "What's the biggest challenge you face?", "How am I supposed to do that?", "What are we trying to accomplish here?", "How will we know we're on track?"
**Trade-offs**: useless without emotional self-control; "Why" is accusatory unless aimed to serve you.

## Multi-step "No" (bidding against themselves)
**When to use**: bargaining, getting concessions without counteroffering.
**How**: (1) "How am I supposed to do that?" → (2) "Your offer is very generous, I'm sorry, that just doesn't work for me" → (3) "I'm sorry, I just can't do that" → (4) "I'm sorry, no" → (5) "No."
**Trade-offs**: best when the counterpart has no other buyer.

## Emotional Anchoring + Loss Aversion
**When to use**: before delivering a lowball or unwelcome offer.
**How**: accusation audit to anchor expectations low ("you're going to think I'm a lousy businessman…"), then frame the offer as something they'll *lose* to someone else.
**Trade-offs**: manipulative if misused; pairs with empathy.

## Anchoring, Ranges & Odd Numbers
**When to use**: price discussions.
**How**: usually let them anchor first (you lack market info). When you must speak, use a bolstering range (low end = your goal). Use precise non-round numbers ($37,263) for credibility. Surprise with an unrelated gift to trigger reciprocity.
**Trade-offs**: brace for an extreme anchor from a pro; never open if you don't know market value.

## Ackerman Bargaining
**When to use**: brass-tacks haggling toward a target price.
**How**: target → first offer 65% → raises to 85%, 95%, 100% (decreasing increments) → empathy + many "No"s to make them counter before each raise → non-round final number → toss in a token non-cash item at the limit.
**Trade-offs**: mechanical but powerful; the diminishing raises make them feel they squeezed you dry.

## Reading People (verify & detect)
**When to use**: testing commitment and honesty.
**How**: Rule of Three (agree 3× via varied tactics); 7-38-55 (watch tone/body vs. words, label incongruence); pronoun analysis (lots of "I" = low power; "we/they" = real decider); Pinocchio Effect (liars use more words / third-person).
**Trade-offs**: heuristics, not proof; confirm with follow-up.

## Black Swan Hunting
**When to use**: high-stakes or stuck negotiations; whenever the counterpart seems "crazy."
**How**: get face time; mine unguarded moments (start/end of meetings, interruptions); understand their "religion"/worldview; use the similarity principle; diagnose "crazy" as ill-informed / constrained / hidden interests; deploy positive, negative (sparingly), and normative leverage.
**Trade-offs**: hard to find over email; threats (negative leverage) leave toxic residue.

## Three Negotiator Types (the Black Swan rule: treat them as they need)
**Analyst** — prepared data, warn early, give silence/time; apologies have little value.
**Accommodator** — be sociable but use calibrated questions to convert talk into action; uncover hidden objections.
**Assertive** — let them be heard first; mirrors, labels, summaries; aim for "that's right."

## The Negotiation One Sheet (preparation)
**When to use**: before any significant negotiation (≈7:1 return on time).
**How**: 5 sections — (I) optimistic high-end Goal; (II) Summary the counterpart will answer "That's right" to; (III) 3–5 labels / accusation audit; (IV) 3–5 calibrated questions (incl. behind-the-table & deal-killer questions); (V) non-cash offers.
**Trade-offs**: don't over-script; stay agile.
