# Cheatsheet — Never Split the Difference

## Decision rules (if → then → because)
- **If you're about to compromise** → don't; hold out or walk → "No deal is better than a bad deal"; splitting = one black + one brown shoe.
- **If you want a "Yes" fast** → ask a "No"-oriented question instead → pushing for "Yes" makes people defensive.
- **If you hear "You're right" / "I'll try"** → keep going with calibrated "How" questions → no buy-in; nothing will get implemented.
- **If you hear "That's right"** → you've reached a breakthrough; advance → they've embraced your framing as their own.
- **If they drop "Fair" on you** ("We just want what's fair") → don't concede; say "I apologize — show me where I treated you unfairly" or mirror "Fair?" → it's a destabilizing move.
- **If you're attacked/insulted** → pause, bite your tongue, ask a calibrated question → counterattacking triggers a showdown.
- **If you don't know market value** → let them anchor first → their opening may beat your target.
- **If pushed to name a price first** → cite a high number "someone else" charges, or a bolstering range → avoids over-anchoring yourself low.
- **If the deal seems done but only with the people on the call** → ask "How does this affect the rest of your team?" → behind-the-table players kill more deals than table players make.
- **If the counterpart seems "crazy"** → assume ill-informed, constrained, or hidden interest → and dig for the Black Swan.
- **If a deadline is pressuring you** → remember it's usually imaginary and cuts both ways → revealing yours often gets a better deal.

## Voice selection
| Tone | When | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Positive/playful (smile) | **default** | mental agility, collaboration |
| Late-night FM DJ (downward) | selectively, to make a point | calm authority, "I've got it covered" |
| Direct/assertive | rarely | triggers pushback — avoid |

## The core verbal toolkit
| Tool | Opener | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Mirror | (repeat last 1–3 words)? | elaborate, build rapport |
| Label | "It seems like…" | defuse/reinforce emotion |
| Calibrated Q | "How…/What…" | illusion of control, info |
| Summary | label + paraphrase | trigger "That's right" |
| Accusation audit | "You'll probably think I'm…" | pre-empt negatives |

## "No" without the word (in order)
1. "How am I supposed to do that?"
2. "Your offer is very generous, I'm sorry, that just doesn't work for me."
3. "I'm sorry, I just can't do that."
4. "I'm sorry, no." → 5. "No."

## Ackerman numbers (memorize)
**65 → 85 → 95 → 100%** of target · decreasing raises · **non-round** final number · + a token non-cash item at the limit.

## Three leverage types
- **Positive** — give/withhold what they want ("I want…").
- **Negative** — make them suffer (use sparingly; label, don't shove).
- **Normative** — use their own norms/beliefs; expose hypocrisy.

## Negotiator types & their silence
| Type | Time means | Silence means | Approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Analyst | preparation | thinking | data, warn early, give time |
| Accommodator | relationship | anger | sociable + calibrated questions for action |
| Assertive | money | "talk more" | let them be heard; mirrors, "that's right" |
→ **Black Swan rule**: treat them as *they* need, not as you'd want.

## Tells & smells
- Lots of "I/me/my" → low decision power; "we/they/them" → the real decider.
- More words + third-person pronouns → likely lying (Pinocchio Effect).
- Tone/body ≠ words (7-38-55) → label the incongruence.
- "You're right" / "I'll try" → not bought in.
- Specific, escalating threats → real consequences approaching; vague threats → early stage.
- Any response that isn't outright rejection → you have the edge.

## Go-to calibrated questions
- "What is the biggest challenge you face?"
- "What are we trying to accomplish here?"
- "How am I supposed to do that?"
- "How will we know we're on track?" / "…if we're off track?"
- "How on board are the people not on this call?"

## One Sheet (prepare every time — ~7:1 ROI)
**I** high-end Goal · **II** Summary (→"That's right") · **III** 3–5 labels/accusation audit · **IV** 3–5 calibrated questions · **V** non-cash offers.
