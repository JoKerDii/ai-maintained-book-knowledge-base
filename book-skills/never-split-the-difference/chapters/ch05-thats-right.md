# Chapter 5: Trigger the Two Words That Transform Any Negotiation

## Core Idea
The breakthrough phrase is **"That's right"** — not "Yes." Trigger it with a summary (paraphrase + label) that makes the counterpart feel fully understood. That sense of unconditional positive regard opens the door to real behavioral change. Beware "You're right," which means nothing changes.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Summary → "That's right"**: reflect "the world according to [them]" so completely that their only response is "That's right."
  - When to use: to break deadlock, shift a perspective, or confirm genuine buy-in.
  - How: **paraphrasing + labeling = summary**. Rearticulate the meaning *and* acknowledge the underlying emotion.
- **Behavioral Change Stairway Model (BCSM)**: five stages — active listening → empathy → rapport → influence → behavioral change. You can't skip steps.
- **Active-listening arsenal** (the toolkit for a "That's right"): Effective Pauses, Minimal Encouragers ("uh-huh," "I see"), Mirroring, Labeling, Paraphrasing, Summarizing.

## Key Concepts
- **Unconditional positive regard** (Carl Rogers): accepting a person as they are creates the safety needed for change.
- **"That's right" vs. "You're right"**: "That's right" = genuine epiphany and buy-in; "You're right" = a brush-off to make you go away (no ownership, no change).
- **Subtle epiphany**: the "That's right" moment is invisible to the counterpart; they embrace your framing as their own.

## Mental Models
- "Yes is nothing without How" — and "That's right" beats "Yes."
- "Sleeping in the same bed and dreaming different dreams": rapport-niceties aren't real understanding.
- A good summary is identifying, rearticulating, and emotionally affirming the counterpart's whole worldview.

## Anti-patterns
- **Chasing "Yes"** as the endpoint: it's often counterfeit and changes nothing.
- **Settling for "You're right"**: signals the counterpart isn't vested (Brandon the linebacker kept playing his old way after every "You're right").

## Worked Example
The Schilling case: after months stuck on Abu Sabaya's "$10M war damages" demand, Voss scripts negotiator Benjie to run the full active-listening arsenal — pauses, minimal encouragers, mirroring, labeling, and finally a complete summary of "the world according to Abu Sabaya" (five hundred years of oppression, fishing rights, all of it). Sabaya goes silent for nearly a minute, then says "That's right." The war-damages demand evaporates and he never asks for money again — buying time for the rescue.

## Key Takeaways
1. Strive for "That's right," not "Yes."
2. Build it with a summary = paraphrase + label.
3. "You're right" is a warning sign of no buy-in.
4. Create unconditional positive regard to enable real change.
5. Climb the BCSM in order — listening before influence.
