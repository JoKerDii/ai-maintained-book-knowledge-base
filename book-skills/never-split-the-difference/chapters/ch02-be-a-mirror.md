# Chapter 2: Be a Mirror

## Core Idea
Quickly establish rapport through active listening. Quiet the voice in your head and theirs by making the other person your sole focus, slow the process down, and use mirroring to keep them talking and reveal their strategy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Mirroring (isopraxism)**: repeat the last 1–3 words (or the critical words) of what someone just said, with an upward, inquisitive inflection.
  - When to use: to build rapport, keep people talking, buy time, and get them to elaborate/reveal strategy; especially to "disagree without being disagreeable."
  - How: (1) FM DJ voice, (2) "I'm sorry...", (3) mirror, (4) silence ≥4 seconds, (5) repeat. Intent: "Please, help me understand."
- **The Late-Night FM DJ Voice**: deep, soft, slow, downward-inflecting — conveys calm, authority, "I've got it covered." Use selectively to make a point.
- **Three voice tones**:
  - *Positive/playful* — your default; relax and smile (audible even on the phone).
  - *FM DJ (downward inflection)* — calm authority, used selectively.
  - *Direct/assertive* — used rarely; triggers pushback.

## Key Concepts
- **Assumptions vs. hypotheses**: don't commit to assumptions; treat them as hypotheses to test during the negotiation.
- **Wants vs. needs**: wants are aspirations (easy to talk about); needs are the bare minimum, the survival floor.
- **Schizophrenic state**: when both sides only listen to the voices in their own heads.
- **Pronoun tell**: the less important a counterpart makes himself ("the others decide"), the more important he probably is.

## Mental Models
- Negotiation is a **process of discovery**, not a battle of arguments — goal is to uncover maximum information.
- "If someone is talking, they're not shooting" — slowing down also calms down.
- Mirroring = the art of insinuating similarity ("you and I, we're alike").

## Anti-patterns
- **Going too fast**: makes people feel unheard, undermines rapport and trust.
- **Stepping on your mirror**: asking a closed follow-up ("Are you Chris Watts?") instead of staying silent kills its power.
- **"Why do you mean by that?"**: incites defensiveness; a mirror gets clarity while signaling respect.

## Worked Example
A student's "drive-by" boss demands "Let's make two copies of all the paperwork." She mirrors with the DJ voice and inquisitive tone: "I'm sorry, two copies?" He elaborates. "...so the client is asking for a copy and we need one internally?" He starts checking himself. "Anywhere?" (mirror) → long pause → he downgrades to "just create two digital backups," then next day emails that two digital backups are fine. A week of unnecessary work avoided — entirely through mirrors + silence.

## Key Takeaways
1. Prepare to reveal surprises, not just react to them; test assumptions as hypotheses.
2. Make the other person your entire focus to quiet both internal voices.
3. Slow. It. Down.
4. Smile — positivity creates mental agility in both parties.
5. Mirror with the last 1–3 words, then go silent and let it work.
