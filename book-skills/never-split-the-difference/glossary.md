# Glossary — Never Split the Difference

**Accommodator** — negotiator type who prizes the relationship and free-flowing exchange; friendly, win-win seeking; for them silence = anger (Ch 9).

**Accusation Audit** — listing every negative thing the counterpart could say about you, then voicing it first to "take the sting out" (Ch 3, Ch 6).

**Ackerman Model** — offer-counteroffer haggling system: target → 65% / 85% / 95% / 100%, non-round final number + a token non-cash item (Ch 9).

**Active Listening** — the foundation skill set: pauses, minimal encouragers, mirroring, labeling, paraphrasing, summarizing (Ch 2, Ch 5).

**Amygdala** — brain's fear center; labeling a fear shifts activity to rational areas and defuses it (Ch 3).

**Analyst** — methodical, data-driven negotiator type that hates surprises; silence = thinking (Ch 9).

**Anchor (emotional)** — setting low expectations via an accusation audit before an offer, to trigger loss aversion (Ch 6).

**Anchor (extreme/price)** — an aggressive first number that bends the counterpart's reference point (Ch 6, Ch 9).

**Anchor-and-adjustment effect** — tendency to extrapolate from the first number heard (Ch 6).

**Assertive** — direct, time-is-money negotiator type that must be heard first; silence = a chance to talk more; mirrors work well (Ch 9).

**BATNA** — Best Alternative To a Negotiated Agreement; critiqued for tricking negotiators into aiming low (Ch 1, Appendix).

**Behavioral Change Stairway Model (BCSM)** — five stages: active listening → empathy → rapport → influence → behavioral change (Ch 5).

**Behind-the-table (Level II) players** — parties not at the table who can kill or implement a deal (Ch 8, Appendix).

**Black Swan** — a hidden, game-changing piece of information (unknown unknown); ~3–5 exist per negotiation (Ch 10).

**Bolstering range** — citing a range whose low end is what you actually want (Ch 6).

**Calibrated Question** — open-ended "What"/"How" question with no fixed answer that directs the conversation while giving the illusion of control (Ch 1, Ch 7).

**Certainty Effect** — preferring sure things to better-odds gambles (Ch 6).

**Chris Discount** — using your own name to humanize yourself and gain concessions ("What's the Chris discount?") (Ch 8).

**Cognitive Bias** — unconscious, irrational mental processes distorting perception (Ch 1).

**Counterfeit Yes** — a "yes" meant to stall or escape, with no commitment (Ch 4, Ch 8).

**Forced Empathy** — asking for help (e.g. "How am I supposed to…?") so the counterpart engages with your problem via reciprocity (Ch 7, Ch 8).

**FM DJ Voice** — late-night, deep, slow, downward-inflecting voice conveying calm authority (Ch 2).

**Fairness ("the F-word")** — emotionally loaded term; used defensively, distractingly, or positively ("stop me if I'm being unfair") (Ch 6).

**Illusion of Control** — making the counterpart feel in charge while you frame the conversation (Ch 7).

**Known knowns / known unknowns / unknown unknowns** — info you have / gaps you're aware of / Black Swans (Ch 10).

**Labeling** — naming an emotion aloud ("It seems like…") to validate and defuse/reinforce it (Ch 3).

**Leverage (Positive / Negative / Normative)** — ability to give what they want / make them suffer / use their own norms against them (Ch 10).

**Loss Aversion** — acting more strongly to avoid a loss than to gain an equal amount (Ch 1, Ch 6, Ch 10).

**Minimal Encouragers** — short cues ("uh-huh," "I see") signaling full attention (Ch 5).

**Mirroring (isopraxism)** — repeating the last 1–3 words to prompt elaboration and build rapport (Ch 2).

**Multi-step "No"** — four soft ways to say no before the word, to get the counterpart bidding against themselves (Ch 8).

**Neural resonance** — brains aligning during good listening, enabling anticipation (Ch 3).

**"No"-oriented question** — a question inviting a "No" ("Is now a bad time?") to give safety/control (Ch 4).

**Pinocchio Effect** — liars use more words and more third-person pronouns (Ch 8).

**Pivot to nonmonetary terms** — offering items cheap to you but valuable to them (Ch 6, Ch 9).

**Prospect Theory** — Kahneman/Tversky theory of choice under risk (Ch 6).

**Religion (knowing their)** — the worldview/values that hold authority over the counterpart; where Black Swans live (Ch 10).

**Rule of Three** — getting agreement to the same thing three times to test genuine commitment (Ch 8).

**Similarity Principle** — we trust those we see as similar/in-group (Ch 10).

**Strategic Umbrage** — well-timed, controlled offense-taking to wake the counterpart to a problem (Ch 9).

**Summary** — paraphrasing + labeling; reflecting "the world according to them" to trigger "That's right" (Ch 5).

**System 1 / System 2** — fast emotional mind / slow logical mind; System 1 steers System 2 (Ch 1).

**Tactical Empathy** — recognizing and vocalizing the counterpart's perspective *and* what's behind it, to influence (Ch 1, Ch 3).

**"That's right"** — the breakthrough phrase signaling genuine understanding and buy-in (better than "Yes") (Ch 5).

**"You're right"** — a brush-off signaling no buy-in; nothing changes (Ch 5, Ch 8).

**Wants vs. Needs** — aspirations vs. the bare minimum/survival floor (Ch 2).

**Wimp-win** — settling low to protect self-esteem, dressed up as "win-win" (Ch 9, Appendix).

**7-38-55 Rule** — 7% words, 38% tone, 55% body language (Mehrabian) (Ch 8).
