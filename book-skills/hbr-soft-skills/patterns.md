# Patterns & Techniques

## Decompose "Soft Skills" Before Assessing (Ch 1)
**When to use**: Hiring, promoting, or developing anyone where "good with people" is the criterion.
**How**: Score separately on self-awareness, listening/communication, cross-group facility, and theory of mind. Place high-potentials in roles that force contact with varied constituencies and monitor performance there.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than a résumé screen; no established tool exists at the executive level, so you must build one.

## The Triad Diagnostic (Ch 2)
**When to use**: A capable leader is failing and you can't name why.
**How**: Map symptom to missing focus — rudderless (inward), clueless (others), blindsided (outward).
**Trade-offs**: Diagnostic only; each focus is trained differently, and strength in one doesn't transfer.

## Pick the Right Empathy (Ch 2)
**When to use**: Any high-stakes interpersonal moment.
**How**: Cognitive empathy to explain yourself and to de-escalate an overheated conversation; emotional empathy to mentor and read a room; empathic concern to judge what someone needs from you.
**Trade-offs**: Too much emotional empathy causes compassion fatigue; too little numbs you.

## Attention Reps (Ch 2)
**When to use**: Building focus as a trainable capacity.
**How**: Notice the mind has wandered, return it to the chosen object, hold. Protect uninterrupted reflection time to enable exploration.
**Trade-offs**: Sleep loss, stress, and overload disable the switch from exploitation to exploration.

## Amplify Existing Norms Instead of Announcing Ideals (Ch 3)
**When to use**: Any culture change, especially when current behavior contradicts the target.
**How**: Measure the actual norm; find where the desired behavior already happens; make it visible via recognition and incentives; identify connectors and co-create the initiative with them.
**Trade-offs**: Slower and less visible than a values launch — but a values launch that outruns the norm produces cynicism.

## Self-Talk Substitution (Ch 4)
**When to use**: You or someone you coach is resisting required learning.
**How**: Diagnose which attribute is blocked, then swap the phrase. "I don't need to learn this" → "What would my future look like if I did?" "I'm already fine" → "Am I really? How do I compare with peers?" "This is boring" → "I wonder why others find it interesting." "I'm terrible at this" → "I'm making beginner mistakes, but I'll get better."
**Trade-offs**: Requires catching the thought in flight; not a substitute for real capability gaps.

## Import Curious Language (Ch 4)
**When to use**: A required topic bores you.
**How**: Notice the *How…? Why…? I wonder…?* phrasing you use about a genuine interest; aim it at the boring topic; take exactly one action to answer one question.
**Trade-offs**: You only need one spark, but arguing someone into interest never works.

## The Three Reinforcements (Ch 5)
**When to use**: Asking anyone for help, and in day-to-day priming.
**How**: In-group (shared *experience* over shared trait); positive identity (identity nouns, tailored, plus pre-emptive gratitude); effectiveness (state the need and the impact, follow up afterward, allow alternative forms of help).
**Trade-offs**: Reciprocity framing, apology, and minimizing all backfire; ask individually, never by group email.

## Single-Frame Pitching (Ch 6)
**When to use**: Proposing anything upward.
**How**: Read the boss's tells for promotion vs. prevention focus, then pitch opportunity *or* threat — never both. Pitch in private, tie it to their stated goals, and pre-answer resources, coalition difficulty, and political cost.
**Trade-offs**: Raises endorsement 15–18%; mixed frames get the least support of any framing.

## Recruit Allies Before Pitching (Ch 6)
**When to use**: Before raising anything contentious.
**How**: Socialize with colleagues first; ask to cite their support or have them co-present; ideally let a well-positioned colleague who won't benefit make the case.
**Trade-offs**: Speaking on behalf of others is 57% more influential — but takes time and cedes some ownership.

## The Three A's Filter (Ch 7)
**When to use**: Before any unsolicited feedback, mandatorily across power or identity lines.
**How**: Is it meant to assist? Is it actionable? Was it asked for — and if not, ask for feedback on your own work first.
**Trade-offs**: The third A costs a step but neutralizes the power transfer that makes advice read as dominance.

## Match Upgraders and Downgraders (Ch 7)
**When to use**: Cross-cultural feedback.
**How**: Listen for the intensity words your counterpart uses and mirror them. In indirect cultures, praise the good specifically and omit the bad; the silence carries the message.
**Trade-offs**: Feels evasive to direct-culture speakers and blunt to indirect ones — calibrate to the recipient, not your comfort.

## Feedback Loops by Team Maturity (Ch 7)
**When to use**: Building a candid culture on a diverse team.
**How**: One-to-one chats (new teams) → speed-dating rounds, three minutes each way (closer teams) → live 360 circles over a meal (mature teams). Structure content as one strength + one improvement, or start/stop/continue.
**Trade-offs**: Public formats require courage but prevent backchannel gossip and normalize feedback.

## Choose Your Listening Attitude (Ch 8)
**When to use**: Any conversation where the relationship matters.
**How**: Name whether you're listening to protect, to judge, or to co-create. In co-create mode hold four questions: What are they trying to say? What are they thinking? What do they expect to explore with me? How can I connect to their world?
**Trade-offs**: Costs attention up front; the alternative is a threat response you then have to repair.

## The Third Entity Reframe (Ch 9)
**When to use**: A conflict has become personal and polarized.
**How**: Imagine three parties — you, them, and the dynamic. Make progress on the dynamic (a decision, an assignment, the tension itself) rather than trying to change the person.
**Trade-offs**: Doesn't fix genuinely abusive behavior; sometimes you must cut losses.

## Conflict Experiments (Ch 9)
**When to use**: A difficult relationship you haven't been able to shift.
**How**: Pick two or three tactics, define what you'll do differently, set a time period, observe. Keep, tweak, or abandon. Do something the other person doesn't expect to interrupt the pattern.
**Trade-offs**: Treat failures as information; repeating a dead tactic is the real error.

## Compassionate Coaching Sequence (Ch 10)
**When to use**: Helping someone through a real behavioral or career change.
**How**: Check readiness → ask ideal-self questions and let them talk 80% → write a short personal vision statement → build a personal balance sheet including enduring qualities → set a learning agenda from strengths → experiment, review regularly, and surround them with a developmental network.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than advice-giving; advice is faster but triggers stress and blocks learning for broad goals.

## The Eight S's Revision Pass (Ch 11)
**When to use**: Revising any business writing.
**How**: Ask which S is missing. Simplicity (cut, activate, drop tangents); specificity (concrete nouns, memorable shorthand); surprise (novelty, wordplay); stirring (emotional verbs, metaphor); seductiveness (open with a question or mystery); smart (fresh distinction, universal truth); social (people, your own voice, second person); story.
**Trade-offs**: Passive voice can cut comprehension accuracy 10%; nested clauses slow reading and add errors.

## Five Voices Selection (Ch 12)
**When to use**: Choosing how to show up in a given moment.
**How**: Character before hard conversations; context before details and when presenting up; clarity when the team is scattered (including empowering them to say no); curiosity when work is interdependent, when coaching, or when emotions run high; connection to stay visible as your span grows.
**Trade-offs**: Requires the self-awareness to notice which voice the moment wants.

## The Newspaper Headline Question (Ch 12)
**When to use**: Annual or quarterly prioritization with a direct report.
**How**: Ask, "If we were to publish this in a newspaper, what would you want the big headlines to be for you and your team at the end of the year?" Then use the answer as the reference point for later re-prioritization.
**Trade-offs**: Forces hard trade-offs some people would rather defer.

## Ethical Pre-Commitment (Ch 13)
**When to use**: Before you face a moral challenge — which is the only time it works.
**How**: Write eulogy-virtue goals; make specific if-then plans aloud; recruit ethical mentors inside and outside; screen employers for moral fit and ask about it in interviews; signal your standards subtly.
**Trade-offs**: Feels theoretical until needed; that's precisely the ethical mirage it counters.

## The Three Rationalization Tests (Ch 13)
**When to use**: Any decision where you notice yourself constructing a justification.
**How**: Publicity (front page?), generalizability (precedent for everyone?), mirror (would you like that person?). Also run rules / consequences / virtues. If any answer is no, stop.
**Trade-offs**: Requires slowing down — and rushing is itself a strong predictor of unethical action.
