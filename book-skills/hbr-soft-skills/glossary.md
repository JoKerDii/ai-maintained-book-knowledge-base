# Glossary

**Affinity bias** — Unconscious tendency to align with people similar to you in appearance, beliefs, and background (Ch 9)

**Anticipatory utility** — The pleasure of expectation itself; readers' reward circuits peak before a passage's high point (Ch 11)

**Aspiration** — The wanting-to-learn attribute; raisable by shifting focus from challenges to future benefits (Ch 4)

**Attentional blink** — Momentary lapse causing missed words in a list; emotionally significant words produce none (Ch 11)

**Automated social hierarchy detection** — Inferring org standing from response latency; slower repliers hold more power (Ch 2)

**Coachable moment** — An opening for development: an evident challenge, a wake-up call, or a vague unease (Ch 10)

**Coaching for compliance** — Drilling into problems and targets; works for predetermined goals, fails for behavioral change (Ch 10)

**Coaching with compassion** — Coaching from the person's dreams and vision; activates brain regions for openness and learning (Ch 10)

**Cognitive control** — Putting attention where you want it and holding it against temptation; colloquially, willpower (Ch 2)

**Cognitive empathy** — Understanding another's perspective by thinking about feelings rather than feeling them (Ch 2)

**Confirmation bias** — Reading new evidence as proof of existing beliefs, making it harder for others to disprove you (Ch 9)

**Connectors** — The most-connected people in a group, through whom information, ideas, and values flow (Ch 3)

**Developmental network** — The extended circle supporting someone's change; a personal board of advisers (Ch 10)

**Downgraders** — Words that soften criticism ("kind of," "a little," "maybe") plus deliberate understatement (Ch 7)

**Effectiveness (reinforcement)** — Making clear what you need and what impact the help will have (Ch 5)

**Emotional empathy** — Feeling what another feels; used for mentoring, clients, and reading group dynamics (Ch 2)

**Empathic concern** — Sensing what another person needs *from you*; requires managing distress without numbing (Ch 2)

**Ethical mirage** — Overestimating the virtuousness of your future self (Ch 13)

**Eulogy virtues** — What people praise after you've died: character and what you did for others (Ch 13)

**Fair witness** — An inner voice that reports the facts of a situation in balance, neither defensive nor punishing (Ch 4)

**Fundamental attribution error** — Attributing others' behavior to personality and your own to circumstances (Ch 9)

**Humble inquiry** — Setting aside your own assumptions and letting the other person do 80% of the talking (Ch 10)

**Identity segmentation** — Compartmentalizing personal and professional life under different moral codes (Ch 13)

**Ideals vs. norms** — What a group says people ought to do vs. what most members actually do; norms win (Ch 3)

**Implementation intentions** — "If X happens, then I will do Y," specified in advance and voiced aloud (Ch 13)

**In-group (reinforcement)** — Assuring a potential helper you're on the same team and the team matters (Ch 5)

**Intentional Change Theory** — Ideal self → real self → learning agenda → experimenting and practicing (Ch 10)

**Job crafting** — Proactively reshaping your tasks, relationships, and view of your job to make it meaningful (Ch 13)

**Know-it-all vs. learn-it-all** — Status-seeking expertise display vs. humility to hear criticism and diverse views (Ch 7)

**Learning agenda** — A road map from strengths and passions toward the ideal self; not a performance improvement plan (Ch 10)

**Listening to co-create** — Listening to connect and explore, with psychological safety (Ch 8)

**Listening to protect** — Defensive listening that guards your identity; the speaker feels ignored (Ch 8)

**Manvising** — Men giving women advice they neither asked for nor want (Ch 7)

**Mirror test** — Would you like the person in the mirror after this decision? (Ch 13)

**Moral humility** — Recognizing that anyone can transgress without vigilance (Ch 13)

**Naive realism** — Believing you see an issue objectively, so dissenters must be uninformed or biased (Ch 9)

**Open awareness** — Noticing broadly without judging, censoring, or getting swept up in any one thing (Ch 2)

**Personal balance sheet** — Strengths, weaknesses, and enduring distinctive qualities, weighed against a long-term vision (Ch 10)

**Personal vision statement** — A short written statement of the ideal self, kept present as a daily reminder (Ch 10)

**Phantom norms** — Behaviors that seem dominant only because a few loud or prominent people display them (Ch 3)

**Positive identity (reinforcement)** — Framing the helper as a helpful person uniquely placed to assist (Ch 5)

**Prevention focus** — Orientation toward vigilance, downside management, and playing not to lose (Ch 6)

**Processing fluency** — How little brainpower a reader spends extracting your meaning (Ch 11)

**Promotion focus** — Orientation toward aspirations, ideals, the future, and playing to win (Ch 6)

**Psychic attrition** — Erosion of attention to others that accompanies rising rank and power (Ch 2)

**Publicity test** — Would you be comfortable with this choice on the newspaper's front page? (Ch 13)

**Radical candor** — Kim Scott's argument that blunt feedback beats withholding it ("ruinous empathy") (Ch 7)

**Readiness to change** — Whether someone is open to the reflection and experimentation coaching requires (Ch 10)

**Resonant relationship** — The caring, trusting bond that makes coaching effective (Ch 10)

**Résumé virtues** — Listable skills, abilities, and accomplishments; largely what you've done for yourself (Ch 13)

**Reward circuit** — Midbrain neurons that light up in response to pleasurable stimuli, including good prose (Ch 11)

**Self-talk** — Internal narration about yourself; the lever for aspiration, self-awareness, curiosity, and vulnerability (Ch 4)

**Social skills (C-suite sense)** — Self-awareness, listening/communication, cross-group facility, and theory of mind (Ch 1)

**Somatic markers** — Gut sensations from the insula and amygdala signaling that something feels right or wrong (Ch 2)

**Status incongruence** — When your role's societal status doesn't match the role you play in context (Ch 7)

**Systems effect** — One weak team member degrading the whole group's performance (Ch 1)

**The Eight S's** — Simplicity, specificity, surprise, stirring language, seductiveness, smart thinking, social content, storytelling (Ch 11)

**The Third Entity** — You, your colleague, and the dynamic between you; work on the third thing (Ch 9)

**Theory of mind** — Inferring how others think and feel; the hardest-to-fake social skill (Ch 1)

**Three A's of feedback** — Assist, actionable, asked for (Ch 7)

**Triad of Awareness** — Focus on yourself, on others, and on the wider world (Ch 2)

**True North groups** — Small trusted groups where self-knowledge begins with self-revelation (Ch 2)

**Upgraders** — Words that intensify criticism ("absolutely," "totally," "completely") (Ch 7)

**Vulnerability (learning attribute)** — Tolerating your own beginner mistakes on the way up the curve (Ch 4)
