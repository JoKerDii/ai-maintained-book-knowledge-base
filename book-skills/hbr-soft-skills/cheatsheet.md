# Cheatsheet — Decision Rules

## Core if/then rules

| Situation | Do this | Because |
|---|---|---|
| A capable leader is failing | Ask: rudderless, clueless, or blindsided? | Each maps to a different missing focus and a different fix (Ch 2) |
| Conversation is overheating | Switch from emotional to cognitive empathy | Understanding the perspective intellectually keeps you calm enough to help (Ch 2) |
| Current behavior contradicts the value you want | Amplify existing good behavior; do NOT announce the ideal | When norms and ideals clash, people follow what others do (Ch 3) |
| Someone resists needed learning | Diagnose the attribute first, then swap the self-talk | "No time / not relevant" is an aspiration symptom, not an argument (Ch 4) |
| You need help | Ask one person directly, name the impact, follow up | Group emails diffuse responsibility; help rates run ~48% above expectation (Ch 5) |
| Pitching your boss | One frame only, in private, tied to their stated goals | Mixed opportunity+threat framing gets the least support of any option (Ch 6) |
| About to give unsolicited feedback | Ask for feedback on your own work first | Advising confers power; asking first hands it back (Ch 7) |
| Feedback recipient is from a more indirect culture | Praise the good specifically; omit the bad | The omission communicates the criticism clearly enough (Ch 7) |
| Conflict has gone personal | Work the third entity, not the person | Converts an adversary into a co-owner of a shared problem (Ch 9) |
| Someone needs to change a behavior | Start from their dreams, not their deficits | Compliance coaching triggers guilt and self-consciousness; vision coaching activates learning circuits (Ch 10) |
| Writing feels flat | Find the missing S | Diagnosis beats "make it better" (Ch 11) |
| Under time pressure on a moral call | Sleep on it | Rushing strongly predicts unethical action (Ch 13) |

## Which frame for which boss (Ch 6)

| Boss tells | Focus | Pitch |
|---|---|---|
| Obeys rules and SOPs, meticulous, careful planner, hates mistakes | Prevention | The problem or loss this avoids |
| Starts more than she finishes, talks about the future, delegates details, lets small errors slide | Promotion | The new opportunity and its upside |
| Unclear | Watch one meeting for which language dominates; never hedge by using both | — |

## Which empathy, which listening, which voice

| If you need to… | Empathy (Ch 2) | Listening (Ch 8) | Voice (Ch 12) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Explain yourself so it lands | Cognitive | Co-create | Context |
| Mentor or read the room | Emotional | Co-create | Connection |
| Decide what someone needs from you | Empathic concern | Co-create | Curiosity |
| Refocus a scattered team | — | — | Clarity |
| Enter a likely conflict | Cognitive | Co-create | Character |

## Feedback loop by team maturity (Ch 7)

- Never exchanged feedback → **one-to-one chats**, private
- Some trust established → **speed-dating**, 3 min each way, rotate, each names one action
- Mature, strong relationships → **live 360 circle** over a meal; recipient listens and says thank you

## Thresholds & numbers the authors commit to

| Number | Meaning |
|---|---|
| 0.07 sec | Time to form a trust judgment in conversation (Ch 8) |
| 200 ms | Emotional connotation processed — before meaning (Ch 11) |
| 80% | Minimum share of talking the *coachee* should do (Ch 10) |
| 48% | How much more willing helpers are than askers predict; also the persistence gain from "together" (Ch 5) |
| 30% | Reduction in a manager's felt threat when you pitch privately (Ch 6) |
| 15–18% | Endorsement gain from matching the boss's regulatory focus (Ch 6) |
| 57% | Influence gain from speaking up on behalf of others vs. yourself (Ch 6) |
| 5× | How much more often men give women unrequested advice than the reverse (Ch 7) |
| 10% | Comprehension accuracy lost to passive voice (Ch 11) |
| 18–254 days | Range for forming a new habit — plan for the long end (Ch 10) |
| 2.5% | Actual recognition rate in the song-tapping study (predicted: 50%) — your clarity is illusory (Ch 9) |

## Tells & smells

- **You're being clueless**: you're technically strong, people avoid you, and when told, you blame back (Ch 2)
- **You've hit a phantom norm**: the attitude you think is dominant belongs to the loudest one or two people (Ch 3)
- **It's an aspiration problem, not a time problem**: the objections are "too long," "old way works," "flash in the pan" (Ch 4)
- **Your ask will fail**: you opened with "May I ask you a favor?", apologized profusely, offered reciprocity, or minimized the need (Ch 5)
- **Your pitch will fail**: it names both the upside of acting and the risk of not acting (Ch 6)
- **Your feedback will land as dominance**: majority member → underrepresented colleague, unasked (Ch 7)
- **You're listening to protect**: you're rehearsing your reply and guarding your position (Ch 8)
- **Confirmation bias has set in**: a colleague's occasional missteps have hardened into "X is difficult" (Ch 9)
- **You're coaching for compliance**: you're doing most of the talking and naming gaps (Ch 10)
- **You're rationalizing**: "everyone does this," "just following orders," "not like I'm robbing a bank" (Ch 13)
- **Your environment is eroding self-control**: high uncertainty, cognitive overload, late nights, back-to-back stretch goals (Ch 13)

## Defaults

- Assume your view is *a* view; ask the challenge questions anyway (Ch 9)
- Assume the quiet majority is already empathic; your job is volume (Ch 3)
- Assume people will help; the constraint is how you ask (Ch 5)
- Assume delivery, not content, decides whether feedback lands (Ch 7)
- Assume your future self is less virtuous than you think (Ch 13)
- Set your relationship goals low if needed — "functional" is a legitimate target (Ch 9)
