# Glossary — The Stimulated Mind

**3-S Model** — Wood's capstone systems model: brain function is driven by Stimulus, Supply, and Support acting as an interconnected network. (Ch 14)

**Active grandparent hypothesis** — The idea that humans evolved to remain physically and socially active into old age, so continued contribution is the ancestral norm. (Ch 9)

**Allostasis / allostatic load** — Achieving stability through change; allostatic load is the cumulative cost of unresolved stress, which impairs the support arm. (Ch 11, 14)

**Amyloid Cascade Hypothesis (ACH)** — Model in which amyloid beta accumulation triggers inflammation, tau tangling, neuron death, and decline. Explains only 20–25% of cognition and decline. (Ch 2)

**ApoB** — Lipoprotein marker of cardiovascular risk; concerning above 118 mg/dL. (Ch 13)

**ApoE4** — Most common genetic risk factor for dementia. Amplifies risk from other factors rather than changing which factors matter. (Ch 14)

**Astrocytes** — Star-shaped glia regulating fluid flow, sleep, injury response, and neuronal energy; one human astrocyte can contact up to two million synapses. (Ch 3)

**Autophagy** — Cellular housekeeping that recycles damaged proteins and components; impaired autophagy is a hallmark of aging. (Ch 3)

**Brain reserve (brain maintenance)** — The structural "hardware": how much brain tissue you have and how well it is preserved. (Ch 1)

**Car Maintenance Model** — Wood's metaphor: amyloid is rust, your brain is the car, and the brain is its own mechanic. (Ch 3)

**Closed-skill vs. open-skill exercise** — Closed = predictable, self-paced (running, cycling); open = reactive and unpredictable (tennis, martial arts), which adds cognitive stimulus. (Ch 7)

**Cognitive Athlete** — Framing daily cognitive work as training that requires programmed recovery, not just more stimulus. (Ch 12)

**Cognitive efficiency** — Doing more with less neural activation as a skill becomes practiced. (Ch 9)

**Cognitive Gears (high / middle / low)** — Hewitt & Hintsa's model for matching task type to available cognitive capacity across the day. (Ch 12)

**Cognitive reserve** — The "software": total cognitive capacity, and how much can be lost before impairment appears. (Ch 1)

**Cognitive resilience** — Redundancy: the ability to tolerate accumulated brain damage without losing function. (Ch 1)

**Community connector / compassionate community** — Frome model of embedding social connection into healthcare; ER admissions fell 14% while surrounding areas rose 28% over four years. (Ch 10)

**Crystallized intelligence** — The components of cognition that form wisdom; does not automatically decline and may peak in the sixth or seventh decade. (Ch 4)

**Default mode network (DMN)** — Medial parietal plus prefrontal regions active when not focused on a task; heavily affected by aging. (Ch 4)

**Digital dementia** — Cognitive decline attributed to offloading memory and navigation to devices. (Ch 9)

**Digital twin** — A data replica used to predict which intervention would most reduce your personal dementia risk. (Ch 14)

**Exercise snacks** — Very short bouts of activity (e.g., 15 squats every 20 minutes, 5 flights of stairs daily) that accumulate meaningful benefit. (Ch 7)

**FINGER** — Finnish multidomain trial (1,200+ adults 60–77) combining diet, aerobic + resistance exercise, cognitive training, and cardiovascular monitoring; improved cognition over two years. (Ch 5, 14)

**Glia** — Non-neuronal brain cells (astroglia, microglia, oligodendroglia); nearly as numerous as neurons and central to maintenance. (Ch 3)

**Google effect** — Failing to encode information you know you can look up. (Ch 9)

**Headroom** — The book's central prize: reserve cognitive capacity above daily demand, available when you need it. Composed of the Three R's. (Ch 1, 6)

**Homocysteine** — B-vitamin status marker; keep below 13, ideally below 10–11 μmol/L. (Ch 8)

**Hormesis** — Beneficial adaptation to a mild, recoverable stressor. (Ch 11)

**Hypostress** — Too little challenge; the understimulated end of the general adaptation syndrome. (Ch 11)

**Last-in first-out hypothesis** — Recently evolved brain regions decline fastest; Wood amends it to include the regions they connect to. (Ch 4)

**Lazebnik's broken radio** — Test case for whether component cataloging can repair a tunable system. It cannot. (Ch 2)

**Mismatch** — The gap between the environment the brain evolved for and the one it now inhabits; retirement is the archetype. (Ch 1, 4)

**Movement Funnel** — Wood's exercise hierarchy: Snack → Propel → Resist → Sprint → Coordinate. (Ch 7)

**MHT (menopausal hormone therapy)** — Symptom-guided hormone treatment; Wood's position is treat symptoms, not brains. (Ch 13)

**Neurovascular coupling** — Active neurons and astrocytes dilate local vessels, directing oxygen and nutrients where needed — the physical link between stimulus and supply. (Ch 3, 14)

**NSDR (non-sleep deep rest) / yoga nidra** — Deliberate waking rest protocol; 20 minutes daily for two weeks in the cited work. (Ch 12)

**Nutrient Cascade** — Prioritizing scarce nutrients toward short-term survival over long-term maintenance (see Triage Theory). (Ch 8)

**Physiological sigh** — Double inhale followed by long exhale; ~5 minutes/day for stress reduction. (Ch 11)

**POINTER** — US replication of FINGER in adults 60–79; gains in executive function and processing speed, equal across ApoE4 genotype and sex. (Ch 14)

**PRIME information** — The categories of personal knowledge that mark a genuinely close relationship. (Ch 10)

**Primal Play** — Darryl Edwards' approach to unstructured, playful movement. (Ch 7)

**Reductionism** — Understanding a system by isolating components; legitimate for mechanism discovery, insufficient for human cognition. (Ch 2)

**Resolve** — Wood's third R: shedding the expectation of decline and committing to lifelong engagement. (Ch 1)

**Rule of thirds** — Expect roughly a third of any pursuit to feel good, a third average, a third bad. (Ch 12)

**Satiety per calorie** — Choosing foods by how full they make you per unit energy. (Ch 8)

**Stereotype-embodiment theory** — Langer's finding that people age according to their stereotypes about older people. (Ch 4)

**Stimulus / Supply / Support** — The three nodes of the 3-S model: cognitive challenge; oxygen, energy and nutrients; and repair processes of rest and sleep. (Ch 14)

**Striver's curse** — The decline experienced by those whose identity rests on peak fluid-intelligence performance. (Ch 9)

**Superagers** — People whose cognitive function stays stable from their fifties into their eighties; most of us are capable of it. (Ch 14)

**Tau tangles / tauopathy** — Abnormal tau strands forming when neurons are injured; Alzheimer's and CTE are both tauopathies. (Ch 2)

**Thirds-of-a-plate method** — Wood's unnamed plate heuristic (he presents it as a recommendation, not a branded framework): divide a ten-inch dinner plate into thirds — lean protein, nutrient-dense veg and fruit, and a final third flexed to energy needs. (Ch 8)

**Transfer (near / intermediate / far)** — How far a trained skill generalizes; far transfer is rare, which is why brain-training games disappoint. (Ch 9)

**Triage Theory** — Bruce Ames' theory that under nutrient shortage the body sacrifices long-term maintenance for short-term survival. (Ch 8)

**Tsimané** — ~17,000 Indigenous Bolivians with the world's lowest estimated dementia rates and slower brain aging. (Ch 5)

**WOOP** — Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan — the behavior-change protocol Wood uses for dietary change. (Ch 8)

**Yerkes–Dodson curve** — Inverted-U relationship between arousal and performance. (Ch 11)
