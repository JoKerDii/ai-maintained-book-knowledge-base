# Cheatsheet — The Stimulated Mind

## Where to start: the 3-S decision tree
- **Which node is weakest?**
  - Demanding job, constant problem-solving → stimulus is fine. **Add supply or support**: exercise, sleep, people.
  - Unrewarding work, retired or near it → **add stimulus**: a class, a hobby, volunteering.
  - Chronically time-poor → **set-and-forget**: water filter, air purifier, multivitamin, sardines at breakfast.
  - Genuinely unsure → **start with sleep**. It's only the support arm, but it greases everything else.
- **Then pick exactly one.** Two competing priorities usually produce zero.
- **Prefer multi-node activities**: group/coordinative exercise, outdoor volunteering, in-person classes. These hit all three S's at once.

## Decision rules
- **When you don't know the mechanism, act anyway** — we still can't fully explain how exercise works, and that has never justified not exercising.
- **When a single counterexample appears** ("she exercised daily and still got dementia") — you're stacking a deck, not buying immunity. At 80, ~20% of the 4–5 healthy-factor group was still diagnosed; that's still half the risk of the 0–1 group.
- **When a target feels unreachable, lower it and broaden it** — setting lower goals with a wider definition of "counts" improves both adherence and how you feel about your health.
- **When you slept badly, still train** — 20–30 min moderate aerobic improves nearly every cognitive domain and mood regardless of sleep, possibly more so after poor sleep.
- **When you're deciding whether to genotype** — ApoE4, polygenic scores, and amyloid/tau blood panels change how *serious* you are, not *what you do*. ApoE4 amplifies other risks rather than replacing them.
- **When family history worries you** — look for shared *lifestyle*, not shared DNA. That's the actionable overlap.
- **When choosing a cognitive activity** — expect near transfer only. Train the function you refuse to lose.
- **When offloading to a device or AI** — crutch (replaces a capacity) or orthotic (extends one you still exercise)?

## The five rules (apply to every decision)
1. **Prepare for your own race** — benefit × consistency, not benefit alone.
2. **Never give up** — accumulate; a missed week is noise across decades.
3. **Don't go it alone** — social makes it easier *and* better.
4. **Everything counts** — acknowledge what you already do.
5. **Be kind to yourself (and others)** — recovery is where adaptation happens.

## Tells & smells
- **You're doing the "dead mouse" life** — everything arrives without effort. Adult brains only rewire when given a reason: challenge or social engagement.
- **Retirement is the archetypal mismatch** — often the point where decline accelerates, because work's cognitive and social stimulus was removed.
- **You believe you exercise less than your peers** — associated with the highest mortality risk even after adjusting for actual activity. The belief is itself a risk factor.
- **You're holding a mental list of health tasks you're not doing** — inherently stressful and possibly counteracting your good work.
- **The tracker became the goal** — if the sleep score or glucose monitor is stressing you or keeping you indoors, it's a hindrance.
- **You're waiting for the drug** — two decades of zooming inward have not delivered prevention or cure.
- **Intense exercise inside an hour of bed** — delays melatonin, raises arousal, cuts REM. Move it to morning or afternoon.
- **You're optimizing two things at once** — expect zero.
