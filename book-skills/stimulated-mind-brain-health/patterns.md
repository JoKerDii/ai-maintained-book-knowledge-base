# Patterns — Protocols & Techniques from The Stimulated Mind

## The 3-S Diagnosis
**When to use**: Deciding where to intervene, with limited capacity for change.
**How**: Identify your weakest node. Busy demanding job (plenty of stimulus) → add supply/support: exercise, sleep, time with people. Unrewarding work or at/near retirement (low stimulus) → new hobby, class, volunteering. Chronically time-poor → set-and-forget items: water filter, air purifier, high-quality multivitamin, tin of sardines at breakfast. Then pick **one** change: "when you only have the capacity to do one thing, focusing on two makes it likely that zero will happen."
**Trade-offs**: You lose precision about which mechanism you improved — accept it. Working one risk factor usually improves several at once.

## The Movement Funnel
**When to use**: Building an exercise practice from any starting point.
**How**: Progress through the tiers, adding rather than replacing. **Snack** — break sitting hourly; 15 squats every 20 min; 5 flights (50 stairs)/day; even 1–2 min of vigorous activity gives benefit, 7 min/day offsets sedentary risk. **Propel** — walk; 4,000 steps cuts dementia risk 25% vs. 1,500; each additional 1,000 steps = 7–8% lower dementia and 15% lower mortality, plateauing around 10,000 (Wood's working range 8,000–12,000); brisk = 2.5 mph or 100 steps/min. **Resist** — 1–3 sets of 5–15 reps, 1–3×/week; minimum effective dose 4 sets/week per body part; use Dan John's five movements (Push, Pull, Hinge, Squat, Carry). **Sprint** — Norwegian 4×4 (4 min hard / 3 min easy, ×4, 3×/week) or Tabata (20 s on / 10 s off ×8). **Coordinate** — open-skill and complex-coordination activity.
**Trade-offs**: WHO baseline is 150–300 min/week MVPA, but the funnel deliberately starts far below that so you actually begin. Keep intense sessions out of the hour before bed — they delay melatonin and cut REM.

## The Hippocampus Walking Protocol
**When to use**: You want a documented structural brain change from the simplest possible intervention.
**How**: 40-minute brisk walks, 3×/week. Ramp in: start at 10 minutes, add 5 minutes per week until you reach 40. Associated with roughly 2% hippocampal growth per year.
**Trade-offs**: Slow-building; the payoff is structural rather than immediately felt.

## Nutrients / Energy / Pattern
**When to use**: Any dietary decision, in place of arguing about diet ideology.
**How**: Evaluate three things separately. (1) **Nutrients** — are you getting the brain-critical ones? (2) **Energy** — are you in chronic excess? (3) **Pattern** — is the overall dietary pattern whole-food dominant? Governed by two rules: *maximize nutrients* and *fuel for your own needs*. Structure meals with Wood's **thirds-of-a-plate** recommendation: a ten-inch dinner plate divided into lean protein, nutrient-dense veg and fruit, and a final third flexed to energy needs.
**Trade-offs**: Deliberately agnostic about named diets, which frustrates people wanting a verdict.

## Core Nutrient Targets
**When to use**: Auditing the supply arm.
**How**: Protein 0.55 g/lb minimum, 0.7–0.8 g/lb ideal (RDI is only 0.4; Tsimané intake ≈ 2 g/kg). Protein snack rule: grams of protein ≥ calories ÷ 10. Omega-3 2–3 g/day EPA+DHA, or 2–3 servings of 4-oz fatty fish per week. B12 500 μg, folate 700 DFE, B6 20 mg, B2 1–2 mg — verify via homocysteine below 13, ideally below 10–11 μmol/L. Vitamin D3 2,000 IU (deficient below 20 ng/mL; target 40–50). Magnesium 200–400 mg. Creatine 5 g. Berries 1–2 cups/day. Tea or coffee 2–4 cups. Chocolate at 80%+ cacao.
**Trade-offs**: Food first — sardines, mackerel, or herring deliver protein, omega-3s, vitamin D, most B vitamins, copper, selenium, and creatine at once, with lower contaminant load than large fish. Low omega-3 + low vitamin D + high homocysteine was associated with more than a four-fold increase in dementia risk.

## Energy-Excess Screen
**When to use**: Checking whether the supply arm is compromised metabolically.
**How**: Flag any of — BMI >30; waist >35 in / 90 cm; waist-to-hip ratio >0.85 (F) or >0.90 (M); waist-to-height ratio >0.55; fasting glucose >100 mg/dL; HbA1c >5.6%; HDL <40 (M) / <50 (F); triglycerides >150; blood pressure >130/80; ApoB >118 mg/dL. A 10/4 mmHg blood pressure reduction is a meaningful target.
**Trade-offs**: These are screening thresholds, not diagnoses — they point to where to act, not what to prescribe.

## Cognitive Stimulus Selection
**When to use**: Choosing an activity to load the stimulus node.
**How**: Prefer **uniquely human stimuli** — language, music, social interaction, coordinative movement — over generic brain-training. Documented doses: new language 2–5 h/week or 30 min/day via app; music 60-min lesson plus 30 min practice; dance 90 min ×2/week for 3–4 months; juggling 10–30 min/day for 6–12 weeks; video games ~30 min/day; olfactory training 2 h/night with 7 scents for 6 months.
**Trade-offs**: Expect **near transfer only**. ACTIVE (2,800 adults 65+, ten 60-min sessions over 5–6 weeks with boosters at 1 and 3 years) held gains for 5 years and reduced at-fault crashes — but showed no cross-domain transfer. Train the function you actually want to keep.

## The AI Cognitive-Orthotic Test
**When to use**: Deciding whether to offload a task to a tool.
**How**: Ask whether the tool is a *crutch* replacing a capacity you want to keep, or an *orthotic* extending a capacity you're still exercising. Offloading navigation, memory, and reasoning wholesale produces the Google effect and digital dementia.
**Trade-offs**: Some offloading is genuinely freeing; the test is whether the underlying function still gets trained.

## Stress Optimization
**When to use**: When stress is chronic rather than acute-and-resolved.
**How**: Crum's three steps — see the stress, own the stress, use the stress (measured by the Stress Mindset Measure). Layer DiTuro's four moves: Breathe, Prioritize, Change what you see, Change the narrative. Add Kross's distancing shifts and Pennebaker expressive writing (15 min × 4 days). Use a **traffic light** self-check: green, yellow, red.
**Trade-offs**: Reframing works on recoverable stress. Genuine hypostress needs more challenge, not better coping — the Yerkes–Dodson curve runs both directions.

## Breathing & Downregulation
**When to use**: Sleep onset, acute stress, or between cognitive blocks.
**How**: 3:7 breathing (inhale 3, exhale 7) for 20 min before bed — cut sleep onset time by roughly two-thirds in the cited work. Alternatives: 4-7-8, or a 4-6-8 ramp. Physiological sigh ~5 min/day. Bedtime brain-dump list, 5 min. Transcendental-style meditation 10–20 min/day. Mindfulness 13 min/day for 8 weeks. Gratitude practice at least weekly.
**Trade-offs**: All low-cost; the constraint is consistency, not technique choice.

## Sleep Programming
**When to use**: Building the support arm.
**How**: Target 7–9 hours (acceptable range 6–10). Subtract 30–60 min from time in bed to estimate actual sleep. Improve in **15-minute increments** of sleep *opportunity* — even 15–30 extra minutes a day matters. Respect ~90-minute cycles. Caffeine cutoff 9 hours before bed. Bedroom 65–70°F. Judge on the **three components**: quantity, quality, regularity.
**Trade-offs**: Exercise partially rescues short sleep — in UK Biobank (400,000 people), the elevated dementia risk of ≤6 hours was offset by 15–20 min of brisk walking or 7 min of intense exercise daily. Seven hours gave the lowest dementia risk and the largest brains. Don't use this as license to under-sleep.

## The Cognitive Gears Day
**When to use**: Structuring cognitively demanding work.
**How**: Assign tasks to high, middle, or low gear and match them to your capacity through the day. Use 6-step day planning plus Newport's shutdown ritual. Pomodoro 25/5; microbreaks of 5–10 min. Protect against interruption — email interrupts roughly 4×/hour and each costs 10–15 min of recovery; task switches average 50 seconds.
**Trade-offs**: A 20-minute exercise bout before a memory-demanding task is a legitimate substitute for another cycle of grinding.

## Anti-Frailty Tool Kit
**When to use**: Older adults, or anyone protecting against the strength curve (muscle falls ~1%/year after 50; strength falls 2–3× faster).
**How**: Jumping/impact work, blood-flow-restriction (Kaatsu) training, and protein hacks to reach intake targets. Benchmarks: leg press ≈ 2× bodyweight; aim for the top third of strength for your group. Balance training daily.
**Trade-offs**: Falls are the most common cause of head trauma in adults over 65, so strength and balance work is dementia prevention, not just orthopedics.

## Exposure Reduction
**When to use**: Auditing the support arm for things actively impairing repair.
**How**: Alcohol — more than 2 drinks/day is harmful; even 2 UK units/day (a UK unit is 8 g; a US drink is 14 g) associates with brain shrinkage. Nicotine 1–4 mg where used. Review medications against the **Beers Criteria**. Air quality: N95 and PM2.5 monitoring. Oral health: xylitol 2–3 g, 2–3×/day.
**Trade-offs**: Hormone therapy (MHT, testosterone) should be symptom-guided — treat symptoms, not brains.
