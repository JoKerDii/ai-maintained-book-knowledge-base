# Chapter 14: The 3-S Model of Brain Health

## Core Idea
Wood's capstone framework: brain function is a network, not a checklist. Three interconnected nodes — **Stimulus, Supply, Support** — determine brain structure and function, which together produce focus, performance, and lowered dementia risk. Because the nodes interact, multiple small changes across the network outperform one large biochemical lever.

## Frameworks Introduced

- **The 3-S Model** (stimulus, supply, support): "Essentially a causal loop diagram in slightly sexier clothing," visualized as interconnecting train lines.
  1. **Stimulus** — cognitive demand or challenge through social or mental activities. This is where the journey starts and it connects to every other station. Inputs: skill learning, social interaction.
  2. **Supply** — the energy, oxygen, and nutrients that let you respond to stimuli. Requires a healthy heart and vascular system, good energy regulation, and avoidance of excess energy availability and insulin resistance. Oxygen and glucose combine in mitochondria to produce ATP; mitochondria need the nutrients from Ch 8. Inputs: heart health, exercise.
  3. **Support** — the neuroplasticity, maintenance, and repair processes of rest and sleep. Augmented by hormones (testosterone, estrogen) and trophic factors produced during exercise (BDNF, IGF-1). Impaired by unresolved stress (high allostatic load), excessive alcohol, smoking, and environmental exposures.
  - When to use: Whenever choosing where to intervene, or explaining why a lifestyle interaction exists.
  - How: Diagnose which node is weakest for *you*, then pick an activity that loads that node — preferring activities that hit multiple nodes at once.
  - Why it works: The nodes are physically coupled. Challenging the brain increases local blood supply via **neurovascular coupling**; challenged cells then initiate repair and adaptation during subsequent sleep. Improved function lets you take on wider stimuli, which improves function further — a virtuous cycle, "just like lifting weights."
  - Failure mode: Treating dementia risk factors as one long checklist. Pulling a single biochemical lever in a complex system and expecting large-scale impact is unrealistic.

- **Diagnose-your-weakest-node**: The practical entry point.
  - Busy, demanding job (plenty of stimulus) → add supply or support: exercise, more sleep, time with friends/family.
  - Not cognitively rewarded at work, or at/near retirement (low stimulus) → start a hobby, join a class, volunteer.
  - Always pressed for time → pick a set-and-forget item: water filter, air purifier, a high-quality multivitamin, or a tin of sardines at breakfast.

- **A Beginner's Guide to the 3-S Model** — five starting activities chosen because they hit multiple nodes at once:
  - **Group or coordinative physical activity** — dancing, doubles pickleball or any team ball sport, yoga, Pilates, martial arts (ideally varieties that don't involve getting hit in the head). Complex coordinative movement is a stimulus, improves supply via cardiovascular health, and supports via better sleep and lower stress/inflammation; the social component adds more stimulus.
  - **Outdoor volunteering** — parks, gardens, farms: trail maintenance, tree planting, invasive species removal, growing and harvesting. Social benefit + nature + physical activity = all three S's.
  - **Classes and time with friends** — a weekly dinner party, or a group class in an instrument or language. Quality in-person connection is cognitive stimulus and lowers stress/inflammation from high allostatic load.
  - **Sardines** (or mackerel, herring) — protein, omega-3s, vitamin D, most B vitamins, copper, selenium, creatine, and lower contaminant load than large fish. Buy the ones with skin and bones for collagen and calcium.
  - **Sleep** — technically only support, but it facilitates everything: sleep well and you're more sociable, less stressed, more likely to take on cognitive challenge, more likely to exercise, with better blood pressure and blood sugar. **Even an additional 15–30 minutes of sleep opportunity per day makes a big difference.**

- **Start with one thing, not two**: "When you only have the capacity to do one thing, focusing on two makes it likely that zero will happen." Choose something you can define concretely, are interested in, might enjoy, and are reasonably confident you can achieve. Per Harvard neurologist Sanjula Singh, work one risk factor at a time — improving one very often improves multiple simultaneously.

## Key Concepts
- **Causal loop diagram**: A systems-dynamics map where solid lines mark positive effects and dotted lines mark negative feedback; used to find common nodes and identify intervention points.
- **Neurovascular coupling**: The mechanism by which a challenged brain region draws increased blood supply for oxygen and nutrients — the physical link between stimulus and supply.
- **Band of potential cognitive function**: Adapted from Hertzog et al. — you do not follow one predetermined line but occupy a wide band, and can raise your function at any age from any starting point.
- **Superagers**: People whose cognitive function stays stable from their fifties well into their eighties. They have better strength and fitness, less hypertension/diabetes/prediabetes, sleep better, and are more cognitively and socially engaged. Per Schaie's Seattle Longitudinal Study, most of us are capable of becoming superagers.
- **Allostatic load**: Cumulative unresolved stress that impairs the support arm.
- **Digital twin**: A modeled replica of your health data used to predict which intervention would most reduce *your* dementia risk.

## Reference Tables

**Genetic and biomarker testing — what it does and doesn't change**

| Test | What it tells you | Does it change what you should do? |
|---|---|---|
| **ApoE4** | Most common genetic risk factor for dementia and earlier death. ApoE3 is most common (~80% of copies), ApoE2 rarest, ApoE4 evolutionarily oldest. Effect varies by ancestry — *not* associated with higher dementia risk in the Bolivian Tsimané, the Yoruba in Nigeria, or certain Indigenous Northern American groups. | **No.** A large Finnish study found ApoE4 doesn't change *which* lifestyle factors matter — it *amplifies* the risk from excess alcohol and inactivity. ApoE4 carriers often get *more* benefit from physical activity, social engagement, Mediterranean diet, and blood-pressure treatment. It may change how *serious* you are, not what you do. |
| **Blood biomarkers** (Aβ40, Aβ42, pTau181/217/231, NfL, GFAP) | FDA-cleared for clinical use in 2025. In a ~2,000-person Swedish cohort over ~10 years, low levels meant very low dementia risk — but 5% with low markers still developed it, and the best marker combination only found a cutoff where 43% would be diagnosed. | **Not yet.** Even high-marker individuals had a >50% chance of not developing disease over the study. Requires expert interpretation. |
| **Polygenic risk scores** | Amalgamate small effects across hundreds/thousands of genes; may improve prediction when added to ApoE genotype. | **No.** Notoriously unreliable across populations, and healthier lifestyle lowers dementia risk regardless of score. |
| **Family history** | Real, but largely *shared environment and lifestyle*, not just DNA. A ~90,000-person Netherlands study found family history tracked with smoking and cardiometabolic risk factors. | **Use it as a starting point.** UK Biobank data (300,000+) found family-history risk dramatically reduced by a healthy dietary pattern, meeting government physical activity guidelines, sleeping at least six hours a night, and not smoking or drinking excessively. |

**Multidomain intervention trials — the actual protocols**

| Component | FINGER | Maintain Your Brain |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise | Aerobic: 2–5 moderate-intensity sessions of 30–60 min/week. Resistance: 1–3 sessions/week (2–3 sets of 8–20 reps across 8–10 exercises), 30–60 min. | 300 min/week moderate-to-vigorous aerobic. Resistance training 3×/week. Balance training every day. |
| Nutrition | Whole foods, limit sugar and alcohol. Vitamin D and fish oil supplementation if not obtained from diet. | Mediterranean pattern focused on whole foods. Counseled against excess alcohol. |
| Cognitive stimulus | 10–15 min online brain training 3×/week; social interaction via group meetings. | 45 min online brain training 3×/week for 10 weeks, then monthly. |
| Cardiovascular risk | Regular assessment of blood pressure, blood sugar, cholesterol; medication if needed. | N/A |
| Peace of mind | N/A | Online CBT via a digital mental health program over 10 weeks. |

Other trials: **POINTER** (US, ages 60–79) replicated FINGER using resistance + aerobic + balance training in a social setting, the MIND diet (with a monthly blueberry budget), BrainHQ 15–20 min 3×/week, and cardiovascular health coaching — significant gains in overall cognition, especially executive function and processing speed, equal across ApoE4 genotype and sex. **Maintain Your Brain**: 6,000+ adults 55+, personalized across four risk areas (diet, physical activity, cognitive activity, depression/anxiety) over 3 years — greater improvement in females. **SMARRT**: age 70+, personalized goals from ten targets including ≥8,000 steps/day, BP/diabetes control, CBT for depression or insomnia, MIND-style diet. **MCI trial** (n=50, 20 weeks): plant-based whole-foods diet + omega-3s, magnesium, vitamin B12, multivitamin, probiotic; 30 min walking/day + resistance 3×/week; yoga and breathing; online and in-person social groups — improved cognition and slowed decline in diagnosed mild cognitive impairment. A South Korean FINGER replication in MCI saw improvements after just **6 months**.

## Mental Models
- Think of the 3-S model as **train lines, not a checklist** — stimulus is the origin station and connects to everything; supply and support feed in to change structure and function.
- Use **"you can't change just one thing"** — increase physical activity and you automatically decrease sedentary time while improving blood pressure, blood sugar, sleep, and possibly food preferences.
- Think of **structure and function as tightly linked** — long-term functional improvement requires structural improvement, and together they *are* your headroom.
- Use the **Viagra finding** as the model's signature test case (see Worked Example) — when an intervention looks implausibly effective, ask which 3-S nodes it is quietly loading.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating dementia risk as a long checklist of independent factors**: The Lancet Commission's isolated percentages (7% hearing loss, 2% smoking, etc.) dilute reality — smoking *raises* diabetes, hypertension, presbycusis, cataracts, and depression. Quit smoking and the whole network shifts.
- **Chasing a single-pathway drug or supplement**: Countless single interventions have underwhelmed. Each person has a different balance of risk factors.
- **Letting the tracker become the goal**: If you're stressed by your sleep tracker or glucose monitor, or so focused on the perfect recovery score that you're not getting out into the world, the technology is a hindrance.
- **Intense exercise within an hour of bed**: Can delay melatonin release, increase arousal, make sleep onset harder, and reduce REM. Keep intense sessions to morning or afternoon; light evening exercise is fine.
- **Using exercise or crosswords as a substitute for sleep or relationships**: They offset *some* of the damage, but prolonged short sleep still raises inflammation, blood sugar, and blood pressure.
- **Acting on a genetic or biomarker test as if it changes the plan**: It doesn't, yet.

## Worked Example
**The Viagra finding, read through the 3-S model.** In 2024, UCL researchers used a UK health database of 250,000+ men and found that being prescribed a PDE5 inhibitor (Viagra, Cialis) was associated with *lower* Alzheimer's risk, strongest with regular prescriptions and strongest for Viagra specifically. A 2021 study of 7+ million people in US insurance databases had found something similar. The reflexive question — the one the journalist asked Wood — is mechanistic: does sildenafil act on the brain? Plausibly yes: it dilates vessels and could increase cerebral blood flow (**supply**), and in animal models it increases neurogenesis and decreases inflammation (**support**). But Wood's reading is the one generally left unsaid. *You go to your doctor for a Viagra prescription because there is a stimulus you want to respond to.* Men who seek that solution are more likely to be in relationships and willing to discuss intimate problems with a physician — meaning more social connection and social support (**stimulus** and **support**). And depending on how you do it, sexual activity is physical activity (**supply**). So the outsized brain benefit is most likely the synergy of hitting all three nodes at once, not a molecule. Which is exactly why nobody should queue up for a prescription to future-proof their brain — the magic is in the synergy, in the people who actually need it.

## Key Takeaways
1. Diagnose your weakest of the three S's, then choose an activity that loads it — preferring activities that load two or three at once.
2. Start with **one** concrete, enjoyable, achievable change. Two competing priorities usually produce zero.
3. Prefer coordinative + social + outdoor activity: it is the highest-leverage single choice because it hits all three nodes.
4. Sleep is the wheel-greaser. Fifteen to thirty extra minutes of sleep opportunity per day is a legitimate starting intervention.
5. Don't test your way to a plan — ApoE4, polygenic scores, and blood biomarkers change your motivation, not your actions.
6. Exercise partially rescues poor sleep: in UK Biobank (400,000 people), the elevated dementia risk of ≤6 hours' sleep was offset by as little as **15–20 minutes of brisk walking or 7 minutes of intense exercise per day**. Seven hours of sleep gave both the lowest dementia risk and the largest brains.
7. A **20–30 minute moderate aerobic session** (brisk walk, light jog) improves almost every aspect of cognitive function and boosts mood regardless of how well you slept — with possibly greater benefit when you slept badly.
8. Multidomain, personalized, and modest beats single-domain and heroic — FINGER, POINTER, Maintain Your Brain, and SMARRT all replicate this.
