# Chapter 19: Social Networking Sites and Marketing

## Core Idea
Social networking sites hate overt commercial messages, so acceptable marketing means building an authentic presence — usually an offline personality creating a page — and adding value to a niche community rather than blasting product pitches.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Acceptable social-network marketing**: an offline personality (band, character, expert, cause) creates a page to build/expand a following; content must fit the specific community and site.
  - **Choose the right site** (each occupies a niche, per comScore): MySpace = broadest age range; Facebook = college crowd; Friendster = higher % adults; Xanga = younger teens.
- **Squidoo lenses**: a presence built on *expertise*, not personal profile — a "lens" filters your knowledge of a niche onto one page that points visitors to blogs, links, RSS, photos, Amazon books. "It's a place to start, not finish."
- **Optimizing social-network pages** (the checklist): target a specific niche audience; be a thought leader (show expertise, not product blather); be authentic and transparent (no impersonation — "if your mother would say it's wrong, it probably is"); create lots of links (yours *and* others'); encourage contact and follow up; participate (create/lead groups); make it easy to find (tags, del.icio.us/DIGG bookmarks); experiment ("no such thing as an expert in social networking").
- **New-media rule**: "participate in it; don't just try to use it" — be part of the action.

## Key Concepts
- **Lensmaster**: a person who creates a Squidoo lens ("Everyone is an expert").
- **Fictional character marketing**: an obviously made-up, fun persona works (VW's Helga) because it's transparent, not a fake real person.
- **Second Life / virtual worlds**: a "3-D web page" — an emerging presence channel; Reuters got huge buzz simply for opening the first virtual news bureau (rewarded for *trying*).
- **Avatar vs. person question**: in a virtual world, whom do you market to — the avatar or the human behind it?

## Mental Models
- **First-mover in new media wins**: some of the first blogs are still the most popular in their niches; being early to a new channel earns lasting popularity.
- **Add to the experience**: the most successful presences enrich the community (Huyse: not just a billboard — open a club, bring your rocker friends).
- Social pages still **lead into your buying process**: Guster links to tickets, GAF links to donations — not advertising, but a funnel.

## Anti-patterns
- **Overt commercial messages / fake profiles**: the community erupts when it uncovers agency-created sock-puppet profiles.
- **"Put up a billboard and leave"**: brands that don't grow with the community fail.
- **Being on every site**: unnecessary — each site has a different audience; pick the ones that match your persona.

## Worked Example
**Vince Ciulla's Squidoo lens** (30-year auto technician). His "Auto Repair — Trouble Shooting" lens funnels DIY car owners to his 20,000-page main site (9,000 Q&As). "Fixing your car is easy; the hard part is figuring out what's wrong." Most traffic comes from search (210,000 search terms point to his pages) but Squidoo adds more — with minimal maintenance. He monetizes via site advertising and ~2,500 paid phone consultations, plus a syndicated radio guest slot and a Squidoo group with other auto-repair lensmasters. Scott's own five lenses take a few hours each yet rank among the top five referrers to his blog. Contrast **VW's Helga** MySpace page — a transparently fictional, offbeat German character (Likes: "the smell of gasoline… Efficiency") with 8,632 friends — advertising that works *because* it's obviously made-up and fun, versus sleazy fake profiles of supposed real product users.

## Key Takeaways
1. Build an authentic presence (offline personality) and add value to a niche — don't broadcast pitches.
2. Pick the site(s) matching your persona; you don't need all of them.
3. Squidoo lenses turn expertise into a low-effort, high-referral presence.
4. Transparency is non-negotiable; fake profiles cause irreparable harm.
5. Be a first-mover in new channels and *participate* — Reuters earned buzz simply for trying Second Life.
