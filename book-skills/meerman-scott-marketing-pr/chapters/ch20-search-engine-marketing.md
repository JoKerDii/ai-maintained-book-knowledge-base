# Chapter 20: Search Engine Marketing

## Core Idea
Search engine marketing is the one form of marketing that doesn't interrupt — buyers actively want the content. And the whole book *is* a search engine marketing program: build great buyer-focused content with the words buyers use, and search engines index it. Enhancements come from the long tail of search terms and great landing pages.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Three definitions**:
  - **Search engine marketing** — using search engines (general and vertical) to reach buyers directly.
  - **Search engine optimization (SEO)** — ensuring your content is found and highly ranked in *natural* results.
  - **Search engine advertising** — paying for text ads (Google AdWords, Yahoo! Search Marketing) triggered by purchased keywords; placement = bid × click-through rate.
- **The long tail of search**: don't optimize on broad generic terms ("travel" = 124M hits) — create separate programs for dozens/hundreds/thousands of specific phrases buyers actually search ("Costa Rica rainforest tour").
  - **Metaphor**: not one huge drag net (expensive, mostly discarded), but thousands of individually baited hooks on a long line.
- **Carve out your own search real estate**: choose company/product/personal names you can "own" on search — run a web search before committing; drop names with lots of competitors (even in other industries). (Why Scott uses "Meerman" — too many David Scotts.)
- **Landing page guidelines**: short copy + simple graphics; your company's look/tone; written from the prospect's POV; communication not advertising; include a happy-customer quote; make it self-contained (sometimes no links out); clear, easy call to action; multiple calls to action; ask only for necessary info (email minimum); follow up fast.

## Key Concepts
- **Natural vs. paid results**: algorithm-ranked organic listings vs. bid-based text ads.
- **Landing page**: a targeted page for the phrase/offer a searcher clicked — most orgs are "terrible" at building them.
- **Bait-and-switch failure**: ranking for a phrase then dumping searchers on a generic homepage — they click away in a second.
- **Homepage dumping mistake**: sending all bought-keyword traffic to the homepage, which can't serve any single term well.

## Mental Models
- **SEO is not trickery**: ignore "Top Rankings Guaranteed!" snake-oil — "the single best thing you can do is focus on building great content for your buyers."
- **You need dozens/hundreds of landing pages**, one per related term cluster.
- **Content strategy first, enhancements second**: execute the book's plan and you already have SEM; add long-tail terms and landing pages for even more results "for months and years to come."

## Anti-patterns
- **Optimizing on generic keywords**: broad terms don't convert and aren't how people really search.
- **Buying keywords → homepage**: wastes the click.
- **Long sign-up forms**: people abandon them; ask the minimum.
- **Shady SEO manipulation**: keyword tricks over real content.

## Worked Example
**Scala, Inc.** (Gerard Bucas) sells "digital signage" in a market so fragmented buyers can't agree on the category name (Digital In-Store Merchandising, Electronic Display Networks, Electronic Billboards…) and don't gather at any one show, magazine, or portal — so Scala "can't possibly advertise in so many places" and relies on SEM. Bucas continuously monitors the top 30–40 search terms buyers use; when new terms appear he writes content for them and expands as they grow. He targets closely related variants separately ("digital signage," "digital sign," "digital signs" — each gives different results), and uses news releases + case studies to seed long-tail phrases. Search drives buyers to landing pages with offers (free demo DVD) that convert to leads funneled to resellers — a system managing 4,000+ open leads, with **over 50% of Scala's business coming from the Web**. Contrast Scott's Costa Rica trip: he searched "Costa Rica Adventure Travel," ignored generic airline/hotel landing pages (bait-and-switch), and booked with Costa Rica Expeditions because its landing page had the best targeted content.

## Key Takeaways
1. SEM doesn't interrupt — buyers want the content; the whole book builds an SEM program.
2. Play the long tail: many specific phrases, not a few generic ones.
3. Choose names you can "own" on search.
4. Build a dedicated, buyer-POV landing page per term cluster — never dump to the homepage.
5. Great content beats SEO trickery; add long-tail terms and landing pages as enhancements.
