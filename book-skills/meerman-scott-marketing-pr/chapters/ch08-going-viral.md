# Chapter 8: Going Viral — The Web Helps Audiences Catch the Fever

## Core Idea
Viral marketing means having other people tell your story. You can't reliably manufacture it, but you can seed many attempts, monitor for eruptions you didn't start, and — when you're sitting on genuinely explosive news — nurture it across the news cycle for maximum effect.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The viral formula**: great (and free) Web content that is groundbreaking / amazing / hilarious / celebrity-involving + a network of people to light the fire + links that make sharing effortless.
- **The venture-capital / studio model of viral**: since no one knows which attempt will hit, run a portfolio.
  - **How**: create many small campaigns (like GoldenPalace's ~1-in-20 eBay stunts), see what catches, then nurture the winners.
- **Push it along (don't just ride)**: when a positive viral event you didn't start erupts, actively support it.
  - **How (Mentos)**: link to it from your official site, offer the creators support, add real-world presence, launch a follow-on contest with an authentic prize.
- **Nurturing explosive news across the cycle**: when you hold news with viral potential, plan a detailed timeline of who you tell and when.
  - **How (Outsell)**: hint to paying clients first; give selected media an early embargoed look; let one outlet break it; then feed the wave of bloggers and mainstream follow-ups.

## Key Concepts
- **Word-of-blog / buzz**: other people spreading your story for free.
- **Homemade beats slick**: an amateur, authentic feel spreads; polished corporate gimmicks feel forced and get "shot down."
- **Fake viral / astroturf**: paying people to write about you — the Web's collective investigative reporting smokes it out and damages reputations.
- **The brand-meaning feedback loop**: consumers determine what a brand means; the Web makes that loop visible in real time (Healy: "be genuine... the personality of the brand").
- **Embargo**: giving media an early look under an agreed publish-no-earlier-than date.

## Mental Models
- Treat the Internet as **the town square**: be genuine about what your brand actually is; don't pretend it's more than it is.
- **Monitor so "Huh?" is never your answer**: when your brand spikes 10× in blog mentions, know why before the Wall Street Journal calls.
- Don't be a **corporate nanny**: Coca-Cola distanced Diet Coke from the geyser videos and took hits; Mentos embraced them and won free exposure.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trying to guarantee virality**: "virtually impossible" — huge luck and timing required.
- **Faking it**: staged viral campaigns rarely succeed and can cause great harm.
- **Distancing yourself from organic buzz** (Coca-Cola) instead of leveraging it.

## Worked Example
**Mentos + Diet Coke (eepybird).** Fritz Grobe and Stephen Voltz posted videos of Mentos-and-Diet-Coke geysers; the extreme "200 liters + 500 mints" version hit 4 million views in three weeks, hundreds of blogs, then Letterman and the Today Show. Pete Healy, VP marketing at Mentos-maker Perfetti Van Melle, recognized the brand personality fit ("if our brand were a person, it'd be Adam Sandler — quirky, fun") and pushed it: linked from the official site, sent the "Mentos ride" convertible to the TV appearances, and launched a geyser-video contest whose prize (1,000 iTunes downloads + a year of Mentos) was chosen to fit the media-savvy entrants — ~100 videos, ~1 million site views. **Outsell click-fraud report** shows the news-nurturing variant: hint to clients → embargoed advisory to select media → San Francisco Chronicle breaks it July 5 → 100+ bloggers in five days → ~100 mainstream stories in week one → BusinessWeek cover story. A tiny research firm reached national scale by timing the release.

## Key Takeaways
1. Viral = others telling your story; you can seed and amplify, not command it.
2. Run a portfolio of attempts; nurture whatever catches.
3. Homemade and authentic spreads; slick and fake gets exposed.
4. Monitor constantly so you can leverage (or contain) eruptions you didn't start.
5. For truly big news, plan the who/when timeline and use embargoes to build the wave.
