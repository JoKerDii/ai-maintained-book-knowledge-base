# Chapter 16: The New Rules for Reaching the Media

## Core Idea
Broadcast pitching is spam and has made "PR" synonymous with "spammer." The media still matter for legitimacy, but you reach them by being findable, by pitching bloggers, and by sending individually targeted pitches to reporters whose work you've actually read.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The New Rules of Media Relations**:
  - Nontargeted broadcast pitches are spam; releases sent on subjects a reporter doesn't cover are spam.
  - Reporters who don't know you are searching for orgs like yours — make sure they find you on Google/Technorati.
  - If you blog, reporters who cover the space will find you; pitch bloggers (blog coverage gets you noticed by mainstream media).
  - Keep the org "busy" with releases; give journalists a great online media room; offer RSS.
  - Build personal relationships; don't tell journalists what your product *does* — tell them how you solve customer problems.
  - Read/comment on/track back to the reporter's blog; read the outlet before pitching; then send an individualized pitch.
- **How to Pitch the Media** (the tactical rules):
  - **Target one reporter at a time** — reference a specific article; craft a targeted subject line.
  - **Help the journalist see the big picture** — situate your product in a wider trend.
  - **Explain how customers use your product** — offer customer interviews and case studies, not spec recitations.
  - **Don't send unrequested attachments** — plain-text email; attachments only when asked.
  - **Follow up promptly** as promised.
  - **Remember it's a two-way street** — reporters need smart ideas and good pitches.

## Key Concepts
- **PR spam**: shotgun-blasting releases/pitches to hundreds of journalists regardless of beat — brands you a "bad guy."
- **The new moment of truth**: "Now the moment of truth is a link to your site from a blog" (Larry Schwartz) — a Gizmodo mention + link can beat a Wall Street Journal mention for niche products.
- **Media legitimacy**: coverage lends credibility and gives you reprints/links to point prospects to.
- **Targeted subject line**: e.g. "PR pitch for Boston Globe Reporter Peter Howe" beats a cute line, which reads as spam.

## Mental Models
- Treat **influential bloggers exactly like influential reporters**: read them, send targeted useful info, offer interviews/samples/lunch.
- **Become a source, not a shill**: join the reporter's network by sending non-pitch value and commenting on their blog.
- **Do the 10-minute homework**: "put 'Boston Globe Peter Howe' into Google News, read the first ten hits — you'd have done more work than 98% of the PR people who pitch me" (Peter Howe).
- **Think big**: pitch the small thing, but frame the bigger page-one story it fits into — better to appear in a front-page trend piece (even beside competitors) than a 120-word brief.

## Anti-patterns
- **Broadcast/blind pitches**: indiscriminate barrage — ineffective and reputation-damaging.
- **Pitching without knowing the beat**: no sense of what the outlet covers — "you are really wasting your own time."
- **Product-feature pitches**: reporters hear hundreds; customer-in-action stories win.
- **Unrequested attachments / cute subject lines**: deleted as spam.

## Worked Example
**Peter Howe, Boston Globe business reporter** (20 years, covering telecom/Internet/energy/airlines). His single most effective PR interaction: "read what I write and send me personalized, smart pitches for stories I am actually likely to write." He gets 400–500 emails/day, so a plain descriptive subject line ("PR pitch for Boston Globe Reporter Peter Howe") signals not-spam while a cute one gets junked. His maddening complaint: PR people who don't know his beat — 10 minutes of Google News homework would beat 98% of pitchers. Counter-example from Scott's own inbox: a sales-lead-management company's PR person had read his blog, knew his interests, and sent a perfectly positioned pitch — Scott emailed back within minutes to book a CEO interview. And **John Blossom** (Shore Communications) shows the pull side: blogging since 2003 made him a "media personality" quoted by the Financial Times based on his blog alone, without even speaking to the reporter.

## Key Takeaways
1. Broadcast pitching is spam; individualized, beat-aware pitches win.
2. Be findable (Google/Technorati, a great media room) so reporters come to you.
3. Pitch bloggers like reporters — the blog link is the new moment of truth.
4. Pitch the customer story and the big-picture trend, not product features.
5. Plain-text, targeted subject lines, prompt follow-up; do the homework first.
