# Chapter 13: How Web Content Influences the Buying Process

## Core Idea
The moment of truth is when a prospect reaches your site: content organized around the buyer's self-selection and their stage in the buying cycle either draws them into the sales process or lets them click away.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Buyer-centric navigation / self-selection**: organize the site by how buyers self-identify (job function, geography, industry, or *problem*), never by your internal org chart (product/geography/governmental structure).
  - **How**: create homepage links that move each persona from the homepage to pages built for them — e.g. a supply-chain firm's dropdown: "I need to get product to customers faster" / "I want to move products internationally."
- **Content mapped to the sales cycle** (early → middle → late):
  - **Early**: basic info on the problem and how you solve it.
  - **Middle** ("a friendly nudge"): product/service intro kept buyer-focused; offer newsletter/webinar/podcast subscriptions (give equal value for any contact info); be responsive — buyers test you.
  - **Late** ("close the sale"): demos and requirement-matching tools; then **continue the conversation** — add customers to newsletters/community, solicit feedback.
- **Elements of a buyer-centric site** (checklist): serve preferred media/learning styles (text + audio + video, not either/or); develop a site personality (tone/voice); use real (not stock) photos; add interactive tools; provide feedback loops ("contact us," "rate this"); let customers interact with each other; create pass-along/viral content with permanent URLs.
- **RSS to targeted niches**: syndicate discrete content items so micro-audiences self-select welcome updates (Netflix genre feeds) instead of blasted generic email.
- **Measure and improve**: benchmark self-select links; A/B test landing-page offers by clicks *and* by revenue-per-offer; test link ordering.

## Key Concepts
- **Buying cycle length**: seconds (an iTunes song) to months/years (B2B with multiple personas, or a college applicant contacted as a freshman).
- **Site personality**: the consistent tone that builds an emotional relationship (Google's "I'm Feeling Lucky" playful vs. Accenture's "High Performance. Delivered." serious).
- **Pass-along value**: content interesting enough that visitors share the link — helped by easy sharing and permanent URLs.
- **Stock-photo trap**: generic models signal fakeness; "Neither you nor your users are generic."

## Mental Models
- **The homepage must reflect the buyer back at himself**: "The first thing he needs to see is a reflection of himself."
- **Editors obsess over readership — so should you.** People come for content, not advertising.
- **Give equal value for any contact info** you request.

## Anti-patterns
- **Site organized by internal structure**: confuses buyers who don't think in your org chart.
- **Online brochure / one-way advertising vehicle**: "almost wholly ineffective."
- **Untargeted "special offers" email blasts**: high opt-out; contrast with Netflix's welcome, self-selected RSS.
- **Locking your community behind a password**: hides the vibrant proof of a healthy customer base.

## Worked Example
**SugarCRM's open-source marketing model** (John Roberts). Competitors spend ~$7 in sales/marketing per $1 of product development, fielding armies of salespeople. Roberts instead uses Web content *as* the marketing engine: the free, no-registration open-source SugarForge project (200 projects, 60 languages) plus an open user forum and SugarWiki — "investing in engineering... makes the phone ring." The paid enterprise version (SugarCRM.com) is kept strictly separate ("church and state") — the free version is *not* baitware. Role-based demos serve five identified personas (salespeople, marketers, support, executives, admins). Result: 900+ paying customers in two years via "an online ecosystem bigger and more valuable than a closed proprietary company." **New York Public Library** illustrates persona self-selection: one site serving academic researchers, Spanish-speaking Bronx residents, tourists, film studios, and donors — front-page sections ("Find Books & Do Research," "Digital Library," "Support the Library") route each to its own landing page.

## Key Takeaways
1. Organize navigation by buyer self-selection and problems, not your org chart.
2. Map content to the buyer's stage: educate early, nudge in the middle, close and continue late.
3. Serve multiple learning styles; build a consistent site personality; use real photos.
4. Deliver content via RSS to self-selecting niches instead of blasting generic email.
5. Measure clicks *and* revenue-per-offer; keep improving.
6. Open communities and free products can out-market a big closed sales force.
