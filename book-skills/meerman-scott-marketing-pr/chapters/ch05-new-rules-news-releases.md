# Chapter 5: The New Rules of News Releases

## Core Idea
Press releases were never really just for the press — millions now read them directly, unfiltered by the media. Reframe them as "news releases" written for buyers, stuffed with the keywords buyers search, and linked to your sales process.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The New Rules of News Releases**:
  - Don't wait for "big news" — find reasons to send releases all the time.
  - Write for your buyers, not just a handful of journalists.
  - Make copy keyword-rich (target the exact phrases buyers search).
  - Include offers that compel a response.
  - Place links to landing pages on your site.
  - Optimize delivery for both searching and browsing.
  - Add social-media tags (Technorati, DIGG, del.icio.us).
  - Drive people into the sales process.
  - **When to use**: every release. Full execution details in Ch 14.
- **Direct-to-consumer news release**: renamed from "press release" (the word *press* throws people off) — a release aimed at buyers via search engines and RSS, not only reporters.

## Key Concepts
- **Disintermediation of the media**: the Web removes the journalist as required middleman; buyers read your release directly.
- **Keyword-rich copy**: releases written around the phrases buyers actually type (Colin Smith: phrases beat single keywords).
- **Landing page**: the destination a release link drives buyers to, tied to an offer (e.g. a free trial).
- **Coexistence**: a direct-to-consumer strategy sits alongside — not instead of — media relations.

## Mental Models
- Reframe **PR's definition** (per PRSA): PR is about adapting to your "publics" — nowhere does it say *media*. Objections are "based on fear, not the facts."
- Think **just-in-time, just-right publisher**: you deliver useful info directly onto buyers' screens, no press involved.
- Don't let keywords **dominate** the writing — insert phrases where natural; readability still matters.

## Anti-patterns
- **"Direct-to-consumer press releases suck"** thinking (the traditional PR objection): clinging to the fiction that only a dozen reporters see releases.
- **Abusing the channel**: flooding low-value releases erodes credibility with both journalists and buyers.
- **Sending releases only for major announcements**: you forfeit continuous discoverability.

## Worked Example
**WebEx** (Colin Smith, PR director). Scott, prepping a keynote, googled the phrase "accelerate sales cycle" — the #1 organic result *and* the #1 Google News result were both WebEx news releases, because Smith had researched that buyers of WebEx's services search that exact phrase and optimized releases around it. The release headline led with a product launch, embedded the target phrase in the first sentence, and linked directly to a free-trial offer. WebEx tracks how many readers go from release → free trial (significant numbers). ~80% of WebEx releases are product/customer-related; 20% are regulatory. Smith also newsjacks events — donating free service during the Boston Big Dig closures and the NYC transit strike — to create loyal future users. Scott then re-amplified the example to 10,000+ live audience members and 200,000+ e-book downloads: the release reached a buyer who told others.

## Key Takeaways
1. Rename and reframe: news releases speak to buyers, not just press.
2. Publish continuously, not only on "big news."
3. Research and embed the exact phrases your buyers search; phrases beat single keywords.
4. Every release needs an offer and a link into the sales process — and track the conversion.
5. Keep a parallel media-relations track; the two coexist.
