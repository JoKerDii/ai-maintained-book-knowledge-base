# Chapter 17: Blogging to Reach Your Buyers

## Core Idea
The how-to of running your own blog: pick a narrow niche you're passionate about, write authentically for one buyer persona, follow blogging ethics, set up the software correctly, and build an audience over time.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **What to blog about**: start by asking "Whom do I want to reach?" (usually buyers, customers, and influencers/analysts/media); find a topic you're genuinely passionate about; **start narrow** and expand — most first-timers try to cover too much.
- **Blogging ethics (the five checks)**:
  - **Transparency** — never pretend to be someone you're not (no fake-name comments, no undisclosed company blogs).
  - **Privacy** — don't blog what was disclosed to you without permission.
  - **Disclosure** — flag conflicts of interest (e.g. writing about a client).
  - **Truthfulness** — don't lie or invent customer stories.
  - **Credit** — link to sources whose ideas you use.
  - Gut check: "What would your mother say about that post?"
- **Blogging setup basics**: pick a name/tagline carefully (hard to change once indexed); choose software (Blogger, TypePad, WordPress) wisely (switching loses content/subscribers); pick a URL; allow comments (favor open debate; guard against comment spam); enable trackbacks (approve first); use categories + social tags; enable RSS; include an "About" page with photo/bio; make contact easy (obfuscate email against harvesters).
- **"Pimp out your blog"**: express personality — custom masthead, book/Amazon links, links to your other content, "Best of" navigation (since reverse-chronological hides your best older posts), RSS (FeedBurner) and email (FeedBlitz) subscription options.
- **Building an audience**: expect initial silence; link from your homepage/product pages/media room/email signatures; post frequently for search rankings; comment on and trackback to blogs in your space; use traffic stats to improve. Tag posts (Technorati) so buyers find them beyond noisy keyword search.

## Key Concepts
- **Niche escapes the competition**: you're not competing with 57M blogs — a "Kansas family law" blog competes with almost none and reaches exactly the right readers (long tail, Ch 2).
- **Independent voice**: the blog's look should complement but *not* be identical to corporate design — signal it's authentic, not corporate-speak.
- **Trackback**: a message another blogger sends when they reference your post — like a comment that points to their post.
- **Tags vs. keywords**: meta-tags categorize posts so searchers avoid false hits ("tires" NASCAR/freeway-garbage/"spare tires on middle-aged men").
- **Review content, not words**: if posts must be reviewed, reviewers focus on content only — don't let them turn authentic writing into gobbledygook.

## Mental Models
- **Think like a publisher**: use traffic data to learn what resonates and improve.
- **Competitors offline are cooperative online**: bloggers link back and forth "like all the auto dealers on the same street — proximity is good for everyone."
- If **commenting is a painful chore**, maybe you're not cut out to blog — that's fine (far more readers than writers).

## Anti-patterns
- **Flogging products "on message"**: the exact wrong way to blog.
- **Passing every post through PR/legal**: kills authenticity.
- **Covering too much**: dilutes the niche advantage.
- **Ethics violations**: sock-puppet comments, undisclosed conflicts, invented stories, uncredited ideas.

## Worked Example
**Grant Griffiths, Kansas family/divorce lawyer.** His blog (started March 2005) is written for one tight persona: "not for other attorneys; I write for the public — people in Kansas who need a family lawyer." He calls it "my storefront, shingle, office sign, newspaper ad, and yellow-pages ad." Results: ~a dozen email inquiries/week and 2–3 new cases/week; he ranks top-of-page-one for Kansas + family law and dropped his yellow-pages ad entirely — while other lawyers stay scared to. **Linas Simonis (Lithuania)** shows the international version: one of Lithuania's first business blogs (April 2005) hit 400 email subscribers in a country of 3.5M (≈40,000 US-equivalent); his post "How to Position Lithuania" earned two prime-time TV appearances, radio, and a dozen print pickups — no pitching. Three months in, his firm stopped cold-calling; clients came to them, and he was booked for speeches and consulting.

## Key Takeaways
1. Blog a narrow niche you're passionate about, for one persona, in an authentic independent voice.
2. Follow the five ethics checks; gut-check with "what would my mother say?"
3. Choose name/tagline/software/URL carefully — they're hard to change once indexed.
4. Favor open comments and debate; enable trackbacks, tags, and RSS.
5. Build audience over time: cross-link everywhere, post frequently, comment on peers, use stats to improve.
