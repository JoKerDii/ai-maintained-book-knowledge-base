# Chapter 18: Podcasting and Video Made, Well, as Easy as Possible

## Core Idea
The how-to of audio and video content: like all content it must target a buyer persona with a genuine human voice, but it adds a modest hardware/software/editing investment — and lets small players out-compete big ones by showing personality and expertise.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The podcast production steps**:
  1. **Show preparation** (most important) — gather ideas, script for polish, know guests' names/titles/URLs.
  2. **Recording** — microphone + software (CastBlaster); mobile gear (iRiver) for roving; phone-interview switch (Telos).
  3. **Editing** (optional) — micro (remove "um"/"uh") or macro (cut segments); GarageBand.
  4. **Postproduction** — noise reduction, sound compression.
  5. **Tagging** — text metadata that surfaces in search/iTunes and on iPod displays; don't skip.
  6. **Hosting & distribution** — services like Liberated Syndication syndicate to iTunes.
  7. **Promotion** — links to guests, cross-promote with other podcasters, site/email/business-card links, a news release.
  8. **Companion blog** — text that search engines index, describes each show, links guests, and moves listeners into the sales process.
- **Ways to get video to buyers**:
  - Post to video-sharing sites (YouTube) and hope for viral (Smirnoff "teapartay," IBM "The Art of the Sale" mockumentary).
  - Develop an online video channel (Weber Nation grilling classes, Ford Bold Moves).
  - Vlogging (video embedded in a blog with contextual text).
  - Vodcasting (video podcast tied to iTunes/RSS — BMW's weekly series).
  - Invite customer-submitted video contests (Mentos, Converse).

## Key Concepts
- **"Video is an extension of the blogging ethos"** (Owen Mack): do you have an interesting story? Transparency and openness required.
- **Time-shifted, attention-matched length**: Christopher Penn makes 18–24 min shows — 24 min = average commute, 18 min = average attention span.
- **Human voice, no PR stamp**: podcasts feel human because they lack the "on message" spin; "there is no such thing as 'on message' anymore."
- **The marketing shift**: from "we publish and you consume" (1950s TV) to "we publish and you respond."
- **Stealth-insertion danger**: the YouTube community rats out inauthentic corporate video sneaking in as consumer-generated.

## Mental Models
- **Compete on video where you can't compete on a website**: a 4-person shop can beat Williams-Sonoma by showing ethos, personality, and product knowledge on cheap "quick-and-dirty" video.
- **Match the medium to your persona's habits**: young people already have iPods/iTunes, so a podcast is perfect for them.
- **Marketing and customer service converge**: both need a real, authentic human voice; companies that don't jump to blogging/podcasting "look like dinosaurs."

## Anti-patterns
- **Skimping on show prep**: unscripted rambling sounds unpolished.
- **Ignoring audio tags**: hurts discoverability and the listener's iPod display.
- **Sales-pitch podcasts**: "the show is not a sales pitch" — be genuinely helpful; the business follows.
- **Stealth/fake video**: fraught with danger on YouTube.

## Worked Example
**Financial Aid Podcast / Student Loan Network** (Christopher Penn). A student-loan lender ($150–200M/year) runs a six-days-a-week podcast for a persona — college-bound young people — who "all have iPods, which is ideal." Penn covers credit cards, scholarships, private loans, and broader money topics in a human, non-PR voice; shows are 18–24 minutes to fit commutes and attention spans. He drives listeners into the sales process by mentioning URLs (not pitching) and has tracked **over $10 million in loans generated directly from podcasts** — "way beyond beer money." **KitchenArts** (Owen Mack) shows the small-player video play: a 4-person Boston kitchen store shoots quick-and-dirty vlogs (a $300 camera) demonstrating ~100 products (e.g. how to use kitchen knives), linked to its eBay store — total cost ~$50/month for eBay + blog software — out-personalizing Williams-Sonoma on terrific margins.

## Key Takeaways
1. Preparation and a script make audio sound polished; tag files for discoverability.
2. Always run a companion blog — it feeds search and moves listeners into the sales process.
3. Use a genuine human voice; podcasts/videos work because they're not "on message."
4. Match the medium to your persona's existing habits and attention span.
5. Small players can beat big ones on cheap, personality-rich video.
6. Never fake consumer-generated video; the community exposes it.
