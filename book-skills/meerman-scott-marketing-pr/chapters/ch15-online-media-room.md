# Chapter 15: The Online Media Room — Your Front Door for Much More Than the Media

## Core Idea
Your online media room isn't just for journalists — buyers, customers, partners, investors, and employees all visit it to learn what's current about you. So design it for buyers (which also makes it better for journalists), and control it yourself.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Design the media room for buyers, not just journalists**: radical to traditional PR, but the best media rooms are built with buyers in mind — and serve journalists better too.
- **Best-practices checklist for online media rooms**:
  - **You control the content** — use a dedicated media-room CMS (The Fuel Team, PR Newswire's MediaRoom) so you update instantly without IT/Webmaster/approval bottlenecks.
  - **Start with a needs analysis** — map to persona profiles and real journalist needs before design.
  - **Optimize for searching *and* browsing** — provide search plus multiple browse paths (by persona, product, geography, market); add printer-friendly PDF.
  - **Background/media kit** — history, timeline, exec bios, product info, analyst coverage, and customer case studies in the customers' own words.
  - **Multimedia** — pre-approved exec/product photos, logos, audio and video clips journalists can reuse.
  - **Detailed specs and data** — post the "proprietary"-feeling content (specs, price lists) that actually convinces journalists to write.
  - **Go global** if appropriate; **serve all levels of media understanding**; list **executive appearances/events**; include **calls to action for journalists** (interviews, trials, a journalist landing page); **embrace bloggers** as journalists; **avoid jargon/acronyms**.
- **RSS in the media room** ("Really Simple Marketing"): offer subscription feeds (like Intel's product/press/investor/country feeds) so media and buyers get just-right updates.

## Key Concepts
- **Media room as free SEO**: news-release pages update most often, so search algorithms rank them highest — frequency drives rankings ("ten releases great, twenty better, one hundred better still").
- **"Feed the beast"**: get news up instantly for journalists who work in real time.
- **Case studies in the customer's own words**: valuable to both journalists and buyers, and rarely done.
- **Static vs. fresh expectation**: visitors expect main pages static but the media room to reveal the very latest.

## Mental Models
- **"If your mother doesn't understand your news, a journalist probably won't either."**
- Think like a **publisher**: identify target audiences and build content for each; graphics take a back seat during needs analysis.
- A lack of recent releases signals a **dead company** — keep the room "busy."

## Anti-patterns
- **Online-brochure media rooms**: pretty design, a pile of jargon-filled releases, no buyer or journalist value.
- **Gating your best content**: hiding specs/prices behind registration or a PR gatekeeper — the exact content that wins stories.
- **Routing updates through IT**: kills the speed journalists need.
- **Cute email subject lines**: read as spam.

## Worked Example
**Cingular Wireless** (Clay Owen, ex-CNN producer). Frustrated that IT took "a week from Thursday" to post releases, Owen implemented a self-controlled online media room so he could publish instantly, optimized pages for keywords, and loaded it with images (high-res + web), fact sheets, and an online press kit — because "journalists are looking for value-add... much more than just a news release." He also fed and encouraged the wireless/gadget bloggers. The room was stress-tested in the 2005 hurricane season: he built a consumer section with an FAQ ("What if I can't pay my bill this month?") and toll-free numbers, and it became a channel to employees in disaster zones. Traffic proved it serves far more than media — from a ~2,000 page-views/day August baseline to a ~10,000/day September average and an 18,000 peak on Sept 5, 2005. Lesson: consumers turn to media rooms for current info, especially in emergencies.

## Key Takeaways
1. Everyone — not just journalists — uses your media room; design it for buyers.
2. Control it with a dedicated CMS so you can publish instantly, no IT bottleneck.
3. Optimize for both search and browse; give journalists a rich, jargon-free media kit with customer case studies.
4. Post the valuable "proprietary" content and multimedia that actually earns coverage.
5. Frequent releases = higher search rankings and a "busy company" signal; add RSS feeds.
