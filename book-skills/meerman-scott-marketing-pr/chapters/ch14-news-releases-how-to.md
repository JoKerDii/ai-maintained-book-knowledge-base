# Chapter 14: How to Use News Releases to Reach Buyers Directly

## Core Idea
The how-to execution of Chapter 5: run a news-release program like an online news service — write for buyers, publish continuously, distribute via a wire *and* your own site, embed buyer keywords and links, and tag socially — so buyers find you via search and RSS.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **"Big news is great, but don't wait"**: reasons to write a release — new take on an old problem, unique market served, interesting info, CEO speaking, award won, product feature added, new customer, whitepaper published. Continuous releases signal a "busy," active market player.
- **The dual-publish method**: simultaneously (1) post the release to your own site (media room), and (2) send it to a news-release wire (Business Wire, Market Wire, PrimeNewswire, PR Newswire, PRWeb).
  - **Why both**: the wire gets you indexed on Google News/Yahoo/vertical sites (short-term); your own permanent link keeps it findable for years after the wire archives expire.
  - **Coverage tip**: buy the cheapest geographic distribution — online news inclusion (Google News) comes with any geography; broad newsroom reach matters less when your goal is search/RSS, not journalists.
- **Links do double duty**: links from a release (1) drive buyers to landing pages in your sales process, and (2) raise your site's search rankings via inbound links from every site that republishes the release.
- **Social media news release template** (Todd Defren/SHIFT): a format that reminds you to include tags (Technorati, DIGG, del.icio.us), photos, and audio feeds so the release is maximally findable.

## Key Concepts
- **Keyword-rich copy**: use the exact words/phrases buyers search — "news releases are only as valuable as the keywords and phrases contained in them."
- **RSS distribution**: tag a release to a market category so RSS subscribers and keyword-alert users receive it in near-real-time.
- **Social media tags**: make releases surface in Technorati/DIGG/del.icio.us category feeds alongside blog posts.
- **Repurpose**: rewrite releases into newsletter paragraphs, RSS feeds, and internal comms — "if it's important enough to tell the media, tell your clients, prospects, and employees too."

## Mental Models
- Run the release program as an **editorial calendar**, like a publisher — informed by buyer persona research.
- **"For a hundred bucks you can talk to the world"** (Hennigan) — write releases like news stories.
- **Frequency compounds rankings**: "if you do ten news releases, that's great, twenty is better, one hundred is better still."

## Anti-patterns
- **Jargon in releases**: "don't get trapped by your own lexicon" — write in the buyer's words.
- **Only telling journalists**: burying releases in a hard-to-find news section instead of repurposing across channels.
- **Sending releases only on big news**: forfeits continuous discoverability and the "busy company" signal.
- **No links / no landing pages**: wastes the SEO and sales-funnel benefit.

## Worked Example
**CruiseCompete.com** (Heidi Allison-Shane). Ahead of the 2006 holidays, a Market Wire release headlined "Cruise Lines Set Sail With Hot Holiday Vacation Prices" deliberately embedded three buyer search phrases in an early sentence — "Thanksgiving cruises," "Christmas cruises," "New Year's cruises" — each hyperlinked to a purpose-built landing page showing exactly those deals. Results: the release topped Google News for all three phrases, *and* the inbound links pushed the landing pages up organic search — the "Christmas cruise" page ranked 4th of 5,830,000 hits. Every targeted release produced a spike in site traffic. Her summary: "simply understanding what people are likely to be searching on and then linking them to the correct page... This stuff is not difficult."

## Key Takeaways
1. Write for buyers on a continuous editorial calendar — don't wait for big news.
2. Dual-publish: wire (for search/RSS reach) + your own permanent site link (for longevity).
3. Embed the exact phrases buyers search; every release is only as good as its keywords.
4. Include links — they funnel buyers *and* raise your search rankings.
5. Add social tags (use the social media release template); repurpose releases across all channels.
