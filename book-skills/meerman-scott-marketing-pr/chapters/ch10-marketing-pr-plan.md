# Chapter 10: You Are What You Publish — Building Your Marketing and PR Plan

## Core Idea
The core action-plan chapter: set your organization's real goals, put your products aside, deeply understand your buyer personas, learn the exact words they use, decide what you want each to believe, then publish content built for them.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The four-step planning sequence** (the book's action plan):
  1. **Set organizational goals** — revenue growth, donations, votes, applications; not marketing "balls."
  2. **Identify & profile buyer personas** — segment buyers, build a biography for each.
  3. **Learn buyers' own words** + decide **what you want each to believe** (messages).
  4. **Develop content** — an editorial plan/calendar publishing to each persona in their preferred media.
- **The Buyer Persona Profile**: "a short biography of the typical customer, not just a job description but a person description" (Adele Revella).
  - **How**: capture goals, aspirations, problems, daily activities, current solutions, the media they trust, and their exact words/phrases. Best built by **interviewing** real people who fit the persona, then triangulating with the publications/sites/blogs they consume. Name the persona (like "NASCAR Dads," "Security Moms," "Sam the Athlete") and even clip a representative photo.
- **In Your Buyers' Own Words**: research and use the precise phrases buyers search — critical for both relationship and search engine ranking.

## Key Concepts
- **Buyer persona / microtarget**: a representative of a buyer type with a specific problem your product solves.
- **The soccer analogy**: marketers (and their management "coaches") chase the "ball" (leads, clips, traffic) instead of the "goal" (revenue). Seven-year-olds swarm the ball; so do most marketing departments.
- **Rejecting the four Ps**: standard product/place/price/promotion education is "nonsense" under the new rules — start with buyers, not product.
- **Editorial plan/calendar**: a scheduled content program (site pages, e-book, whitepaper, blog, releases) per persona.
- **"You are what you publish"**: the plan's guiding maxim.

## Mental Models
- **"Listen before you talk"** (Revella): building personas eliminates the agony of guessing and endless message-debate meetings.
- Ask **"What is each buyer persona really buying?"** — Volvo sells safety, Starbucks sells a place to hang out / meeting spot / wifi / splurge, not just coffee.
- Different personas have **different problems** — even Gatorade could serve the athlete *and* the hungover young professional.
- You don't need a full site redesign: **add persona-specific pages and links** ("high school athletes start here") and leave the rest.

## Anti-patterns
- **Marketing to-do-list goals**: trade shows, logos, T-shirts, clips, traffic — none appear on a mission statement or balance sheet.
- **Wrong success measures**: unique visitors and press mentions instead of revenue/donations/members.
- **One-size-fits-all brochure site**: organized by your products, not by persona problems.
- **Giving up under pushback**: old-rules colleagues, agencies, and comms teams will resist — "They are wrong."

## Worked Example
**Shareholder.com Whistleblower Hotline** (Bradley Smith). Entering a market with zero share against incumbents, the team interviewed CFOs, read what buyers read (CFO, Directors Monthly), even read the full Sarbanes-Oxley text, and mined the exact phrases buyers use ("SEC mandates," "complete audit trail," "Sarbanes-Oxley rule 301," "confidential and anonymous submission"). They built thought-leadership content — a webinar "Whistleblower Hotlines: More than a Mandate" featuring ex-SEC chair Harvey Pitt and Enron author Lynn Brewer — which gave them search terms ("Harvey Pitt," "Enron") and a celebrity draw; 600 watched live. Result: #1 of 258,000 Google hits for "whistleblower hotline," a Wall Street Journal mention, and 75 clients in four months. Expanding to Canada, fresh persona research found *different* phrases ("governance hotline," "forensic accounting investigation") so they built separate Canadian content — unlike rivals who reused US materials. **Pleo the robot dinosaur** (UGOBE, Diana Stern): a survey (42% response) revealed unexpected personas — not just 7–12-year-olds but 25+ male tech early-adopters (→ shipped an SDK for hacking) and nurturing women in their 40s–60s (→ positioned Pleo as a "family pet"). Each persona got tailored messaging and channels.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run the four steps in order: goals → personas → words/beliefs → content.
2. Chase the goal (revenue), not the ball (leads, clips, traffic).
3. Build named buyer-persona biographies from real interviews; use their exact search phrases.
4. Decide what each persona should believe; different personas buy different things.
5. Persona pages can be added incrementally — no full redesign needed.
6. Expect resistance from old-rules colleagues; hold the line.
