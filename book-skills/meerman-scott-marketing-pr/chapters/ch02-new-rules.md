# Chapter 2: The New Rules of Marketing and PR

## Core Idea
On the Web, marketing and PR converge into one discipline whose engine is content: great content in all its forms (blogs, releases, sites, podcasts) reaches niche buyers directly at the moment they're searching, and drives them to action.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The New Rules of Marketing and PR** (the canonical list):
  - Marketing is more than advertising; PR is for more than mainstream media; **you are what you publish**; people want authenticity, not spin; people want participation, not propaganda; deliver content at the precise moment your audience needs it; shift from mass marketing to reaching vast underserved audiences; PR is about buyers seeing you on the Web, not your boss seeing you on TV; it's about your organization winning business, not your agency winning awards; the Internet made PR public again; drive people into the purchasing process with great online content; blogs/podcasts/e-books/releases let you communicate directly with buyers.
  - **How to use**: adopt as the operating creed; every tactic in the book instantiates one of these.
- **The Long Tail of Marketing** (adapted from Chris Anderson): the economy shifts from a few mass "hits" at the head of the demand curve toward countless niches in the tail.
  - **When to use**: when you serve niche/underserved audiences (i.e. most non-megabrands).
  - **How**: build many purpose-built microsites and landing pages with "just-right" content for narrow constituencies instead of one one-size-fits-all site.
- **The Long Tail of PR**: skip the fruitless chase for the one blockbuster hit; cultivate many small relationships (bloggers, analysts, niche publications) that add up.

## Key Concepts
- **Convergence of marketing and PR**: online, the line between a Web page, a news release, a magazine article, and a blog post disappears — to a searching buyer it's all just content.
- **Content drives action**: the book's thesis in three words — content brands you as a trusted resource and calls people to buy, subscribe, apply, or donate.
- **Search vs. browse**: the two ways people use Web content; great sites serve both — answering questions *and* telling people something they didn't think to ask.
- **Micromarkets / micro-campaigns**: precise messages aimed at narrow segments at the point of consumption.

## Mental Models
- Think of your buyer as **researching over time**: design for the person who does their own research and considers a decision for weeks — anticipate needs before they know to ask (Amazon's model).
- Use **"word-of-blog"**: hundreds of bloggers writing about you can reach millions — the modern, more-efficient word-of-mouth network.
- Prefer **many small wins over one mega-hit**: "The big hits come by getting to the little guy."

## Anti-patterns
- **"Content only works for tech companies"**: false — Scott's counterexample is concrete (literally).
- **One-size-fits-all mass message**: appeals to nobody in a long-tail world.
- **Writing releases only for "big news"**: you should be publishing expert ideas continuously.

## Worked Example
**Cervélo Cycles.** Co-founder Gerard Vroomen insists he's "an engineer, not a marketer" — yet he's a great marketer. He writes all the site content himself (design by a moonlighting chiropractor), offers detailed engineering content, an online museum, race reports, rider interviews, and Cervélo.tv. His rule: buyers must "see the product five different ways" to gain credibility — site, pro race on TV, dealer, blog, search. Keyword-rich cycling content earns Cervélo the same search traffic as competitors 10× its size. Customer Ryan Patch buys because he can verify the pros ride the same bike — "based in fact, not fluff." **The Concrete Network** (Jim Peterson) proves the point in a bricks-and-mortar industry: two direct-to-consumer news releases per week, buyer guides, photo galleries → traffic grew from 550K to 850K monthly visitors. "Content sells concrete."

## Key Takeaways
1. Internalize the single New Rules list — it's the operating system for everything that follows.
2. **You are what you publish**: your content *is* your reputation.
3. Serve the long tail: build narrow, targeted content for many micromarkets, not one broad message.
4. Optimize for both search and browse — tell buyers something they didn't think to ask.
5. Success = the cumulative effect of many small right things (a "tipping point"), not one blockbuster.
