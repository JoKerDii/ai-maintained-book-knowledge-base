# Patterns — Techniques & Methods from The New Rules of Marketing and PR

## The Four-Step Marketing & PR Plan
**When to use**: building any New Rules marketing/PR program from scratch (Ch 10).
**How**: (1) Set organizational goals (revenue, donations, votes, applications — not "balls" like leads/clips/traffic). (2) Identify and profile buyer personas. (3) Learn buyers' exact words and decide what you want each persona to believe. (4) Develop content (editorial calendar per persona in preferred media).
**Trade-offs**: requires up-front research and interviews; expect old-rules pushback — but eliminates message-debate guessing.

## Build a Buyer Persona Profile
**When to use**: before creating any content.
**How**: segment buyers; for each, write a named biography (like "NASCAR Dads," "Sam the Athlete") — goals, problems, daily activities, current solutions, trusted media, exact words. Best built by interviewing real people and triangulating with the media they consume; even clip a representative photo.
**Trade-offs**: time-consuming, but "so much easier to listen before you talk."

## Think Like a Publisher
**When to use**: any content, site, or media-room project.
**How**: content strategy first, then design/mechanics. Answer: Who are my readers? How do I reach them? What are their problems? How do I inform and entertain? What content compels action?
**Trade-offs**: fights the agency/IT pull toward design and technology.

## The Three Uses of Blogs (Monitor → Comment → Create)
**When to use**: entering the blog world.
**How**: (1) Monitor what's said about you/market/products; (2) comment on others' blogs to become known; (3) start your own. Do them in order.
**Trade-offs**: monitoring is low-risk and wins internal permission before the leap to writing.

## Rapid In-Forum Crisis Response
**When to use**: a negative thread about you gains traction (Ch 7).
**How**: respond fast and honestly *in the same forum*, in your own name — acknowledge, apologize, state your plan. Web linking amplifies one good post.
**Trade-offs**: can't fully reverse a crisis, but "one guy with one post" (B&H) can flip a thread; going dark (Sony) makes it worse.

## Direct-to-Consumer News Release Program
**When to use**: reaching buyers via search/RSS (Ch 5, 14).
**How**: write for buyers not just journalists; publish continuously ("big news is great, but don't wait"); embed exact buyer search phrases; include an offer + links to landing pages; dual-publish (wire + your own permanent site page); add social tags.
**Trade-offs**: don't abuse the channel; coexists with media relations.

## Keyword-Rich Content + Links (the SEO engine)
**When to use**: all web content and releases.
**How**: research the phrases buyers search; embed them naturally; include links (they drive buyers *and* raise search rankings via inbound links).
**Trade-offs**: don't let keywords dominate readability.

## Thought-Leadership Content
**When to use**: branding yourself an expert/trusted resource (Ch 11).
**How**: solve buyers' problems, never mention your product; pick the form buyers consume (whitepaper, e-book, newsletter, webinar, survey report, blog, podcast, video); grabbing title; decide free vs. gated; promote hard; alert bloggers/reporters.
**Trade-offs**: free = reach but anonymous; gated = fewer downloads but a contact list.

## Survey/Research Report
**When to use**: you own the definitive data buyers crave (Ch 11).
**How**: run a real, statistically significant survey; publish free; promote via newsletter. (Pragmatic Marketing salary survey.)
**Trade-offs**: only works if data is genuine and interesting; builds authority + evergreen SEO.

## Write for Buyers (kill gobbledygook)
**When to use**: every piece of writing (Ch 12).
**How**: start with the buyer's problem in their plain words, then prove it. Run the substitution test — if swapping a competitor's name still makes sense, the copy is worthless. Write like talking to a relative you don't see often.
**Trade-offs**: plain language stands out precisely because the industry writes in jargon.

## Buyer-Centric Site Navigation & Sales-Cycle Content
**When to use**: building/redesigning a site (Ch 13).
**How**: organize by buyer self-selection (job/geography/industry/problem), not your org chart. Map content to cycle: educate early → nudge in middle (offer subscriptions) → close late (demos, tools) → continue conversation. Add persona pages incrementally — no full redesign.
**Trade-offs**: requires knowing personas and the sales process in detail.

## RSS to Targeted Niches
**When to use**: delivering welcome, self-selected updates (Ch 13, 15).
**How**: syndicate discrete items (releases, posts, product updates) as category/keyword feeds (Netflix genre feeds; Intel product/country feeds).
**Trade-offs**: bypasses crowded email; underused — a competitive edge.

## Build a Buyer-First Online Media Room
**When to use**: any organization's site (Ch 15).
**How**: control it with a dedicated CMS (no IT bottleneck); needs analysis first; optimize for search + browse; rich jargon-free media kit with customer case studies; multimedia; post "proprietary" specs; event calendar; journalist calls to action; embrace bloggers.
**Trade-offs**: fight legal/exec reluctance to post valuable content — but that content earns the story.

## The New Rules of Media Relations (targeted pitching)
**When to use**: you genuinely want a specific outlet (Ch 16).
**How**: target one reporter; read their work first; reference a specific article; targeted (not cute) subject line; pitch the customer story and big-picture trend, not features; plain-text, no unrequested attachments; follow up promptly. Pitch bloggers like reporters.
**Trade-offs**: slow and manual, but broadcast pitching is spam and brands you a "bad guy."

## Nurture Explosive News Across the Cycle
**When to use**: you hold news with viral potential (Ch 8).
**How**: plan a who/when timeline — hint to clients, embargoed advance to select media, let one outlet break it, then feed the blogger + mainstream wave. (Outsell click-fraud.)
**Trade-offs**: you can't guarantee virality; timing and luck matter.

## Push a Viral Event You Didn't Start
**When to use**: organic buzz erupts about you (Ch 8).
**How**: link to it, support the creators, add real-world presence, launch an authentic follow-on contest (Mentos). Don't distance yourself (Coca-Cola's mistake) or fake it.
**Trade-offs**: homemade beats slick; astroturf gets exposed.

## Podcast/Video Production
**When to use**: buyers prefer audio/video (Ch 18).
**How**: prep and script; record/edit/tag; host + distribute (iTunes); promote + cross-promote; run a companion blog. Human voice, persona-matched length (18–24 min).
**Trade-offs**: modest hardware/software/editing investment; lets small players out-personalize big ones.

## Squidoo Lens / Social-Network Presence
**When to use**: building a low-cost, authentic niche presence (Ch 19).
**How**: offline personality or expertise page; target a niche; be a thought leader; lots of links; transparent (no fake profiles); tag and bookmark; participate and lead; experiment.
**Trade-offs**: communities hate overt pitches and expose fakes; first-movers win.

## Long-Tail Search + Landing Pages
**When to use**: enhancing an already content-rich SEM program (Ch 20).
**How**: many specific-phrase programs (not generic terms); one buyer-POV landing page per term cluster (short, self-contained, one clear CTA, minimal form, follow up). Choose "ownable" names.
**Trade-offs**: needs dozens/hundreds of landing pages; never dump paid traffic on the homepage.

## Start in Bits and Pieces
**When to use**: overwhelmed and unsure where to begin (Ch 21).
**How**: pick one first step — persona research, a few thought-leadership pages, or read/comment/start-a-blog. Experiment; the Web is iterative, delete what fails.
**Trade-offs**: nobody does all of it — do what works for you.
