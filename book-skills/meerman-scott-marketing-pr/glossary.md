# Glossary — The New Rules of Marketing and PR

**Badge** — a graphical image (banner-like or RSS-style) supporters add to their blog/site that links back to your content (Ch 9)

**Bait-and-switch (landing page failure)** — ranking for a phrase then dumping searchers on a generic homepage; they click away (Ch 20)

**Blog (weblog)** — a special Web site, usually by one passionate person, posts in reverse-chronological order, tagged into categories, built on easy CMS-like software (Ch 4)

**Blogosphere monitoring** — using text-mining/blog search (e.g. Technorati) to track and measure what's said about you across millions of blogs (Ch 4)

**Browse (vs. search)** — one of two ways people use content: being told something you didn't think to ask (Ch 2, 15)

**Buyer persona** — a representative biography of a distinct buyer type with a specific problem your product solves; the foundation of the plan (Ch 3, 10)

**Buyer persona profile** — "a short biography of the typical customer, not just a job description but a person description" (Revella): goals, problems, daily activities, media, exact words (Ch 10)

**Call to action** — the clear next step (buy/donate/subscribe/download) that content funnels buyers toward (Ch 3, 13)

**Clip book** — old-rules scrapbook of press mentions PR people showed bosses to prove their worth (Ch 1)

**Companion blog** — the text blog nearly every podcaster runs so search engines index the show and listeners move into the sales process (Ch 18)

**Content drives action** — the book's thesis: great content brands you a trusted resource and calls people to act (Ch 2, 3)

**Convergence of marketing and PR** — online, the line between a web page, release, article, and blog post disappears; there's one set of rules (Ch 2)

**Direct-to-consumer news release** — a release (renamed from "press release") aimed at buyers via search/RSS, not only reporters (Ch 5, 14)

**Disintermediation (of the media)** — the Web removing the journalist as required middleman between you and buyers (Ch 5)

**E-book** — a PDF that names a problem and answers it; landscape, graphical, lighter than a whitepaper; best free/no-registration (Ch 11)

**Editorial calendar/plan** — a scheduled content program (site pages, e-book, blog, releases) per buyer persona (Ch 10, 14)

**First-mover advantage** — early adopters of a new channel (blog, podcast, Second Life) earn a lasting reputation for innovation (Ch 4, 19)

**Gobbledygook** — meaningless overused jargon adjectives (next generation, flexible, robust, world class, scalable, cutting edge) that make companies sound identical (Ch 1, 12)

**Go dark** — not participating during a crisis; reads as "what are they hiding?" (Ch 7)

**Journalistic black hole** — the fate of untargeted press releases that reach reporters and produce nothing (Ch 1)

**Landing page** — a targeted page for the phrase/offer a searcher or release-reader clicked; buyer-POV, self-contained, one clear CTA (Ch 13, 14, 20)

**Lens / lensmaster** — a Squidoo page that filters one person's niche expertise onto a single page pointing to other content; its creator (Ch 9, 19)

**List serve** — a group email list; members post and messages go out by email (Ch 7)

**Long tail (of marketing / of PR)** — the shift from a few mass "hits" to countless niches; cultivate many small wins over one blockbuster (Ch 2)

**Long tail of search** — optimizing for many specific phrases buyers actually search rather than broad generic terms (Ch 20)

**Media room (online)** — the part of your site built for media but visited by everyone; you control it; free SEO via frequent releases (Ch 15)

**Micromarket / micro-campaign** — precise messages aimed at a narrow segment at the point of consumption (Ch 2)

**News release** — see Direct-to-consumer news release; the book's preferred term (Ch 5)

**Old Rules (of Marketing / of PR)** — the two obsolete rule-sets: interruption advertising and media-gatekeeper PR (Ch 1)

**New Rules of Marketing and PR** — the unified list: be authentic, participate, publish content buyers want, reach buyers directly (Ch 2)

**Pass-along value** — content interesting enough that visitors share the link; helped by permanent URLs (Ch 13)

**Podcast** — audio content connected to an RSS feed; no iPod required (Ch 6, 18)

**Podsafe music** — music the artist/label has cleared for podcasting (Ch 6)

**RSS (Really Simple Syndication)** — a subscription format delivering discrete content items to self-selecting niches, bypassing email (Ch 13, 15)

**Search (vs. browse)** — using content to answer a specific known question (Ch 2, 15)

**Search engine advertising** — paid text ads (AdWords, Yahoo! Search Marketing) triggered by purchased keywords; placement = bid × click-through rate (Ch 20)

**Search engine marketing (SEM)** — using search engines to reach buyers directly; the whole book is an SEM program (Ch 20)

**Search engine optimization (SEO)** — ensuring content is found and highly ranked in natural results (Ch 20)

**Site personality** — the consistent tone/voice that builds an emotional relationship (Google playful vs. Accenture serious) (Ch 13)

**Social media tags** — tags (Technorati, DIGG, del.icio.us) that make releases/pages surface in category feeds (Ch 14)

**Stealth insertion** — sneaking corporate video onto YouTube as if consumer-generated; the community exposes it (Ch 18)

**Thought leadership** — content that solves buyers' problems without mentioning your product, branding you an expert (Ch 3, 11)

**Third-party ink** — old-rules coverage earned when a journalist writes about you (Ch 1)

**Trackback** — a message another blogger sends when they reference your post; like a comment pointing to their post (Ch 17)

**TV-industrial complex** (Seth Godin) — the old interruption-advertising system (Ch 1)

**Vlog / vodcast** — video embedded in a blog / a video podcast tied to RSS-iTunes (Ch 18)

**Whitepaper** — a document that defines a business problem and offers a solution without pitching product; often gated for leads (Ch 11)

**Wiki** — a site anyone can edit (Wikipedia); red links = unwritten pages inviting expert contribution (Ch 7)

**Word-of-blog / word-of-mouse** — the online, more-efficient form of word-of-mouth; hundreds of bloggers can reach millions (Ch 2, 21)

**You are what you publish** — the plan's guiding maxim: your content is your reputation (Ch 2, 10)
