## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The New Rules of Marketing and PR** (Ch 2) — the operating creed. Marketing is more than advertising; PR is for more than mainstream media; **you are what you publish**; people want authenticity not spin, participation not propaganda; deliver content at the precise moment of need; reach vast underserved audiences (not the masses); win business, not awards; the Internet made PR public again. Online, **marketing and PR converge** — to a searching buyer, a web page, news release, article, and blog post are all just content. Thesis: **Content drives action.**

**Old Rules vs New Rules** (Ch 1–2) — Old Rules = interruption advertising to the masses + media-gatekeeper PR measured in clips. Diagnostic: if your program still matches the Old Rules lists, it's a liability. The Web replaced both with one unified set.

**The Four-Step Action Plan** (Ch 10) — the spine of the book: (1) **Set organizational goals** (revenue/donations/votes — not "balls" like leads/clips/traffic; the soccer analogy). (2) **Identify & profile buyer personas.** (3) **Learn buyers' exact words + decide what you want each to believe.** (4) **Develop content** (editorial calendar per persona). "Listen before you talk."

**Buyer personas** (Ch 3, 10) — segment buyers into named biographies ("NASCAR Dads," "Sam the Athlete") built from real interviews + the media they consume. Capture goals, problems, and exact search phrases. The single most important step. Different personas buy different things (Volvo sells safety; Starbucks sells a hangout).

**Think Like a Publisher** (Ch 3, 9) — manage content as an asset; content strategy first, design/tech second. "Content is king, president, and Pope."

**Thought leadership** (Ch 3, 11) — content that solves buyers' problems and **never mentions your product**; brands you a trusted resource. Forms: whitepaper, e-book, newsletter, webinar, survey report, blog, podcast, video. Prefer free/no-registration for reach; gate for leads.

**The Long Tail (of Marketing / of PR / of Search)** (Ch 2, 20) — shift from a few mass "hits" to countless niches; cultivate many small wins over one blockbuster ("the big hits come by getting to the little guy"). In search, optimize many specific phrases, not generic terms.

**Direct-to-consumer news releases** (Ch 5, 14) — "big news is great, but don't wait." Write for buyers, keyword-rich, with offers + links to landing pages; dual-publish (wire + permanent site page); add social tags. Links drive buyers *and* raise search rankings.

**Newsjacking / real-time marketing (early forms)** (Ch 5, 8) — inject your brand into breaking events (WebEx during the Boston Big Dig; nurture explosive news across the cycle with embargoes, as Outsell did with click fraud). Viral = others telling your story; you can seed and push, not command — homemade beats slick, fakes get exposed.

**Write for buyers / kill gobbledygook** (Ch 12) — start with the buyer's problem in plain words, then prove it. Substitution test: if a competitor's name fits your copy, it's worthless.

**Search Engine Marketing = the whole book** (Ch 20) — build great buyer-focused content with buyers' words and search engines index it. SEM doesn't interrupt — buyers *want* the content. Add long-tail terms and dedicated landing pages (never dump paid traffic on the homepage).

**Monitor → Comment → Create** (Ch 4) — the on-ramp for blogs (and forums/social). Web as a city, not a newspaper: blogs are independent citizen voices. Govern employee behavior, not the technology.
