# Glossary — The 48 Laws of Power

Key terms, coined phrases, and recurring concepts. Chapter references (ChN) point to this skill's thematic chapters, not the book's law numbers.

**Air of unpredictability** — Deliberate inconsistency in behavior to keep others off-balance and unable to model you (Law 17, Ch8).

**Assume formlessness** — Keeping no fixed shape, plan, or position so you present no target; being "like water" (Law 48, Ch1).

**Bait** — A dangled gain used to make an opponent abandon his plan and come to you (Law 8, Ch1).

**Boldness** — Full, confident commitment to action; it hides deficiencies and eliminates obstacles that hesitation creates (Law 28, Ch6).

**Cat's-paw** — A disposable intermediary used to do dirty work and absorb blame, keeping your own hands clean (Law 26, Ch6).

**Concentrate your forces** — Massing energy at your single strongest point or richest source; "intensity defeats extensity" (Law 23, Ch6).

**Court / courtier** — The circle of power around a ruler; the courtier survives by indirection, flattery, and political dexterity (Law 24, Ch4).

**Court attention** — Making yourself conspicuous so you're talked about; obscurity is powerlessness (Law 6, Ch3).

**Crush totally** — Finishing a real enemy completely in body and spirit so no "ember" survives to seek revenge (Law 15, Ch6).

**Cultlike following** — Devotion won by supplying people a vague-but-promising cause, rituals, and sacrifices (Law 27, Ch7).

**Disdain (as revenge)** — Refusing to acknowledge a petty problem or thing, denying it existence and appearing superior (Law 36, Ch1).

**Effortlessness** — Concealing all toil and tricks so accomplishments look like natural, godlike gift (Law 30, Ch4).

**Fat cow** — The single rich patron or source worth mining deeply rather than scattering effort (Law 23, Ch6).

**Formlessness** — See *Assume formlessness*.

**Free lunch** — Anything offered free; it hides a trick or an indebting obligation and should be despised (Law 40, Ch8).

**Infection** — Emotional contagion; misfortune and misery spread from the unhappy/unlucky to those near them (Law 10, Ch5).

**Interpretation** — The book's section, after each historical story, extracting the strategic lesson (structural term).

**Judgment** — The one-paragraph statement of each law that opens its chapter (structural term).

**Keys to Power** — The book's how-to section giving tactics for applying each law (structural term).

**Mirror Effect** — Reflecting others' actions (to confuse enemies) or values (to seduce allies) back at them (Law 44, Ch7).

**Never outshine the master** — Making superiors feel superior; excess talent inspires their fear and retaliation (Law 1, Ch4).

**Observance of the Law** — The book's historical example of someone succeeding by following a law (structural term).

**Play a sucker** — Feigning stupidity so a mark feels superior, relaxes, and drops his guard (Law 21, Ch2).

**Pyrrhic victory (of argument)** — A won argument that costs you through lasting resentment; hence win by action, not words (Law 9, Ch7).

**Re-create yourself** — Forging a deliberate public identity and persona rather than accepting society's assigned role (Law 25, Ch3).

**Reputation** — The cornerstone of power; a story that intimidates and wins for you, and whose cracks invite attack (Law 5, Ch3).

**Reversal** — The book's closing section for each law noting when the opposite approach applies (structural term).

**Scarcity (absence)** — Withdrawing to raise your value; "too much circulation makes the price go down" (Law 16, Ch3).

**Selective honesty** — A single sincere/generous act (a "Trojan horse") used to disarm suspicion and enable manipulation (Law 12, Ch2).

**Smoke screen** — Bland, expected, or decoy behavior that hides your real intention (Law 3, Ch2).

**Spectacle** — Striking imagery and grand symbolic gestures that move people and distract from your real aims (Law 37, Ch3).

**Strike the shepherd** — Removing the single instigator whose influence holds a troublesome group together (Law 42, Ch5).

**Surrender tactic** — Yielding when weaker to buy time, deny the victor satisfaction, and undermine him from within (Law 22, Ch6).

**Sphinxlike** — Vague, brief, open-ended speech that seems profound and keeps control; "say less than necessary" (Law 4, Ch1).

**Thumbscrew** — Each person's exploitable weakness: an insecurity, uncontrollable need, or secret pleasure (Law 33, Ch8).

**Timing** — Reading the "spirit of the times," waiting out the unripe moment, and striking at fruition (Law 35, Ch1).

**Trojan horse** — See *Selective honesty* — a gift or candid gesture that opens the target's guard (Law 12, Ch2).

**Wolf in lamb's clothing** — A thin-skinned, vengeful person who looks harmless; never offend them (Law 19, Ch5).
