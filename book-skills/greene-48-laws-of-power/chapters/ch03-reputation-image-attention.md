# Chapter 3: Reputation, Image & Attention

**Covers Laws 5, 6, 16, 25, 34, 37, 46.** The perception laws: people judge you by appearance and story, not by hidden reality. Power is won and lost in the theater of how you are seen — so author your image deliberately instead of letting others cast you.

## Core Idea
Reputation is the cornerstone of power — build it, guard it, and attack rivals through theirs. Everything unseen counts for nothing, so control attention, scarcity, spectacle, and self-presentation as deliberately as you'd control any strategic asset.

## The Laws (essence · key tactic · reversal)

- **Law 5 — So much depends on reputation; guard it with your life.** Reputation lets you intimidate and win before you act; once it cracks, you're attacked on all sides. *Tactic:* make your reputation unassailable on one or two solid traits; destroy enemies by opening holes in *their* reputation and letting opinion hang them. *Reversal:* never worry so much about your name that you become paralyzed by others' opinions.
- **Law 6 — Court attention at all cost.** Being talked about — even controversially — beats being ignored. *Tactic:* stand out, be conspicuous, attach your name to a striking image or an air of mystery; controversy and scandal can serve if they keep you visible. *Reversal:* once established, over-exposure devalues you (bridges to Law 16) — calibrate.
- **Law 16 — Use absence to increase respect and honor.** Too much circulation lowers your price; scarcity raises it. *Tactic:* withdraw at the peak of your presence to become more talked-about and desired; know when to leave. *Reversal:* you must first *be present and valued* — vanishing before you've established worth just makes you forgotten.
- **Law 25 — Re-create yourself.** Don't accept the role society assigns; forge a memorable identity you control. *Tactic:* craft a public persona with dramatic, theatrical devices; master your own image before others define it. *Reversal:* a persona too outlandish invites ridicule — the new self must still command respect (ties to Law 34).
- **Law 34 — Be royal in your own fashion: act like a king to be treated like one.** Self-carriage sets others' expectations. *Tactic:* project calm, confident, regal bearing; ask boldly and expect to be honored. *Reversal:* regal ≠ arrogant or tyrannical toward those who could unseat you — read the room (see Law 1).
- **Law 37 — Create compelling spectacles.** Striking visuals and symbols move people more than words or logic. *Tactic:* stage arresting imagery and grand gestures; dazzle so no one examines what you're actually doing. *Reversal:* none endorsed — spectacle is presented as near-universally effective.
- **Law 46 — Never appear too perfect.** Envy makes silent enemies; flawlessness is the most dangerous look of all. *Tactic:* occasionally admit a harmless vice or defect to appear human and deflect envy. *Reversal:* only "gods and the dead" can seem perfect safely — among the living, disarm envy first.

## Mental Models
- **Appearance is the operative reality:** in the arena of power, the story people tell about you *is* the fact you must manage (Law 5, 6, 37).
- **Scarcity pricing of the self:** presence follows supply-and-demand — control your visibility to control your value (Law 6 ↔ Law 16).
- **Envy management:** the higher you rise, the more you must *lower* your apparent perfection to survive the resentment you generate (Law 46 ↔ Law 1).
- **You are a character you cast:** identity is not fixed — it is authored, staged, and re-authored (Law 25, Law 34).

## Worked Example
*Guard your reputation, attack theirs (Law 5).* Greene's pattern: your own reputation should rest on one unassailable trait (generosity, competence, boldness) that precedes you into every room and does half your work before you speak. Against a rival, you don't attack directly — you open a hole in *their* reputation (a well-placed doubt about their reliability or motives) and then stand aside, letting public opinion do the hanging. The asymmetry: building your name is slow and deliberate; a rival's name, once cracked, collapses fast — because reputation is the load-bearing wall of everyone's power.

## Anti-patterns
- **Blending into the crowd** — invisibility is powerlessness (violates Law 6).
- **Over-exposure** — being seen and heard constantly makes you common (violates Law 16).
- **Appearing flawless** — perfection breeds envy and silent enemies (violates Law 46).
- **Letting others define you** — accepting an assigned role cedes the image war (violates Law 25).
- **Ignoring a smear** — an unanswered attack on reputation metastasizes (violates Law 5).

## Key Takeaways
1. Build one unassailable reputation trait; it works before you do (Law 5).
2. Court attention — notoriety beats obscurity (Law 6) — but ration your presence (Law 16).
3. Author your own persona and carry yourself as royalty (Laws 25, 34).
4. Move people with spectacle and symbol, not argument (Law 37).
5. Show a harmless flaw to disarm the envy your success creates (Law 46).
