# Chapter 1: Master Yourself — Restraint, Patience & Timing

**Covers Laws 4, 8, 35, 36, 39, 47, 48.** The self-command laws: before you can control others, you must control your words, your emotions, your appetite, and your clock. Power is lost far more often through impatience and over-reaching than through timidity.

## Core Idea
The player who can wait, say little, stay calm, and know when to stop dictates the game. Emotional reactivity and over-extension hand your opponent the strings; self-possession and formlessness make you impossible to read or trap.

## The Laws (essence · key tactic · reversal)

- **Law 4 — Always say less than necessary.** The more you say, the more common and less in control you appear; vague, sphinxlike brevity intimidates. *Tactic:* answer questions with a shorter, more ambiguous statement than expected; let silence pull the other person into filling it. *Reversal:* sometimes a flood of words is a smokescreen — talkative distraction can hide your real move (see Law 3).
- **Law 8 — Make other people come to you; use bait if necessary.** Whoever forces the other to act is in control. *Tactic:* dangle a gain (a deal, information, an opening) so your opponent abandons his plan and enters your terrain. *Reversal:* coming to your opponent works when you need to surprise or attack on a weak point they've left undefended.
- **Law 35 — Master the art of timing.** Never seem to hurry; hurrying betrays lack of self-control. Read the "spirit of the times," wait out the unripe moment, strike at fruition. *Tactic:* separate "forced time" (the tempo you impose on others) from "end time" (planning to the finish). *Reversal:* none — timing has no downside; only misreading the moment does.
- **Law 36 — Disdain things you cannot have; ignoring is the best revenge.** Attention gives a petty problem existence; the more you react to an enemy or a slight, the stronger it grows. *Tactic:* when you can't have or can't win a thing, show sovereign contempt and turn your back — the non-reaction reads as superiority. *Reversal:* do not ignore a genuinely dangerous threat you can still crush (see Law 15); disdain is for the petty, not the lethal.
- **Law 39 — Stir up waters to catch fish.** Stay calm and objective yourself while provoking your enemy into anger; a rattled opponent overreacts and reveals himself. *Tactic:* find the chink in their vanity, needle it, then wait. *Reversal:* never stir waters you can't master — a provoked opponent stronger than you may drown you.
- **Law 47 — Do not go past the mark you aimed for; in victory, learn when to stop.** The moment of victory breeds the arrogance that overreaches and makes new enemies. *Tactic:* set the goal in advance; when you hit it, halt and consolidate. *Reversal:* none the author endorses — over-reaching is presented as pure danger.
- **Law 48 — Assume formlessness.** A visible shape or fixed plan is a target; fluidity like water is the ultimate protection. *Tactic:* keep intentions and strategy adaptable; never bet on stability or lasting order. *Reversal:* formlessness suits the outmatched and the strategist; institutions and figureheads sometimes need a fixed, imposing form.

## Mental Models
- **Patience as power multiplier:** treat time as a weapon you own and others lack. The appearance of never being rushed signals control over yourself, which others read as control over events.
- **The reaction tax:** every emotional reaction you give an opponent is intelligence you hand them for free. Calm is not politeness — it is information asymmetry.
- **Water, not rock:** be the formless thing that flows around obstacles rather than the fixed fortress that gets besieged (Law 48 ↔ Law 18).

## Worked Example
*Say less than necessary (Law 4) — the sphinx effect.* Greene contrasts talkers who diminish themselves with figures who dominate through calculated brevity. The pattern to reproduce: in a negotiation or review, when pressed for your position, give a single short, slightly ambiguous sentence and stop. The other party, unsettled by the silence and unable to read you, keeps talking — and reveals their priorities, their ceiling, their fears. You gained information and gave none. The power came entirely from what you *withheld*.

## Anti-patterns
- **Talking to fill silence** — every extra sentence lowers your stature and leaks your hand.
- **Chasing** — running to opponents or opportunities cedes tempo (violates Law 8).
- **Victory drunkenness** — pressing past the goal in the flush of success (violates Law 47) is the single most common way winners self-destruct.
- **Taking the bait** — reacting to provocation is being played by someone running Law 39 on you.

## Key Takeaways
1. Say less than necessary; let ambiguity and silence do the work.
2. Make others come to you — whoever acts first, under pressure, is being controlled.
3. Never hurry; read the moment and strike only when it's ripe.
4. Refuse to react to the petty (Law 36) and refuse to overreach at the peak (Law 47).
5. Stay the calm one while others burn (Law 39); calm is leverage, not virtue.
6. Keep no fixed shape — adaptability is armor (Law 48).
