# Chapter 8: Leverage & Positioning — Making Others Need You

**Covers Laws 7, 11, 17, 33, 40, 41.** The structural-power laws: arrange the board so power flows to you by position, not by constant effort. The recurring move is creating *dependence* and finding *leverage points* — others' needs, weaknesses, and habits — while protecting your own independence and originality.

## Core Idea
Durable power comes from position: make others depend on you, harness their labor while keeping the credit, find each person's weakness to turn, and stay unpredictable and free of hidden obligations. Establish your *own* identity rather than living in another's shadow.

## The Laws (essence · key tactic · reversal)

- **Law 7 — Get others to do the work for you, but always take the credit.** Others' effort and knowledge, credited to you, create a "godlike aura of efficiency." *Tactic:* delegate the legwork, position yourself as the visible author of the result; never do yourself what others can do for you. *Reversal:* do the work yourself when the credit must be unmistakably yours and delegation would let someone else claim it — and never let a cat's-paw become indispensable enough to blackmail you.
- **Law 11 — Learn to keep people dependent on you.** The more you're needed, the more freedom and security you have. *Tactic:* make yourself indispensable — hold unique knowledge, skills, or relationships; never teach others enough to do without you. *Reversal:* dependence must be mutual enough that you're not resented into replacement; indispensability is safest when it's genuinely valued, not extorted.
- **Law 17 — Keep others in suspended terror: cultivate an air of unpredictability.** Predictability hands others control; deliberate unpredictability keeps them off-balance and exhausted trying to read you. *Tactic:* vary your patterns, act against expectation occasionally; let a calculated inconsistency unsettle rivals. *Reversal:* total unpredictability can look unstable and alienate allies — use it selectively, from a base of reliability.
- **Law 33 — Discover each man's thumbscrew.** Everyone has a weakness — an insecurity, an uncontrollable need, a secret pleasure. *Tactic:* find the gap in the castle wall (often insecurity or a strong appetite) and apply gentle pressure there to control or persuade. *Reversal:* misjudging the weakness — or pressing a weakness that makes an enemy desperate — backfires; probe carefully (ties Law 19).
- **Law 40 — Despise the free lunch.** What's free hides a trick or an obligation; what has worth is worth paying for. *Tactic:* pay your own way to stay free of gratitude, guilt, and debt; be lavish and keep money circulating, since generosity signals and attracts power. *Reversal:* strategic gifts *to others* are potent (Law 12) — the warning is against accepting things that quietly indebt *you*.
- **Law 41 — Avoid stepping into a great man's shoes.** Succeeding a famous predecessor or parent traps you in their shadow; what comes first always seems better. *Tactic:* establish your own name by changing course — "slay the father," disparage the old legacy, and shine in a domain of your own making. *Reversal:* occasionally you can revive and exploit a great predecessor's legacy — but only by making it distinctly yours.

## Mental Models
- **Dependence as moat:** the most durable power is being *needed*; indispensability buys freedom that talent alone can't (Law 11 ↔ Law 7).
- **Leverage points:** every person and system has a weak point — the thumbscrew — where small pressure yields large control (Law 33).
- **Unpredictability tax:** when others can't model you, they burn energy guessing and cede initiative (Law 17).
- **No free lunch:** every unearned benefit is a hidden liability; keep your obligations clean to keep your freedom (Law 40).
- **Escape the shadow:** originality and a self-made identity beat inheriting a great predecessor's diminishing comparison (Law 41 ↔ Law 25's re-creation).

## Worked Example
*Make yourself indispensable (Law 11).* Greene's model courtiers and advisors survived murderous courts by ensuring the ruler could not function without them — controlling a unique relationship, a body of knowledge, or a network no rival possessed, and pointedly *never* training a replacement. **Reproduce it deliberately:** become the single person who understands the critical system, holds the key client relationship, or can navigate the essential process. Your security then rests not on goodwill (fragile) but on the structural cost of losing you (durable). Pair it with Law 7 — let the org see the results flow through *you* — and with Law 40 — owe no one — so your position is leverage without counter-leverage.

## Anti-patterns
- **Doing everything yourself for no visible credit** — labor without recognition builds others' power, not yours (violates Law 7).
- **Teaching others to no longer need you** — training your own replacement dissolves your leverage (violates Law 11).
- **Being perfectly predictable** — a readable person is a controllable one (violates Law 17).
- **Accepting free gifts and favors** — every free lunch is a hidden debt that constrains you (violates Law 40).
- **Living in a predecessor's shadow** — inheriting a legend invites unfavorable comparison (violates Law 41).

## Key Takeaways
1. Harness others' work while owning the credit and the aura (Law 7).
2. Make yourself indispensable and never teach away your leverage (Law 11).
3. Stay unpredictable enough to keep rivals off-balance (Law 17).
4. Find and gently turn each person's thumbscrew (Law 33).
5. Owe no one — pay your way and refuse the indebting free lunch (Law 40).
6. Build your own name instead of inheriting a great man's shadow (Law 41).
