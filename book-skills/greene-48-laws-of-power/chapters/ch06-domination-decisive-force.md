# Chapter 6: Domination & Decisive Force

**Covers Laws 15, 22, 23, 26, 28, 29.** The hard-power laws: when and how to apply overwhelming, decisive force — and, paradoxically, when strategic surrender or clean concealment serves better than a fight. The theme is *totality and finish*: half-measures create surviving enemies.

## Core Idea
When you must act, act completely — crush the threat, concentrate your forces, enter with boldness, and plan all the way to the end. When you're too weak to win, surrender to buy time; and whatever the deed, keep your own hands visibly clean.

## The Laws (essence · key tactic · reversal)

- **Law 15 — Crush your enemy totally.** A feared enemy left half-defeated will recover and seek revenge; more is lost by stopping halfway than by total annihilation. *Tactic:* when you strike a real enemy, finish them in body and spirit — leave no ember to reignite. *Reversal:* showing mercy is viable only when it wins genuine loyalty or serves your image (bridges Law 46) — and even then, watch the survivor.
- **Law 22 — Use the surrender tactic: transform weakness into power.** When weaker, don't fight for honor — surrender to gain time to recover and undermine the victor from within. *Tactic:* yield first, deny your conqueror the satisfaction of the fight, then torment and outlast him. *Reversal:* surrender only when truly outmatched; a premature surrender from a position of strength squanders advantage.
- **Law 23 — Concentrate your forces.** Intensity beats extensity — a deep, rich source outperforms scattered shallow ones. *Tactic:* focus energy on your single strongest point or richest patron ("the fat cow"); don't dilute across many fronts. *Reversal:* over-concentration on a single source is dangerous if that source fails — diversify enough to avoid total dependence.
- **Law 26 — Keep your hands clean.** Appear a paragon of civility; never be visibly soiled by the nasty deeds power requires. *Tactic:* use others as scapegoats and "cat's-paws" to do the dirty work and absorb the blame. *Reversal:* none endorsed — clean hands are presented as essential to durable reputation (ties Law 5).
- **Law 28 — Enter action with boldness.** Doubt infects execution; timidity creates obstacles while boldness eliminates them. *Tactic:* if unsure, don't act — but once committed, commit fully; correct any mistake with *more* audacity, not retreat. "The bolder the lie, the better." *Reversal:* boldness must be calculated, not reckless; and feigned timidity can occasionally lure an opponent into overconfidence.
- **Law 29 — Plan all the way to the end.** The ending is everything; foresee obstacles, consequences, and twists so you're never overwhelmed and know when to stop. *Tactic:* backward-plan from the desired endgame; account for every likely reversal of fortune. *Reversal:* over-planning can breed rigidity — pair end-planning with the adaptability of Law 48.

## Mental Models
- **Totality principle:** a half-crushed enemy is a future war; finish decisively or don't strike (Law 15). Applies to conflicts, negotiations, and market moves alike.
- **Boldness eliminates friction:** confident, full commitment reshapes others' responses — hesitation invites resistance, audacity clears the path (Law 28).
- **Concentration of force:** Clausewitz/Sun-Tzu logic — mass your strength at the decisive point rather than spreading thin (Law 23).
- **Cat's-paw:** power's dirty necessities should be executed by disposable intermediaries so blame never reaches you (Law 26).
- **Backward planning:** design from the endgame so no twist of fortune catches you unprepared (Law 29).

## Worked Example
*Crush your enemy totally (Law 15) — Cesare Borgia and Ramiro.* Borgia installed the ruthless Ramiro de Lorqua to pacify the Romagna, then — once order was restored and Ramiro had absorbed the population's hatred — had him executed and his body left, halved, in the public square at Cesena. In one stroke Borgia eliminated a man who had become a liability, transferred all the cruelty's blame onto him (Law 26, the cat's-paw), and terrified the region into submission. **The pattern to reproduce:** when you move against a real threat, leave nothing half-done and no surviving grudge poised for revenge — and, where possible, arrange for someone else to carry the odium of the hard act.

## Anti-patterns
- **Stopping halfway** — a wounded, surviving enemy returns for revenge (violates Law 15).
- **Fighting a losing battle for honor** — pride over strategy wastes you; surrender and outlast instead (violates Law 22).
- **Scattering your forces** — thin effort across many fronts wins nothing decisively (violates Law 23).
- **Doing your own dirty work** — visible involvement in nasty deeds stains your reputation (violates Law 26).
- **Half-hearted, hesitant execution** — doubt telegraphs and invites resistance (violates Law 28).
- **Acting without an endgame** — improvising to the finish gets you overwhelmed (violates Law 29).

## Key Takeaways
1. Strike real enemies with totality — leave no ember (Law 15).
2. When outmatched, surrender to buy time and undermine from within (Law 22).
3. Concentrate force at your single strongest point (Law 23).
4. Keep your hands clean; use cat's-paws for dirty work (Law 26).
5. Commit boldly; correct mistakes with more audacity (Law 28).
6. Plan backward from the endgame; know in advance when to stop (Laws 29 ↔ 47).
