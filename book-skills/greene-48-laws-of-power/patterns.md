# Patterns — Recurring Power Techniques

The reusable *mechanics* that appear across many of the 48 laws. Each is a technique you can deploy, not a single law.

## The Decoy / Smoke Screen
**When to use:** you want to advance a goal without opponents preparing a defense.
**How:** publicly pursue a plan you don't care about, or adopt bland, expected behavior, while quietly advancing the real objective. Guide observers "far enough down the wrong path."
**Trade-offs:** requires patience and discipline (Law 4); if exposed, erodes trust. (Laws 3, 14)

## The Trojan Horse (Selective Sincerity)
**When to use:** a target is suspicious and guarded.
**How:** make one genuine-seeming gift or candid admission — costly enough to seem sincere. It opens a hole in their armor that dozens of manipulations couldn't.
**Trade-offs:** works because it's rare; overuse trains people to see the pattern. (Law 12)

## Control the Options (Choice Architecture)
**When to use:** you need a decision to go your way without ordering it.
**How:** pre-select the menu. Offer two-or-more paths that all serve you — lesser-of-two-evils, or a heavy option beside the one you want framed as reasonable. Let them "choose freely."
**Trade-offs:** if the framing feels manipulative, it backfires; the freedom must feel real. (Law 31)

## Appeal to Self-Interest
**When to use:** asking anyone for help or a favor.
**How:** find what *they* gain (visibility, skill, a solved problem of theirs), and lead with it "out of all proportion." Never invoke mercy, pity, or past favors.
**Trade-offs:** requires knowing the person's motives (Law 19, Law 33). (Law 13)

## Find the Thumbscrew
**When to use:** you need leverage over a specific person.
**How:** identify their insecurity, uncontrollable need, or secret pleasure — the "gap in the castle wall" — and apply gentle pressure there.
**Trade-offs:** misreading the weakness, or cornering someone into desperation, backfires. (Laws 33, 19)

## The Cat's-Paw (Clean Hands)
**When to use:** power requires a nasty deed that would stain your reputation.
**How:** have a disposable intermediary execute it and absorb the blame; you remain the visible paragon of civility. (Classic case: Cesare Borgia and Ramiro.)
**Trade-offs:** the cat's-paw may gain leverage over you; discard or distance carefully. (Laws 26, 7)

## Manufacture Scarcity (Strategic Absence)
**When to use:** your presence or output is becoming common and undervalued.
**How:** withdraw at a peak of visibility; let absence raise demand and talk. Return with restored value.
**Trade-offs:** only works after you've established worth; premature absence = being forgotten. (Laws 16, 6)

## Stage a Spectacle
**When to use:** you need to move a group emotionally or mask your real activity.
**How:** deploy arresting visuals, symbols, and grand gestures; dazzle so no one examines the substance.
**Trade-offs:** empty spectacle collapses if reality is later demanded (pair with Law 32's fantasy caution). (Laws 37, 32)

## Stir the Waters (Provoke, Stay Calm)
**When to use:** a rival's judgment is your target.
**How:** stay calm and objective while needling the chink in their vanity; a rattled opponent overreacts and reveals himself.
**Trade-offs:** never provoke an opponent stronger than you can manage. (Law 39)

## Surrender to Outlast
**When to use:** you are genuinely weaker and would lose an open fight.
**How:** yield first, denying the victor the satisfaction of battle; use the bought time to recover and undermine from within.
**Trade-offs:** surrendering from strength squanders advantage; timing is everything. (Laws 22, 35)

## Concentrate Force
**When to use:** your resources are being spread across many fronts.
**How:** identify the single decisive point or richest source ("fat cow") and mass everything there. Intensity beats extensity.
**Trade-offs:** total dependence on one source is fragile if it fails — retain a fallback. (Law 23)

## Manufacture Belief (Cult Dynamics)
**When to use:** you need fervent, self-sustaining devotion, not mere compliance.
**How:** offer a vague-but-promising cause; emphasize enthusiasm over reason; create rituals; ask for escalating sacrifices that deepen commitment.
**Trade-offs:** belief is volatile and can turn on its leader; also a defensive pattern — recognize it used on you. (Laws 27, 32)

## Convert the Enemy
**When to use:** staffing a role where loyalty matters.
**How:** hire/elevate a capable former enemy who has something to prove, rather than a friend prone to envy and entitlement.
**Trade-offs:** requires accurate judgment of character (Law 19). (Law 2)

## Build the Dependence Moat
**When to use:** securing a durable position.
**How:** make yourself indispensable — unique knowledge, key relationships, control of a critical system — and never train a replacement; route visible results through yourself (Law 7); owe no one (Law 40).
**Trade-offs:** extorted (vs. valued) indispensability breeds resentment and eventual replacement. (Laws 11, 7, 40)
