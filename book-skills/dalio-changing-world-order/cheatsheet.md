# Cheatsheet — The Changing World Order (Dalio's judgment, distilled)

## Decision Rules (if X → do/expect Y, because Z)
- **If all three big cycles are in good phases → country is strong and rising; if all three are bad → weak and declining.** (The 3: debt/money, internal order, external order.)
- **If a government has bankrupt finances AND big wealth gaps → treat civil war/revolution as the single most likely lead outcome** — a broke government can't save the system.
- **If a country runs big deficits producing more debt than non-central-bank buyers will absorb → it's the Stage-5 trigger toward Stage 6** (must raise taxes/cut spending, or print heavily).
- **If debt is in your own currency → expect devaluation, not default** (repay 50–70% in real terms). **If in a currency you can't print → expect true default.**
- **If real interest rates are deeply negative late in the debt cycle → don't hold cash/bonds; buy "any stuff" that keeps pace with inflation** (the payback period is the longest ever).
- **If you see capital controls on a currency during a domestic debt problem → run from that currency.**
- **If money/credit is being printed cheaply → own assets** (asset value is the reciprocal of money's value; money's value is the reciprocal of its quantity).
- **If a rival's power ≈ yours AND differences are existential/irreconcilable → highest war risk; trade red lines to avoid it.**
- **If your relative power is declining → fight/negotiate EARLY; if rising → LATER.**
- **If you don't want to be in a civil war → get out late in Stage 5, while the doors (and capital) are still open.**
- **If a great power cuts off a rival's ESSENTIAL imports (oil, chips, rare earths) → major escalation toward a shooting war.**

## Cycle Clocks (rough durations — read conditions, not the calendar)
| Cycle | Typical length |

| Long-term debt cycle | ~50–75 years (6–10 short cycles) |

| Bad destruction/restructuring period | ~10–20 years |
| Peaceful prosperous period | ~40–80 years |

## The 6 Internal-Order Stages → the leader each needs
| Stage | Condition | Best leader |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | New order, consolidate power | Consolidator of power |
| 2 | Build systems/bureaucracy | Civil engineer |
| 3 | Peace & prosperity (sweet spot) | Inspirational visionary |
| 4 | Excess / bubble | Disciplined restrainer (rare) |
| 5 | Bad finances + conflict | Strong peacemaker (ideal) or fighter |
| 6 | Civil war / revolution | Inspirational general |

## Stage-5 → Stage-6 Tells (watch these on any country, esp. the US)
Decadence · high bureaucracy blocking good decisions · populism & polarization (moderates vanish) · class warfare + scapegoating · loss of truth in media · rules disregarded · **blood spilled = Stage 6 has begun.**

## The 4 Debt-Reduction Levers (order of use)
1. Austerity (deflationary, brief) → 2. Defaults/restructurings (deflationary, painful) → 3. Transfers/taxes (political) → 4. **Print & devalue** (most expedient, most common).

## The Rise Sequence (and it reverses in the same order)
Education → innovation/tech → competitiveness → output & trade share → military → financial center → **reserve currency (last to rise, last to fall).**

## Reserve-Currency Reserves Snapshot (~2019)
USD 51% · EUR 20% · Gold 12% · JPY 6% · GBP 5% · CNY 2%. **No attractive alternative to the dollar — but a currency devalues anyway as capital flees to gold/commodities/stocks/property.**

## Current Readings (as of the book, 2021)
- **US**: ~70% (±10%) through its Big Cycle; internal order Stage 5; ~30% civil-war-dynamic odds in 10 yrs.
- **China**: internal order Stage 3 (peace/prosperity); rising fast; roughly tied with US in trade/output/innovation.
- **US-China**: in 4 of 5 wars (trade/tech/capital/geopolitical), not military; ~35% odds of a big war in 10 yrs; MAD holds it down.
- **Biggest long-run risk**: currency-value-of-money (creditors' assets devalued below inflation), not debtor default.

## Portfolio Rules of Thumb
- Risk = failing to meet your needs (unmet-needs, ruin, confiscation) — **not** volatility.
- All markets driven by: growth, inflation, risk premiums, discount rates.
- Diversify so no environmental bias; tilt tactically. Beware survivorship bias (don't extrapolate US/UK only).
- In war: **sell debt, buy gold** (printing devalues money/debt; credit isn't accepted).
- Ask constantly: does the interest compensate for the devaluation risk?

## One-Line Principles
- "Peace is profitable and war is costly." · "Power rules; tests of power reveal who rules."
- "All currencies devalue or die." · "Debt eats equity — but central banks feed debt by printing."
- "Bet on evolution's upside, but not so much that a bump knocks you out."
- "In war, the ability to withstand pain matters more than the ability to inflict it."
- The best way to win a war: get strong and show it, so the opponent won't fight. "Deserve Victory!"
