# Glossary — Principles for Dealing with the Changing World Order

**Acts of Nature** — Droughts, floods, pandemics; historically bigger killers than wars/depressions and a determinant of decline (Ch 2, 14).

**Anti-capitalist phase** — Decline-era backlash blaming capitalism, capitalists, and elites; drives redistribution and populism (Ch 1, 5).

**Balance of Power Cycle** — The timeless allies-and-enemies dynamic driving struggles for power, internal and external (Ch 2, 6).

**Beautiful deleveraging** — Creating *enough* money/devaluation to offset a deflationary depression without triggering an inflationary spiral (Ch 3).

**Big Cycle** — The self-reinforcing rise-and-decline arc of an empire (~250 yrs), composed of the three cycles + 18 determinants (Ch 1).

**Big Five** — The five most important forces: the 3 cycles + innovation/technology + acts of nature (Ch 2).

**Bretton Woods (1944/45)** — The post-WWII monetary system linking the dollar to gold and other currencies to the dollar; start of the current long-term debt cycle (Ch 3, 11).

**Capital war** — Conflict via sanctions, asset freezes, and blocking capital-market access ("no money = no power") (Ch 6, 13).

**Classic Toxic Mix** — Bad finances + large wealth/values gaps + a severe negative economic shock; ignites big internal conflict (Ch 5).

**Culture is destiny** — Cultural differences in how people believe they should treat each other matter enormously (Ch 2).

**Debt eats equity** — Debt is senior and must be paid first, forcing asset sales — "but central banks can feed debt by printing money" (Ch 3).

**Decadence** — Late-cycle shift of time/money from productive investment to luxury/consumption; reduces productivity, breeds resentment (Ch 5).

**Determinant** — A single causal factor (e.g., money supply); a *cycle* is a self-reinforcing chain of determinants (Ch 2).

**Determinants (18)** — The measurable + qualitative forces explaining rises/declines; 8 headline powers + inherited + human/cultural factors (Ch 2).

**Eight measures of power** — Education, competitiveness, innovation/technology, economic output, share of world trade, military, financial-center, reserve-currency status (Ch 1).

**Evolution + cycles (the upward corkscrew)** — Steady productivity uptrend with pendulum cycles around it (Ch 1).

**Exorbitant/extraordinary privilege** — A reserve-currency issuer's ability to borrow cheaply and abundantly — which sows the seeds of its decline (Ch 1, 3).

**Fiat money (Type 3)** — Money not linked to anything of intrinsic value; printable without restriction (Ch 3).

**Financial economy vs. real economy** — Two systems with separate supply/demand; central banks move money/credit to steer the real economy imperfectly (Ch 3).

**Financial wealth** — Promises to deliver money (cash, bonds, stocks); no intrinsic value — only what it buys is useful. Invented ~1350 in Italy (Ch 2, 7).

**Guns and butter** — A country must fund both military strength and domestic living standards without excess (Ch 6, 11).

**Hard money (Type 1)** — Money with intrinsic value (gold, silver, copper coins) (Ch 3).

**Human capital / "self-sufficient plus"** — People (or countries) who produce more than they spend; the most sustainable capital (Ch 2).

**Inflationary depression** — Monetary inflation (currency devaluation) coexisting with real-economy deflation (Ch 3).

**Internal order cycle (6 stages)** — New order → build systems → peace/prosperity → excess → bad finances/conflict → civil war → repeat (~100 yrs) (Ch 5).

**Legalism** — Chinese philosophy favoring autocratic conquest and strict obedience; Western equivalent = fascism (Ch 12).

**Linked currency system (Type 2)** — Paper notes convertible to hard money at a fixed rate (Ch 3).

**Long-term debt cycle** — ~50–75 yr arc across 6 stages ending in debt/monetary restructuring; contains 6–10 short-term (~8 yr) cycles (Ch 3).

**Mandate of heaven** — The Chinese conception of legitimate rule that persists while the dynasty's approach works (Ch 12).

**Monetary Policy 1/2/3 (MP1/MP2/MP3)** — MP1 = interest rates; MP2 = printing to buy assets (QE); MP3 = fiscal-monetary coordination (govt spends, central bank buys the debt) (Ch 4, 11).

**Monetary systems (Type 1/2/3)** — Hard money → paper claims on hard money → fiat; countries rotate 1→2→3 then back (Ch 3).

**New world order** — The system of power set by the winners after a big war (Westphalia 1648, Vienna 1815, Versailles 1919, 1945) (Ch 6, 8).

**Populism** — Anti-elite politics of ordinary people; of the left or right; a Stage-5 marker (Ch 5).

**Prisoner's dilemma** — Game-theory reason rivals attack first; peace requires mutually assured protections (Ch 2, 6).

**Real wealth** — Goods with intrinsic value (house, car) — distinct from financial wealth (Ch 2).

**Reserve currency** — A widely saved/traded currency; grants cheap borrowing; the last strength to fade in a decline (Ch 1, 3).

**Resulting / survivorship bias** — Judging by (limited, winner-only) samples; using US/UK returns as "representative" distorts perspective (Ch 7).

**Run (on the bank)** — More claims on hard currency than the bank can satisfy; forces default or devaluation (Ch 3, 4).

**Short-term debt cycle** — "The business cycle," ~8 years (Ch 3).

**Systemically beneficial vs. destructive devaluation** — Debt relief that flows into productivity (beneficial) vs. one that wrecks the credit system but wipes out debt (destructive) (Ch 4).

**Three big cycles** — Money/credit/debt; internal order/disorder; external order/disorder (Ch 2).

**Win-win vs. lose-lose** — Relationship types both parties choose; win-win (grow and divide the pie) beats fighting (Ch 2, 6).

**World order** — The prevailing system of power domestically and internationally; changes when leading powers change (Ch 1).
