# Patterns & Techniques — The Changing World Order

Concrete, reusable methods from the book for diagnosing and acting on Big Cycle conditions.

## Rate a Country on the 18 Determinants (1–10 Scorecard)
**When to use**: To gauge whether a country is likely to rise or decline on a *relative* basis.
**How**: Score the country 1–10 on each of the 8 headline powers (education, competitiveness, innovation/tech, output, trade share, military, financial center, reserve currency) plus the other determinants. Sum them. Higher total → higher probability of relative rise; lower → decline. The *average* reading, not any single one, defines the Big Cycle stage.
**Trade-offs**: Approximate, not precise; strengths are mutually reinforcing so they move together.

## Locate the Internal-Order Stage (Disease-Diagnosis Method)
**When to use**: To assess civil-conflict risk and the right policy response.
**How**: Match symptoms to Stage 1–6 (new order → build → peace → excess → bad finances/conflict → civil war). Watch the Stage-5 markers: decadence, bureaucracy, populism/polarization, class warfare/scapegoating, loss of truth in media, rule-following fading into raw fighting. Stage 6 begins when people die in the fighting.
**Trade-offs**: Being in a stage doesn't make the next inevitable — but it sets the risk profile.

## Red-Flag Count → Civil-War Probability
**When to use**: Quantifying internal-conflict risk.
**How**: Count economic red flags (high inequality, high debt/deficits, inflation, bad growth). 60–80% present → ~1-in-6 chance of severe internal conflict; >80% → ~1-in-3.
**Trade-offs**: Base rates from ~2,200 yrs of nine powers; probabilistic, not deterministic.

## The Financial Safety-Margin Projection
**When to use**: Assessing any entity's (your own, a company's, a country's) true financial health.
**How**: Project income vs. expenses and assets vs. liabilities forward. Ask: if income disappeared and assets fell by half, how long would you last? Distinguish "looks rich" (high spending on borrowed money) from "is rich." For countries, check surplus/deficit, assets/liabilities, own-vs-foreign-currency debt, and who funds it.

## The 4 Levers to Reduce Debt Burdens
**When to use**: Diagnosing how a debt crisis will be resolved.
**How**: Expect the progression — (1) austerity (deflationary, brief), (2) defaults/restructurings (deflationary, painful both sides), (3) transfers/taxes (politically hard, part of the mix), (4) print & devalue (most expedient, most common). Aim for a "beautiful deleveraging" (enough printing to offset deflation, not enough to spiral into inflation).

## Read the Monetary System Type (1/2/3) and Its Transition
**When to use**: Judging currency/devaluation risk.
**How**: Identify Type 1 (hard money), 2 (paper claims on hard money), or 3 (fiat). Countries move 1→2→3 for printing flexibility under stress/war, then back to 1 after hyperinflation. Transitions happen under financial stress + military conflict and precede runs and devaluations.

## Spot a Reserve-Currency Decline
**When to use**: Anticipating a currency's fall.
**How**: Watch for chronic devaluations, falling net central-bank reserves (often years ahead), suspended convertibility, claims-on-gold exceeding reserves, and a war/defeat with large debts. Reserve status lags all other strengths, then plunges "slowly then suddenly" once the interest rate can't offset the decline.

## The Win-Win Point-Allocation Negotiation
**When to use**: Resolving conflicts (personal to geopolitical) without a lose-lose fight.
**How**: Each party ranks its top-10 wants, allocating 100 points. Identify and trade items that are low-priority for you but high for them (and vice versa). Know and respect each other's existential red lines. Winning = getting what's most important without losing what's most important.

## Power Timing (When to Fight vs. Negotiate)
**When to use**: Deciding whether to press a conflict now or later.
**How**: Use power when it's greatest. Fight *early* if your relative power is declining; fight *later* if rising. Have power, respect power (don't fight a war you'll lose), use power wisely (soft power/generosity often beats bullying; hide the knife until needed).

## Watch the Escalation Ladder of the 5 (+2) Wars
**When to use**: Tracking how a great-power rivalry could turn hot.
**How**: Trade → technology → capital → geopolitical wars typically precede military war by ~5–10 years. The escalation marker is **cutting off essential imports** (oil, tech, rare earths). Also add culture war (what each will die for) and the war with ourselves (internal strength). Identify each side's red lines and trip wires.

## The "Dropped into 1900" Portfolio Stress Test
**When to use**: Testing an investment strategy against tail risk.
**How**: Simulate your strategy across every decade and many countries since 1900, not just US/UK since the 1950s. Because 7 of 10 great powers of 1900 saw wealth wiped out at least once, always hold protections against big adverse moves and against being wrong. Define risk as failing to meet your needs — not volatility.

## The 4-Determinant Market Read
**When to use**: Connecting the world to the markets.
**How**: All markets are driven by growth, inflation, risk premiums, discount rates. Forecast how each will move (given fiscal/monetary policy) and balance assets as building blocks so the portfolio has no environmental bias, then tactically tilt. When money/credit is printed cheaply, assets rise — own them; ask always whether interest compensates for devaluation risk.
