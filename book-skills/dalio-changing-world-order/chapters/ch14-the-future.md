# Chapter 14: The Future

## Core Idea
You can't predict the future, but you can (1) perceive and adapt to what's happening, (2) assign probabilities, and (3) protect against the unacceptable. Extrapolate evolution's upward trend, watch the cycles and indicators for the bumps that can knock you out — and treat the currency-value risk and the ~35% war / ~30% US civil-war odds as real.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The three lenses for the future**: (a) **evolution** (steady improvement, mostly extrapolatable — population, life expectancy, real GDP/capita all trend up), (b) **cycles** (rhythmic ups/downs like debt bubbles), (c) **bumps** (non-rhythmic shocks like acts of nature). Use indicators to locate yourself in the cycles.
- **The master survival principle**: "Bet on the upside that comes from (a) evolution/productivity improvements, but not so aggressively that (b) cycles and bumps knock you out of the game." Betting on real earnings being greater is nearly a sure bet; betting so much that a bump ruins you is fatal.
- **The 5 highest-impact determinants for the years ahead**: (1) humanity's inventiveness, (2) the debt/money/capital-market cycle, (3) the internal order/disorder cycle, (4) the external order/disorder cycle, (5) acts of nature. The future is "a battle between humanity's inventiveness and these other challenges."
- **The debt-burden gauge** (foreshadows loss of real money value): combines debt/assets, external & internal deficits, debt-service/GDP, own-vs-foreign-currency debt, domestic-vs-foreign holders, and credit rating — deliberately *excluding* reserve-currency status so you can see a country's exposure if it lost that status.

## Key Concepts
- **Currency-value-of-money risk = the biggest long-run risk** and the one most people ignore. Reserve-currency countries can print to service their own-currency debt, so the risk isn't debtor *default* — it's that **creditors hold assets devalued below the inflation rate**. A great transfer of wealth from creditors to debtors (a Jubilee) is coming.
- **Reserve-currency decline pattern**: "slowly for a long time and then very quickly." Reserve status lags all other measures of strength, lives on by entrenchment, then plunges once holding the debt is clearly a bad deal — the decline outpaces the interest rate, triggering self-reinforcing selling (as with the guilder and pound, both amid geopolitical defeats + large debts).
- **The US verdict**: ~70% through its Big Cycle; internal conflict as high as since the late-1960s (political-conflict index highest since the early 1920s); ~30% odds of Stage-6 civil-war dynamics within 10 years — "a dangerously high risk." But the US has the longest-lasting, most admired constitutional order — less likely to be abandoned, more traumatic if it is.
- **War odds**: US-China already in 4 of 5 wars (trade, tech, capital, geopolitical), not yet military; those four historically precede military war by ~5–10 years. ~35% odds of a big war in 10 years (a "wild guess"). Mutually assured destruction should prevent it — unless a breakthrough (e.g., quantum computing) gives one side an asymmetric advantage.

## Mental Models
- **"He who lives by the crystal ball is destined to eat ground glass"**: Study the past to gauge what's likely *and* to protect against being wrong — not to make point predictions.
- **Extrapolate, but expect surprise**: Pure extrapolation would have failed anyone standing in 1750 (they'd never have imagined capitalism, democracy, or the US). Big curveballs are **paradigm shifts** — you can't anticipate them but you can perceive, understand, and adapt; good indicators also help you tell a real shift from a passing fad.
- **"The empire long divided must unite and long united must divide"** (Romance of the Three Kingdoms) — and **"peace is profitable, war is costly."**
- **"Power rules; tests of power are how one learns who rules."** Watch whether governance rules are respected: contested elections adjudicated and accepted = order respected; power grabbed = revolutionary-change risk.
- **Best way to fight a war = get strong and show it** so the opponent doesn't want to fight (the Chinese approach; MAD in modern form).
- **Human + computer**: Computers supply memory, speed, and unemotional processing; humans supply imagination, intuition, common sense, values, and emotional intelligence — together, radical improvement (quantum computing + AI could be "the greatest shift in wealth and power the world has ever seen").

## Anti-patterns
- **Extrapolating only the good post-1945 trends**: Dalio's father's generation missed the post-war boom because their mindset was formed by depression and war; symmetrically, ignoring worst cases (wealth wiped out/confiscated) is equally dangerous. "Unless someone shows me better evidence that they won't happen than the fact that they haven't happened yet, I assume they will and protect myself."
- **Overpaying for great companies**: You can own great innovators and lose money because they're expensive, or own bad ones and profit because they're cheap — price matters, and returns depend on how governments divide productivity's profits.
- **Ignoring the currency risk**: Most people watch their assets, not their currency — the costliest blind spot late in the debt cycle.

## Worked Example
**Reading the world through the five determinants (2021 snapshot).** *Inventiveness:* US #1 by a marginal lead over China (driven by US share of R&D, researchers, VC), but US steady while China rises fast — and whoever wins the tech war usually wins the rest. *Debt/money cycle:* reserve-currency promises (esp. the dollar) are too large to be paid in hard money → money will be printed, rates held below inflation → wealth redistributed from creditors to debtors; the US has high debt but in its own reserve currency (low default risk, high devaluation risk — "if the US lost reserve status it would be in serious financial trouble"); Russia/Germany least indebted, China mid-pack with mostly domestic-currency, domestically-held debt and emerging reserve status. *Internal order:* US in Stage 5 (bad finances + intense conflict; polarization extreme — e.g., 15% of Republicans and 20% of Democrats thought the country better off if many of the other side "just died"), China in Stage 3 (peace/prosperity). *External order:* US-China conflict is 1 standard deviation above normal, the most dangerous pairing. *Acts of nature:* extreme environmental events rose from <50/yr (1970) to ~200/yr (2020) and are trending higher — slow, well-telegraphed, and adaptable-to, but costly. ## Key Takeaways
1. Handle the future by perceiving/adapting, assigning probabilities, and protecting against the unacceptable — not by predicting.
2. Bet on evolution's upside, but never so much that a cycle or bump knocks you out; quality indicators are the safeguard.
3. The currency-value-of-money risk is the biggest long-run risk — the danger is devaluation of creditors' assets, not debtor default.
4. Reserve currencies decline slowly then suddenly; the dollar most probably follows the guilder/pound pattern.
5. The US is ~70% through its cycle with ~30% civil-war-dynamic odds in 10 years; US-China war odds ~35%, held down by mutually assured destruction.
6. Whether the US declines gracefully and whether both powers avoid war depends most on their *internal* capacities — measurable via the 18 determinants and within their control.
