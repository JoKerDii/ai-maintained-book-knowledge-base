# Chapter 11: The Big Cycle Rise and Decline of the United States and the Dollar

## Core Idea
The US followed the same rise script (education → innovation → competitiveness → output → financial center → reserve currency), peaked with ~2/3 of the world's gold and half its production in 1945, and is now, by Dalio's model, **roughly 70% (±10%) through its Big Cycle** — not yet in civil war, but with high and rising internal conflict.

## Frameworks Introduced (applied)
- **The 3 types of monetary policy (MP1/MP2/MP3)**: (1) **MP1** — interest-rate-driven (preferred, used first); (2) **MP2** — printing money to buy financial assets ("quantitative easing," used when rates hit 0%); (3) **MP3** — fiscal/monetary coordination: the government does debt-financed spending and the central bank buys the debt (last resort). 2008 forced MP1→MP2; 2020 forced MP2→MP3.
- **The value-reciprocals principle** (Dalio's 1982 lesson): the value of assets is the reciprocal of the value of money/credit (cheaper money → more expensive assets), and the value of money is the reciprocal of its quantity. → When central banks print a lot and make money cheap, be more aggressive owning assets.
- **The US Big-Cycle verdict**: ~70% through; the three Stage-5→6 markers Dalio is watching now: (1) rules being disregarded, (2) both sides emotionally attacking each other, (3) blood being spilled.

## Key Concepts
- **The peaceful founding**: The US uniquely built its new domestic order through negotiation and near-total respect for agreements (Articles of Confederation → Constitution) — a great start.
- **The dollar's long, non-linear ascent**: A century of underdeveloped, panic-prone banking (8 NYC panics 1836–1913, no central bank) → Federal Reserve created 1913 → WWI (US kept gold convertibility, Allies owed it debts) → New York overtakes London → Bretton Woods (1944) makes the dollar the reserve currency.
- **Guns and butter, again**: 1960s Americans assumed they could afford the space program, War on Poverty, *and* Vietnam indefinitely because the dollar seemed secure — the classic Top-phase overextension.
- **Nixon's default (Aug 1971)**: Claims on gold exceeded US gold reserves (which were visibly falling); the US "defaulted" on gold convertibility, moving to a Type 3 fiat system → 1970s stagflation.
- **The left/right swing**: Economics and politics swing between left and right "like fashion — the widths of ties and lengths of skirts" — when one extreme peaks, expect a comparable move back (1970s inflation → Volcker/Reagan/Thatcher discipline).

## Mental Models
- **International relations run on raw power** → strong military and alliances (NATO 1949) matter more than treaties (League, UN, WTO).
- **The biggest military risk is losing the economic/technology wars**: Military spending competes with social spending, and military tech tracks private-sector tech — the USSR bankrupted itself out-spending the US.
- **Debt in your own currency is manageable**: Dalio's formative 1982 loss — he bet on a debt-default depression that never came because the Fed could print/restructure dollar debt and US accounting didn't force banks to book the losses.

## Anti-patterns
- **Treating early stagflation as "a temporary setback"**: 1970s Americans didn't recognize the balance-of-payments problem as a sign of things to come until inflation traumatized them.
- **Spending borrowed money for votes**: "Most people pay attention to what they get, not where the money comes from" — the political incentive to over-borrow and over-promise (1990–2008).
- **QE that doesn't trickle down**: 2008–2020 money-printing inflated financial-asset prices (owned by the rich) → wealth/income gaps grew to the largest since 1930–45, feeding populism (Trump 2016) and record political polarization.

## Worked Example
**The 1971–1991 dollar cycle, decade by decade.** *1960s:* guns-and-butter deficits, dollar claims on gold ballooning while US gold reserves fell. *1971:* Nixon closes the gold window → fiat/Type 3. *1970s:* unanchored money growth + OPEC oil + drought → stagflation; by decade's end real GDP ~2%, inflation ~14%, short rates ~13%; gold ran from $35 (fixed since 1944) to $850 (1980), stocks and bonds crushed in real terms. *1979–82:* Carter appoints Volcker, who caps M1 growth at 5.5% despite fierce backlash, driving rates "to the highest since Jesus Christ" (Schmidt) → worst downturn since the Depression; Mexico defaults (Aug 1982) — yet US stocks *rallied*. *1980s:* Reagan/Thatcher discipline; falling inflation and rates → a stock/bond boom in the US even as debt-burdened emerging economies (no central bank to bail them) had inflationary depressions (resolved via the 1989 Brady Plan). Through it all the dollar stayed the leading reserve currency — a demonstration of the benefit of having the world's debts denominated in your currency.

## Key Takeaways
1. The US rose on the classic sequence and peaked in 1945 as the uncontested new-order power (Bretton Woods, UN, IMF/World Bank, NATO).
2. Reserve status let the US over-borrow ("guns and butter"), setting up 1971's break from gold and the fiat era.
3. MP1 → MP2 (2008) → MP3 (2020): each debt crisis pushed monetary policy to the next, more extreme tool; the pattern persists "until it no longer works."
4. When money/credit is printed cheaply, asset prices rise (own assets); debt in your own currency is manageable via printing/restructuring.
5. QE-driven booms widen wealth gaps → populism and record polarization — the Stage-5 internal-conflict signature.
6. The US is ~70% through its Big Cycle; watch the three Stage-5→6 markers (rules disregarded, emotional mutual attacks, blood spilled).
