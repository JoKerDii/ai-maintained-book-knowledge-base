# Chapter 5: The Big Cycle of Internal Order and Disorder

## Core Idea
Every country cycles through six internal stages — from new order to peace/prosperity to excess to conflict to civil war and back — over roughly 100 years. Like the stages of a disease, each has diagnosable symptoms, a different risk profile, and calls for a different kind of leader. The US is now in Stage 5.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 6 Stages of the Internal Cycle**:
  1. **New order begins; leadership consolidates power** (often via purges). Best leader: the *consolidator of power* (strong, smart, politically astute fighter).
  2. **Resource-allocation systems & bureaucracies are built** ("early prosperity"). Best leader: the *civil engineer* (Adenauer, Lee Kuan Yew, Deng Xiaoping).
  3. **Peace and prosperity** ("mid-prosperity" — the sweet spot). Best leader: the *inspirational visionary* (Gladstone, Bismarck, JFK).
  4. **Excesses** ("bubble prosperity"). Best leader: the *well-grounded, disciplined leader* who restrains the crowd (rare).
  5. **Bad financial conditions & intense conflict**. Best leader: a *strong peacemaker* (ideal, rare) or a *strong fighter*.
  6. **Civil wars / revolutions**. Best leader: the *inspirational general* (brutal enough to win). → back to Stage 1.
- **The Classic Toxic Mix** (what ignites big internal conflict): (1) bad financial shape (big debts/obligations), (2) large income/wealth/values gaps, (3) a severe negative economic shock. Financial condition = the shock absorber; the size of the gaps = the fragility.
- **The Red-Flag Index → civil-war probability**: Count economic red flags (high inequality, high debt/deficits, inflation, bad growth). 60–80% present → ~1-in-6 chance of severe internal conflict; >80% → ~1-in-3. **The US is in the 60–80% bucket.**
- **The single most reliable leading indicator of civil war/revolution**: bankrupt government finances **combined with** big wealth gaps — because a broke government can't save the private sector, can't buy what it needs, and is out of power.

## Key Concepts
- **The Stage-5 markers to watch** (further along = closer to Stage 6): **decadence**, **bureaucracy** (blocks obviously-good decisions), **populism & polarization** (moderates become the minority in 5, cease to exist in 6), **class warfare & demonization/scapegoating**, **loss of truth in the public domain** (media as political weapon), and **rule-following fading into raw fighting**.
- **The tax cycle / hollowing-out**: When the "haves" realize they'll be taxed to service debt, they leave — cutting revenue and accelerating decline. Raising taxes + cutting spending amid big wealth gaps and bad conditions is *the* leading indicator of civil war/revolution.
- **The Stage-5→6 line**: Crossed when the system for resolving disagreements stops working — people are violent, leadership has lost control. **People dying in the fighting** is the marker that Stage 6 has begun.
- **Middle-class test (Aristotle)**: States are well-administered when the middle class is large; without it, factions and dissension end the state.

## Mental Models
- **Diagnose the stage like a disease stage**: Being in Stage 5 doesn't make Stage 6 inevitable, but it defines the risks and the right treatment — and it's best to halt progress before the late stages.
- **Match the leader to the stage**: "Inspirational generals" (Churchill, Mao) are lousy "civil engineers." The rarest greats (Tang Taizong, Augustus) took a country through Stages 6→1→2 at scale.
- **"When in doubt, get out"**: If you don't want to be in a civil war, leave while you can — late in Stage 5 the doors (and capital controls) close on people, money, and investments.
- **How much will the system bend before it breaks?** The central Stage-5 question. Democracies bend more (peaceful regime change) but break down in great conflict, risking a populist autocrat who "gets control of the chaos."
- **Averages don't matter as much as the number of people suffering and their power**: Policies good for "the whole" (free trade, automation) endanger the whole if the pie isn't divided so most people benefit.

## Anti-patterns
- **"It won't happen here"**: The default assumption after a long peace; better to watch the markers.
- **Giving away printed money without productivity gains**: Money must fund productivity/returns (education, infrastructure, R&D) or it just devalues the currency — the essential ingredient for a successful restructuring.
- **Letting causes matter more than the system**: When passions override the rules for making decisions, and rules aren't crystal-clear and valued, the legal system is in jeopardy and raw fighting begins.
- **Rigidly believing one economic/political system is always best**: Circumstances change; systems that don't reform to adapt die. Constantly reform.

## Worked Example
**Reading the US in Stage 5 (the author's own window).** Dalio lives in Connecticut — highest per-capita income, largest wealth/income gap, and among the largest per-capita debt and unfunded pension obligations in the country. The pattern he watches: the richest US cities/states (San Francisco, Chicago, NYC; Connecticut, Illinois, Massachusetts, New York, New Jersey) are also the *most indebted with the largest gaps* — precisely the places most likely to see the tax-cycle hollowing-out as "haves" flee taxation. He sees the haves and have-nots living without contact, stereotyping each other, neither viewing the other empathetically as one community — the classic toxic mix assembling. COVID's health and budget shocks surfaced the have-nots' conditions and widened the gaps. The federal government (which *can* print) runs large deficits with the Fed buying its debt; states (which *can't* print) must raise taxes/cut spending — the more dangerous position. The recommended cure is the same in every case: restructure/devalue enough debt, then invest the created money in **broad-based productivity** (education, infrastructure, research) so living standards rise and debts get paid.

## Key Takeaways
1. Identify the stage by its symptoms; the stage tells you the range of possibilities and the right actions.
2. The lead indicator of civil war = **broke government + big wealth gaps**; the trigger into Stage 6 = deficits producing more debt than non-central-bank buyers will absorb.
3. To have peace and prosperity, a society must have **productivity that benefits most people** — grow *and* divide the pie well.
4. Internal and external wars tend to come close together: domestic conflict creates vulnerabilities foreign powers exploit, and leaders use external enemies to unite the country.
5. Revolutions are typically led by well-educated, charismatic, middle-class people who evolve from idealists into win-at-all-costs fighters.
6. Skilled win-win collaboration to grow and divide the pie is far more rewarding and less painful than fighting civil wars over wealth and power — the loudest lesson of history.
