# Cheatsheet — *Shift* (Ethan Kross)

## Is this emotion a problem yet? (two tests only — never valence)
- **Intensity** — is the reaction proportional? If you exploded at the soccer coach or despaired over jeans, no.
- **Duration** — has it kept running after delivering its message? Clinicians diagnose partly by duration; use the same marker.
- **Neither tripped** → leave it alone. All emotions are functional; you're extracting information.

## Which shifter? (decision tree)
- **Can you look away from the trigger?**
  - **No** (fire in the room, terminal diagnosis, declining parent) → **Perspective shifters** (Ch 5). Distanced self-talk first, then reframe.
  - **Yes** → **Is what you're currently doing working?**
    - **Yes** → keep doing it. Don't overthink.
    - **No, and you're approaching** → switch to avoidance/distraction, or change category.
    - **No, and you're avoiding** → switch to approach, or change category.
- **Are you depleted, flooded, or distracted?** → **Sensory shifter** (Ch 3). It's the only category that works below the cognitive-control threshold.
- **Have you tried the internal three and nothing moves?** → go external:
  - Environment is the driver → **Space** (Ch 6): switch or modify.
  - You need another mind → **Relationship** (Ch 7): an emotional adviser, not a venting partner.
  - The group you're in is the problem → **Culture** (Ch 8): diagnose beliefs/norms/practices, then change or leave.
- **You know the tool and still don't use it** → **WOOP** (Ch 9).

## When feeling X, do Y, because Z
| Feeling / situation | Do | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Stressor **outside** your control (broken leg) | Positive reframe | Reframing helps when the situation is fixed |
| Stressor **inside** your control (cheating partner, toxic job) | Act, don't reframe | Silver-lining a fixable problem predicts greater depression |
| Flooded, can't think straight | Distanced self-talk | Costs no prefrontal effort; works in seconds during the stress "brownout" |
| Intrusive image looping | Stop the video / re-shoot it | Trigger is uncontrollable; trajectory isn't |
| Post-incident anger at an email or a sibling | Walk away, come back tomorrow | Time is the active ingredient of the psychological immune system |
| Grief that ambushes you at bad moments | Pre-set if-then with a 10-second future-projection | Deliberation is unavailable mid-emotion |
| Can't stop comparing on social media | Reframe the comparison, then cut frequency | Comparison is reflexive; only frame and dose are controllable |
| Need a lift, no tool at hand | Do something small for someone nearby | Prosocial acts match mindfulness/gratitude in effect size |
| Just took an emotional hit | Look at a photo of someone you love | Restorative effect substantial; **buffering effect nonexistent** |
| Anxiety about a performance | Read your racing pulse as readiness | Reinterpretation of physiological cues speeds recovery |

## Tells & smells — which failure mode am I in?
| Tell | Diagnosis | Move |
|---|---|---|
| Circling the same thoughts, no progress ("traffic circle with no exit") | **Chatter** — approach is failing | Switch to avoidance or perspective |
| Trying not to think about it, thinking about it constantly | Avoidance Warning Sign 1 | Psych immune system can't handle it alone; approach |
| Substances, compulsive sex/gambling/eating | Avoidance Warning Sign 2 | Bright red flag; different category of help |
| Endless research, unnecessary tests, re-checking locks, asking loved ones repeatedly | Avoidance Warning Sign 3 — **counterfeit approach** | Name the real fear; stop the reassurance loop |
| Rising "I / me / my" in your speech or writing | Zoomed-in immersion | Distance before reframing |
| You're as wound up as the person you're advising | Co-rumination | Pivot: "I have a thought on this, are you okay to hear it?" |
| Only surfaces when someone else raises it | Nothing. Already metabolized. | Don't excavate |

## Trade-off matrix — internal shifters
| | Effort | Speed | Works when flooded? | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Sensory** | Lowest | ms–seconds | **Yes** | Congruency drift; becomes a crutch (food, sex) |
| **Attention (avoid)** | Low | Minutes–days | Yes | Chronic avoidance → anxiety, depression |
| **Attention (approach)** | High | Slow | No | Chatter; dredging up settled things |
| **Distanced self-talk** | Near zero | Seconds | **Yes** | Feels awkward; not a substitute for acting |
| **Reframing** | High | Slow | **No** — the brownout kills it | Negative reframing; worry loops |
| **Mental time travel** | Medium | Seconds–minutes | Partly | Rumination/anxiety if uncorralled |

## Thresholds, durations & defaults the author commits to
- **Two questions** for picking a sensory default: biggest punch / lowest cost. Kross's answer: music (immediate, effective, zero cost), playlists pre-loaded, one tap away.
- **Three "thens" per "if"** — the SEAL contingency standard.
- **Ten seconds** — the duration in the grief WOOP's future-projection plan.
- **The more intense the emotion, the longer before someone is receptive to perspective.** Empathize first, always.
- **Get three phone numbers at every meeting and practice calling while stable** (AA) — you follow through in crisis only if you rehearsed out of it.
- **A little** emotional sharing and validation is healthy; a group-text spiral is contagion.
- **Success criterion**: "not overtaken," never "not feeling." Fear in a threatening situation should stay.

## Exact phrasings worth keeping
- Self-talk: *"Okay, [Name]. You've got to get going. You need to start acting."*
- Advising: *"Tell me more about that. That sounds hard. I'm not surprised you feel this way."*
- Pivot request: *"I have a thought on this, are you okay to hear it?"*
- Front-loading an advice-giver: *"Can I just vent about this for a bit, and then maybe you have some ideas for me?"*
- Anti-catastrophizing (AA): *"Where are your feet?"*
- Response vs. reaction: a response is intentional and constructive; a reaction is knee-jerk.

## The four-question spaces audit
1. What can I do **today** to curate this space?
2. What can I **remove** to turn stress *down*?
3. What can I **add** to turn calm or joy *up*?
4. What **local emotional oases** exist, and when do I visit them this week?

## WOOP template
**W** — what do I want? (challenging but feasible)
**O** — best thing about achieving it, and how will it feel?
**O** — the main obstacle **within myself** (never external)
**P** — IF ____(obstacle)____, THEN ____(specific action, named shifter, with a duration)____.

## Culture: diagnose in three layers
- **Values** stated but vague → make them concrete and spell out the specifics.
- **Norms** absent or contradicting values → run a norms audit; change is easier than expected because people want to belong.
- **Practices** missing → this is almost always the gap. Never issue a value as an instruction ("be vulnerable"); build the training for it.
- **Nothing fixable** → leaving is a legitimate move. People opt out of toxic cultures every day.

## The one rule above all
There is **no one-size-fits-all solution**. Experiment to find *your* combination, then use WOOP to make sure you actually deploy it.
