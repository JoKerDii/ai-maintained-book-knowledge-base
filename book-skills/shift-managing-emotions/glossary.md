# Glossary — *Shift* (Ethan Kross)

**ABCs** — Adverse event → Belief → Consequence; change B and C follows. Taught to Kross by David Williams, 1999. (Ch 5)

**Attention** — the mental spotlight determining what enters awareness; usually automatic, but consciously steerable toward (approach) or away from (avoidance) a target. (Ch 4)

**Beliefs and values** — what the people in a group care about; the foundation layer of the cultural blueprint. (Ch 8)

**Chatter** — getting stuck in a negative thought loop; the shared alarm bell signaling that either approach or avoidance has stopped working. (Ch 4)

**Cognitive control** — the prefrontal capacity to modulate automatic responses, think abstractly, divert attention, and switch priorities. Works to amplify emotion as well as dampen it. (Ch 2)

**Cognitive miser** — the human default of conserving mental resources by taking low-friction, rutted paths. (Ch 3)

**Cognitive support** — helping someone work through a situation rather than only commiserating; the routinely missing half of emotional support. (Ch 7)

**Collectivist vs. individualistic cultures** — where social harmony is paramount (e.g., Japan) suppression is more common and viewed positively; where it isn't, expression is prized. Makes "suppression is unhealthy" a local verdict. (Ch 8)

**Compensatory control** — control gained from performing a ritual's prescribed steps, offsetting a situation that feels out of control. (Ch 8)

**Co-rumination / venting** — rehashing a problem together past the point of usefulness; good for bonding, bad for the problem. (Ch 7)

**Distanced self-talk** — silently referring to yourself as "you," by name, or in third person to gain perspective; Kross's stated go-to tool. (Ch 5)

**Duration** — how long an emotion persists after it has delivered its message; one of the two check-engine lights. (Ch 1)

**Dwelling** — fixating on obstacles so completely you can't see a path forward; counterproductive alone, essential when paired with a vision. (Ch 9)

**Ecological intelligence hypothesis** — cognitive control evolved partly to manage food-supply uncertainty (don't eat all the berries now). (Ch 2)

**Emotion** — a loosely coordinated response of feeling, thinking, and bodily reaction to events judged meaningful; an instrument for responding to a situation. (Ch 1)

**Emotional advisers** — the subset of your network who both validate *and* broaden your perspective; the people to call in real distress. (Ch 7)

**Emotional congruency effect** — when feeling bad, spontaneously choosing sensory input that perpetuates the mood. (Ch 3)

**Emotional contagion** — emotions spreading person to person like a virus, beginning with unconscious mimicry. (Ch 1, Ch 7)

**Emotional oases** — accessible restorative places; refilling stations along a marathon route. (Ch 6)

**Emotional Stradivarius** — the metaphor for your emotional apparatus: a priceless instrument nobody taught you to play. (Ch 1)

**Emotion regulation** — altering the trajectory of an emotional response by speeding it up, slowing it down, or changing its intensity. (Ch 1)

**Feelings** — the consciously accessible part of an emotional experience; the "fever" readout. (Ch 1)

**Flexibility** — the capacity to deploy attention both toward and away as context demands; Bonanno's best predictor of resilience. (Ch 4)

**Foreign language effect** — reasoning more objectively and with less emotional charge in a non-native language. (Ch 5)

**Functional interdependence** — a condition where people must cooperate to succeed, as in rice farming; shapes regional emotional culture. (Ch 6)

**Garcia effect** — a sensory-learned aversion acquired after a *single* experience, unlike most learning. (Ch 3)

**Green space** (Laurie Santos's sense) — the location of epiphany you decamp to for conceptual distance; needn't be green, only significant and restorative. (Ch 6)

**Implementation intention** — a rehearsed if-then link between a specific situation and a specific action (Gollwitzer). (Ch 9)

**Indulging** — fantasizing about a desired outcome without confronting obstacles; actively lowers goal attainment. (Ch 9)

**Intensity** — whether an emotional reaction is proportional to its circumstance; one of the two check-engine lights. (Ch 1)

**Intrusive thoughts** — spontaneous, often distressing thoughts arising outside control; reported by 94% of psychologically healthy people. (Ch 2)

**Law of least work / least effort** — organisms choose the path of least physical and mental effort; the main reason effortful tools go unused. (Ch 3, Ch 9)

**Linguistic markers of immersion** — overuse of "I," "me," "my" signaling absorption in a problem; the verbal zoomed-in selfie. (Ch 5)

**Master switch** — the metaphor for culture: one press governs many settings at once. (Ch 8)

**Mental contrasting** — pairing a vivid desired future with the crucial internal obstacle (Oettingen). (Ch 9)

**Mental representation** — the image, memory, or thought tagged as threat whose activation produces fear (Foa & Kozak). (Ch 4)

**Mental time travel** — deliberately recalling the past or simulating the future to gain distance and surface impermanence. (Ch 5)

**Myth of universal approach** — the belief that difficult feelings must always be confronted, expressed, and processed. (Ch 4)

**Negative reframing** — using cognitive control to construe a situation as worse; the engine of worry loops. (Ch 5)

**Norms** — spoken and unspoken rules that promote and maintain a culture's values; enforced by inclusion and exclusion. (Ch 8)

**Norms audit** — cataloguing a group's current norms and testing them against its stated values. (Ch 8)

**Place attachment** — an emotional bond to a specific place; about resonance with you, not the place's qualities. (Ch 6)

**Practices** — rituals, exercises, and teachings that let a group actualize its beliefs and adopt its norms. (Ch 8)

**Propinquity effect** — proximity as a leading determinant of whom you befriend and marry. (Ch 6)

**Prosocial spending** — spending resources on others; produces a happiness boost regardless of amount. (Ch 7)

**Proust effect** — a sensory cue unlocking an involuntary autobiographical memory and its original emotion. (Ch 3)

**Psychological immune system** — the built-in system metabolizing psychological threat; its key component is time. (Ch 4)

**Reframing / reappraisal / reconstrual / cognitive change** — four names for changing how you construe a situation to change how you feel; the foundation of CBT. (Ch 5)

**Reframing paradox** — worriers try to reframe and neurally fail, exerting more effort for less benefit. (Ch 5)

**Ritual** — a fixed, meaning-infused sequence of behaviors performed the same way each time. (Ch 8)

**Self-efficacy** — belief in your capacity to create change in an area of life; a "master belief," linked to a 28% performance increase. (Ch 2)

**Sensory bundle** — an immersive experience braiding several sensory channels together (baking, surfing, cooking, nature). (Ch 3)

**Sensory shifter** — a deliberate sensory input used to change emotional state; the lowest-effort tool in the book. (Ch 3)

**Signal, not noise** — treating each emotion as directional information rather than interference. (Ch 1)

**Situation modification** — deliberately altering your environment to remove emotional triggers or add emotional supports. (Ch 6)

**Social intelligence hypothesis** — cognitive control evolved to navigate group life, where unmanaged anger meant exile. (Ch 2)

**Solomon's paradox** — the asymmetry between wisdom dispensed to others and wisdom applied to one's own life. (Ch 5)

**Space shifters** — switching or modifying physical space to change emotional state. (Ch 6)

**Trajectory** — the ongoing arc of an emotion after onset; the controllable half of the equation. (Ch 2)

**Trigger** — the event that sets off an emotional reaction plus its automatic response; uncontrollable. (Ch 2)

**Wise reasoning** — open-mindedness, perspective-taking, and intellectual humility; increased by distanced language. (Ch 5)

**WOOP** — Wish, Outcome, Obstacle, Plan; the merger of mental contrasting and implementation intentions. (Ch 9)
