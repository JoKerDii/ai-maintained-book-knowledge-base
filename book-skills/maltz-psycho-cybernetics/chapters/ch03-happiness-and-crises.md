# Chapter 3: Happiness and Unhappiness / Winning Out Over Crises (Book One: The Conquest of Frustration, ch. 4–5)

## Core Idea
Happiness is a **habit** you install deliberately, like brushing your teeth — not a reward that arrives when conditions improve. And a crisis is survivable because you carry a built-in servo-mechanism ("your wonderful computer") that works if you stop jamming it with worry.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Happiness Habit**
  - When to use: daily, especially on grey days.
  - How: (1) decide happiness is now, not conditional; (2) dig out your buried assets — compassion, resilience, laughter; (3) own your free eight hours (the hours that are neither work nor sleep) and spend them on activities *you* like, not ones that please someone else; (4) live in a climate of happiness — avoid the "it can't be done" people; (5) help someone; (6) start each morning with a small smile in the mirror and a resolution to do the best you know how today.
  - Failure mode: forcing an activity on yourself to please others. That is unhappiness wearing a hobby.
- **The Seven Key Characteristics of Frustration** — Maltz's failure-mechanism roster, later formalized as the F-A-I-L-U-R-E acronym (see Ch 24): **Fear, Aggressiveness, Insecurity, Loneliness, Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness.**
  - Fear → with a goal in mind, converts into an opportunity for growth.
  - Aggressiveness → stepping on others' toes; "whenever you hurt someone else, you hurt yourself twice as much."
  - Insecurity / Loneliness → require your consent.
  - Uncertainty → bends to a goal.
  - Resentment → "the gout of the mind"; termites boring holes in spirit.
  - Emptiness → putting your integrity in a valise, locking it in an airport locker, throwing away the key.
- **The decompression chamber**: a designated room in your mind where, under frustration, you get back to yourself, reassert identity, regroup your positive instincts, and give yourself another chance.

## Key Concepts
- **Your "wonderful computer"**: the goal-striving mechanism in the mid-brain — smaller than a hazelnut, and no committee of scientists could build it. It diagnoses your *health*, not your disease.
- **Success instincts / success-mechanism**: reactivated by supplying a goal, not by trying harder.
- **The free eight hours**: the daily block outside work and sleep. "You're a king, you're a queen, you own stock in yourself."
- **Consent principle**: no one makes you unhappy, lonely, or robbed of identity without it.

## Mental Models
- **Let the telephone ring.** At a lecture, a phone kept ringing and annoying Maltz; he simply took the receiver off the hook. Under pressure, don't make a mountain out of a molehill — remove the small aggravation before it recruits you into a large one.
- Think of yourself as an **archaeologist of your own negative feelings**: dig, dig, dig to uproot them — but dig for the buried assets too.
- **Positive doing, not positive thinking.** Turn a thought into a creative opportunity: set the goal, then go.
- Relaxation is not a luxury but **the enabling condition for problem-solving** — "denied sometimes even to millionaires and kings; you cannot buy it."

## Anti-patterns
- **Conditional happiness**: "I'll be happy if X." External conditions may or may not deliver; the "if" is the trap.
- **Evangelizing at the uninterested**: don't push Psycho-Cybernetics on people who don't want to listen. Listen instead.
- **Believing you were born unhappy**: "Whoever told you that you were born to be unhappy?" A disadvantaged background is a fact, not a verdict.
- **Retaining tension as a way of life** rather than treating it as a stimulus you pass through toward a goal.

## Worked Example
Maltz's morning protocol for installing the happiness habit, as he actually spells it out:

- Wake up. However you wake — scratching your back, yawning, burying your head in the pillow — **think of yourself with a little kindness**. "I think you're entitled to it."
- Go to the mirror. Start with a small smile. Try to like yourself.
- Resolve out loud: *I will do the best I know how, under whatever circumstances, to reach my goals today.*
- Accept the escape clause in advance: **"And if you fail, you fail: there's no disgrace in it, and the next day, you try all over again."** This is not resignation; it is what makes the practice repeatable.
- Hold a "meeting with your self-image" — remind it that it was born to fulfillment, achievement, success.
- During the day, guard the climate: avoid the negative broadcasters, entertain good thoughts about yourself *and* others rather than poisoning yourself with depressed thoughts about both.
- In the free eight hours, do the thing you actually enjoy. Maltz's claim is mechanical: the better you use those hours, the more you relax, and the better you sleep.

## Key Takeaways
1. Happiness is created inside and given by you to you — money, situations, and people cannot hand it over.
2. Install it as a habit with a fixed daily trigger (the mirror on waking), including a pre-agreed permission to fail.
3. Run the seven-characteristic scan (Fear, Aggressiveness, Insecurity, Loneliness, Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness) to name what is actually jamming you.
4. Protect the free eight hours — they are where fulfillment is actually manufactured.
5. Build a decompression chamber in your mind before you need it.
