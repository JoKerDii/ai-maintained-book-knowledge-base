# Chapter 2: Winging Away From Frustration / The Road to Self-Respect (Book One: The Conquest of Frustration, ch. 2–3)

## Core Idea
The two things that keep frustration alive are the **Tomorrow Illusion** (deferring your life to a day that never arrives) and a misread of **conscience** (which is really your self-image punishing you). Both are dissolved by forgiveness, which Maltz treats as an operational technique, not a sentiment.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Tomorrow Illusion / "Mañana, Incorporated"**: the habit of saying "when tomorrow comes, things will be better."
  - When to use: whenever you catch a plan sliding one day forward.
  - How: reverse the deferral. Do the constructive thing *today*; postpone only the destructive things. "When resentment strikes you, when hatred strikes you — look in the mirror and say: wait till tomorrow. I'll give vent to my anger tomorrow."
  - Why it works: frustration is the thief of time; deferral gives negative feeling a permanent lease.
- **Conscience as self-image**: Maltz rejects Shakespeare's "conscience doth make cowards of us all." His formulation: *we make cowards of our conscience*. Conscience is simply your opinion of yourself, and it only becomes audible after a blunder.
  - How to use it: when you say "I can't live with my conscience," translate it literally — you can't live with *yourself*. Then treat it as a self-image problem, which is fixable.
- **Image-maker and image-breaker**: you are both. Forgiveness is the tool that breaks the old image so the new one can be built.
- **The mirror address**: speak to yourself in the mirror by name ("Charlie, old boy" / "Betsy, baby") and declare the ceasefire out loud. Maltz's most-repeated physical technique — it recurs as the Goal Builders (Ch 10) and Mirror Magic (Ch 16).

## Key Concepts
- **Your wings**: faith and belief in yourself. Frustration clips them.
- **Negative warhead**: the accumulated charge of hurt feelings, remorse, distress, loneliness and hatred that detonates as misunderstanding between people.
- **Blueprint / road map of yourself**: your explicit opinion of yourself plus where you intend to go. "If it's no good, you will fail."
- **Peace of mind**: the port Psycho-Cybernetics steers toward — not achievement.
- **The great escape**: escaping back *to* yourself. The world is rooting for this one.

## Mental Models
- Think of self-criticism as a **tennis match against your doubles partner** — you are on the same side; stop returning serve at yourself. Maltz: "Let's play ball together."
- Use **imagination as a sculptor's chisel**: chip the negative feelings off the self-image so it can shine, rather than adding new positive material.
- **Compliment people while they are alive.** Waiting until the funeral to say what a great person someone was is the same deferral error as Mañana, Inc., applied to others.
- **Creative imagination is domestic-scale.** A woman cooking dinner for her family and a man visiting a hospital bedside are both using imagination creatively; you don't need world fame to qualify.

## Anti-patterns
- **Chronic self-criticism**: the single practice Maltz asks you to stop first. "Once you stop criticizing yourself, you stop criticizing other people."
- **Treating forgiveness as a word**: mumbling "I forgive him" and calling the slate clean. Real forgiveness is an operation, not an incantation.
- **Living in fantasy** rather than accepting yourself — because reality then feels like a frustrating place to land.
- **Hiding behind the mask** while telling yourself you are protecting your dignity; the mask *is* the leak.

## Worked Example
Maltz walks the "operation on the face of your mind" step by step, and it is deliberately literal because he is a surgeon describing his own routine — cap, gown, mask, gloves, then the incision.

1. Identify the scar: a specific error, blunder, hatred or bigotry you are still nursing.
2. Scrub in: go to a mirror, alone, so nobody thinks you've gone crazy.
3. Excise: say aloud that you refuse from this moment on to play games with yourself. "There have been some rumors about your being a conscience. But what does that mean? You're my friend. I like you."
4. Close: forgive yourself for that specific item, shed the guilt.
5. Post-op check: look again. The "prune wrinkles" on the face of your mind are gone. Maltz's claim is that this is where youth actually lives — "you are old whether you are three or one hundred and three" if you keep the negative feelings.

He then tells the treasure story against himself: a rumor said a million dollars was hidden on the eighteenth floor of his building, and one moonlit night he found his butler out on the terrace in a white coat with pick and shovel, hacking at the rock garden. The point: everyone digs outside for treasure that is buried in the self-image.

## Key Takeaways
1. Postpone only your resentment. Never postpone your goal.
2. When "conscience" bothers you, restate it as "I am annoyed with my opinion of myself" — now it is workable.
3. Forgiveness is a *procedure*: name the specific item, cancel it aloud, verify in the mirror.
4. Stop criticizing yourself first; criticism of others stops as a side effect.
5. You are an image-breaker as well as an image-maker — demolition is half the work.
