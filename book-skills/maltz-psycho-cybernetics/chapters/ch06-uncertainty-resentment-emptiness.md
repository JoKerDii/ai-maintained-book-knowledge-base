# Chapter 6: Winning Out Over Uncertainty / Resentment / Emptiness (Book Two: The Search for Self-Respect, ch. 4–6)

## Core Idea
Maltz names three enemies of self-respect and treats each as mechanically fixable: uncertainty yields to a goal, resentment yields to rationally audited anxiety, and emptiness yields to direction.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Living hopefully with anxiety** — you don't eliminate uncertainty, you stop personalizing it.
  - When to use: when the future is genuinely unknown and you are treating that as a verdict about you.
  - How: (1) don't take uncertainties personally — the world is uncertain for everyone; (2) accept anxiety as *creative while choosing* and dead weight after; (3) translate theory into action — move toward the objective with wisdom and determination *in spite of* the uncertainty.
  - Why it works: "When you reach for a goal, you bend uncertainty to your will." A goal converts an open-ended threat into a bounded problem.
- **The Resentment Complex**: resentment is downstream of unmanaged anxiety. The intervention is therefore at the anxiety layer, not the grievance layer.
  - How: audit your fears retrospectively (see Worked Example) and discover empirically how many never materialized.
- **Five Ways to Vanquish Emptiness**:
  1. **Stop sleepwalking.** Sleep eight hours soundly; when awake, *stay awake*. Concentrate on what you are doing; stop doing things halfheartedly. Concentration is exhilarating, not exhausting.
  2. **Resign from Mañana Incorporated.** Members never get dividends — they are in the business of rationalizing why a thing must wait for a tomorrow nobody has ever experienced.
  3. **Renew your sense of direction.** Set goals daily and don't downgrade them.
  4. **Enrich your self-image.** A good opinion of yourself dissolves the empty feeling directly.
  5. Orient outward — give your self-respect away, and involvement fills the void.

## Key Concepts
- **Emptiness**: the third enemy; "the empty person lacks goals," and his emptiness is linked to indifference about the events of his day.
- **The torture of resentment in fantasy**: rehearsing grievances mentally, which delivers all the physiological cost of the injury with none of the resolution.
- **Twentieth-century emptiness**: affluence with no direction — activity without purpose.
- **The secure person in an anxious age**: not someone with fewer uncertainties, someone with goals.
- **Yesterday Tomorrow Complex**: living roughly 60% in yesterday and 20% in tomorrow, leaving almost nothing for today.

## Mental Models
- **The circling airplane.** Imagine boarding a plane that leaves New York, whisks over Philadelphia, circles back, jets to Chicago, drifts toward Canada, circles back, and never lands. Absurd — and it is exactly what a person with no sense of direction does with a life. "An airplane with no sense of direction is a horrifying thing to contemplate."
- **Anxiety as a pre-decision instrument.** Worry before the decision is creative; worry after the decision is a fifty-pound weight carried all day for nothing.
- **Emptiness as a locked airport locker**: you put your integrity, self-respect and self-image in a valise, lock them away, and throw away the key — the contents are intact, the access is not.
- **Concentration compounds.** Living more fully while awake makes you sleep more soundly, which makes the next day sharper.

## Anti-patterns
- **Taking uncertainty personally** — reading a volatile market, a reorganization, or a diagnosis as a statement about your worth.
- **Nursing grievances in fantasy** rather than auditing the anxiety that feeds them.
- **Downgrading your goals so they don't count**: "So you won't make a million dollars. So your name won't be in the headlines. It doesn't matter. What does matter is that your goals mean something *to you*."
- **Waiting for external sanction** that you are somebody. "No one else has to sanction that you are somebody; this is something that you give yourself."
- **Halfhearted engagement** — the sleepwalker's default, which produces emptiness reliably.

## Worked Example
**Goal Builder 5 — Overcoming Resentment.** Maltz's premise: anxieties lead to resentment, so handle the anxieties more rationally and the resentment loses its fuel.

1. Write down what you are worried about *today*.
2. Think back to last week. What were your anxieties then? Write them down.
3. Think back to last month. Write those down.
4. Reach back a year, if you can recall them. Write those down too.
5. Now study the list and check each item out. **Was this one realistic? Did it make any sense? Or that one?**
6. Observe the result Maltz predicts: "Many people worry about things that never happen, and there is a splendid chance that, as you check out your list of anxieties, you will find that many — perhaps most — of your worries were unrealistic."
7. Carry the insight forward: this is evidence about your *overreaction rate*, and it applies to today's fears too.
8. Go to the mirror. Write **OVERCOME RESENTMENT**. Say: "I will try to overcome my resentment every way I can. I will try to reduce my anxieties. I will set constructive goals. I want self-respect."

**Goal Builder 6 — Getting Rid of Emptiness** (the pair exercise): write NO EMPTINESS on the mirror, say "Time is my treasure, and I will not waste it. I will set goals to give meaning to my precious days." Then take pad and pencil, write a goal, and weigh it against four tests: Is it *your* goal? Does it mean something to you? Can you achieve it? Do you have a fighting chance, at least? Keep writing until you find goals that pass.

## Key Takeaways
1. Uncertainty is not a personal insult; a goal converts it into a navigable problem.
2. Audit last year's anxieties in writing — the hit rate is the argument, not the reassurance.
3. Spend anxiety *before* the decision; after the decision, call on past successes instead.
4. Emptiness is a directional problem, not an emotional one — the cure is goals, not feelings.
5. Concentrate fully on whatever you are doing; halfhearted action is the manufacturing process for emptiness.
