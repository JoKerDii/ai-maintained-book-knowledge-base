# Chapter 13: The Quiet Place / Relaxation (Book Three: Live and Be Free Thru Psycho-Cybernetics, ch. 12–13)

## Core Idea
Relaxation is **learned**, not given, and it is the enabling condition for everything else in the method — the servo-mechanism only produces solutions when it is not jammed. Maltz's core tool is a mental retreat you build once and enter daily.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Quiet Place**
  - When to use: at least once a day, and immediately on being agitated.
  - How: **construct it in imagination** — plan, build, paint and decorate it to your own taste so you genuinely feel at home. It can be furnished simply or luxuriously: soft chairs, particular pictures on the walls, a stereo with all the world's music, even a computer that solves any problem. It can be a house on a quiet beach on an uncharted island, a picture window over the water, a cloud, deep in the earth, or a planet millions of light-years away.
  - Rules: it is **completely isolated from the outside world**; nobody else knows where it is; nobody else can go there. "This is the atmosphere where you are anything you care to be."
  - Travel time: instant. "Close your eyes and recall the last time you were in San Francisco… Did you get a picture of the Golden Gate Bridge? That's how long it will take you to get to your secret place."
  - Why it works: "When you retreat into your quiet place, your nervous system gets a rest every day." The tranquility carries over into whatever action follows.
- **Relaxation is learned.** Not a temperament, not a purchase. Maltz splits it into physical relaxation (stillness, immobility, closing the door to the senses) and **psychological relaxation** (releasing the grip of the negative picture).
- **The daily 21-day recitation** — Maltz's affirmation script, to be read every day for twenty-one days (see Ch 16 for the text).
- **Response, not event, determines your state.** "It is not outside events that are your key to being agitated or calm, but your own response to the things that happen around you."

## Key Concepts
- **Peaceful retreat**: a place for *self-renewal*, distinct from distraction or entertainment.
- **Truman's foxhole**: Harry Truman described "a foxhole in my mind where I allow nothing to bother me," and retreated into it whenever anything bothered him. Maltz cites it as proof the technique scales to high-stakes work.
- **Clearing the cobwebs**: the stated output of quiet time — you come back free for new and challenging activities, not merely calmer.
- **Conscious awareness of environment**: a listed component — relaxation involves noticing where you actually are rather than where your worry is.
- **Escaping from reality** — Maltz uses the phrase without apology. Everyone needs an occasional vacation, a change of scene, to get out of a rut.

## Mental Models
- **Marcus Aurelius on retreats.** Maltz opens with it: men seek houses in the country, seashore and mountains, "but this is altogether a mark of the most common sort of man. For it is in your power, whenever you shall choose to retire into yourself. Constantly then, give to yourself this retreat, and renew yourself." The external retreat is the amateur version.
- **The quiet place is furnished, not found.** You build it deliberately, in detail, once — then you have an asset you can enter in an instant for the rest of your life.
- **Relaxation is a skill with a training curve**, like any other. People fail at it because they have never practiced it, not because they are constitutionally tense.
- **Don't fight relaxation — join it.** Maltz's phrasing elsewhere, and the correct posture: relaxation is not another thing to achieve by effort.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trying to relax by effort** — the contradiction that defeats most people.
- **Never building the place**, then trying to improvise calm in the middle of a crisis.
- **Telling people where your quiet place is.** Maltz is emphatic and it isn't whimsy: shared, it becomes a topic; private, it stays a refuge.
- **Treating relaxation as time lost.** It is the operating condition for the creative mechanism — "the greater energies of the mind are available only when the body is still and the door to the senses is closed."
- **Waiting for the annual vacation** to discharge a year of tension.

## Worked Example
**Building and entering the Quiet Place — the full protocol:**

*One-time construction (do this once, carefully):*
1. Sit somewhere undisturbed and close your eyes.
2. Decide the location. Maltz's own suggestions span the plausible and the impossible deliberately — a small house on a quiet beach on an uncharted Pacific island dotted with palm trees, a light breeze pushing the waves ashore, a picture window taking in the whole view. Or a cloud. Or deep in the earth. Or a distant planet. The constraint is not realism; it is that *you* feel at home there.
3. Furnish it in specific detail: the chairs, the pictures on the walls, the music available, the light. Detail is what makes re-entry instant later.
4. Establish the two rules: total isolation from the outside world, and total privacy — you are the only one who knows where it is.

*Daily entry (at least once a day):*
5. Close your eyes. Go there. It takes an instant — the same instant it takes to picture Fifth Avenue or the Golden Gate Bridge.
6. Stay. Let the nervous system rest. A feeling of freedom takes over; calmness and peace follow.
7. Return, and notice the carry-over: Maltz's claim is that the tranquility "will undoubtedly carry over into any actions that follow" and that the quiet time "will literally clear out the cobwebs."

*Crisis use:* enter it the moment agitation starts, rather than after the argument. Truman's foxhole was used mid-presidency, not on weekends.

**The relaxation practice exercise** that accompanies it: begin each day by reciting the daily statement — including the lines "My thinking and my attitudes are calm and cheerful," "I respond in a calm and intelligent manner without alarm, no matter what the situation," and "If I cannot control a situation, I try always to react in a positive manner, even to negative facts" — and **read it every day for twenty-one days.**

## Key Takeaways
1. Build the quiet place once, in detail, before you need it.
2. Enter it at least once daily — this is nervous-system maintenance, not indulgence.
3. Keep it private and keep it isolated; both rules are functional.
4. Relaxation is learned. Practice it as a skill, don't wait to feel relaxed.
5. Your response, not the event, sets your state — which is the whole reason the technique can work at all.
