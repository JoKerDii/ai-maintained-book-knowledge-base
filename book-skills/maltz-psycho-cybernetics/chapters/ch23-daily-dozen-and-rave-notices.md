# Chapter 23: Your Daily Dozen — 12 Ways to a New Self-Image / Rave Notices for You (Book Four: The Magic Power of Self-Image Psychology, ch. 17–18)

## Core Idea
Book Four's summary weapon: twelve concepts that "pack the kick of an artillery cannon" — the same twelve as Book One's Faces of Success, restated as daily instruments rather than diagnostics.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Daily Dozen — 12 Ways to a New Self-Image**
  1. **Truth.** The Greeks had it: "Know thyself" — but "your 'truth' about yourself is so often *false*." Most people downgrade their abilities, value and assets, "dwelling on failures and overlooking successes… with an almost sadistic intensity." Test: is your truth about yourself real, or an alien concept destroying you from within? **Learn to see yourself as you really are in your best moments.**
  2. **Imagination.** Shelley: "The great instrument of moral good is the imagination"; "Imagination is the eye of the soul." Maltz's warning: "a neglected imagination will not lead you into the green pastures of abundant life." Instruction: visualize yourself in the roles and situations you have relished, over and over, **till your "success pictures" blot out your "failure pictures."** Make imagination "a friend to be treasured instead of a storehouse of fears."
  3. **Relaxation.** Franklin: "He that can take rest is greater than he that can take cities" — taking cities involves superior firepower; taking rest involves a deep spiritual capacity. The content of relaxation is forgiveness: forgive others, because it brings peace of mind, *and* because "when you hold a grudge against someone for years, you may be blaming him for an inconsiderate act that you, in your own imperfection, might have committed." Accept others with their faults, relax with yourself, relax with your failures — **"And forgive yourself!"**
  4. **That Winning Feeling.** "I'm not a soothsayer, I have no crystal ball, and I don't believe in palmistry, but I can foresee victories for the person who gets that winning feeling." Emerson: "Self-trust is the first secret of success." The spirit with which you tackle a project "almost predetermines the results of your efforts." Once part of your basic personality, this belief pulls you through crises and revives you after catastrophes. **"As long as you keep stoking the fires of this feeling, you are rich. You feed your automatic success mechanism, and it produces for you."**
  5. **Good habits.** Aristotle: "Men acquire a particular quality by constantly acting in a particular way." Ovid: "Habits change into character." Against the fixed-habit belief: "this is not so. You can discard bad habits and develop good ones if you're willing to work hard at change."
  6. **The Aim of Happiness.** "Don't take it away from yourself."
  7. **Unmasking.**
  8. **Compassion.**
  9. **Accepting Your Weakness.**
  10. **Living with Your Mistakes.**
  11. **Being Yourself.**
  12. **Never Retiring.**

## Key Concepts
- **Success pictures vs. failure pictures**: the competition inside the theater of the mind. You do not delete the failure pictures — you crowd them out by repetition.
- **Stoking the fires**: the winning feeling is maintained, not achieved once.
- **Rave notices**: the closing conceit — you have re-written the script, the spotlight's on you, take a bow. Maltz ends Book Four by casting the reader as the reviewed performer of their own life.
- **"You are the master of your fate."**
- **A lifetime of living / each day can be joyful.**

## Mental Models
- **Twelve instruments, not twelve rules.** "They are just words outlining concepts, but they pack the kick of an artillery cannon. With one exception: They will not destroy fortresses but will simply guide you toward the good life."
- **Your grudge may be for something you'd have done yourself.** Item 3's sharpest line, and the most practical forgiveness lever in the omnibus: before renewing a years-old grudge, ask whether the act you're condemning is one your own imperfection might have committed.
- **Self-trust as prediction.** Not that confidence guarantees the outcome, but that "the spirit with which you tackle projects… almost predetermines the results."
- **The mind's imagery keeps things alive.** Maltz's example is Kennedy, months dead as he writes: you can still see him, vivid, in the theater of your mind. He uses it as evidence for how durable and operative mental pictures are — which is also why the failure pictures persist unless deliberately displaced.

## Anti-patterns
- **Sadistic self-appraisal disguised as honesty** (item 1). Dwelling on failures and skipping successes is not accuracy; it is a systematically biased sample.
- **Neglecting imagination**: "Neglected fields will not produce prolific crops."
- **Letting imagination become a storehouse of fears** — the same faculty, uncultivated, fills with worry by default.
- **Believing habits can't change**, which Maltz names as a widespread belief and contradicts flatly.
- **Rationalizing away defeat, then resenting the world for not recognizing you.** "First, you have to recognize the good in yourself."

## Worked Example
**Running the Daily Dozen as an actual daily pass** — Maltz presents these as summary concepts; here is the compact working form implied by the surrounding practice exercises.

*Morning (with the Mirror Magic practice from Ch 16):*
- **Truth (1).** One question: what is one thing I am currently believing about myself that is a downgrade rather than an observation? Correct it to the best-moments version.
- **That Winning Feeling (4).** Recall one specific past success in sensory detail, and hold the *feeling* for a minute. Stoking the fire is a daily act, not an insight.
- **Imagination (2).** Ten minutes of The Method (Ch 14): one situation, seen from inside, handled as you intend. Repetition is what makes the success picture crowd out the failure picture.

*During the day:*
- **Relaxation (3).** The daily vacation into the quiet room. And when a grudge surfaces, run item 3's test: *is this an inconsiderate act I might have committed myself?* Then cancel it.
- **Aim of Happiness (6) / Compassion (8).** The two active postures — toward yourself first, then outward.
- **Unmasking (7) / Being Yourself (11).** Catch one moment of performance and drop it.

*On error:*
- **Accepting Your Weakness (9) / Living with Your Mistakes (10).** Accept the limit — that's the floor. Take the correction (Ch 15's negative feedback). Do not add punishment; it purchases nothing.

*Ongoing:*
- **Good habits (5).** Work on one habit at a time, deliberately, accepting that change requires hard work rather than insight.
- **Never Retiring (12).** One goal every day, however small. This is the item that makes the other eleven have somewhere to go.

Maltz's own closing framing: "You've re-written the script." The Daily Dozen is what a person runs *after* deciding the script is theirs to write.

## Key Takeaways
1. Repetition displaces failure pictures with success pictures — you crowd them out, you don't delete them.
2. Before renewing a grudge, ask whether you might have committed the same act.
3. Stoke the winning feeling daily; it is maintained, not attained.
4. Your habits, summed and consolidated, *are* you — and they are changeable with work.
5. Twelve instruments; run them as a daily pass, not a reading.
