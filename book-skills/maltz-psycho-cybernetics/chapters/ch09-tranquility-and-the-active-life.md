# Chapter 9: Tranquility and Self-Respect / The Active Life: No Retirement Clock (Book Two: The Search for Self-Respect, ch. 11–12)

## Core Idea
Tranquility is an **active** achievement — you get it by waging war on twelve named negative forces, not by withdrawing. And retirement from *life* (as distinct from a job) is a mechanism with ten identifiable components, each of which has a constructive alternative.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Your Daily Dozen for Tranquility** — twelve forces to combat, spelling TRANQUILITY:
  **T**ension · **R**esistance · **A**rrogant aggressiveness · **N**egation as a way of life · **Q**uandary · **U**nbelief · **I**nferiority · **L**ife of loneliness · **L**aissez-faire · **I**rritation · **T**repidation · **Y**esterday Incorporated.
  - When to use: daily, on a walk or sitting quietly. "Focus on these twelve negative forces and wage war on them — so that you can have peace."
  - Trade-off: this is a *combative* conception of peace. Maltz is explicit that tranquility is not passivity; passivity is what makes you helpless to forces you can't cope with, and helplessness is what ties you up in tension.
- **Tension → Tranquility protocol**: tension and tranquility cannot coexist. But stresses "are there for you to overcome, not to retain as a way of life." Look at tension as a stimulus giving you a creative opportunity to prove yourself. Move *through* the tension to the goal — then, with tension shrugged aside, relax. Relaxation is the reward on the far side of goal-directed action, not a substitute for it.
- **The Retirement Mechanism vs. the Active Life** — ten paired components. The retirement clock "ticks away emptiness and despair"; each item has an alternative that moves you toward self-respect:

| Retirement component | Constructive alternative |
|---|---|
| Retreat | **Return** |
| Eviction | **Conviction** |
| Tendency to withdraw | **Courage to Act** |
| (loss of imagination) | **Imaginative Power** |
| (self-negation) | **Reaffirmation** |
| (abdication) | **Responsibility** |
| Mañana | **Today** |
| Ennui | **Emancipation** |
| No direction | **Goal Orientation** |
| Traitor | **Friend** |

## Key Concepts
- **Tranquility as an active concept**: the chapter's own heading, and the correction to every spa-brochure reading of the word.
- **Yesterday Incorporated**: the sister firm to Mañana Incorporated — living in the past rather than the future, equally fatal to today.
- **Laissez-faire**: passivity toward your own life dressed up as tolerance.
- **Retreat**: the fundamental concept of retirement — you presuppose a dangerous, threatening world and withdraw from it as soon as you can.
- **Traitor → Friend**: the final pairing, and the sharpest. Retiring from life makes you a traitor to yourself.

## Mental Models
- **Resistance can be positive — check which side you're sabotaging.** Maltz's extended analogy: the French Resistance dynamiting a munitions train bound for the front was a good thing. But "when you sabotage your worthwhile daily goals, placing roadblocks all around you in resistance of your legitimate satisfactions, that is not so good." Same skill, wrong target.
- **Change creates tension because people must readjust to newness.** In a fast-moving world this is unavoidable — which is why the *unnecessary* tension (worrying about things that never happen, obsessive anxiety) is the part worth attacking.
- **Retirement is voluntary confinement.** "No police come in the middle of the night and enforce this indignity upon you; you do it to yourself."
- **Have a goal every day, no matter how small** — this is the single anti-retirement intervention that scales to any age or circumstance.

## Anti-patterns
- **Passive tranquility**: waiting for calm to descend. Passivity "renders us helpless to forces we cannot cope with, so that we can only tie ourselves up with tension in our helplessness."
- **Retaining tension as identity** — the "tension habit," acquired by people readjusting to constant change.
- **Self-resistance**: blocking your own legitimate satisfactions because you have decided you are undeserving. "You must appreciate that you have rights, that you are deserving."
- **The generation gap as an excuse for withdrawal** — Maltz treats it as a communication failure, not a reason to retire from the young.
- **Killing time**: the specific phrase Goal Builder 12 targets.

## Worked Example
**The Tension drill** (force #1), exactly as Maltz sequences it:

1. Wake up in the morning and **think of something constructive to brighten your day.** Not a general resolution — a specific item.
2. Get off your back. Physically start.
3. Fight off the worries and doubts as they arrive; expect them rather than being surprised.
4. Get out into the world and live the best way you can.
5. **"After a good go at your goals, you are ready for your trip to the good land — tranquility."**

Note the ordering: the relaxation is scheduled *after* the effort, and it is earned by it. Maltz's diagnosis of chronic tension is that people try to relax *instead of* pursuing goals, which fails, because unpursued goals are themselves a tension source.

**Goal Builder 11 — Tranquility**, the sensory companion exercise: close your eyes and use your *ears*. Listen — in imagination or reality — for the sounds that bring you tranquility. This is personal: some people love the sound of rain falling on city streets, others the roar of the ocean pounding the shore, many respond to music (for which no imagination is required — play the record). Soothe yourself with the sounds you love. Then open your eyes, go to the mirror, write **TRANQUILITY**, and say: *"I will be tranquil today. No matter how discordant the world and its problems, I will give myself tranquility."*

**Goal Builder 12 — No Retirement Clock**: listen to a clock ticking (real or imagined). Time is ticking away, and it comprises your life. Write **CLOCK** on the mirror. Say: *"I must not use this valuable time to retire from life. I must not resort to 'killing' time. My life is for translating constructive thinking and constructive imaging into constructive action."*

## Key Takeaways
1. Tranquility is won by combat against twelve named forces, not by withdrawal.
2. Schedule relaxation *after* a genuine go at your goals — that ordering is the mechanism.
3. Distinguish unavoidable tension (change, real problems) from the manufactured kind (worry about things that never happen); attack only the second.
4. Retiring from a job is optional; retiring from life is always self-inflicted.
5. A goal every day, however small, is the complete anti-retirement protocol.
