# Chapter 14: The Psychology of Goals / Goals / Synthetic Experience / Mental Picturing / Think Sheets / Imagination / The Method (Book Three: Live and Be Free Thru Psycho-Cybernetics, ch. 14–20)

## Core Idea
The operational heart of the book. Because **the human nervous system cannot distinguish between a real experience and one vividly imagined**, you can manufacture the experience of success before you have any — and that synthetic experience reprograms the self-image.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Method — mental rehearsal** (Maltz's central technique)
  - **Ten minutes each day**, alone, without distractions, eyes closed.
  - **Vivid imagining**: make the picture as close to an actual experience as possible — lifelike, complete in every detail, built from all five senses. Environmental detail matters: "if the experience you create is intense enough, your nervous system will accept it, the same as a real one."
  - **Imagine yourself successful**: your past actions are irrelevant during these ten minutes. It does not matter how you behaved before. Picture yourself *already* as you would like to be — and ask how you feel.
  - **Be a participant, not an onlooker.** The critical instruction: use imagination "not as an onlooker (objectively), but as a participant (subjectively). We actually must be there." A goal you *think of* stays a possibility; a goal you *think from* is overpoweringly real.
  - **"Begin by knowing you have arrived."** Assume the feeling of the goal already accomplished.
  - **21 consecutive days.** "After you have practiced this for twenty-one days, you will find yourself behaving differently, without conscious thought or effort."
  - Three essentials Maltz lists: (1) a strong desire for the goal; (2) the physical state of immobility, induced at will — "the greater energies of the mind are available only when the body is still and the door to the senses is closed"; (3) the *felt* experience of already having achieved it, daily, for 21 days.
- **Synthetic experience / affirmation.** "Nothing succeeds like success" — we learn to function successfully by experiencing success. But how does someone with no successful experience draw on it? Answer: manufacture it. Successful affirming requires three things: **Desire · Information · Repetition.**
- **The nine rules for wording an affirmation** — reproduced in the Worked Example below.
- **Reconditioning windows**: any time you can be completely relaxed and aware only of your thoughts — immediately on waking (still unaware of anything but your thoughts), just before sleep, after lunch, or any solitary moment.
- **The controlled-attention drill**: just before falling asleep, hold attention on the day's activities *in reverse order* — from getting into bed back to getting out of it. "Just as specific exercises help develop specific muscles, this will help you develop the muscles of your attention."
- **The Principle of Least Action**: minimum time × minimum energy. The future becomes the present when you assume the feeling of possession.
- **You supply the goal; the mechanism supplies the how.** "Once you give it a definite objective to achieve, you can depend upon its automatic guidance system to take you to that objective much better than you ever could by conscious thought."

## Key Concepts
- **Servo-mechanism**: the automatic goal-striving machine. It needs a clearly defined objective and nothing else from you.
- **Descriptive Statement / Statement of Affirmation**: the written pair — first the detailed why, then the short usable line.
- **Think Sheets**: written self-interrogation exercises for surfacing goals and assumptions.
- **The law works impersonally** — "just as the law of gravity or electricity." No devotional or religious commitment required.
- **Goals should be consistent, compatible, and constructive.**
- **Conscious mind not responsible for results**: you decide, gather data, evaluate, choose — then let the outcome take its course.

## Mental Models
- **"Act the way you want to be (in your imagination), and you'll be the way you act."** Maltz's compressed statement of the whole method, and the basis of the "act as if" drill.
- **Think *from* the goal, not *about* it.** The single distinction that separates this from daydreaming.
- **A miracle is only the name given by those who don't know how imagination works.** "Determined imagination, thinking from the end result, is the beginning of all miracles."
- **Attention is a muscle**: directed from within when you deliberately choose what to be preoccupied with; captured from outside when you don't. "You will no longer be dominated by outside circumstances and conditions."

## Anti-patterns
- **Watching yourself from outside** in the rehearsal — the most common execution error; it produces a nice picture and no change.
- **Setting time limits in an affirmation.** Maltz's rule: "Time limits are deadly; they create tension."
- **Comparative or superlative wording** — "better than," "more than," or absolutes and perfection. Both create the tension that jams the mechanism.
- **Trying to specify the *how*.** Your job is the objective; the mechanism's job is the route. Conscious micromanagement is interference.
- **Writing goals for how they'd sound to someone else** — the first checklist question exists to catch this.

## Worked Example
**Writing an affirmation, to Maltz's nine rules:**
1. Be strictly personal — use *I, me, my.*
2. Be absolutely positive — move *toward* what you want; avoid *not* and *don't.*
3. Use the present tense.
4. Do not be progressive — avoid *I am going to.*
5. Do not be comparative — never compare with the past or with others; no *more than* or *better than.*
6. Avoid *able to* and *can.*
7. Keep it balanced — no opposites or extremes.
8. Be realistic — no absolutes or perfection. **Always write for yourself, not for others.**
9. Do not set time limits.

**The Descriptive Statement checklist** (written before the affirmation):
- Do I *really* want this — or would it just sound good if someone read it?
- Is the goal compatible with my other goals?
- Is it positive — what I want, not what I want rid of?
- Is it expressed in total detail?
- Is it realistic — possible for *some* human being, not just "realistic for me today"?
- **Is my goal high enough? Reach out! Set the goal limit high.** And: nothing should enter my mind at this point as to *how* I will achieve it.

**Running the daily ten minutes:** sit or lie down somewhere restful. Become physically still. Close your eyes. Choose one situation. Build it in full sensory detail — the room, the light, the sounds, what you are wearing, who is there. Place yourself *inside* it as participant. See yourself handling it the way you intend to handle it. Then shift attention to the **feeling** of already being that person, and hold it. Ten minutes. Mark the day off. Repeat for twenty-one consecutive days, then judge.

## Key Takeaways
1. Ten minutes a day, twenty-one consecutive days, in vivid multi-sensory detail — this is the core practice of the entire book.
2. Be inside the scene as participant; thinking *from* the goal is what distinguishes rehearsal from daydreaming.
3. Supply the objective only. Never specify the route — that is the mechanism's job.
4. Write affirmations present-tense, personal, positive, non-comparative, and with no deadline.
5. Past performance is irrelevant during the practice period; deliberately ignore it.
