# Chapter 4: On To Success! (Book One: The Conquest of Frustration, ch. 6, with Barker commentary)

## Core Idea
Maltz redefines success as **a growing self-image**, not an accumulation of results — "you cannot be a success unless you are a success to yourself" — and gives the S-U-C-C-E-S-S acronym as the checklist for building it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **S-U-C-C-E-S-S — How to Build a Better Self-Image** (seven ingredients, verbatim from the text):
  - **S — Sense of direction.** Have a goal; you are then going somewhere. Goals must be realistic and within your limitations — and within your limitations, opportunities become limitless.
  - **U — Understanding.** A mistake doesn't make you a failure. You cannot communicate with others until you communicate with yourself.
  - **C — Courage.** To take chances; to start toward another goal after a failure; to think creatively and then *act* creatively. Courage reaches its greatest potential when shared with someone less fortunate.
  - **C — Compassion.** First for yourself, since you are only human; then for others, which is your capacity to forgive.
  - **E — Esteem.** Appreciate your own worth. "If you don't have self-respect, no one will give it to you."
  - **S — Self-acceptance.** Don't imitate. Imitating someone else means "playing second fiddle to someone else's image."
  - **S — Self-confidence.** Built from taking calculated risks and recalling past successes. Confidence builds confidence; it can become a habit like brushing your teeth. Confidence is not conceit — the conceited man is hiding inferiority.
- **The Twelve Faces of Success** — the deliberate inverse of Ch 1's Twelve Faces of Frustration: Truth · Imagination · Relaxation · The Aim of Happiness · Good Habits · Accept Your Weaknesses · Compassion · Unmasking · Live Through Your Mistakes · Never Retire · Consider Yourself a Winner · Accept Yourself for What You Are.
- **The Four Principles of Relaxation** (inside Face 3, and Maltz's most portable technique): **(a) Forgive others. (b) Forgive yourself. (c) See yourself at your best. (d) Keep up with yourself.**
- **The four operating conditions for the servo-mechanism**: (a) one thing at a time, one thought at a time, one goal at a time; (b) live in the present, not the past; (c) use anxiety creatively to reach the goal; (d) if a situation defies solution, **sleep on it, not with it.**
- **The Success Cycle**: success leads to success leads to more success — the constructive counterpart of the failure cycle. Once running, it reactivates the success-mechanism on its own.

## Key Concepts
- **A success to yourself**: the only success that counts. "You will not be a success if you make a million dollars but leave your self-image impoverished."
- **Follow-through**: patience, resourcefulness, ingenuity, aggression channeled at the goal. The successful person changes tactics rather than giving up.
- **All habits are forms of self-hypnosis** — which is why a good habit costs the same to run as a bad one.
- **Keep up with yourself**: the comparison class is your own past, never someone else's present.

## Mental Models
- **The house-and-lawn analogy.** You mow the lawn, paint the house, trim the shrubbery to preserve property value — and then let weeds grow all over the self-image. "You will not be a success if you mow your grass but neglect the building of your self-image."
- **The money-player.** Think like a winner who has lost before: "just because you're a loser once doesn't mean you're going to lose forever."
- **Crisis → opportunity, not opportunity → crisis.** The direction of that arrow is set by whether you accept your own frailty.
- **Practicing to be ninety.** An eighty-two-year-old took Maltz's Monterey workshop; asked why, he said: "I'm eighty-two, and I'm practicing to be ninety."

## Anti-patterns
- **Failing one day and calling it failure.** "If you set a goal and fail one day, this is no failure at all." Reset the goal for the next day, and the day after.
- **Imitation as a success strategy**: the person who copies a politician or movie star becomes "an assembly-line product, not a success."
- **Seeking excuses for errors** — every excuse sidetracks you from a goal. Admit the error and rise above it.
- **Emotional sunglasses**: wearing the mask even at the pillow, hiding from yourself while trying to fall asleep.
- **Retiring from life** at any age — the eight free hours before bed are precisely where senior years are made productive.

## Worked Example
Maltz works the S-U-C-C-E-S-S list not as a poster but as a repair sequence, and Face 9, *Live Through Your Mistakes*, shows the reasoning:

An athlete knows he cannot win every day. Maltz's argument is that you should hold yourself to the same standard: you will not reach all your goals, and **reaching most of them makes you a champion in the art of living**. A blunder therefore has exactly one legitimate function — to stimulate you to rise above it.

The illegitimate responses he names precisely: telling yourself you are unlucky because you failed at one undertaking; concluding that people are inconsiderate and cruel; evading people; evading your commitment to be a full human being. Each of those, he says, deposits you "in a pit of despair and hatred" — and note that all four are *conclusions about the world* drawn from one data point about yourself.

The corrective is Face 11 (Consider Yourself a Winner): call on the confidence of past successes deliberately. Maltz describes the alternative physically — considering yourself a loser produces a feeling "as if the air from your lungs had suddenly disappeared," which then blocks positive performance, producing weariness, ennui, boredom. The failure loop is self-sealing; the Success Cycle is the same loop with the sign flipped.

## Key Takeaways
1. Define success as a growing self-image; audit it with S-U-C-C-E-S-S.
2. Run the Four Principles of Relaxation daily: forgive others, forgive yourself, see yourself at your best, keep up with yourself.
3. One thing, one thought, one goal at a time — and sleep *on* problems, not with them.
4. A single day's failure is not failure; re-set the goal tomorrow, and the day after.
5. Confidence is a habit built from deliberately recalled past successes, not a temperament.
