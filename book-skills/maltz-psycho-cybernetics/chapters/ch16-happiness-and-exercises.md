# Chapter 16: Happiness / Happiness Concept / Exercises and Techniques (Book Three: Live and Be Free Thru Psycho-Cybernetics, ch. 25–27)

## Core Idea
Happiness is a **habit of thinking that can be methodically cultivated** — not a reaction to circumstances. Book Three ends by handing over the concrete daily drills: the 21-day recitation, Mirror Magic, the self-image builders, and "Just For Today."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Habit of Happiness**
  - Working definition (Maltz quotes Dr. John A. Schindler): happiness is "a state of mind in which our thinking is pleasant a good share of the time." Maltz calls it deceptively simple and refuses to improve on it.
  - Why it matters mechanically: when contented you are in better health, more efficient, and enjoy the work more; the brain works with greater competency; memory improves in a relaxed mind; the five senses sharpen on agreeable thoughts.
  - **Happiness is a by-product of a goal.** "No one is really ever alive unless he is striving for a series of goals."
  - **Happiness must be experienced** — and it does not lie in the future. It is in the present or nowhere.
- **The manipulation test**: "When you react automatically to outside circumstances that tell you to be angry or to get upset, you are being manipulated into behavior that you might normally wish to avoid. You are letting outer conditions dictate how you act and feel. **If you learn the habit of happiness, you are the master instead of the slave.**"
- **Perpetual happiness is nonsense.** Maltz says so directly, and it is what keeps the framework honest: "Nothing is ever all black or all white. Our lives are filled with gray days, and it is entirely our own decision as to which direction our thoughts will take."
- **Mirror Magic Practice** (three variants, six days each):
  1. *Morning breathing + mirror.* Do breathing exercises for a few minutes on waking — taking in oxygen "not only to satisfy your physical lungs but the lungs for your soul." Then go to the mirror and ask: **"Am I a friend to myself?"** You must be a friend to yourself before you can be a friend to others.
  2. *Garbage disposal.* Elect to be the one who empties the wastebaskets for six days. As you throw out the household's useless accumulation, discard the emotional and mental garbage you have let accumulate. Both home and self start clean.
  3. *Closing the door.* For six days, as you leave home and close the door, see it as symbolically closing the door on the past — on the moment you made a mistake or failed — resolving that the past shall not intrude into the new day.
- **The traffic-light drill.** For six days, when you hit a red light driving or walking, treat it as your negative feelings and fear of failure holding you back. Realize no circumstance holds you back indefinitely. Expect the green. When it comes, move forward as if toward a useful goal, "with the confidence of past successes guiding you — giving you that winning feeling of accomplishment."

## Key Concepts
- **Recognizing the real self from the false self**: the "Happiness Concept" chapter's task.
- **Maturity is a matter of attitudes**, not age.
- **Self-image consistent with habits**: habits and self-image co-maintain each other, which is why habit change and image change must happen together.
- **"The only cure is the curing of unhappiness"** — you do not add happiness on top; you remove the unhappiness.
- **Is financial success a deterrent to happiness?** Maltz's answer is no in itself — but it is no substitute, and pursuing it at the cost of the self-image reliably produces the tense, unrelaxed man of Ch 18.

## Mental Models
- **Master or slave.** Every automatic reaction to a provocation is a small transfer of control. The habit of happiness is the reclaiming of it.
- **"Do the thing, and you will have the power."** (Emerson, quoted by Maltz.) And the companion line he repeats everywhere: **"Act the way you want to be (in your imagination), and you'll be the way you act."**
- **Gray days are the normal case.** A framework that only works on good days is not a framework. The choice of direction operates precisely on the gray ones.
- **Six-day drills.** Note the consistent design: short, physical, attached to an existing daily event (waking, taking out the trash, leaving the house, a red light). Maltz never asks for a new slot in the schedule — he attaches to one that already exists.

## Anti-patterns
- **Waiting for conditions to be perfect** before permitting yourself a pleasant frame of mind — "you are destined to be gloomy, because that condition will probably never exist."
- **Expecting happiness to arrive** by itself or be delivered by someone. "You may wait forever."
- **Reacting automatically to provocation** — the manipulation failure.
- **Pursuing perpetual happiness**, which guarantees the disappointment that ends the practice.
- **Doing the drills without marking the days.** Maltz's instruction is explicit: "Mark off 21 consecutive days after reciting each day."

## Worked Example
**The daily recitation — Maltz's 21-day script.** Read every day for twenty-one days, marking off each day:

> This is the beginning of a new day. I have been given this day to use as I will. What I do today is important, because I'm exchanging a new day of my life for it. When tomorrow comes, this day will be gone forever, leaving in its place whatever I have traded for it. I pledge to myself that it shall be for gain, good, and success, in order that I shall not regret the price I paid for this day.
>
> My thinking and my attitudes are calm and cheerful. I act and feel friendly toward other people. I am tolerant of other people, their shortcomings and mistakes, and I view their actions with the most favorable understanding possible. I act as though the attainment of my goals is certain to happen. I am the kind of individual I aspire to be, and everything I do and the way I feel expresses this individuality. I will not allow my judgment or attitude to be affected by negativism or pessimism. I try to smile as often as possible, at least several times a day. I respond in a calm and intelligent manner, without alarm, no matter what the situation. If I cannot control a situation, I try always to react in a positive manner, even to negative facts.

Note how the wording obeys the affirmation rules from Ch 14: present tense, first person, positive, non-comparative, no deadline, no absolutes. Maltz's stated result: "a marked decrease in worry and sense of guilt and hostility, and an increase in self-confidence."

**"Just For Today"** — the companion set of one-day commitments, deliberately scoped to twelve hours so they are always achievable:
- *Just for today* I will live through the next twelve hours and not tackle all my life's problems at once.
- *Just for today* — in one thing I know I am equal with others: **TIME.** All of us draw the same salary in seconds, minutes, and hours.
- *Just for today* I refuse to spend time worrying about what might happen. It usually doesn't. **I am going to spend my time making things happen.**

## Key Takeaways
1. Happiness is a cultivable thinking habit and a by-product of goals — never a target you can hit directly.
2. Reacting automatically to provocation hands over control; the happiness habit takes it back.
3. Attach drills to events that already happen daily — waking, the trash, the front door, a red light.
4. Recite the daily statement for 21 consecutive days and mark them off.
5. Expect gray days; the practice is for those, not for the good ones.
