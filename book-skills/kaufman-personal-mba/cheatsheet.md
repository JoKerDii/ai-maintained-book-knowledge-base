# Cheatsheet — The Personal MBA decision rules

One page of Kaufman's judgment: when to do what, and why.

## The master diagnostic — Five Parts of Every Business
A struggling business is failing at one of these. Diagnose in order:
1. **Value Creation** — does it make something people want? (If no market wants it, nothing else matters — Iron Law.)
2. **Marketing** — does the right audience pay Attention?
3. **Sales** — do they trust you enough to buy at a price you can support?
4. **Value Delivery** — do they get what they expected, repeatably?
5. **Finance** — is there enough profit to keep going?
> If you can't clearly describe all five for an idea, you don't have a business yet.

## Decision rules
| When… | Do… | Because… |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluating any idea | Score the market on the **Ten Ways** first | The Iron Law: market beats offer every time |
| You "feel sure" about an idea | List **CIAs** and **Shadow Test** them | Conviction isn't evidence; a preorder is |
| Gathering feedback | Ignore friends/family; seek **paying strangers** | Only money is honest feedback |
| Setting a price | Compute 4 methods, **price on Value Comparison** | Value-to-buyer is almost always the highest support |
| A price feels "too high to charge" | Raise it and **support** it | All prices are arbitrary (Pricing Uncertainty Principle) |
| Prospect hesitates | Add **Risk Reversal** + a **Damaging Admission** | Shifting risk and admitting flaws both build Trust |
| Want more revenue | Pull one of the **Four Methods**; start with **Reactivation** | Lapsed customers are the cheapest to sell |
| Deciding marketing spend | Cap it at **AAC** (marketing slice of LTV) | Spend more and each customer loses money |
| Improving anything | Run **WIGWAM**, maximize **Iteration Velocity** | More at-bats beats one big bet |
| A system underperforms | Find the **one Constraint**, fix only that | Optimizing non-constraints yields nothing |
| Optimizing | Attack the **Critical Few (20%)**; stop at the **85% Solution** | Diminishing Returns make the last 15% costly |
| Tempted to act when unsure | Consider **Cessation** (do nothing) | Absence Blindness makes "do nothing" feel wrong when it's right |
| Designing a new complex system | Start simple, evolve it | **Gall's Law**: complex-from-scratch never works |
| Behavior won't change | Change the **Environment**, not the willpower | Guiding Structure beats discipline |
| Delegating | Give **Commander's Intent**, not step-by-step | People adapt to Change only if they know *why* |

## Numbers & thresholds Kaufman commits to
- **Team size**: 3–8 people (Communication Overhead grows fast past that).
- **Dunbar's number**: ~150 — beyond it, minds treat people as statistics (Cognitive Scope Limitation).
- **Decide at 40–70%** of available information; less is reckless, more is paralysis.
- **Loss Aversion ≈ 2×**: losses feel about twice as strong as equal gains.
- **Pareto**: ~20% of inputs → ~80% of outputs (often more extreme).
- **85% Solution**: stop optimizing where added effort exceeds returns.
- **Personal R&D**: reinvest ~5% of monthly income in your own learning.
- **Attention span**: ~30 min before even motivated minds need Novelty.
- **MITs**: 2–3 per day, chosen before the day starts.

## Quality & value formulas
- **Quality = Performance − Expectations** (manage both; never over-promise).
- **Perceived Value = better End Result + less customer effort**.
- **Profit Margin** = (revenue − cost) ÷ cost; **Value Capture** too high → customers flee.
- **Sufficiency** = the profit point where continuing is worth it ("ramen profitable").

## Tells & smells (recognize the trap fast)
- Measuring an easy proxy (lines of code, hours logged) → you'll reward the wrong behavior.
- A correlation cited as a reason to act → demand the full cause-effect chain first.
- "We'll just add more automation" → recall the **Paradox of Automation**: you need better humans, not fewer.
- Optimizing a metric while the system gets fragile → you're trading **Resilience** for Throughput.
- Defending a choice by past investment → that's a **Sunk Cost**; ignore it.
- Blaming a person's character for a failure → likely **Attribution Error**; check circumstances.
- Tightly coupled system "running fine" → **Normal Accidents**: failure is *when*, not *if*. Build a Fail-safe now.
