# Patterns — Techniques & Procedures from The Personal MBA

Concrete, repeatable methods you can run. Each: when to use, how, trade-offs.

## Test an Idea Before Building It (Shadow Testing + MEVO)
**When to use**: You have a business idea and want evidence of real demand before investing.
**How**:
1. List your **Critically Important Assumptions (CIAs)** — the facts that must be true for it to work.
2. Build a **Prototype** / **Minimum Economically Viable Offer (MEVO)**: the smallest thing that can produce an actual sale (landing page, one-pager, mockup).
3. **Shadow Test**: transparently offer it for sale (or take preorders) before it fully exists.
4. Treat a **preorder/payment** as the only feedback that counts; refund if you can't deliver.
**Trade-offs**: Must stay transparent (don't take money you can't honor). Friends/family feedback is noise — only paying strangers validate.

## Evaluate a Market Before Entering (Ten Ways + Iron Law)
**When to use**: Choosing among business or product ideas.
**How**: Score the market 0–10 on each: Urgency, Market Size, Pricing Potential, Cost of Customer Acquisition, Cost of Value Delivery, Uniqueness of Offer, Speed to Market, Up-Front Investment, Upsell Potential, Evergreen Potential. Sum and compare. Remember the **Iron Law of the Market**: a great offer to a market that doesn't want it still fails.
**Trade-offs**: Scores are estimates, not facts — use to compare options, not to predict revenue. A low total is a strong stop signal; a high total is not a guarantee.

## Run an Improvement Loop (The Iteration Cycle — WIGWAM)
**When to use**: Improving any offer, process, or skill.
**How**: **W**atch what's happening → **I**deate options → **G**uess which will help most → **W**hich? (choose) → **A**ct → **M**easure → repeat. Optimize for **Iteration Velocity** (cycles per unit of time/money).
**Trade-offs**: Favors many small bets over one big bet; needs honest measurement or it degenerates into noise.

## Support a Price (Four Pricing Methods)
**When to use**: Setting or defending a price.
**How**: Compute all four as inputs — **Replacement Cost** (what to rebuild it), **Market Comparison** (what similar things sell for), **Discounted Cash Flow/NPV** (value of future cash it produces), **Value Comparison** (worth to *this* buyer). Use the first three as a floor/baseline; **price on Value Comparison** because it's almost always highest.
**Trade-offs**: Value pricing requires understanding the buyer's Reasons Why; without that you fall back to commodity (market) pricing.

## Close More Sales (Trust + Risk Reversal + Damaging Admission)
**When to use**: Prospects hesitate at the buying moment.
**How**: Dissolve the five **Barriers to Purchase** (too expensive, won't work, won't work for me, can wait, too hard). Build **Trust** via **Education-Based Selling**; volunteer a real drawback (**Damaging Admission**) to raise credibility; apply **Risk Reversal** (strong guarantee) so the buyer, not the seller, is protected.
**Trade-offs**: Risk Reversal can attract abusers; price in the refund/return rate. Works only when you can actually deliver.

## Grow Revenue (Four Methods)
**When to use**: You want more money from an existing business.
**How**: There are only four levers — (1) increase the **number of customers**, (2) increase **average transaction size**, (3) increase **transaction frequency** per customer, (4) **raise prices**. Pick the lever with least Friction first; **Reactivation** of lapsed customers is usually cheapest.
**Trade-offs**: Discounting to add customers can shrink margin; raising prices tests **Pricing Power** and may lose price-sensitive buyers.

## Size Customer Acquisition Spend (Lifetime Value vs AAC)
**When to use**: Deciding how much to spend on marketing/acquisition.
**How**: Estimate **Lifetime Value** (total profit per customer over the relationship). Your **Allowable Acquisition Cost (AAC)** is the marketing share of that — spend up to AAC to win a customer and still profit.
**Trade-offs**: Overestimating LTV (ignoring churn) leads to overspending; revisit as real retention data arrives.

## Find What You Actually Want (Five-Fold Why / Five-Fold How)
**When to use**: Goal-setting, or stuck on motivation.
**How**: Ask "why do I want this?" ~5 times to reach the core desire (a State of Being). Then ask "how can I get this?" ~5 times to descend to a concrete **Next Action** you can take now.
**Trade-offs**: Surfacing a deeper want can reveal your stated goal is the wrong one — useful but unsettling.

## Protect Focus (Monoidealism + Kill the Switching Penalty)
**When to use**: Important cognitive work.
**How**: Eliminate **Conflict** and interruptions; do one thing (**Monoidealism**). Batch similar tasks to avoid the **Cognitive Switching Penalty**. Pre-commit your **2–3 MITs** before the day starts; spend **Willpower** on Environment design, not moment-to-moment resistance.
**Trade-offs**: Requires saying no and protecting blocks of time; multitasking *feels* productive but isn't.

## Change Behavior by Changing Structure (Guiding Structure + Habits)
**When to use**: You want a behavior to stick (yours or a team's).
**How**: Don't rely on willpower — change the **Environment** so the desired action is the path of least resistance. Install **Habits** one at a time with explicit triggers.
**Trade-offs**: Slower than forcing it, but durable; one habit at a time respects Willpower Depletion.

## Delegate With Autonomy (Commander's Intent + Reason Why)
**When to use**: Assigning work to others.
**How**: State the **Reason Why** and **Commander's Intent** (the purpose and end-state) so people respond intelligently when reality shifts. Treat them with the **Golden Trifecta** (appreciation, courtesy, respect). Keep teams to **3–8** to limit **Communication Overhead**.
**Trade-offs**: Requires trusting judgment over compliance; pays off only with capable people.

## Find and Fix the Bottleneck (Theory of Constraints)
**When to use**: A system underperforms and you want max leverage.
**How**: Identify the single **Constraint** limiting Throughput. Alleviate or eliminate it; the next constraint "gets a promotion." Re-measure and repeat.
**Trade-offs**: Optimizing anything *other* than the constraint produces no system-level gain — and can add cost.

## Choose the Right Metric (KPI + Avoid Correlation/Causation Traps)
**When to use**: Measuring and improving a system.
**How**: Pick a few **KPIs** on the system's critical parts; supply **Context** (Norms, Ratios). Before acting on a pattern, demand a complete repeatable cause-effect chain — **correlation ≠ causation**. Use **Sampling** and **Segmentation** to keep it cheap and revealing.
**Trade-offs**: Bad inputs = **Garbage In, Garbage Out**; vanity metrics (e.g. lines of code) reward the wrong behavior.

## Improve a System Safely (Critical Few + SOP + Fail-safe)
**When to use**: Optimizing an existing, working system.
**How**: Apply the **Critical Few** (find the ~20% of inputs driving ~80% of results) and optimize those. Reduce **Friction**. Codify recurring work as **SOPs/Checklists**. Build **Fail-safes** before they're needed; **Stress Test** in "demon mode." Stop at **Diminishing Returns** (the "85% Solution"). Sometimes the right move is **Cessation** — stop doing something.
**Trade-offs**: Over-optimizing Throughput sacrifices **Resilience**; remember the **Paradox of Automation** — efficient automation makes human operators *more* critical, not less.
