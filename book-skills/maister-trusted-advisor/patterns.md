# Patterns & Techniques — The Trusted Advisor

## The Trust Equation Diagnostic
**When to use**: any relationship that feels stuck, transactional, or fragile.
**How**: Score Credibility, Reliability, Intimacy, and Self-Orientation (1–10). Identify the limiting term. Because Self-Orientation is the divisor, check it first — it caps everything.
**Trade-offs**: quick and clarifying, but scores are subjective; use it to direct attention, not to grade people.

## Give to Get (invest first)
**When to use**: early in a relationship, before trust exists.
**How**: offer something of value with no immediate return expected — an insight, an introduction, candid help. Small trust-building gestures (like a professional who tells you when you *don't* need their service) compound.
**Trade-offs**: requires patience; pays back as intimacy and reliability, not immediate revenue.

## Move Through the Five Stages in Order
**When to use**: any advisory conversation or engagement.
**How**: Engage → Listen → Frame → Envision → Commit. Resist jumping from problem to solution; the value (and the trust) is created in Listen, Frame, and Envision.
**Trade-offs**: slower up front; prevents the far costlier failure of solving the wrong problem or advising someone not ready to act.

## Earn the Right to Advise (Listen First)
**When to use**: whenever you feel the urge to answer immediately.
**How**: listen actively and incisively until the client feels *understood*; only then offer a view. Understanding must precede — and visibly precede — advice.
**Trade-offs**: feels like withholding expertise; actually makes the expertise land.

## Reframe, Don't Blame
**When to use**: when a client's problem invites judgment ("they mismanaged this").
**How**: frame the issue around the real question and the future, stripped of blame and speculative motive. Blame poisons framing and advising.
**Trade-offs**: requires ego discipline; produces a definition the client can own.

## Joint Envisioning
**When to use**: after listening and framing, before pushing to action.
**How**: with the client, describe the end-state in concrete, vivid terms ("imagine it's a year from now and this is solved — what does it look like?"). Sometimes envisioning means expanding the dream; sometimes managing expectations down.
**Trade-offs**: easy to skip under time pressure; it is where much of the added value lives.

## The Lieutenant Columbo Move
**When to use**: when status, polish, or your own expertise is creating distance.
**How**: adopt disarming humility — "I'm probably missing something, help me understand…"; ask naive, curious questions. Lowers self-orientation and makes it safe for the client to open up.
**Trade-offs**: can look unpolished; that's the point — it signals you care more about getting it right than looking smart.

## Name It and Claim It
**When to use**: when there's an obvious but unspoken tension, risk, or emotion in the room.
**How**: say it plainly and kindly ("I sense you're not fully sold on this — can we talk about that?"). Builds intimacy fast.
**Trade-offs**: momentary discomfort; the alternative is a relationship that stays shallow.

## Take a Point of View — Then Hold It Loosely
**When to use**: when a client wants your genuine recommendation.
**How**: commit to a clear position (shows confidence and care) while remaining visibly willing to change it on better information (shows low self-orientation).
**Trade-offs**: fence-sitting erodes credibility; rigidity spikes self-orientation — the skill is doing both.

## Treat Every Client as a Unique Individual
**When to use**: always; especially when tempted to generalize "what clients want."
**How**: resist the sentence "What clients want is…"; tailor to this person's specific hopes, fears, and style (see also client types, Ch 16).
**Trade-offs**: less scalable; it is the essence of relationship (Ch 5).

## Reliability Through Small Promises
**When to use**: building trust during an assignment (Ch 19).
**How**: make and keep many small, explicit commitments (call by Friday, send the draft Tuesday). Each kept promise is a reliability deposit; consistency, not grand gestures, builds it.
**Trade-offs**: requires discipline in follow-through; cheap to do, expensive to break.
