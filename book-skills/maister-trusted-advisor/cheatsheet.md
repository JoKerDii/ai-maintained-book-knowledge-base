# Cheatsheet — The Trusted Advisor

## The one equation to keep beside you
**Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-Orientation**
→ To raise trust fastest, **lower the denominator**. Self-orientation is the master lever.

## Which trust term is failing? (diagnose by the symptom)
| Symptom the client shows | Weak term | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| "I'm not sure they know their stuff / are being straight with me" | Credibility | Show expertise + tell the truth, incl. bad news |
| "They don't do what they say / I have to chase them" | Reliability | Make & keep small explicit promises |
| "I can't really talk to them about what matters" | Intimacy | Name the hard thing; earn safety |
| "It's all about them / their sale / their ego" | Self-Orientation (÷) | Refocus on the client; ask, don't pitch |

## Decision rules
- **When you feel the urge to give the answer → don't yet.** Listen until the client feels understood; advice taken > advice given.
- **When tempted to skip a stage → don't skip Listen or Envision.** They're where value and trust are created.
- **When a problem invites blame → reframe around the real question and the future.** Blame kills framing.
- **When status/polish creates distance → go Columbo.** Humble, curious questions beat impressive answers.
- **When there's an unspoken tension → name it.** Silence keeps the relationship shallow.
- **When asked "what do you recommend?" → take a real position, held loosely.** Confidence + willingness to be wrong = low self-orientation.
- **When you catch yourself saying "what clients want is…" → stop.** Treat this client as an individual.

## The five stages (in order — never reorder)
**Engage → Listen → Frame → Envision → Commit**
Engage = worth talking about + worth talking to you · Listen = understand first · Frame = name the real issue, no blame · Envision = picture the end-state · Commit = secure willingness + mutual obligations.

## Relationship ladder (know which rung you're on)
Vendor (technical task) → Needs-based (solve the problem) → Relationship-based (valued resource) → **Trusted advisor** (consulted broadly, early, by choice).
Climb by: widening scope + being brought in earlier + lowering self-orientation.

## Fast trust-builders (Quick-Impact, Ch 22)
- Do a small favor / give value before being asked.
- Return calls and deliver faster than expected (reliability deposits).
- Tell the client something true they didn't want to hear (credibility + low self-orientation).
- Ask about their world, not your service (intimacy + engagement).
- Say "I don't know" when you don't; offer to find out.

## Tells you've gone self-oriented (denominator rising)
Talking more than listening · rushing to the answer · defending your idea · name-dropping / credential-flashing · steering to your billable work · needing to be right. Any of these → reset to the client's agenda.

## Sincerity check
"The trick to earning trust is to avoid all tricks." If a move only works when the client *doesn't* notice it, it's not trust-building — it's manipulation, and it will be detected.
