## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The Trust Equation (Ch 8) — the master model
> **Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-Orientation**

- **Credibility** — words; "I can trust what she *says*." Built from expertise + honesty (presence, credentials, truth-telling). Fastest to establish but overrated by professionals.
- **Reliability** — actions; "I can trust her to *act*." Dependability across repeated interactions; keep small promises to earn the right to make bigger ones.
- **Intimacy** — safety; "I feel comfortable discussing this." The scarcest, highest-leverage numerator term. Grows by having the courage to talk about difficult/emotional agendas.
- **Self-Orientation** — the *denominator*, and the biggest lever. When your focus is on *yourself* (your revenue, your ego, looking smart, being right) trust collapses no matter how high the numerator. Low self-orientation = genuine focus on the client.

**Use it to diagnose a weak relationship**: score each term 1–10, find the limiting factor, and act on it. Because self-orientation is a *divisor*, reducing it multiplies trust more than adding credentials. Most trust failures are self-orientation failures.

### The Three Basic Skills (Part One)
A trusted advisor must (1) **earn trust**, (2) **give advice effectively**, and (3) **build relationships**. Expertise is table stakes — these three are the differentiators.

### The Five Stages of Trust Development (Part Two) — "Engage, Listen, Frame, Envision, Commit"
1. **Engage** — client feels "there is an issue worth talking about, and this person is worth talking to about it."
2. **Listen** — earn the right to advise by understanding, not by answering.
3. **Frame** — give the issue a clear, fresh, non-judgmental definition (the root cause / real question).
4. **Envision** — jointly picture the desired end-state; the most-skipped, highest-value stage.
5. **Commit** — ensure the client will do what it takes; agree on realistic action and mutual obligations.
Trust is built by moving *through the stages in order* — skipping to solutions (esp. skipping Listen and Envision) is the classic failure.

### The Evolution of Client Relationships (Ch 2)
Service-based (vendor / technical expert) → Needs-based (problem solver) → Relationship-based (valued resource) → **Trust-based** (trusted advisor, consulted on almost anything). Trusted advisors are used across a *widening* range of issues and *earlier* in the client's thinking.

### Recurring principles
- **"The trick to earning trust is to avoid all tricks"** (Ch 7) — sincerity is a *technique* only in the sense that caring genuinely is what works; fakery is detected.
- **Advice is given, but must be *taken*** (Ch 4) — the test of advice is whether it's acted on, which depends on trust, not logic.
- **Name it and claim it** — say the hard, unspoken thing (intimacy) rather than avoiding it.
- **Take a point of view, then be willing to give it up** — commitment plus openness signals confidence without self-orientation.
