# Glossary — The Trusted Advisor

**Commitment** — the fifth stage of trust development: ensuring the client is genuinely willing to do what solving the problem requires, with mutual obligations made explicit (Ch 9, 14).

**Credibility** — the "words" term of the trust equation; being believable through expertise + honesty. Fastest component to build, most overrated by professionals (Ch 8).

**Cross-selling** — selling additional services to an existing client; hard because it usually requires *another* trusted relationship, not a referral of your own trust (Ch 21).

**Engagement** — the first stage of trust development: the client concludes there's an issue worth discussing and you're worth discussing it with (Ch 9, 10).

**Envisioning** — the fourth stage: jointly imagining the desired end-state / alternate reality; the most-often skipped, highest-value stage (Ch 9, 13).

**Framing** — the third stage: giving a problem a clear, fresh, non-judgmental definition that names the real issue or root question (Ch 9, 12).

**Intimacy** — the "safety" term of the trust equation; the client's sense that it's safe to discuss difficult, personal, or emotional agendas. Scarcest and highest-leverage numerator term (Ch 8).

**Levels of client relationship** — service-based (vendor) → needs-based → relationship-based → trust-based; advisors climb by widening scope and earlier involvement (Ch 2).

**Lieutenant Columbo approach** — deliberately setting aside ego and status, using humble, curious, "help me understand" questioning to lower self-orientation and draw out the client (Ch 17).

**Listening** — the second stage: active, incisive, involved attention that earns the right to advise; understanding before answering (Ch 9, 11).

**Mindsets** — the internal attitudes an advisor must hold (curiosity, client-focus, ego control, self-confidence without self-orientation) (Ch 6).

**Naming and claiming** — saying the difficult or unspoken thing directly; a core intimacy-building move (Ch 5, 11).

**Quick-Impact List** — a set of concrete first actions that build trust quickly at the start of or within a relationship (Ch 22).

**Reliability** — the "actions" term of the trust equation; dependability shown by keeping promises across repeated interactions (Ch 8, 19).

**Romance (rules of)** — the relationship-building principles, drawn by analogy to courtship: engage the other person's interests, earn trust in stages, treat each client as a unique individual (Ch 5).

**Self-orientation** — the *denominator* of the trust equation; preoccupation with oneself (revenue, ego, being right). The single biggest driver of distrust; lowering it multiplies trust (Ch 7, 8, 17).

**Sincerity vs. technique** — the question of whether trust-building is manipulation; resolved as "the trick to earning trust is to avoid all tricks" — genuine caring is the technique (Ch 7).

**Trust equation** — Trust = (Credibility + Reliability + Intimacy) / Self-Orientation; the book's master diagnostic model (Ch 8).

**Trusted advisor** — a professional the client consults across a wide and growing range of issues, early and by choice, because trust has been earned (Ch 1, 2).

**Vendor** — the lowest relationship level: engaged for a specific technical task, judged on expertise alone (Ch 2).
