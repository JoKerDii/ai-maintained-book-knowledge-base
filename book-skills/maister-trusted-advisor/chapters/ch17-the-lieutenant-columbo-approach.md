# Chapter 17: The Lieutenant Columbo Approach

## Core Idea
The TV detective Lt. Columbo neutralizes people's expectations of him — appearing rumpled, humble, and underqualified — so they lower their guard and speak openly. Advisors should emulate this by subordinating ego and stripping away the trappings that put clients on guard.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Lieutenant Columbo Approach**: Deliberately neutralize the client's preconceptions and status barriers so they relax and talk honestly, revealing the truth.
  - When to use: When a client is intimidated by your expertise, guarded, presenting themselves as they want to be perceived rather than as they are.
  - How: Puncture the "you expert, me dummy" dynamic — praise the client's intelligence, wear the "anti-uniform," make small talk, downplay your capabilities, ask humbling questions. Subordinate your ego to the service of the client.

## Key Concepts
- **Style over method**: Columbo's power lies in his chosen style and character, not in official methodology — he customizes, situational and instinct-driven.
- **The anti-uniform**: Rumpled trench coat, old car, cheap cigars — visible signals that dismiss status and put the other party at ease.
- **"You expert, me dummy" echoes**: The client's unconscious feelings — resentment, awe, jealousy, competition, desire to be liked — triggered by an advisor's superior expertise.
- **Trappings that put clients on guard**: Framed degrees, lab coats, gadgets, worn travel guides — symbols reinforcing the client's sense of fate in another's hands.
- **The anti-trickster**: Columbo doesn't create illusions; he strips them away, becoming "professional equivalent of beige, of background music."
- **Pogo's dictum**: "We have found the enemy, and it is us" — the real barrier is the advisor's own love of ego-gratifying status symbols.

## Mental Models
- Think of yourself as beige/background music: subordinate your presence so the client's real thoughts emerge.
- Use humbling touches (Columbo's "I may be a little slow here...") to eradicate a preconception and set the other party at ease.
- Think of expertise-signaling as *anti-Columbo*: sagely nodding and dropping wisdom is the professional equivalent of name-dropping.

## Anti-patterns
- **Displaying status and technical mastery for ego gratification**: The degrees and trappings signify your hard-won success but keep the client guarded and withholding.
- **Nodding sagely and dispensing "the Answer"**: Listening with your nose high until you descend with wisdom reinforces the intimidation Columbo dismantles.
- **Adopting actual incompetence**: The authors explicitly warn against appearing genuinely incompetent — the point is neutralizing expectations one level below the surface, not being inept.

## Worked Example
A client arrives with a troubling, high-stakes problem and is quietly intimidated by the advisor's expertise (the framed degree, the confident manner). The anti-Columbo advisor nods knowingly, interjects wisdom, and reaches for the almighty answer — the client stays guarded, describing things as he wants them perceived, withholding information. The Columbo advisor instead praises the client's intelligence, dresses down, makes small talk, and even self-deprecates his own capabilities ("Maybe it's just me, but..."). One by one the client's inhibitions vanish; he begins to talk openly and honestly, offering information rather than withholding it, and real progress toward resolution becomes possible. Columbo wins not by method but by neutralizing expectations about himself.

## Key Takeaways
1. Neutralize the client's preconceptions and status anxieties so they lower their guard and tell the truth.
2. The biggest barrier is the advisor's own attachment to ego, status symbols, and the belief that success is purely technical mastery.
3. Subordinate your ego to the service of the client — become background, not spectacle.
4. Do not fake incompetence; the technique operates a level below the surface.
