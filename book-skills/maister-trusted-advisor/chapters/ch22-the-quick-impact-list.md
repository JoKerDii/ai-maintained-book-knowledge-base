# Chapter 22: The Quick-Impact List to Gain Trust

## Core Idea
A consolidated, do-it-now list of the highest-impact, fastest-payback behaviors for gaining trust. Most are simple, cost nothing, and can be started in the next client conversation — the barrier is emotional, not technical.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Quick-Impact List (11 core tactics)**: The fastest-payback trust builders.
  - When to use: Any client interaction where you want to raise trust immediately.
  - How, item by item:
    1. **Listen to everything** — paraphrase until the speaker says "Yes, that's exactly it."
    2. **Empathize (for real)** — listen to, understand, and *acknowledge* where they're coming from; when you think "this guy's an idiot," ask "why does he believe this?"
    3. **Note what they're feeling** — a valid observation plus a few words from the heart ("You seem distracted; something happening?").
    4. **Build that shared agenda** — open every meeting by sharing your agenda and sincerely inviting theirs; models "we-not-me" and creates buy-in.
    5. **Take a point of view** — float a trial balloon ("This is probably not where we'll end up, but..."); it stimulates reaction and crystallizes issues even if wrong.
    6. **Take a personal risk** — put a piece of yourself out there; intimacy is the act of risking personal loss.
    7. **Ask about a related area** — curiosity beyond your specialty signals that you care.
    8. **Ask great questions** — open-ended questions respect the speaker by letting them set the frame of reference.
    9. **Give away ideas** — respond with three to five ideas ("these might be wild, but..."); expertise is like love — you destroy it only by not giving it away.
    10. **Return calls unbelievably fast** — within minutes; no one expects it and it signals how much you value them.
    11. **Relax your mind** — cleanse internal distraction before a stressful meeting by concentrating on one piece of wisdom.
- **14 Relaxation Sayings**: One-line precepts to focus on before a meeting — e.g., "It's about the client," "The answer is a better question," "It's a 'we' game, not a 'me' game," "A point of view doesn't commit you for life," "Don't ever tell a lie or even shade the truth."
  - When to use: 60 seconds before a call or meeting to calm stress.
  - How: Think about only one at a time; the others wait for another day.
- **Top Things to Remind Yourself (14 in-the-moment prompts)**: e.g., "I don't have to prove myself every ten seconds," "Have I earned the right yet to give an answer?", "Emulate Lt. Columbo: 'I may be a little slow here...'", "Take responsibility for the emotional outcome," "Is my tummy telling me something's wrong? My tummy's right."

## Key Concepts
- **Paraphrase test**: You haven't listened until the speaker confirms your restatement as exactly right.
- **Understand ≠ agree**: You need only understand the other person's view, not endorse it, to earn the right to be heard back.
- **Personal ≠ private**: A risk must be personal to build intimacy, but it doesn't have to be private.
- **Expertise is unlimited**: Like love, it isn't halved by giving it away; problem-redefinition and creativity only improve with practice.
- **Earning the right**: Recurring self-check — "Have I earned the right yet to give an answer?"
- **Emotional data from yourself**: Racing pulse or a "tummy" signal is information — name it out loud and explore it.

## Mental Models
- Use "the answer is a better question" — more value comes from problem *definition* than problem *answer*.
- Think "just because the client asks a question doesn't mean it's the right question to answer."
- Use "turn an argument back into a conversation" whenever you notice you're trying to win.
- Think of a not-known answer as "a really neat question — let's get into it," not a threat.

## Anti-patterns
- **Rushing to answer before earning the right**: Reminder #6 — pause and ask whether you've earned it.
- **Trying to win the argument**: Reminder #7 — convert it back to conversation.
- **Hoarding ideas to protect a "limited store"**: Contradicts tactic #9; withholding destroys expertise rather than preserving it.
- **Ignoring your own gut/physical signals**: Reminder #14 — the tummy is right; raise it.
- **Proving yourself every ten seconds**: Reminder #1 — insecurity-driven over-credentialing erodes trust.

## Worked Example — A "First 90 Days" Application
An advisor starting a new engagement strings several quick-impact tactics into a routine. **Week 1:** Open the kickoff by sharing a draft agenda and sincerely asking the client to add to it (tactic 4), then mostly listen and paraphrase until the client confirms "yes, that's exactly it" (tactic 1), and note an emotional observation — "You seem energized about this one; what's driving that?" (tactic 3). **Weeks 2–4:** Return every call within minutes (tactic 10), and in a working session float a trial balloon — "This probably isn't where we'll land, but what if...?" (tactic 5) — and give away three to five rough ideas rather than guarding a "limited store" (tactic 9). **Ongoing:** Before each high-stakes meeting, spend sixty seconds on one relaxation saying ("It's about the client"; "A point of view doesn't commit you for life") to steady nerves, and ask about an adjacent business area to show genuine curiosity (tactic 7). **The two final suggestions:** Call a client now; and tell your romantic partner how much they're appreciated — today.

## Key Takeaways
1. The highest-payback trust builders are simple, immediate, and mostly free; the obstacle is emotional courage, not skill.
2. Listen and paraphrase until confirmed, then empathize — understanding, not agreement, earns the right to be heard.
3. Take points of view and personal risks; both build intimacy and help the client think, even when the view is wrong.
4. Give ideas away generously and return calls astonishingly fast — scarcity thinking and slowness both erode trust.
5. Manage your own state with pre-meeting sayings and in-the-moment reminders — "Have I earned the right?", "Emulate Columbo," "Take responsibility for the emotional outcome."
6. Act now: call a client today, and appreciate the person closest to you.
