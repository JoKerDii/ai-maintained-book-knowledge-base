# Glossary — Atomic Habits

**Aggregation of Marginal Gains** — the philosophy of improving every component of a system by ~1%; small gains compound into decisive results (Ch 1).
**Annual Review** — yearly reflection: what went well, what didn't, what I learned (Ch 20).
**Atomic Habit** — a tiny, easy routine that is also a building block of a larger system of compound growth (Ch 1).
**Automaticity** — the state where a behavior runs without conscious thought, reached through repetition (Ch 3, 11).
**Cardinal Rule of Behavior Change** — what is immediately rewarded is repeated; what is immediately punished is avoided (Ch 15).
**Commitment Device** — a present choice that controls your future actions, locking in good behavior in advance (Ch 14).
**Craving** — the motivational force behind a habit; the anticipation of a reward / desire for a change in state (Ch 3, 8).
**Cue** — the trigger that initiates a habit by predicting a reward (Ch 3).
**Decisive Moments** — small daily forks (cook vs. takeout) that set the options available to your future self (Ch 13).
**Deliberate Practice** — focused effort to improve specific weaknesses; paired with habits it produces mastery (Ch 20).
**Diderot Effect** — obtaining something new spirals into further consumption/behaviors; behaviors chain (Ch 5).
**Environment Design** — arranging surroundings so good-habit cues are obvious and bad-habit cues invisible (Ch 6, 7).
**Explore/Exploit Trade-off** — sample options early (explore), then double down on the best fit (exploit) (Ch 18).
**Fall in Love with Boredom** — pros stick to the schedule when it's dull; boredom is the main threat to lasting habits (Ch 19).
**Fit** — the match between a habit/field and your natural temperament; good fit multiplies returns (Ch 18).
**Flow State** — full immersion "in the zone," reachable when challenge is ~4% beyond current ability (Ch 19).
**Four Laws of Behavior Change** — Make it Obvious, Attractive, Easy, Satisfying (build); invert to break (Ch 3).
**Frequency, Not Time** — habits form from number of repetitions, not days elapsed (Ch 11).
**Gateway Habit** — the ≤2-minute entry point on the path to a larger goal (Ch 13).
**Goldilocks Rule** — peak motivation comes from tasks of just manageable difficulty — not too hard, not too easy (Ch 19).
**Habit** — a behavior repeated enough to become automatic; a reliable solution to a recurring problem (Ch 3).
**Habit Contract** — a written, witnessed agreement stating a habit and the penalty for failing (Ch 17).
**Habit Loop** — Cue → Craving → Response → Reward, the neurological feedback cycle of every habit (Ch 3).
**Habit Shaping** — scaling a two-minute habit up in stages, mastering the first two minutes of each level (Ch 13).
**Habit Stacking** — pairing a new habit with a current one: "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]" (Ch 5).
**Habit Tracking** — measuring whether you did a habit; obvious, attractive, and satisfying at once (Ch 16).
**Habits Scorecard** — listing daily habits and scoring each +/–/= against your desired identity (Ch 4).
**Identity-Based Habits** — habits chosen from who you wish to become rather than what you want to achieve (Ch 2).
**Immediate-Return vs. Delayed-Return Environment** — ancestral rewards were instant; modern good habits pay off later (Ch 15).
**Implementation Intention** — a pre-set plan: "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]" (Ch 5).
**Integrity Report** — midyear reflection on core values and whether you're living them (Ch 20).
**Inversion of the Four Laws** — Make it Invisible, Unattractive, Difficult, Unsatisfying (to break habits) (Ch 3, 7, 10, 14, 17).
**Lagging Measure** — an outcome (weight, net worth) that trails the habits producing it (Ch 1).
**Law of Least Effort** — behavior gravitates to the option requiring the least energy (Ch 12).
**Motion vs. Action** — motion is planning/preparing; action is the rep that produces results (Ch 11).
**Never Miss Twice** — a slip is fine; two in a row starts a bad habit — recover immediately (Ch 16).
**One Space, One Use** — give each context a single association so cues don't compete (Ch 6).
**Plateau of Latent Potential** — the phase where effort is "stored" before results appear (Ch 1).
**Pointing-and-Calling** — saying actions aloud to make automatic behavior conscious (Ch 4).
**Priming the Environment** — pre-arranging surroundings so the next good action is ready to go (Ch 12).
**Reflection and Review** — periodic review that prevents habits from becoming mindless and off-course (Ch 20).
**Reward** — the end of the loop that satisfies the craving and teaches the brain to repeat the cue (Ch 3).
**Sweet Spot of Desire (50/50)** — motivation peaks near a 50/50 split of success and failure (Ch 19).
**Systems vs. Goals** — goals set direction; systems drive progress. "You fall to the level of your systems" (Ch 1).
**Temptation Bundling** — linking a wanted action to a needed one: "After [NEED], I will [WANT]" (Ch 8).
**Three Layers of Behavior Change** — Outcomes → Processes → Identity; change from the inside out (Ch 2).
**Three Social Groups We Imitate** — the Close, the Many, the Powerful (Ch 9).
**Two-Minute Rule** — a new habit should take less than two minutes to start (Ch 13).
**Valley of Disappointment** — the stretch where effort feels wasted before results surface (Ch 1).
**Votes (for an identity)** — each habit is a piece of evidence for the type of person you're becoming (Ch 2).
