# Chapter 14: How to Make Good Habits Inevitable and Bad Habits Impossible (Inverted 3rd Law — Make It Difficult)

## Core Idea
Lock in future behavior now, while you have willpower, by changing what's physically possible later. Automate good choices and make bad ones require heroic effort — ideally, impossible.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Commitment Device**: a choice made in the present that controls your actions in the future — locking in good behavior in advance.
  - When to use: any habit where future-you predictably caves.
  - How: raise the cost of the bad option before temptation strikes (Victor Hugo locking away his clothes to force writing; buying single-serving packages; using a website blocker).
- **Make It Difficult** (inversion of the 3rd Law): increase friction so bad habits take more steps than you'll bother with.
- **One-Time Actions That Automate Habits**: a single decision that pays compounding dividends (auto-enroll in a savings plan, unsubscribe, buy a good mattress, install a water filter).

## Key Concepts
- **Automating the decision**: the best way to guarantee a good habit is to make the desired behavior the default that doesn't depend on willpower.
- **Commitment devices increase the odds you do the right thing in the future** by binding present intentions to future constraints.
- **Onetime choices** (buy vs. rebuy the decision daily) deliver ongoing returns from a single act.

## Mental Models
- Design decisions once, at a moment of strength, so future-you can't easily undo them.
- The most reliable behavior is the one you don't have to decide about at all.

## Anti-patterns
- Relying on in-the-moment willpower for a habit you know you'll rationalize away.
- Leaving bad habits at the same friction as good ones and hoping discipline wins.

## Worked Example
John Henry Patterson (NCR founder) automated ethics/behavior via strict systems rather than trusting individual willpower. Personal commitment devices: pre-pay a personal trainer to guarantee you show up; delete social apps from your phone (keep them only on desktop) so checking takes deliberate effort; ask your bank to auto-transfer savings on payday so you never see the money. Victor Hugo, to finish *The Hunchback of Notre-Dame*, had his assistant lock away all his clothes so he couldn't leave the house — writing became the path of least resistance.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use commitment devices to bind future behavior while willpower is high.
2. Automate good habits with one-time setup decisions.
3. Add friction to bad habits until they're more trouble than they're worth.
