# Chapter 16: How to Stick with Good Habits Every Day (4th Law)

## Core Idea
Track your habits to make progress visible and satisfying — and when you slip, never miss twice. Consistency, not perfection, compounds.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Habit Tracking**: a simple measure (calendar X, journal tick, app) of whether you did the habit.
  - Why it works: it's **obvious** (a record cues the next rep), **attractive** (progress is motivating), and **satisfying** (marking it feels good) — it hits three laws at once.
  - How: keep it simple; automate measurement where possible; "don't break the chain."
- **Never Miss Twice**: missing once is an accident; missing twice is the start of a new (bad) habit. Get back on track immediately.
  - When to use: the moment you slip on any habit.
  - How: the day after a miss, do the habit no matter how small (even the two-minute version).

## Key Concepts
- **"Don't break the chain"** (Seinfeld strategy): a visual streak of marked days becomes its own reward and pressure to continue.
- **Never miss twice**: the second miss, not the first, is what derails you — bounce back fast.
- **Measurement ≠ the goal**: track the *identity-building* behavior, and don't let the metric distort the aim (Goodhart-style).

## Mental Models
- What gets measured gets managed — a visible record reinforces the identity ("I'm the kind of person who doesn't miss").
- A bad day handled well (get back on immediately) barely dents long-term progress.

## Anti-patterns
- All-or-nothing thinking: one miss → "I've blown it" → total abandonment.
- Over-optimizing the metric until it replaces the real goal (tracking becomes the point).

## Worked Example
Trent Dyrsmid, a young stockbroker, kept two jars on his desk: 120 paper clips in one, none in the other. Each sales call moved one paper clip to the empty jar. The visible tally of progress made the behavior satisfying and self-reinforcing; within ~18 months his book of business grew to $5 million. The jar was a low-tech habit tracker — an immediate, visible reward for showing up.

## Key Takeaways
1. Track habits so progress is visible, satisfying, and self-cueing.
2. Never miss twice — recover instantly after any slip.
3. Keep the tracker simple and don't let the measure become the mission.
