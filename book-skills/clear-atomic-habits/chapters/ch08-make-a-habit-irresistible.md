# Chapter 8: How to Make a Habit Irresistible (2nd Law — Make It Attractive)

## Core Idea
We're motivated by the *anticipation* of reward, not the reward itself. The more attractive an opportunity, the more likely it becomes habit-forming — so engineer attractiveness into the habits you want.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Temptation Bundling**: link an action you *want* to do with an action you *need* to do.
  - Format: **"After [HABIT I NEED], I will [HABIT I WANT]."** (Often combined with habit stacking: "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEED]; after [NEED], I will [WANT].")
  - When to use: a beneficial habit that isn't intrinsically appealing.
  - How: allow yourself a craved pleasure only while/after doing the needed habit (watch Netflix only while on the exercise bike).
- **Dopamine-Driven Feedback Loop**: dopamine spikes on *anticipation*, not just receipt — craving is what drives action. Make the anticipation pleasurable.

## Key Concepts
- **Craving = anticipation of a reward**: "It is the anticipation of a reward — not the fulfillment of it — that gets us to take action."
- **Attractiveness as motivation**: dopamine rises when we predict a reward; a habit becomes irresistible when it reliably promises one.

## Mental Models
- Bundle "want" with "need" so the needed habit inherits the wanted habit's pull.
- Reframe cravings: what you're really after is the predicted state change, so make that prediction vivid and positive.

## Anti-patterns
- Expecting a dull-but-good habit to stick without any built-in appeal.
- Bundling in a way that undercuts the habit (rewarding a workout with something that erases its benefit).

## Worked Example
Ronan Byrne, an engineering student, wanted to exercise more but preferred Netflix. He hacked a stationary bike to a laptop and wrote code that let Netflix play only while he pedaled above a set speed — bundling the want (Netflix) with the need (cycling). Other templates: "After I get home, I will only listen to my favorite podcast while I make dinner"; "I can only read celebrity gossip at the gym."

## Key Takeaways
1. Make good habits attractive by tying them to something you already crave.
2. Motivation lives in anticipation — build a pleasurable expectation into the habit.
3. Temptation bundling turns a "should" into a "get to."
