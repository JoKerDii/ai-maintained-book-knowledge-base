# Chapter 5: The Best Way to Start a New Habit (1st Law)

## Core Idea
Don't rely on motivation to remember a habit — pre-decide *when* and *where* you'll act. Concrete plans anchored to time, location, or an existing habit make the cue obvious.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Implementation Intention**: a plan made beforehand about when and where to act.
  - Format: **"I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]."**
  - When to use: any habit you keep "meaning to" do but forget or delay.
  - Why it works: time and location are the two most common cues; specifying both removes ambiguity and the need to decide in the moment.
- **Habit Stacking**: pair a new habit with a current one (a special implementation intention using an existing habit as the cue).
  - Format: **"After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."**
  - When to use: you already have reliable daily routines to anchor to.
  - How: identify a rock-solid current habit → attach the new one immediately after → chain multiple for a routine (a "habit stack").

## Key Concepts
- **Diderot Effect**: obtaining a new possession creates a spiral of consumption/new behaviors (Diderot's scarlet robe made the rest of his belongings look shabby). Behaviors chain — use this to stack good habits onto existing ones.
- **Cue specificity**: vague intentions ("I'll exercise more") lose to specific ones ("at 6 p.m. in the gym").

## Mental Models
- Motivation is overrated for *initiation*; a clear cue beats high motivation.
- Every current habit is a potential trigger you can build on.

## Anti-patterns
- Setting goals without a plan for execution ("I want to write more") — no when/where means it competes with everything else and loses.

## Worked Example
Habit-stacking formulas in practice:
- "After I pour my morning coffee, I will meditate for one minute."
- "After I take off my work shoes, I will immediately change into my workout clothes."
- "After I sit down to dinner, I will say one thing I'm grateful for."
Build a chain: coffee → meditate → put on running shoes → run. Each completed link cues the next.

## Key Takeaways
1. Fill in "I will [X] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]" for every habit you want to start.
2. Anchor new habits to established ones with "After [CURRENT], I will [NEW]."
3. Be specific; specificity is what makes the cue obvious enough to fire.
