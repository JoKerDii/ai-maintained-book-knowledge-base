# Chapter 15: The Cardinal Rule of Behavior Change (4th Law — Make It Satisfying)

## Core Idea
We repeat what feels good *immediately*. Because our brains evolved for an "immediate-return environment" but we live in a "delayed-return" world, you must add an immediate reward to good habits whose real payoff is far off.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Cardinal Rule of Behavior Change**: "What is immediately rewarded is repeated. What is immediately punished is avoided."
  - Refined: "What is *immediately* rewarded is repeated; what is *immediately* punished is avoided." Immediacy is everything.
- **Add an Immediate Reward**: attach a small, satisfying reward to a good habit that only pays off long-term — closing the loop so the brain wants to repeat it.
  - When to use: habits with delayed benefits (saving, exercising, studying).
  - How: pick a reward that reinforces (doesn't contradict) your desired identity.

## Key Concepts
- **Immediate-return vs. delayed-return environment**: ancestral behaviors paid off now; modern good habits pay off later, so they lack the built-in satisfaction that reinforces them.
- **The habit's ending matters**: "The first three laws increase the odds that a behavior will be performed *this* time. The fourth law increases the odds that a behavior will be repeated *next* time."
- **Reinforcement**: use an immediate reward to make the moment you finish a good habit feel successful.

## Mental Models
- The cost of your good habits is in the present; the cost of your bad habits is in the future — reverse the incentives.
- Make the ending of a good habit satisfying so your brain files it under "do again."

## Anti-patterns
- Choosing an immediate reward that undermines the habit (a bowl of ice cream after a workout, a shopping spree after saving).
- Trusting that delayed benefits alone will sustain a habit — they usually won't.

## Worked Example
A user quit smoking and cut spending by opening a "Freedom Fund" savings account. Every time she skipped a purchase or a cigarette, she immediately transferred the money she would have spent. Watching the balance grow gave her an instant, satisfying reward for the good behavior — and the identity payoff ("I'm becoming financially free"). The immediate visible gain reinforced the delayed benefit until the habit stuck.

## Key Takeaways
1. Make good habits satisfying in the moment, not just beneficial in the future.
2. Immediacy beats magnitude — a small instant reward can outweigh a big delayed one.
3. Pick rewards that reinforce your desired identity.
