# Chapter 13: How to Stop Procrastinating by Using the Two-Minute Rule (3rd Law)

## Core Idea
Scale any new habit down until it takes less than two minutes. Master the art of *showing up* first; you can't optimize a habit that doesn't exist.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Two-Minute Rule**: "When you start a new habit, it should take less than two minutes to do."
  - When to use: starting any habit, or reviving one you keep dropping.
  - How: find the "gateway habit" — the ≤2-minute entry point on the path to your goal ("read before bed" → "read one page"; "do yoga" → "take out the mat"; "run a marathon" → "put on my running shoes").
- **Gateway Habits**: map goals on an easy-to-hard scale; the very-easy end is your gateway.
- **Habit Shaping**: once the two-minute version is established and automatic, scale it up in stages, always mastering the first two minutes of each new level before advancing.

## Key Concepts
- **Decisive moments**: small forks in the day (cook vs. takeout, bike vs. drive) that set the menu of options for your future self. Master these and you master the day.
- **Ritualize the beginning**: the two-minute version standardizes the start, and a habit must be established before it can be improved.
- Test: if a habit feels forced, do it for two minutes and *stop* — "it's not a strategy for starting, it's the whole thing."

## Mental Models
- "A new habit should not feel like a challenge." Make the on-ramp trivially small.
- Standardize before you optimize — showing up is the habit; performance comes later.

## Anti-patterns
- Starting too big: excitement makes you attempt too much too soon, and the habit collapses.
- Skipping the ritual of showing up and going straight for peak performance.

## Worked Example
Gateway-habit ladder for "become a reader": read one page (gateway) → read for two minutes → read one chapter → read three chapters → read most days. Or "become an early riser": be home by 10 p.m. → have lights off by 10:30 → lie in bed by 10:45. The learner masters the first two minutes at each rung — habit shaping — until the full behavior is automatic. Twyla Tharp's dancer ritual: her habit wasn't the workout, it was *hailing the cab* at 5:30 a.m. — the two-minute trigger that made the rest inevitable.

## Key Takeaways
1. Downscale any habit to under two minutes to guarantee you start.
2. Master the gateway; a standardized start beats an ambitious plan.
3. Use habit shaping to grow the two-minute version once it's locked in.
