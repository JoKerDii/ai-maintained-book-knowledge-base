# Chapter 20: The Downside of Creating Good Habits (Advanced Tactics)

## Core Idea
Habits create automaticity — but automaticity breeds complacency and mindless repetition. Mastery = Habits + Deliberate Practice, kept honest by reflection and review.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Habits + Deliberate Practice = Mastery**: habits make the basics automatic so attention is freed for the next level; deliberate practice keeps you advancing.
  - When to use: any pursuit of true excellence, not just consistency.
  - How: automate the fundamentals via habit, then deliberately push the frontier of skill.
- **Reflection and Review**: a periodic look back that prevents habits from sliding into mindlessness and keeps them aligned with your identity and goals.
  - How: an **Annual Review** (what went well, what didn't, what I learned) plus a shorter **Integrity Report** (midyear) revisiting core values and whether you're living them.

## Key Concepts
- **Downside of habits**: once a behavior is automatic, you stop paying attention to small errors — you get comfortable and plateau ("You do it good enough on autopilot").
- **Deliberate practice**: focused effort to improve specific weaknesses, the opposite of coasting on automaticity.
- **Reflection/review** guards against complacency, surfaces mistakes, and lets you adjust before drifting off course.

## Mental Models
- "Habits + Deliberate Practice = Mastery." Habits are the foundation, not the ceiling.
- Identity should be flexible: don't let one aspect of who you are become so rigid it breaks when circumstances change ("The tighter we cling to an identity, the harder it becomes to grow beyond it").

## Anti-patterns
- Coasting on autopilot and mistaking repetition for improvement.
- Over-identifying with a single role/habit so that any threat to it feels existential (keep identity small and adaptable: "I'm the kind of person who is adaptable," not "I'm a CrossFitter").

## Worked Example
The author's own review system: each December he runs an **Annual Review** answering three questions — What went well this year? What didn't go well? What did I learn? Around midyear he writes an **Integrity Report** revisiting his core values and asking whether his habits and behavior still reflect the person he wants to be. These reviews catch drift, break complacency, and keep the habit system pointed at the right identity.

## Key Takeaways
1. Habits automate the basics; deliberate practice pushes past the plateau.
2. Schedule reflection and review to avoid mindless repetition and drift.
3. Keep identity flexible so growth doesn't threaten who you think you are.
