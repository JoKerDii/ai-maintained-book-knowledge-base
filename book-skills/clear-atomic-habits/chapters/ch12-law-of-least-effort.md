# Chapter 12: The Law of Least Effort (3rd Law)

## Core Idea
Humans gravitate toward the option requiring the least work. Make good habits low-friction and bad habits high-friction, and behavior follows the path of least resistance.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Law of Least Effort**: energy is precious; the brain is wired to conserve it, so we default to whatever's easiest.
  - When to use: designing any habit or breaking any bad one.
  - How: reduce friction (steps, decisions, distance) for good habits; increase friction for bad ones.
- **Addition by Subtraction / Reduce Friction**: strip steps out of good habits until they're nearly effortless.
- **Prime the Environment**: pre-arrange your surroundings so the next good action is ready to go.
  - How: lay out gym clothes the night before; prep the coffee maker; put healthy food front and center.

## Key Concepts
- **Friction**: the number of steps/energy between you and a behavior. Good habit → decrease friction. Bad habit → increase friction.
- **Priming**: setting up your space in advance so the desired habit requires almost no activation energy.
- The point isn't to *do* easy things — it's to make it easy to do things that pay off later.

## Mental Models
- "Create an environment where doing the right thing is as easy as possible."
- Add just enough friction (even a few extra steps) to a bad habit and it often fades on its own.

## Anti-patterns
- Making good habits require many steps or decisions (buried gym bag, complicated meal prep) — friction wins.
- Leaving bad habits one tap away (app on the home screen, snacks in reach).

## Worked Example
Japanese factory "lean production" removes wasted motion from every workflow. Applied at home: an author reset his TV each night by unplugging it and removing the batteries from the remote — adding ~just enough friction that he stopped watching by default, while still able to watch deliberately. Inverse priming: set out your workout clothes, water bottle, and shoes the night before so the morning habit is nearly automatic.

## Key Takeaways
1. Behavior follows the path of least resistance — engineer that path.
2. Reduce friction for good habits; add friction for bad ones.
3. Prime your environment in advance so the good choice is the easy choice.
