# Cheatsheet — Atomic Habits (decision aid)

## The core decision: build or break?

| Step | To BUILD a good habit | To BREAK a bad habit |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Cue | **Make it Obvious** | Make it Invisible |
| 2. Craving | **Make it Attractive** | Make it Unattractive |
| 3. Response | **Make it Easy** | Make it Difficult |
| 4. Reward | **Make it Satisfying** | Make it Unsatisfying |

## Which law do I reach for? (diagnose the failing step)
- "I keep **forgetting** / never get around to it" → 1st Law: implementation intention, habit stacking, environment cue.
- "I **don't feel like** doing it" → 2nd Law: temptation bundling, join a culture where it's normal, reframe "get to."
- "It's **too much effort** / I start too big" → 3rd Law: Two-Minute Rule, reduce friction, prime the environment.
- "I **do it once then drop it**" → 4th Law: immediate reward, habit tracking, never miss twice, accountability.

## Fill-in formulas (memorize these)
- Implementation intention: **"I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]."**
- Habit stacking: **"After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]."**
- Temptation bundling: **"After [HABIT I NEED], I will [HABIT I WANT]."**
- Identity: **"I am the type of person who [does X]."** → prove with small wins.

## Rules of thumb & thresholds
- **1% better/day ≈ 37× in a year**; 1% worse/day ≈ near zero.
- **Two-Minute Rule**: any new habit must start in **< 2 minutes**.
- **Never miss twice**: one miss = accident; two = new habit. Recover next day.
- **Goldilocks / sweet spot**: aim for **~50% success rate**; flow ≈ ~4% beyond current skill.
- **Immediacy > magnitude**: a small *instant* reward beats a big *delayed* one.
- **Frequency > time**: count reps, not days, to build automaticity.

## Tells & smells (recognize the trap)
- You're reading/planning/researching a lot but not doing → **motion, not action** (Ch 11).
- "I'll start Monday / when I'm ready" → readiness is a byproduct of reps, not a prerequisite.
- Relying on willpower to resist a visible cue → self-control is short-term; **remove the cue** instead.
- "No thanks, I'm *trying* to quit" → still the old identity; say "I'm not a [X]."
- One slip → "I've blown it" → all-or-nothing thinking; apply never-miss-twice.
- Habit feels boring so you seek novelty → boredom is the threshold pros push through, not a stop sign.

## Goals vs. Systems
- Goal = direction (fine for setting aim). System = the process (what actually moves you).
- **"You do not rise to the level of your goals. You fall to the level of your systems."**
- If you keep resetting the same goal → fix the system/inputs, not the outcome.

## The three questions to find your fit (Ch 18)
1. What feels like fun to me but work to others?
2. What makes me lose track of time?
3. Where do I get greater returns than the average person?
→ If you can't win by being better, **win by being different.**

## Sustaining loop (Advanced)
Mastery = **Habits + Deliberate Practice**, kept honest by **Reflection & Review** (Annual Review + midyear Integrity Report). Keep identity *flexible* so growth doesn't threaten who you are.
