## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The thesis**: You do not rise to the level of your goals; you *fall to the level of your systems*. Tiny habits compound — 1% better every day is ~37× better in a year — but results lag effort (the Plateau of Latent Potential), so judge yourself by trajectory, not today's score.

**Systems > Goals** (Ch 1): Goals set direction; systems drive progress. Fix the inputs and the outputs fix themselves. Goals have four problems: winners/losers share them, they're momentary, they restrict happiness, they yo-yo.

**Identity-Based Habits** (Ch 2): Lasting change works from the inside out — **Identity → Processes → Outcomes**, not the reverse. Every action is a *vote* for the type of person you wish to become. Two steps: (1) decide who you want to be; (2) prove it with small wins. Reframe: not "run a marathon," but "become a runner."

**The Habit Loop** (Ch 3): **Cue → Craving → Response → Reward.** Diagnose which step is failing, then apply the matching law.

**The Four Laws of Behavior Change** — the operating system of the book:

| | Build a good habit | Break a bad habit (inversion) |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Cue** | Make it **Obvious** | Make it Invisible |
| **2. Craving** | Make it **Attractive** | Make it Unattractive |
| **3. Response** | Make it **Easy** | Make it Difficult |
| **4. Reward** | Make it **Satisfying** | Make it Unsatisfying |

**1st Law — Make it Obvious** (Ch 4–7): Build awareness (Habits Scorecard, pointing-and-calling). Use **implementation intentions** — "I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION]" — and **habit stacking** — "After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT]." Design your **environment** so good cues are visible; to break a habit, remove the cue (self-control is a short-term strategy).

**2nd Law — Make it Attractive** (Ch 8–10): Motivation is the *anticipation* of reward. Use **temptation bundling** — "After [HABIT I NEED], I will [HABIT I WANT]." Join a **culture where your desired behavior is normal** (we imitate the Close, the Many, the Powerful). To break a habit, reframe it as unattractive by highlighting its true costs.

**3rd Law — Make it Easy** (Ch 11–14): Habits form by **frequency, not time** — get reps (action), don't just plan (motion). Obey the **Law of Least Effort**: reduce friction, prime the environment. Use the **Two-Minute Rule** — scale any habit to under two minutes to master showing up. To break a habit, add friction or use **commitment devices**.

**4th Law — Make it Satisfying** (Ch 15–17): **Cardinal Rule** — what is *immediately* rewarded is repeated. Add an immediate reward to good habits with delayed payoff. Use **habit tracking** ("don't break the chain") and **never miss twice**. To break a habit, add an immediate cost via an **accountability partner** or **habit contract**.

**Advanced** (Ch 18–20): Pick the **right game** (fit > grit; explore then exploit). The **Goldilocks Rule** — peak motivation at just-manageable difficulty (~50% success); fall in love with boredom. Mastery = **Habits + Deliberate Practice**, kept honest by **Reflection & Review**.
