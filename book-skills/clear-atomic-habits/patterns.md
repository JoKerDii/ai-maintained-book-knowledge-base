# Patterns & Techniques — Atomic Habits

Concrete, reusable techniques with their fill-in formulas. Organized by which Law they serve.

## Implementation Intention (1st Law)
**When to use**: any habit you keep forgetting or postponing.
**How**: write `I will [BEHAVIOR] at [TIME] in [LOCATION].`
**Trade-offs**: requires specificity up front; vague versions don't fire.

## Habit Stacking (1st Law)
**When to use**: you have reliable daily habits to anchor to.
**How**: `After [CURRENT HABIT], I will [NEW HABIT].` Chain several into a routine.
**Trade-offs**: the anchor habit must itself be rock-solid, or the chain breaks.

## Environment Design / Make the Cue Obvious (1st Law)
**When to use**: any good habit you want automatic.
**How**: place the cue in your line of sight; give each space one dedicated use.
**Trade-offs**: only as strong as the most obvious competing cue.

## Habits Scorecard (1st Law — awareness)
**When to use**: before changing anything.
**How**: list every daily habit → mark +/–/= vs. your desired identity.
**Trade-offs**: awareness only; no change without follow-through.

## Pointing-and-Calling (Inverted 1st Law)
**When to use**: catching an automatic bad habit in the act.
**How**: say the action and its consequence aloud.
**Trade-offs**: works in the moment; doesn't remove the cue.

## Remove the Cue / Make It Invisible (Inverted 1st Law)
**When to use**: breaking an environment-triggered bad habit.
**How**: delete the trigger — phone in another room, snack out of the house.
**Trade-offs**: requires controlling the environment; harder in shared/public spaces.

## Temptation Bundling (2nd Law)
**When to use**: a good habit that isn't intrinsically appealing.
**How**: `After [HABIT I NEED], I will [HABIT I WANT].` Allow the craved thing only with the needed thing.
**Trade-offs**: pick a "want" that doesn't cancel the habit's benefit.

## Join the Right Culture (2nd Law)
**When to use**: making a habit socially attractive.
**How**: join a group where the desired behavior is normal and you share common ground.
**Trade-offs**: the same force works against you in the wrong group.

## Reframe / Highlight Benefits (2nd Law & inverted)
**When to use**: making good habits attractive, bad habits unattractive.
**How**: "get to" not "have to"; associate a bad habit with its true hidden costs.
**Trade-offs**: mindset shift takes repetition to stick.

## Reduce Friction / Prime the Environment (3rd Law)
**When to use**: any good habit.
**How**: cut steps and decisions; set up in advance (clothes out, gear ready).
**Trade-offs**: setup effort now for effortlessness later.

## Two-Minute Rule (3rd Law)
**When to use**: starting or reviving any habit.
**How**: scale it to <2 minutes (the gateway habit); master showing up, then use habit shaping to scale up.
**Trade-offs**: risk of staying at two minutes forever — shape upward once established.

## Add Friction / Commitment Device (Inverted 3rd Law)
**When to use**: bad habits you'll rationalize away in the moment.
**How**: increase steps for bad habits; lock in good ones with a present decision (auto-transfer savings, delete the app, one-time purchases).
**Trade-offs**: must set it up while willpower is high.

## Add an Immediate Reward (4th Law)
**When to use**: good habits with delayed payoff (saving, exercise).
**How**: attach a small instant reward that reinforces your desired identity.
**Trade-offs**: reward must not contradict the habit (no ice cream after the gym).

## Habit Tracking / Don't Break the Chain (4th Law)
**When to use**: sustaining consistency.
**How**: mark each completion (calendar X, jar, app); keep the streak alive.
**Trade-offs**: don't let the metric become the goal; keep it simple.

## Never Miss Twice (4th Law)
**When to use**: recovering from a slip.
**How**: the day after a miss, do at least the two-minute version.
**Trade-offs**: none — the key defense against all-or-nothing collapse.

## Habit Contract / Accountability Partner (Inverted 4th Law)
**When to use**: high-stakes bad habits needing external teeth.
**How**: write the habit, schedule, and immediate penalty; have partners co-sign.
**Trade-offs**: needs a trustworthy partner who'll enforce it.

## Explore/Exploit + Pick the Right Game (Advanced)
**When to use**: choosing which habits/fields to commit to.
**How**: sample broadly, then double down where you have natural fit; if you can't be better, be different.
**Trade-offs**: over-exploring delays mastery; over-exploiting too early risks the wrong game.

## Goldilocks Tuning (Advanced)
**When to use**: sustaining long-term motivation.
**How**: keep difficulty at just-manageable (~50% success); raise the bar in small steps.
**Trade-offs**: too easy → boredom; too hard → discouragement.

## Habits + Deliberate Practice + Review (Advanced)
**When to use**: pursuing mastery, not just consistency.
**How**: automate fundamentals via habit, deliberately push weaknesses, run Annual Review + Integrity Report.
**Trade-offs**: reflection takes scheduled time; skipping it invites complacency.
