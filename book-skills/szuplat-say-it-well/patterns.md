# Patterns — Concrete Techniques from Say It Well

## The 50–25–25 Rule
**When to use**: Every presentation, from a keynote to a tonight-only toast.
**How**: Split prep into 50% thinking/researching/organizing, 25% writing, 25% editing + practicing. Scale to deadline (1 month = 2wk/1wk/1wk; 1 week = 3d/2d/2d; tonight = 1hr/30min/30min).
**Trade-offs**: Front-loads work before any words exist, which feels unproductive to anxious speakers — but it's the strongest predictor of a good talk and reduces nerves.

## The Ten Questions (Self-Knowledge)
**When to use**: Before developing any talk; revisit as life changes.
**How**: Answer in writing — who am I, where from, working toward what, what's important, what values, what do I believe, why this work, my fears, my hopes, how is my life part of a larger uniting story.
**Trade-offs**: Introspective and slow; but without it you can't say what only you can say.

## The Only You Test
**When to use**: After drafting, to check for generic content.
**How**: Imagine someone else delivering your script. If it still makes perfect sense, it's generic — rewrite around your unique stories.
**Trade-offs**: Personal material can feel exposing, but it's more memorable and less scary to deliver.

## Ten Words / North Star
**When to use**: In the "Think It Out" phase, before writing.
**How**: Boil your message to one sentence, ten words or fewer; print it, keep it visible, ask "Am I on message?" Then find 3-4 (max 5) supporting points.
**Trade-offs**: Forces you to sacrifice secondary points for focus.

## Scope It Out — 10 Questions / 100% Situational Awareness
**When to use**: As soon as you're asked to speak.
**How**: Ask who's the audience, what do we share, when/what/why is it, why me, where do I fit in the run of show, where is it, what's the staging, is there a podium, how long. Get name pronunciations and VIPs.
**Trade-offs**: Time up front; prevents surprises (e.g., attacking a plan whose author is in the front row).

## Three-Act Structure
**When to use**: Structuring any speech.
**How**: Beginning (setting, characters, hook), Middle (the problem/argument), End (solution + hope). Add signposts ("First… Second… Finally…").
**Trade-offs**: Can feel formulaic; keeps a long, intimidating speech manageable.

## Opening Menu (Have 'em at Hello)
**When to use**: Crafting your first words.
**How**: Choose from — say hello; shout out the home crowd; introduce yourself with authority (ethos); killer opening line; start with a story in medias res; invoke Scripture (audience-dependent); lift up the Three Fs; shock 'em with a fact; greet in the local language.
**Trade-offs**: Scripture/humor can misfire with the wrong audience — always know who you're addressing.

## Weave the Acknowledgments
**When to use**: Whenever you'd otherwise open with a name list.
**How**: Keep to 5-6 names max, or move them later; deliver with feeling as a celebration so the crowd cheers along.
**Trade-offs**: Risk of omitting someone who expected a mention; brevity is worth it.

## Add Humor (Not Jokes)
**When to use**: To warm an audience and reveal a shared connection.
**How**: Be self-deprecating, surprise them, crib a comedian's line (with credit), or pun. Aim for chuckles; give the humor a point.
**Trade-offs**: Inside jokes and canned jokes fall flat; humor without a point wastes time.

## The Three Shareds (Be a Uniter)
**When to use**: Stating your purpose early in the talk.
**How**: Rally around a shared challenge (concrete, vivid, Threat-Dictionary language); evoke a shared identity (Power of You/We/What We Do); appeal to/challenge/defend a shared idea. Say what you *believe*.
**Trade-offs**: Requires resisting the easy path of attacking opponents.

## Values-Based Public Speaking (Moral Framing)
**When to use**: Persuading across ideological or cultural difference.
**How**: Identify the audience's moral foundations (Care, Fairness, Authority, Loyalty, Sanctity, Liberty) and frame your argument in *their* value language (Kansas abortion vote framed as "freedom" and anti-government-interference).
**Trade-offs**: Requires setting aside your own preferred framing and truly understanding the other side.

## Speak From the Heart / To Theirs
**When to use**: The middle, when making your case.
**How**: Feel it first; use emotional words (Emotions Wheel); get emotional; be vulnerable. Then speak to one person (your "Uncle Dan"), to their experience (empathy), and show a human face ("look at the one").
**Trade-offs**: Emotional exposure feels risky, especially for women and people of color who face bias — but audiences always want more emotion, not less.

## Use Statistics Sparingly (Story Test)
**When to use**: Deciding whether a number belongs.
**How**: Keep a stat only if it clarifies, increases the chance of action, or tells a story; otherwise cut. Bundle stats into a narrative with a beginning/middle/end.
**Trade-offs**: Data feels authoritative but rarely persuades and can backfire (psychic numbing).

## BBQ Rule + Get Real + SWAP
**When to use**: Editing for clarity.
**How**: Say only what you'd say at a family BBQ; talk about people and outcomes, not programs and inputs; cut perplexing acronyms; avoid negative contractions, double negatives, legalese, and loftiness.
**Trade-offs**: You lose the appearance of expertise/sophistication — which is the point.

## Make It Sing
**When to use**: Polishing the language.
**How**: Be unpredictable (avoid finish-that-sentence clichés); swap in mellifluous words sparingly; write like a script (left-margin sentences, blank lines) or a song (verse-like line breaks) to build in breaths, pauses, and varied sentence length; use refrains/anaphora, the Rule of Three, and light alliteration; loosen tight lines.
**Trade-offs**: Overdone (alliteration avalanche, everything at a "ten") it reads contrived.

## Ten Commandments for Telling the Truth
**When to use**: Fact-checking and framing before delivery.
**How**: Don't steal (credit sources), don't fabricate, get facts right, find the original source, be honest about the past, avoid happy talk, face the elephant early, use absolutes sparingly, don't fear nuance, speak hard truths. Verify via primary sources, Britannica, FactCheck/PolitiFact, Pew, wire services; quotes via Bartlett's/QuoteInvestigator.
**Trade-offs**: Nuance takes more words and can be "less effective" politically, but protects credibility.

## Have an Ask, Every Time
**When to use**: The end of every talk.
**How**: Make one specific, small request; for change, list demands one by one. Reinforce with lead-by-example (deeds, money) and a big, catchy vision.
**Trade-offs**: Specificity commits you publicly; vagueness would be safer but useless.

## End With Hope
**When to use**: The finale — always.
**How**: Recap main points, end with your most hopeful story, bookend, end with a question, or paint the vision realized. Keep hope realistic (confront brutal facts) and action-bound.
**Trade-offs**: Must avoid false/blind optimism; honesty about roadblocks is required.

## Cut 15 Percent + Omit Needless Words
**When to use**: The editing 25%.
**How**: Cut roughly 15% of any draft; interrogate every paragraph, sentence, adjective, and word ("Is it truly necessary?"). Use a bot to "remove 50 words." Get honest feedback from several people.
**Trade-offs**: Painful to cut carefully crafted lines; the result is sharper every time.

## Rehearse Like a Performer
**When to use**: The practice 25% and day-of.
**How**: Print in 24pt, bold, 1.5 spacing, each page ending on a period, bottom third blank; don't memorize; practice aloud to time and de-tangle words; do interoceptive exposure (rehearse with heart pounding); visualize success; do a dry run in the room.
**Trade-offs**: Generates many pages (use a binder); time-intensive but builds calming muscle memory.

## Delivery Toolkit (Stand and Deliver)
**When to use**: At the podium.
**How**: Smile; make eye contact person to person; ~150 wpm; lower voice to pull in, raise to stress, slow to emphasize, pause for effect; two body-language rules (relax; let body match words); keep water at hand; don't apologize for stumbles; own tech fails with humor; end with "Thank you very much."
**Trade-offs**: Many cues to track — treat them as goals, not marks you must hit; being natural beats being perfect.

## Channel Anxiety into Excitement
**When to use**: Final minutes before speaking.
**How**: Say "I am excited" (emotional reappraisal), box-breathe, play a hype song; remember nerves are your body preparing you and the audience can't see them.
**Trade-offs**: Doesn't eliminate nerves; reframes them productively.
