# Cheatsheet — Say It Well (Decision Rules)

## Time Budget (the 50–25–25 Rule)
- **Rule**: Spend 50% thinking/researching/organizing, 25% writing, 25% editing + practicing — because prep, not podium performance, predicts a good talk.
| Deadline | Think/organize | Write | Edit + practice |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 month | 2 weeks | 1 week | 1 week |
| 1 week | 3 days | 2 days | 2 days |
| Tonight | 1 hour | 30 min | 30 min |

## Length & Word Count (~150 words/minute)
| Format | Time | Words |
|---|---|---|
| Speech / keynote | 15-20 min | 2,250-3,000 |
| Eulogy | 5-7 min | 750-1,050 |
| Toast / public comment | 3-5 min | 450-750 |
- **When time is tight, cut**: right before a meal (short), right after a meal (shorter), during a reception (shortest), audience standing/outdoors (short). Cut ~15% of any draft no matter what.

## Audience-Size Rule
- **<30-40 people** → don't give a big formal scripted speech; convert to notes/talking points and speak conversationally.

## Structure Defaults
- **Every speech** = Beginning (hook + unite + values) / Middle (heart + human language + cadence) / End (ask + hope).
- **Number and count your pages** before you speak; carry the script even if memorized.

## Opening — do / don't
- **Do**: say hello, shout out the crowd, introduce yourself with authority, start a story *in medias res*, drop a shocking fact, greet in the local language.
- **Don't**: open with a long acknowledgments list (Death by Acknowledgments); chase a "perfect line" before getting the story right; tell canned or inside jokes.
- **Acknowledgments**: 5-6 names max, or weave them in later; deliver as a celebration.

## Persuasion Rules
- **Frame in the audience's values, not yours** (Values-Based Public Speaking). Match to moral foundations: Care, Fairness (progressive-leaning); Authority, Loyalty, Sanctity (conservative-leaning); Liberty (universal).
- **Statistics test**: keep a number only if it (a) clarifies, (b) increases action, or (c) tells a story — else cut. Pathos beats logos.
- **To motivate giving/action**: "look at the one" — one identifiable person beats mass statistics (psychic numbing).

## Demagogue Smells (avoid / detect)
- Otherizes ("us" vs. "them"), demonizes ("evil/sinister"), dehumanizes ("vermin/animals"), or brands opponents "virus/poison" to "eradicate."
- Questions motives, attacks character, uses Polarization-Dictionary labels (idiots, liars, fascists, elites), or threatens/incites violence ("fight," "take back," "enemies to destroy").
- Rule: attack the **problem/ideas, not the people**. Watch your *buts* ("no violence, but…" negates everything before).

## Plain-Language Tells & Fixes (BBQ Rule)
- Aim ~8th-grade level. Swap: utilize→use, leverage→harness, align→coordinate, incentivize→motivate, "paradigm shift"→change.
- Talk **people/outcomes**, not programs/inputs. SWAP: cut perplexing acronyms ("if you have to explain it, don't say it").
- Avoid **negative contractions** ("can't"→"cannot"), **double negatives**, legalese, and loftiness ("Let's" not "Let us," except solemn occasions).

## Make-It-Sing Rules
- Be unpredictable: if the audience can finish your sentence, it's a cliché — replace it.
- Write like a script (left-margin sentences, blank lines) or a song (verse breaks) to build in breaths and pauses.
- Vary sentence length; don't keep everything at a "ten" — aim some sixes/sevens.
- **Alliteration cap**: ≤3 alliterative words per sentence; never sentence after sentence.
- Rule of Three pleases the ear — but use four if you have four.

## Truth Checklist (verify before delivery)
- Circle every fact, figure, quote; verify against ≥2 reputable sources (primary sources, Britannica, FactCheck/PolitiFact, Pew, AP/Reuters; quotes via Bartlett's/QuoteInvestigator).
- Face the elephant in the room **early**, not as an afterthought.
- Quantifiers: "some" <50% · "most" >50% · "vast majority" ≥70% · "nearly/almost all" ≥90% · beware "many" (true but misleading).
- Prefer most/often/rarely over all/always/never.

## Ending Rules
- **Always end with hope** — the finale is what they remember; ~80% of people are wired for optimism, and hope motivates better than fear.
- Hope must be realistic (Stockdale Paradox: confront brutal facts) and action-bound (hope without deeds = false hope).
- Fearmonger test: if the audience leaves more afraid/angry/vengeful/violent, you've gone dark.
- Ending options: recap · best hopeful story · bookend · a question ("What will you do?") · vision realized.
- Have a specific **ask**; list demands one by one. Close with four words: "Thank you very much."

## Nerves & Delivery
- Nerves are natural (fight/flight/freeze/appease) — the audience can't see them (illusion of transparency); you'll be better than you think (98% rated themselves better on video).
- Reframe: say **"I am excited"** (not "I am calm"); box-breathe (4-4-4-4); play a hype song; "the more I sweat, the more I'll succeed."
- Rehearse aloud; don't memorize; feel your fears (rehearse with heart pounding); do a dry run in the room.
- At the podium: **SMILE** (write it atop the draft), eye contact person-to-person, ~150 wpm, lower voice to pull in / raise to stress / slow to emphasize / pause for effect, silence beats "ums."
- Body language: don't overthink it (the 7-38-55 "93% nonverbal" claim is a myth). Two rules: relax; let body match words. **What you say matters more than how you say it.**

## Print Format for Delivery
- 24pt, bold, 1.5 spacing, each page ends on a period, bottom third left blank (chin stays up), binder + plastic sleeves outdoors.

## AI / Chatbot Rules
- **Don't** use bots to write personal remarks (collator = unoriginal; no humanity/voice).
- **Do** use bots to: brainstorm topics/outlines, find surprising quotes (with sources), simplify jargon ("10 simpler words for ___"), and edit ("remove 50 words"). Verify facts — bots hallucinate.
