# Chapter 4: The 50–25–25 Rule

## Core Idea
The best predictor of a good presentation isn't what happens at the podium — it's the preparation before you write a single word. Divide your time: 50% thinking/researching/organizing, 25% writing, 25% editing and practicing.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The 50–25–25 Rule**: Time allocation for any prep, scaled to the deadline.
  - When to use: Every presentation, no matter how much time you have.
  - How: 50% thinking, researching, organizing; 25% writing; 25% editing + practicing. One month = 2 weeks / 1 week / 1 week. One week = 3 days / 2 days / 2 days. Toast tonight = 1 hour / 30 min / 30 min. More preparation also means less nervousness.
- **100% Situational Awareness — Scope It Out (10 Questions)**: Assume nothing, know everything.
  - The 10: (1) Who is my audience? (2) What do we have in common? (3) When is it? (4) What is it / why is it happening? (5) Why me? (6) Where do I fit in the program (run of show)? (7) Where is it? (8) What's the staging? (9) Will there be a podium? (10) How long should I speak?
- **Think It Out — Know Your 10 Words**: Boil your message to one sentence, ten words or less; make it your North Star, print it, keep asking "Am I on message?" Then find the 3-4 (max 5, not 10) key points that support it.
- **Figure It Out — Find the Gold**: Research is sifting for nuggets — Learn (read what others said), Listen (convene experts/community), Be Inclusive (invite the affected community to shape drafts), Ask Everyone (ideas from anyone), Find Quotes That Surprise (avoid the over-quoted), Look for the Little Things ("in the particular is contained the universal"), Include Stories That Touch Your Heart.
- **Three-Act Structure**: Every speech = Beginning (setting/characters), Middle (the problem), End (the solution/resolution).

## Key Concepts
- **The Venn diagram of connection**: You + audience as two circles; your best speech lives in the overlap (what you share).
- **Word/time ratios**: ~150 words/minute. Speeches/keynotes 15-20 min (2,250-3,000 words); eulogies 5-7 min (750-1,050); toasts/public comments 3-5 min (450-750).
- **The Download**: Obama's pre-speech meeting to set purpose — "What's the story we're trying to tell here?" / "you have to decide on the purpose of your communication."
- **Write it out (don't wing it)**: If you haven't written it, you haven't thought it out; writing reveals holes in logic before you speak; it's also how you can practice.
- **Show, don't tell**: Paint small snapshots (mementos at the Vietnam wall) — the universal lives in the particular.

## Mental Models
- Don't hit the gas: starting to write immediately is the common mistake; you'll hit a wall with no idea where you're going.
- Use a bot to *brainstorm* topics/outlines — never to write — then flesh out what's unique to you.
- When blocked, don't write: walk, nap, shower ("the shower effect"), listen to music, or talk it out with a friend.

## Anti-patterns
- **Skipping the Download**: Szuplat drafted an Israel speech via the "usual process" but never got the purpose from Obama after events shifted; Obama: "this is not what I want to say." Get the purpose first.
- **Rushing to write / endless research rabbit hole**: The India-speech paralysis (and panic attack) came from no structure — solved by the three-act frame.
- **Predictable quotes**: The MLK "content of their character" line — everyone knows it, so you miss the chance to surprise.

## Worked Example
Assigned Obama's address to the Indian parliament ("only seen by one billion Indians"), Szuplat froze, spiraled into endless research, and had the only panic attack of his life. The fix was the three-act structure: Beginning = the friendship/partnership between Indians and Americans; Middle = the shared global challenges; End = how the two countries working together make the world more equal, prosperous, secure. Suddenly a 4,000-word speech was just three simple parts, and it got written.

## Key Takeaways
1. Spend 50% of your time before writing; preparation beats podium performance.
2. Get 100% Situational Awareness via the 10 questions; assume nothing.
3. Distill your message to 10 words and 3-4 supporting points.
4. Sift for gold: surprising quotes, small vivid details, heart-touching stories.
5. Always write it out; not writing means not thinking, and blocks practice.
6. Structure everything as Beginning / Middle / End.
