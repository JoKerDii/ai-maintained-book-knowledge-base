# Chapter 5: Have 'em at Hello

## Core Idea
The purpose of your opening is to forge an emotional connection and a first impression — fast. Always ask: "What will be the very first words out of my mouth?" Never open with a long list of acknowledgments ("Death by Acknowledgments").

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Great Ways to Start a Speech (the opening menu)**: Pick one that fits the moment.
  - Say Hello! (a listener perceives a greeter as more trustworthy — "I'm a friend, you can trust me").
  - Give a Shout-Out to the Home Crowd ("Hello, Chicago!" / "Go Gators!").
  - Introduce Yourself + establish authority (ethos): "As a lifelong member of this community…" (Frances Haugen: who she is, why listen, why she's there — in four sentences).
  - A Killer Opening Line (maybe) — distill the moment ("They said this day would never come") but don't obsess; "get the story right first, and the lines will follow."
  - Start with a Story — begin *in medias res* at the moment of maximum emotion (9/11 Museum: the South Tower).
  - Invoke Scripture (powerful in tragedy; but ~36% of Americans aren't Christian — know your audience).
  - Lift Up What They Love — the Three Fs: firsts, foods, fans.
  - Shock 'em — an unexpected/jarring fact or statement.
  - Speak Their Language (literally) — greet in the local tongue.
- **A Better Way to Do Acknowledgments**: Keep them short (after 5-6 names audiences squirm), or weave them later; turn them into a celebration said *with feeling* so the crowd joins ("Thank you, Principal Smith… [Cheers]").
- **You Don't Need a "Joke" — Just Add Humor**: Humor is a tool to surprise and delight by revealing a shared connection; go for chuckles, not belly laughs — Take Yourself Down a Notch (self-deprecation), Surprise Them, Steal from the Best (credit the comedian), Have Some Pun. Your humor should have a point.

## Key Concepts
- **Ethos**: Aristotle's element — persuading by your character/authority ("As president and commander in chief…").
- **In medias res**: Start a story "in the midst of things" for immediate hook.
- **The Three Fs**: Firsts, foods, fans — show love for what the audience is proud of.
- **Humor with a point**: Zander Moricz's "curly hair" as a coded stand-in for his identity — funny *and* it made the argument.

## Mental Models
- A speech is a story, not a sound bite — if you're obsessed with the perfect line, you're working backward.
- Statistics rarely change minds, but a stunning statement *at the very start* hooks attention.
- Gender/pronoun courtesy in acknowledgments: don't reinforce stereotypes; avoid assuming identity — drop the pronoun ("Parker, thank you for your leadership").

## Anti-patterns
- **Death by Acknowledgments**: Reciting every dignitary's name and title; CNN literally cut away from an Obama speech that opened this way.
- **Chasing the Perfect Opening Line**: Banging your head for a monument-worthy phrase instead of getting the story right.
- **Telling canned jokes / inside jokes**: "A priest, a rabbi…" or "Bruh, remember Cancun?!" — no shared connection, no laugh.

## Worked Example
Zander Moricz, gay senior-class president in Osprey, Florida, is warned by his principal that his mic will be cut if he discusses his sexuality, the "Don't Say Gay" law, or his activism at graduation. Under death threats and immense pressure, he opens like any class president — then: "I must discuss a very public part of my identity… As you know, I…" — removes his cap, touches his hair — "…have curly hair." Laughter and applause. He continues, using curly hair as a sustained metaphor ("having curly hair in Florida is difficult… due to the humidity"), builds to coming out to his gym teacher and family, and calls on classmates to support "the thousands of curly-haired kids." The humor had a point; his mic stayed on; standing ovation; the speech went viral.

## Key Takeaways
1. Plan your literal first words; the opening exists to make an emotional connection.
2. Say hello, shout out the crowd, introduce yourself with authority, or open with a story/Scripture/shock.
3. Never open with a long acknowledgments list; weave them in, keep them short, celebrate.
4. Show love for the audience's Three Fs.
5. You don't need jokes — add warmth and surprise that reveal a shared connection.
