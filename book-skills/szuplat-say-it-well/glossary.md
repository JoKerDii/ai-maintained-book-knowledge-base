# Glossary — Say It Well

**100% Situational Awareness** — Knowing everything about the event and audience before you speak; "assume nothing, know everything, ask lots of questions" (Ch 4).

**7-38-55 rule (myth)** — The debunked claim that 93% of communication is nonverbal; the source study addressed only how listeners identify *emotions* (Ch 15).

**Anaphora** — Starting several successive sentences with the same word or phrase ("We, the people, still believe…") (Ch 10).

**Absolutes** — All/always/everyone/never; use sparingly because most issues aren't black and white (Ch 11).

**BBQ Rule** — If you wouldn't say it at a BBQ with family (including your 13-year-old niece), don't say it in a speech; use everyday words (Ch 9).

**Blemishing effect** — Sharing a little negative information can strengthen a positive overall impression (Ch 11).

**Bookending** — Opening with a story and saving its ending for the close of the speech (Ch 5, Ch 13).

**Box breathing** — Calming technique: inhale 4s, hold 4s, exhale 4s, hold 4s, repeat (Ch 15).

**Cadence** — The rhythmic flow, beat, and tempo of spoken words; the mark of writing for the ear (Ch 10).

**Call to action / the Ask** — The specific thing you want the audience to do; every good speech has one (Ch 12).

**Collator (bots)** — A chatbot's content is by design an unoriginal amalgamation of existing material (Ch 2).

**Curse of Knowledge** — Assuming your audience shares your knowledge and vocabulary (Ch 9).

**Death by Acknowledgments** — Opening with a long recited list of names/titles; one of the worst ways to start (Ch 5).

**Demagogue** — A speaker who villainizes whole groups by appealing to prejudice; otherizes, demonizes, dehumanizes (Ch 6).

**The Download** — Obama's pre-speech meeting to set purpose; also the recap section ending each chapter (Ch 1, Ch 4).

**Earbashing** — Australian term for too much talking; the sin every speaker should swear off (Ch 14).

**Elephant in the room** — A controversy/crisis; make it the main event early rather than hiding from it (Ch 11).

**Emotional reappraisal** — Relabeling identical physical arousal from "anxiety" to "excitement" ("I am excited") (Ch 15).

**Emotions Wheel / Feelings Wheel** — A tool of emotion words to convey precise feelings ("indignant" not just "angry") (Ch 8).

**Ethos** — Aristotle's element of rhetoric: persuading through your character/authority ("As a resident of 30 years…") (Ch 5).

**Fired Up! Ready to Go!** — Edith Childs's chant; example of ad-libbing to re-energize a flat crowd (Intro, Ch 15).

**Fifty-Twenty-Five-Twenty-Five (50-25-25) Rule** — 50% thinking/researching/organizing, 25% writing, 25% editing + practicing (Ch 4).

**Find the Gold** — Researching by sifting a ton of material for the interesting nuggets — quotes, details, stories (Ch 4).

**Golden Rule of Public Speaking** — Be yourself; speak in a way natural to you (Ch 3).

**Happy talk** — One-sided positivity that ignores the negatives; erodes credibility (Ch 11).

**Hope-monger** — Obama's reclaimed label for an optimistic speaker; be one (Ch 13).

**Identifiable victim effect** — Greater empathy for one specific person than for a large group or statistics (Ch 8).

**Illusion of transparency** — The audience usually can't see your nerves (Ch 14, Ch 15).

**Illusory truth effect** — The more we hear something, even if false, the more we believe it (Ch 11).

**In medias res** — Starting a story "in the midst of things," at the moment of maximum emotion (Ch 5).

**Interoceptive exposure** — Rehearsing while your heart is pounding to build tolerance to anxiety (Ch 14).

**Look at the one** — Motivate by telling the story of a single person rather than citing mass statistics (Ch 8).

**Loosen it up** — Turning tight, intense lines conversational so the speech can breathe (sixes and sevens, not all tens) (Ch 10).

**Mellifluous words** — Elegant, pleasant-sounding words used sparingly for surprise and memorability (Ch 10).

**Moral empathy gap** — Failing to appreciate that others hold a different moral worldview (Ch 7).

**Moral foundations** — Six value prisms: Care, Fairness, Authority, Loyalty, Sanctity, Liberty (Ch 7).

**Moral framing** — Presenting an argument in the value language of the person you're persuading (Ch 7).

**Muscle memory** — Ease and naturalness gained by saying the words many times in rehearsal (Ch 14).

**Neural coupling** — Alignment of speaker's and listeners' brain waves during a speech (Ch 3).

**Omit needless words** — Sorensen's "Fish" lesson; the core of editing (Ch 14).

**Only You Test** — If someone else could deliver your script and it still makes sense, it's generic — rewrite it (Ch 2).

**Optimism bias** — ~80% of people believe the future will be better than the present; "hardwired for hope" (Ch 13).

**Pathos / logos / ethos** — Aristotle's three elements: emotion, logic, character; pathos beats logos for moving people (Ch 8).

**Polarization Dictionary** — ~200 words used by the most partisan accounts; avoid them when persuading (Ch 6).

**Power of You / We / What We Do** — Three ways to evoke shared identity (Ch 6).

**Power pose (caveat)** — Popular claim its original author later called "flimsy"; use only if it helps you feel confident (Ch 15).

**Psychic numbing** — The larger the suffering, the more overwhelmed and less helpful people become (Ch 8).

**Reps** — Repeated practice under real conditions; Obama's word for what he lacked early on (Ch 1).

**Rule of Three** — Ideas/phrases in threes please the ear ("of the people, by the people, for the people") (Ch 10).

**Sacred story** — Everyone's essential personal narrative; listening to others' helps you find your own (Ch 1).

**Science of Hope** — Hope = goals + willpower + pathways; a leading predictor of success (Ch 13).

**Speak for the ear** — Writing so every word is clear to a listener who cannot re-read (Ch 3, Ch 10).

**Stockdale Paradox** — Keep faith you'll prevail *and* confront the brutal facts of current reality (Ch 13).

**SWAP** — Stop With Acronyms that Perplex (Ch 9).

**Ten Commandments for Telling the Truth** — Szuplat's checklist for honest speaking (Ch 11).

**Ten Questions of Self-Knowledge** — Written self-inventory to find your voice before your words (Ch 1).

**Ten Words / North Star** — Your core message boiled to one sentence, ten words or less (Ch 4).

**Threat Dictionary** — ~240 vivid words (attack, chaos, collapse, suffering…) that rally collective action (Ch 6).

**Three Fs** — Firsts, foods, fans — the things an audience is proud of (Ch 5).

**Three-Act Structure** — Beginning (setting/characters), Middle (problem), End (solution) (Ch 4).

**Turning actions into identities** — Fewer verbs, more nouns ("be a voter" beats "plan to vote") (Ch 6).

**Uncle Dan** — Szuplat's device: write for one real, specific person you want to reach (Ch 8).

**Values-Based Public Speaking** — Framing arguments in the audience's values, not your own (Ch 7).

**Venn diagram of connection** — Your best speech lives in the overlap between you and your audience (Ch 4).

**Voice of Doubt** — The inner narrator that makes us reject ourselves to preempt others' rejection (Ch 1).
