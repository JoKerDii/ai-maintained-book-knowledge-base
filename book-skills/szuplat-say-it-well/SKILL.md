## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### The 50–25–25 Rule (Ch 4) — the master planning tool
Spend **50%** of your time thinking, researching, and organizing; **25%** writing; **25%** editing and practicing. Preparation before you write a single word — not podium performance — predicts a good talk, and it reduces nerves. Scales to any deadline (month = 2wk/1wk/1wk; tonight = 1hr/30min/30min).

### Finding Your Voice (Ch 1-3) — before what you say
- **Love Your Sacred Story (Ch 1)**: No one is born a gifted speaker; it's a learnable skill. Step one is *believing you can*. Ignore the **Voice of Doubt**. Do the **Ten Questions of Self-Knowledge** in writing (who am I, where from, what I believe, my values, my fears, my hopes, how my life is part of a larger uniting story).
- **Say What Only You Can Say (Ch 2)**: Give the speech only *you* can give; tell your own stories. **Only You Test** — if someone else could deliver your script and it still makes sense, it's generic; rewrite it. Don't outsource personal remarks to AI (a bot is a "collator" of unoriginal content).
- **What a Speech Really Is (Ch 3)**: A speech is a *performance* and a shared *experience*, meant to be *heard* — not an essay read aloud. Write for the ear. **Golden Rule: be yourself**; never imitate another speaker. You control script, direction, spectacle, set, and participation.

### Prepare (Ch 4)
- **100% Situational Awareness** — scope the event with 10 questions (audience, what you share, when/what/why, why me, run of show, venue, staging, podium, length).
- **Know Your 10 Words** — boil the message to one sentence (your North Star) + 3-4 supporting points.
- **Find the Gold** — research by sifting for surprising quotes, small vivid details, and heart-touching stories.
- **Three-Act Structure** — Beginning / Middle / End.

### THE BEGINNING — Hook 'em (Ch 5-7)
- **Have 'em at Hello (Ch 5)**: The opening forges an emotional first impression. Say hello, shout out the crowd, introduce yourself with authority (ethos), start a story *in medias res*, or shock 'em. Never open with Death by Acknowledgments. Add humor, don't tell jokes.
- **Be a Uniter (Ch 6)**: A good speech has one purpose — unite people around a good cause. Connect via a **shared challenge** (concrete, vivid), a **shared identity** (Power of You / We / What We Do), or a **shared idea**. Never demagogue (otherize / demonize / dehumanize); attack problems, not people.
- **Appeal to Values (Ch 7)**: Frame arguments in the *audience's* values, not yours (**Values-Based Public Speaking**). Six **moral foundations**: Care, Fairness, Authority, Loyalty, Sanctity, Liberty.

### THE MIDDLE — Hold 'em (Ch 8-10)
- **Speak from Your Heart… and to Theirs (Ch 8)**: **Pathos beats logos.** Feel it, use emotional words, be vulnerable. Speak to one person (your "Uncle Dan"), to their experience, and show a human face ("look at the one"). Use statistics only to clarify, drive action, or tell a story.
- **Talk Like a Human (Ch 9)**: **BBQ Rule** — everyday words. Talk about people/outcomes, not programs/inputs. **SWAP** (cut perplexing acronyms). Avoid negative contractions, double negatives, legalese, loftiness.
- **Make It Sing (Ch 10)**: Give it cadence. Be unpredictable; write like a script/song for rhythm, pauses, and varied sentence length; use refrains/anaphora, the Rule of Three, and light alliteration.

### THE END — Wow 'em (Ch 11-13)
- **Tell the Truth (Ch 11)**: Credibility rests on truthfulness. **Ten Commandments** — don't steal/fabricate, get facts right, trace sources, be honest about the past, avoid happy talk, face the elephant early, use absolutes sparingly, embrace nuance, speak hard truths.
- **Turn Your Words into Deeds (Ch 12)**: Words without deeds are empty. Always have a specific **ask**; list demands one by one; lead by example; offer a big, catchy **vision**.
- **The Only Way to End a Speech (Ch 13)**: **End with hope** — the finale is what they remember, and ~80% are wired for optimism. Hope must be realistic (**Stockdale Paradox**) and action-bound.

### SHOWTIME (Ch 14-15)
- **Make It Better (Ch 14)**: The final 25%. **Cut 15%**; omit needless words (Sorensen's "Fish"); get honest feedback; rehearse aloud (don't memorize); feel your fears; visualize; dry-run.
- **Stand and Deliver (Ch 15)**: Get in the zone (make friends, box-breathe, reframe anxiety as excitement — "I am excited," get pumped). At the podium: **smile**, eye contact, vary your voice (~150 wpm). Don't overthink body language (the "93% nonverbal" claim is a myth). **What you say matters more than how you say it.** End with "Thank you very much."
