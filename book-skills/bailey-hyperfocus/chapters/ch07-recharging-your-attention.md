# Chapter 7: Recharging Your Attention

## Core Idea
The more often you scatterfocus to replenish mental energy, the more energy you have for your most important tasks. Rest is not idleness — taking a break is one of the most productive things you can do.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Energy determines attentional space**: Attentional space expands and contracts with mental energy. Enough sleep can grow it by up to **58%**; taking frequent breaks does the same — and a ~60% larger space can lift productivity by as much on demanding tasks.
- **Signs you need to recharge**: switching often and unable to sustain focus; losing grip on intentions / working reactively; doing tasks noticeably slower (rereading the same email); drifting to mindless work; unintentionally slipping into scatterfocus.
- **Three traits of a refreshing break**: it should be (1) low-effort and habitual, (2) something you *actually want* to do, and (3) not a chore (unless you enjoy it). In short: **pleasurably effortless.** (Checking email/news is *not* a real break — it still consumes attention.)
- **Two timing rules**: (1) take a break **at least every 90 minutes**; (2) break **~15 minutes for each hour** of work. (Ninety minutes matches our natural energy/ultradian rhythm; DeskTime's most-productive 10% took a 17-min break after every 52 min.)
- **Sleep**: Bailey's rule of thumb — "for every hour of sleep you miss, you lose two hours of productivity." Sleep deficit can shrink attentional space by up to **60%**, and dreaming is "scatterfocus on steroids" (it connects dots and consolidates memory).

## Key Concepts
- **Rest = exchanging time for energy**: The investment doesn't evaporate; skipping breaks is what you should feel guilty about.
- **Ultradian rhythm**: ~90-minute energy cycles (mirroring sleep cycles) — rest ~90 min, then a 20–30 min dip.
- **Nighttime ritual**: A wind-down routine (reading, tea, no bedroom TV) is the best way to actually get enough sleep.

## Mental Models
- **The best time to take a break is *before* you need one** — like thirst, fatigue means you're already depleted.
- Low-energy dips are your most *creative* moments (the brain is least inhibited) — good for scatterfocus.
- Fun breaks give the benefits of habitual scatterfocus (future-planning, ideas) *and* restore energy.

## Worked Example
Bailey's own recharge rhythm shows the rules in action: he steps back roughly every 90 minutes and takes genuine breaks — walking to the local coffee shop *without his phone*, working out, catching up with a colleague, or listening to a podcast — never "breaking" by checking social media (which isn't restorative because it still consumes attention). He pairs this with the timing math: ~15 minutes of break per hour of work is really just a normal lunch plus a morning and afternoon break — practical within any schedule. And he protects sleep with a nighttime ritual, because a sleep deficit shrinks attentional space up to 60% and even makes you *overestimate* your own productivity.

## Anti-patterns
- **"Breaks" that aren't**: Social media / news still consume attention — no real recharge.
- **Skipping breaks when busy**: The busier you are, the *more* you need them (overwhelm rises).
- **Trading sleep for work hours**: You lose more (shrunken attentional space, slower complex work) than you gain.

## Key Takeaways
1. Scatterfocus recharges the energy that hyperfocus burns.
2. A real break is pleasurably effortless — not email or news.
3. Break at least every 90 minutes, ~15 minutes per hour worked.
4. Guard sleep: a deficit shrinks attentional space up to 60%.
5. Take breaks *before* you need them; low-energy dips are prime creative time.
