# Hyperfocus — Glossary

**Active laziness** — Staying busy with unnecessary work that produces nothing (Ch 1).

**Attention residue** — Fragments of a previous task lingering in attentional space after switching; makes work ~50% slower (Sophie Leroy) (Ch 2).

**Attentional space** — Bailey's term for working memory; the mental scratch pad ("RAM") holding what you're aware of now (~4 chunks, ~10-second lifespan) (Ch 2).

**Autopilot mode** — Managing attention reactively, without deliberate choice; fine for habits, toxic for attention (Ch 1).

**Biological Prime Time (BPT)** — Your peak-energy hours; best for focus/hyperfocus work (Ch 10).

**Boredom** — The restlessness of transitioning from high to low stimulation; "unwanted scatterfocus" (Ch 6).

**Capture mode** — Scatterfocus style: let the mind roam and write down whatever surfaces (Ch 6).

**Chunking** — Grouping information into meaningful units to hold more in attentional space (Ch 2).

**Clustering** — The brain's grouping of related dots; builds expertise and frees attentional space (Ch 9).

**Creative Prime Time (CPT)** — Your low-energy hours; best for creative/scatterfocus work (least-inhibited brain) (Ch 10).

**Default network** — The brain network active when unfocused; scatterfocus lights it up to connect dots (Ch 6).

**Distraction-free mode** — Eliminating every controllable distraction in advance to enable hyperfocus (Ch 4).

**Dot** — Any idea or piece of information in your mind; creativity = connecting dots (Ch 6, 8).

**Flow** — Total immersion when challenge ≈ skill (Csikszentmihalyi); often produced by hyperfocus (Ch 3, 5).

**Four types of distractions** — 2×2 of in/out-of-control × annoying/fun (Ch 4).

**Four types of tasks** — 2×2 grid: necessary, unnecessary, distracting, purposeful (Ch 1).

**Hyperfocus** — Keeping one important, complex object of attention filling your attentional space; the brain's most productive mode (Ch 3).

**Habitual scatterfocus** — The most powerful scatterfocus style: do a simple enjoyable task and capture rising ideas (Ch 6).

**Hourly awareness chime** — An hourly timer prompting you to check what's in your attentional space (Ch 3, 10).

**Implementation intention** — A specific when/where/which-action plan; doubles/triples follow-through (Gollwitzer) (Ch 3).

**Insight trigger** — A cue (from environment or wandering mind) that sparks a solution to an incubating problem (Ch 8).

**Intention precedes attention** — Decide what to accomplish before you begin; attention without intention is wasted (Ch 3).

**Jobs to be done** — Asking what "job" you "hire" each device to do (Christensen); redundant jobs = unnecessary device (Ch 4).

**Meta-awareness** — Noticing what's occupying your attentional space; the master attention skill (Ch 2).

**Novelty bias** — The prefrontal cortex rewards task-switching with dopamine, so busyness feels productive (Ch 2).

**Open loops** — Unresolved tasks/commitments held in your head that hijack attentional space (Ch 4).

**Parkinson's Law** — Work expands to fill available time; busywork can signal a lack of real work (Ch 5).

**Positive affect** — How good you feel; a good mood expands attentional space (Ch 10).

**Problem-crunching mode** — Scatterfocus style: hold one problem loosely and let thoughts circle it (Ch 6).

**Productivity** — Accomplishing what you intend to (not being busy) (Ch 2).

**Prospective bias** — The wandering mind's tendency to think about the future (48% of the time) (Ch 6).

**Purposeful work** — Attractive + productive tasks; the sweet spot; your ~3–4 highest-impact tasks (Ch 1).

**Reduced-distraction mode** — Taming the biggest distractions while staying reachable for collaboration (Ch 4).

**Rule of 3** — Choose the three things you want accomplished by day's end (Ch 3).

**Scatterfocus** — Intentional mind-wandering; the brain's most creative mode (Ch 6).

**Ten-thousand-hour rule** — Rough length of deliberate practice to build expert-level dots (Ch 9).

**Zeigarnik effect** — Unfinished tasks weigh more heavily on the mind than finished ones (Ch 8).

**40 bits / 4 chunks** — We consciously process ~40 of 11M sensory bits/second, and hold ~4 chunks in short-term memory (Ch 2).

**40 seconds** — Average time we focus at a computer before being interrupted or self-interrupting (Ch 0, 2).

**90/15 break rule** — Break at least every 90 minutes, ~15 minutes per hour of work (Ch 7).
