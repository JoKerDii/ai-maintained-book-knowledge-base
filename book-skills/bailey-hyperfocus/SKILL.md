## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The central idea**: Attention is your most limited, most valuable resource — *you are what you pay attention to.* Managing it deliberately (not on **autopilot**) is the core skill. Two modes do the work: **Hyperfocus** (the most *productive* mode) and **Scatterfocus** (the most *creative* mode). Master both.

### Attentional space (the foundation)
Your **attentional space** is working memory — the mental "RAM" holding what you're aware of now. It's tiny: of ~11M sensory bits/sec you consciously process **~40**, and hold **~4 chunks** in short-term memory. You can pair a complex task with a habit, but never two complex tasks. Keep it clear and you think clearly. The master skill is **meta-awareness** — noticing what's in your attentional space (the mind wanders 47% of the time). **Productivity = accomplishing what you intend** — busy ≠ productive.

### The Four Types of Tasks (Ch 1)
A 2×2 of productive/unproductive × attractive/unattractive → **Necessary**, **Purposeful** (the sweet spot — your ~3–4 highest-impact tasks), **Distracting** (small doses), **Unnecessary** ("active laziness"). Live in the top two quadrants.

### HYPERFOCUS — the productive mode (Ch 3–5)
Keep **one important, complex object of attention** filling your attentional space. **Intention precedes attention.** Four stages: **Choose** a purposeful object → **Eliminate** distractions in advance → **Focus** for a preset time → **Draw** attention back when it wanders.
- Set intentions with the **Rule of 3** (3 things by day's end), **most consequential tasks** (the domino toppling a hundred), and the **hourly awareness chime**.
- Make intentions **implementation intentions** (when/where/which action) → 62% vs 22% follow-through.
- **Tame distractions in advance** — build a **distraction-free mode** (block sites, phone away, headphones) vs. a **reduced-distraction mode** for collaboration. Attack the five pain points (notifications, phone, email, meetings, internet). **Externalize open loops** (Getting Things Done).
- Wander less by matching **challenge to skill** (Flow); enlarge attentional space via **meditation** (brain-training apps don't work). Beware **Parkinson's Law** — busywork can mean too little real work.

### SCATTERFOCUS — the creative mode (Ch 6–9)
Intentional mind-wandering — deliberately leave attentional space free. It **plans the future, recharges, and connects dots** at once (the wandering mind is 48% future-focused). Three styles: **Capture** (roam + write it down), **Problem-crunching** (circle one problem), **Habitual** (simple enjoyable task + notepad — most powerful).
- **Creativity = connecting dots.** The **Zeigarnik effect** keeps open problems front-of-mind; **insight triggers** from a rich environment + wandering mind spark solutions (Newton's apple). Connect more dots: richer environment, write the problem down, sleep on it, step back, leave tasks unfinished.
- **Collect better dots**: you can only connect what you've collected. Rank inputs **useful > balanced > entertaining > trashy**; audit and upgrade what you consume.
- **Recharge** (Ch 7): break at least every **90 min**, ~**15 min per hour**; protect sleep (a deficit shrinks attentional space up to 60%).

### Blend them (Ch 10)
The modes compound. Amplify both: invest in genuine **happiness** (a good mood expands attentional space; positive *thinking* backfires); do focus work at your **Biological Prime Time**, creative work at your **Creative Prime Time**; **caffeine** for hyperfocus, (occasional) **alcohol** for pure-creative work. **Awareness** is the thread through it all.
