# Glossary

**Accumulation of antagonists** — Stacking successive obstacles in a story so the audience genuinely doubts a happy ending (Ch 3)

**Apology Formula** — The who/what/where/when/how framework for executing an organizational apology (Ch 8)

**Apology rehearsal** — Role-playing likely apology scenarios before they occur (Ch 8)

**Battle exhortation** — Precombat oratory; Keith Yellin identified 23 recurring "common topics" across history (Ch 6)

**Board of advisers** — A small designated circle who repeatedly hear and react to your evolving transition story (Ch 9)

**Bundling** — Attaching a small idea to a larger initiative to raise its prominence (Ch 7)

**Catching the wave** — Timing an issue to a trend more people are starting to care about (Ch 7)

**Click and viz** — Converting spreadsheet cells straight to a chart without thinking about purpose (Ch 10)

**Coach out vs. coach in** — Delivery flaws can be coached away; a missing central idea cannot be coached in (Ch 1)

**Coherence** — The quality of a life story in which past, present, and future link causally; the main generator of listener trust (Ch 9)

**Companyspeak** — PowerPoint, dry memos, and hyperbolic corporate communications that draw cynicism (Ch 3)

**Core vs. noncore violation** — Whether a failure strikes at the organization's central mission or a peripheral function (Ch 8)

**Crutch words** — Verbal fillers ("um," "ah," "like," "you know") that erode credibility; replace with pauses (Ch 1)

**Declarative vs. exploratory** — Communicating information vs. figuring something out; the second dataviz question (Ch 10)

**Desire** — The protagonist's core need, "the blood of a story"; not a shopping list (Ch 3)

**Detective-story structure** — Present a problem, describe the search, deliver an "aha" that shifts perspective (Ch 1)

**Direction giving** — Uncertainty-reducing language: exactly how to do the task and how performance will be judged (Ch 6)

**Dread** — Knowing what will happen and being unable to stop it; distinct from fear, which is not knowing (Ch 3)

**Education plot** — A transition story driven by growing insight and self-understanding (Ch 9)

**Empathetic language** — Praise, encouragement, gratitude, and acknowledgment of difficulty (Ch 6)

**Everyday dataviz** — Declarative + data-driven: simple charts for formal presentations (Ch 10)

**Expertise** — One of the two sources of credibility: track record and demonstrated knowledge (Ch 5)

**Fact tellers** — People who recite chronological credentials instead of telling a story; hard to help (Ch 9)

**Four Aims (Intents)** — Be open, connect, be passionate, listen; rehearse these instead of gestures (Ch 2)

**Framing for common ground** — Describing a position in terms of the listener's tangible benefits (Ch 5)

**Happy Warrior** — A leader combining high warmth with high strength; faces troubles without being troubled (Ch 4)

**Idea generation** — Exploratory + conceptual: whiteboard sketching to work through ambiguity (Ch 10)

**Idea illustration** — Declarative + conceptual: org charts, process diagrams, frameworks (Ch 10)

**Inciting incident** — The event that throws life out of balance and starts a story (Ch 3)

**Inside-out vs. outside-in** — Preparing by setting your emotional state vs. executing prescribed nonverbal cues (Ch 4)

**Issue selling** — Winning attention and resources from senior decision makers for an idea from below (Ch 7)

**John Wayne approach** — Stating a strong position up front and pushing to a close; gives opponents a target (Ch 5)

**Lion tamer** — Engaging the other party obliquely rather than presenting a clear target (Ch 5)

**Mask** — The persona individuals and institutions present; compelling stories live beneath it (Ch 3)

**Maturation plot** — A transition story about becoming your own person (Ch 9)

**Meaning making** — Language explaining why a task matters, linking mission to listeners' goals; the hardest MLT element (Ch 6)

**Motivating Language Theory (MLT)** — Mayfield framework: direction giving + empathy + meaning making (Ch 6)

**New bliss** — The closing depiction of how much better the audience's world becomes (Ch 11)

**Persuasive story pattern** — What is / what could be oscillation across beginning, middle, and end (Ch 11)

**Placeholder apology** — A fast interim apology issued while facts are still being gathered (Ch 8)

**Poise** — Stillness plus full-height posture; telegraphs equilibrium and stability (Ch 4)

**Power poses** — Open, expansive postures held two minutes; raise testosterone, lower cortisol (Ch 4)

**Private conformity vs. outward compliance** — Trust changes beliefs; fear changes only behavior (Ch 4)

**Profluence of development** — John Gardner's term for a story's felt sense of going somewhere (Ch 9)

**Psychological contract** — Stakeholders' unwritten expectations of an organization's responsibilities (Ch 8)

**Pyramid search** — Finding experts via adjacent-field experts who refer you onward (Ch 10)

**Relationships** — The second source of credibility: a history of listening and acting in others' interest (Ch 5)

**Report–story continuum** — Every presentation sits between data-rich report and engaging story (Ch 1)

**Second conversation** — Nick Morgan's term for the nonverbal channel, which fires before words (Ch 2)

**Self-knowledge** — The root of great storytelling; building characters by asking what you would do (Ch 3)

**Seven issue-selling tactics** — Tailor, frame, manage emotions, time, involve others, adhere to norms, suggest solutions (Ch 7)

**Signposting** — Explicit verbal markers ("to begin," "finally") essential in speech though inelegant in writing (Ch 2)

**Skepticism** — Believing nothing at face value; distinguishing text from subtext (Ch 3)

**Spontaneous trait inferences** — Snap judgments from brief exposure; warmth registers faster than competence (Ch 4)

**Strategic unapologeticness** — Deliberately refusing to apologize in order to affirm a value (Ch 8)

**Talk as a journey** — Framing a presentation by choosing where to start and where to end (Ch 1)

**Turning point** — The point of no return closing act two; usually made significant by the telling, not by the event (Ch 9)

**Valley of awkwardness** — The half-memorized stage where a talk sounds recited; rehearse past it or use notes (Ch 1)

**Visual confirmation** — Rapid prototyping of charts to test a hypothesis (Ch 10)

**Visual discovery** — Exploratory + data-driven: mining large data sets visually (Ch 10)

**Visual exploration** — Open-ended, inclusive plotting when you don't know what you're looking for (Ch 10)

**Vivid evidence** — Stories, metaphors, and analogies that make data emotionally memorable (Ch 5)

**Warmth–competence matrix** — The two dimensions of social judgment accounting for >90% of impressions (Ch 4)

**What is / what could be** — The two poles whose contrast generates presentation tension (Ch 11)
