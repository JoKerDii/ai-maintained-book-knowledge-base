# Cheatsheet — Decision Rules

## Should you speak at all?
- **No compelling central idea → decline the invitation.** Delivery can be coached out; the story cannot be coached in (Ch 1).
- **Idea indicts the audience's intelligence, judgment, or morality → expect to lose** regardless of technique. Proceed only if it matters enough to your company *and* your identity (Ch 7).
- **You can't or won't change behavior → don't apologize.** It will sound hollow (Ch 8).

## Choosing your delivery mode (Ch 1)
| Situation | Do this |
|---|---|
| High stakes + weeks of rehearsal time | Memorize verbatim — but only if you can get past the valley of awkwardness |
| Limited prep time | Bullet points on note cards; focus on the transitions |
| Any amount of prep | Never read from script or teleprompter |
| Forced to read | At minimum, look up and make eye contact |

## Which persuasion frame? (Ch 7)
| Frame | Verdict |
|---|---|
| Business benefits | Safest and strongest — quantify, and find a second department that benefits |
| Opportunity ("don't miss this") | Strong — gives audience control, inspires optimism |
| Threat ("here's what we lose") | Coin flip — may produce fight or flight |
| Moral ("this is wrong") | Weakest — reads as judgment of character |

## Which motivating language, how much? (Ch 6)
- Unfamiliar task or unknown opponent → **direction giving** dominant.
- Underdog, high stakes, or shaken confidence → **empathy + meaning making** dominant.
- Veterans mid-operation → mostly direction; they're all business.
- Purpose self-evident (e.g. staff who lost loved ones to the disease you treat) → dial meaning making down; it's already there.
- Default sequence: direction → meaning → empathy → recap.

## Warmth/competence diagnosis (Ch 4)
| Emotion you elicit | Your position | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Envy (respect + resentment) | Competent, cold | Lead with warmth; validate feelings; share something personal |
| Pity (compassion + neglect) | Warm, incompetent | Full-height posture, deliberate movement, stillness; demonstrate track record |
| Contempt | Neither | Rebuild both, warmth first |
| Trust | Happy warrior | Maintain; stay a member of the group, not "them" |

- **Threshold**: a strongly disliked manager's odds of being rated a good leader ≈ **1 in 2,000**.
- **Rule**: stand up straight *and* smile — the only move that raises strength without costing warmth.

## Should you apologize, and how hard? (Ch 8)
1. Violation real **or perceived**? → perception counts; decide before the investigation finishes.
2. **Core** (strikes the mission) → maximum apology on every dimension. **Noncore** → flexibility.
3. Large/high-status org harming a low-power person → assume public outrage.
4. Committed to change? → if no, don't apologize; if yes, name the specific change.

| Dimension | Core violation setting |
|---|---|
| Who | CEO or most senior leader available |
| What | Candor + remorse + concrete change |
| Where | High profile; consider the site itself |
| When | Immediately; placeholder if facts pending |
| How | Spoken, plain, self-implicating |

## Which visualization work am I doing? (Ch 10)
| | Conceptual | Data-driven |
|---|---|---|
| **Declarative** | Idea illustration → editing + design | Everyday dataviz → design + storytelling |
| **Exploratory** | Idea generation → facilitation, rapid sketching | Visual discovery → BI, programming |

- **If it takes >3 seconds to read → it's a handout, not a slide.**
- **If discussion is about the chart rather than the idea → the chart failed.**
- **Exploring → iterate fast, ignore aesthetics. Declaring → aesthetics are the job.**

## Thresholds & defaults
- **Prep ratio**: ~1 hour practice per minute of performance; ≥3 full trial runs (Ch 1).
- **Speech rate**: reading ~300 wpm vs. listening ~150–160 wpm; 10 minutes ≈ 1,300 words (Ch 2).
- **Pauses**: aim for 2–3 seconds at key moments; average speaker manages only ~3.5 pauses/minute (Ch 1).
- **Video clips**: under 60 seconds or you lose the room (Ch 1).
- **Eye contact**: pick 5–6 friendly faces spread across the room (Ch 1).
- **Power pose**: 2 minutes before the encounter (Ch 4).
- **Finalize content**: one month before the event (Ch 1).
- **Issue selling**: all seven tactics combined ≈ 40% of the success/failure difference (Ch 7).
- **Warmth + competence**: >90% of the variance in impressions (Ch 4).

## Tells & smells
- Audience calls a polished talk "calculated," "phoned in," or "not real" → you rehearsed gestures instead of intents (Ch 2).
- You're describing your organization's achievements → you've lost them; switch to the problem you're solving (Ch 1, Ch 3).
- Your draft has no antagonists → nobody will believe it; rosy pictures foment distrust (Ch 3).
- You're arguing headquarters' logic to a skeptical field → reframe around *their* profit (Ch 5).
- People comply but don't adopt → you led with strength, not warmth (Ch 4).
- Your networking pitch is chronological → you're a fact teller; lead with where you're going (Ch 9).
- Your presentation ends with a to-do list → add the new bliss (Ch 11).
- Your chart shows two decades when the point is three years → subtract (Ch 10).
- You feel defensive and unfairly blamed → that's the apology-delay reflex, not analysis (Ch 8).

## Timing rules for selling upward (Ch 7)
- Deadline far off, decision makers exploring → **open-ended inquiry** beats a specific proposal.
- Imminent launch or release → **propose now**.
- Priorities shifting, new players arriving, boss's preoccupations changing → **window open**.
- Informal first to gauge interest → then formal to create pressure to respond.
- Boss lacks power or interest → ask to accompany whoever pitches; if going direct, keep the boss in the loop.
