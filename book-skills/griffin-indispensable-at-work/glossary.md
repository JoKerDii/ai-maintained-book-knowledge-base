# Glossary — How to Say It: Be Indispensable at Work

**Achievement inventory** — a list of what you did rewritten with dollar values and percentages attached, turning activity into quantified result (Ch 2)

**Ambush / "gotcha"** — publicly cornering a colleague; the satisfaction is momentary and the conflict long-term (Ch 6, Ch 9)

**Blanket indictment** — "You never come in at budget!" More provocative than helpful and rarely the whole truth (Ch 6)

**Boss as customer** — the frame governing every boss interaction: he trades value (a paycheck) expecting value (your productivity) (Ch 4, Ch 8)

**Brand** — a set of information, values, and expectations attached to a product, giving buyers confidence and loyalty (Ch 7)

**Bully** — a difficult type whose objective is to make you feel inadequate; requires a victim to exist (Ch 9)

**Bumbler** — the marginally incompetent boss; handled with patience, written confirmations, and no visible exasperation (Ch 8)

**Career** — a job with a future (Ch 3)

**Career kryptonite** — the strengths that make you exceptional and simultaneously vulnerable (Ch 10)

**Chronic complainer** — the difficult type who channels productive energy into whining to dodge work (Ch 9)

**Clutch player** — the person who reliably makes or beats hard deadlines; by definition indispensable (Ch 7)

**Corporate karma** — the workplace you help create by what you do, on the "what goes around comes around" model rather than "nice guys finish last" (Ch 5)

**Creative austerity** — devising ways to do more with less (Ch 7)

**Cultivate, don't plow** — rebuilding a drifting relationship with no agenda and no pushing (Ch 10)

**Dunbar's number** — ~150, the theorized ceiling on people you can truly know; connections beyond it have indirect value (Ch 11)

**Efficiency vs. effectiveness** — the cheapest path to a result vs. the one that actually produces it; confusing them can be fatal (Ch 10)

**Eternal Truth (Part 1 / Part 2)** — Part 1: there is no substitute for doing your job well. Part 2: being liked can outlast your numbers, and being a top performer guarantees nothing (Ch 8)

**Exclusionary language** — *I, mine, you, yours* plus words that negate, deny, contradict, reject, or refuse (Ch 5)

**External customers** — clients and consumers on the other side of the counter (Ch 7, Ch 8)

**Feature vs. benefit** — what the product *is* vs. how it makes the customer *feel*; FedEx's feature was overnight delivery, its advertised benefit was peace of mind (Ch 4)

**FIDO** — Forget It, Drive On; the first test applied to any workplace irritation (Ch 5)

**Finger pointer** — the blaming boss; answered by accepting responsibility without accepting blame (Ch 8)

**Foxhole illusion** — the felt safety of hunkering down, which turns a moving target into a stationary one (Ch 10)

**Friction** — necessary workplace physics: without it you can neither move nor stop (Ch 6)

**Helping vocabulary** — *we, us, our* plus collaboration verbs (brainstorm, collaborate, confer, cooperate, huddle, learn, listen, solve, team up, work together) (Ch 5)

**High-maintenance employee** — the real reason most people get fired; a problem *creator* (Ch 7)

**Hub of the corporate wheel** — what you become by convening the community rather than joining it (Ch 11)

**Human capital** — you as an asset with intrinsic, portable value, not an interchangeable plug in a socket (Ch 2)

**In-house snake charmer** — the reputation earned by consistently creating satisfaction in difficult people (Ch 9)

**Informational interview** — a knowledge hunt deliberately not framed as a job hunt (Ch 12)

**Internal customers** — subordinates, colleagues, and bosses, who expect value all of the time (Ch 7, Ch 8)

**Internal networking** — the neglected half of networking: connections inside your own company, including the next cubicle (Ch 11)

**Language of business** — money: money made, money saved, money your skills will make and save (Ch 2)

**Lateral move** — a change at roughly the same compensation made for a non-salary strategic gain; serial ones are career killers (Ch 4)

**Leap gap** — the distance between your current title+field unit and your target one (Ch 12)

**Lever** — an influential person through whom your idea reaches people you can't reach (Ch 10)

**Musketeer Solution** — "All for one, one for all"; the workplace cannot thrive as a zero-sum game (Ch 6)

**Negative inventory** — your honest list of deficiencies; essential to know, never to volunteer (Ch 2)

**Office diplomat** — the team player who brings the team together, keeps it together, and positions it to win — as opposed to the **office politician**, who manipulates to get ahead (Ch 6)

**Passive aggressor** — the difficult type characterized by reliable unreliability: procrastination, sulking, claimed helplessness (Ch 9)

**Permission to fail** — the concession that unblocks an evasive complainer, at the cost of extra work for you (Ch 9)

**Plug-and-socket mentality** — the boss's view of your job as a generic slot and you as an interchangeable plug (Ch 3)

**Poacher** — the difficult type who presents your idea as his own (Ch 9)

**Poisoning the well** — making an idea toxic before anyone evaluates it, e.g. "This is a joke, right?" (Ch 9)

**Positioning** — how a product, service, or person is perceived by its best potential customer; its three dimensions are Benefit, Target, and Competition (Ch 4)

**Principle of Scarcity** — a multitude of needs and wants against a shortage of resources; the structural root of most workplace conflict (Ch 6)

**Problem solver** — the one position that can never be filled redundantly (Ch 3, Ch 7)

**Prospect pool** — the universe of employers your current career reaches (Ch 12)

**Proxemics** — the anthropological study of how we define and use personal space (Ch 5)

**Rapport** — a relationship of mutual trust and emotional affinity; cumulative, built and eroded by interactions great and small (Ch 5)

**Redundancy** — the merger/acquisition euphemism that reliably precedes layoffs (Ch 1)

**Responsibility without blame** — owning a problem you didn't cause (Ch 8)

**Schemer** — the difficult type who uses gossip and rumor with malicious intent (Ch 9)

**Skills vs. transferable (portable) skills** — knowledge affixed to one job ("I know how to sell the Model A Widget") vs. know-how you own and carry ("I know how to sell") (Ch 2, Ch 3)

**Task oriented, not time defined** — measuring your day by the job done, not the clock (Ch 7)

**Transferable Skills Ladder** — skills ordered by complexity, where perceived height equals perceived value: following directions < communicating < persuading < negotiating < mentoring (Ch 3)

**Trigger object** — a pre-committed physical reminder to stop, listen, and think before reacting emotionally (Ch 6)

**Two-stage career transition** — changing job title *or* field first, the other later (Ch 12)

**Utility player** — someone who goes beyond the expected job description; more valuable in downsizing eras (Ch 7)

**Vertical integration** — why several apparently separate industries may now be one, making career changes possible without leaving your employer (Ch 12)

**"I think I am a verb"** — Grant's and Fuller's identical self-description; define yourself by what you actively do (Ch 3)

**War face / work mask** — a deliberately practiced expression worn as professional discipline; Patton practiced his scowl, you practice your smile (Ch 7)
