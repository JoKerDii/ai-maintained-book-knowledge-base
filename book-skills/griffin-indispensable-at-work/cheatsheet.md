# Cheatsheet — Be Indispensable at Work (Griffin)

## The one rule everything reduces to
**When describing value, convert to money.** Nouns and verbs beat adjectives; nouns and verbs *linked to numbers* beat everything. Your boss has exactly one motive: save and make money.

## Decision rules
| When… | Do… | Because |
|---|---|---|
| You feel an irritation | Run **FIDO** — Forget It, Drive On | Most annoyances are transitory; acting enshrines them |
| FIDO fails | Act on the **situation**, never the person | Situations can be fixed; people are not readily fixable |
| You're about to describe yourself | Convert trait → verb → verb+object | "I am persuasive" is a trait; "I persuade people" is a skill |
| You have a question for your boss | Bring an **offer** instead | It's hard to feel persuasive with your hand out |
| You're offered a salary number | **Counteroffer**, even if it's good | The number *you* name first caps your year; a counteroffer bonds you more than acceptance does |
| A raise is refused | Accept gracefully, then ask *"When can we schedule another review?"* | Never express dismay or threaten to leave — he already assumes it |
| Business is bad and you want more | Ask for a **promotion**, not a raise | A raise rewards the past; a promotion is a proposal about the future, so it's easier to sell in a downturn |
| You miss a deadline | Give an **explanation with a plan**, never an excuse | Late work is lost value; the plan is what restores the clutch brand |
| You err | Report it yourself, fast: sorry → own it → empathize → remedy | Far worse if the news reaches your boss any other way |
| Someone offers criticism | Thank them; listen ≠ agree | Even unfounded criticism tells you how you're coming across |
| You want to criticize | Ask permission, in private, one issue, with alternatives | Never criticize *anyone* — only an issue, action, or outcome |
| You need help | Ask (don't apologize) | A request is a vote of confidence that empowers the other person |
| You're about to say "my idea" to a boss | Say "**our** idea" | No idea sells easier than one he thinks he already owns |
| A bully attacks you personally | Ignore the question; answer with facts; show no emotion | A bully cannot be a bully without a victim |
| Someone's complaint blocks work | Verify merit **first**, then stop debating merits entirely | You can't accuse a work-dodger before checking the assignment isn't genuinely impossible |
| You're about to share an idea | Write and date it first | Poaching is the most common workplace scheme |
| The danger signs appear | Engage **more**, not less | A stationary target is easier to hit (Patton's foxhole) |
| Target career differs in title *and* field | Change one, then the other | Both at once is a foot-long sandwich in one gulp |
| You're employed and job-hunting | **Never post your résumé** | Your boss surfs those sites too |

## Trade-off matrices

**Which skill claim to make** (People ladder — height = perceived value)
| Claim | Reads as | Positions you for |
|---|---|---|
| Following directions | Entry level | Nothing |
| Communicating | Competent | Your current job |
| **Persuading** | Solid | Keeping a sales role |
| **Negotiating** | Strong | Account executive |
| **Mentoring** | Leadership | VP of Sales |

**Effort vs. return on hours worked**
| Hours/week | Likely outcome |
|---|---|
| 40 | Barely hang on |
| 60 | Too valuable to fire |
| 70 | Diminishing returns — quantity holds, quality drops |

**Career-change agencies**
| Type | Who pays | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| Permanent employment agency | Employer | Most in-industry moves |
| Headhunter | Employer | Specialized/senior roles |
| Agency you retain | You | Rare; only when you need an advocate |

## Thresholds & defaults
- **25+** items per sheet in the People/Data/Things dig, unedited — then winnow to **8–10**.
- **3+** five-part achievement stories.
- **10 seconds** for a rehearsed self-introduction; **10 minutes** max for an informational interview unless extended.
- **~150** — Dunbar's ceiling on people you truly know; beyond that, connections have indirect value only.
- Show up **10–15 minutes early**, every day.
- Two to three of the **nine career components** change in even a radical career switch.
- **One** failure to share credit can undo months of goodwill.

## Tells & smells
**Your company is in trouble**: stars leaving while back-benchers stay · the strategy stops making sense · lagging the industry · routinely missing numbers · repeat layoffs · cash out > cash in · management communicating in jargon.

**You're the target**: lost your boss · lost the space race (office → cube → basement) · a paper trail starts · frozen out of memos and meetings · handed scut work or doomed missions · merger or sale.

**You're under assault**: a less-qualified colleague promoted · people stop asking your advice · unwarranted criticism in meetings · you're not invited · you're gossip · people ask "is anything wrong?" · you're scapegoated · friends cool · you can't assemble a team anymore · emails go unanswered.

**They're a passive aggressor**: "Don't worry, I'm on it." It won't get done. Rely on it.

**You're the problem**: "I'm not a team player" (toxic, unlike "out-of-the-box thinker") · "That's not my job" · "There's no hurry" · "I'm working till I drop" · "I know my job" · "I'm efficient" (≠ effective) · "I did it, I did it" · "Modesty forbids me."

**You're a thief** (regardless of how normal it seems): office supplies taken home · padded expense reports · billing the estimated six hours for four hours' work.

## Postures to hold by default
- **Fit in while standing out.** Teamwork is a stage with more than one spotlight, not a mass grave.
- **Be task oriented, not time defined.**
- **Wear the smile as discipline** — Patton practiced his scowl; you practice your war face. Never walk through the open door to complain.
- **Always be looking for a job**, even when secure.
- **Your commitment is to the firm, not the position you occupy** — and your deepest loyalty is to yourself.
