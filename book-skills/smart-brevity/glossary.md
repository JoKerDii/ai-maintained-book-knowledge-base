# Glossary — Smart Brevity

**Always be closing** — End a presentation by explicitly asking for the outcome you want; it's your final slide (Ch 13).

**Audience First** — The manifesto's opening move: picture one specific reader and serve their needs, not your ego (Ch 4).

**Axiom** — A bolded lead-in signpost ("Why it matters," "The big picture") that gives skimmers instant context; the sharpened nut graf (Ch 8).

**Axios** — Greek for "worthy"; the media company built on Smart Brevity (Ch 3, 8).

**Axios HQ** — The AI tool that teaches, guides, and scores Smart Brevity writing (Ch 2, 16).

**Big Thought** — A speech's single, word-for-word memorable point; the spoken lede (Ch 13).

**BLUF** — "Bottom Line Up Front," the military's version of Smart Brevity; everyone reads it, few read the memo (Ch 12).

**Brevity is confidence. Length is fear.** — The Axios newsroom maxim (Ch 3).

**Continuous partial attention** — Linda Stone's term: readers looking at your words while thinking about the next alert (Ch 5).

**Core 4** — Smart Brevity's four written parts: muscular tease, strong first sentence, "Why it matters," "Go deeper" (Ch 2).

**Elegant efficiency** — The cleanest, sharpest, most pleasing way to display something (Ch 9, 14).

**5 Big Things** — Axios's weekly, in-order-of-importance leadership newsletter, cascaded to every team (Ch 15).

**Foggy words** — "Could/may/might" and other vague terms that tell the reader nothing; replace with what's actually happening (Ch 10).

**Go deeper** — Core 4 part 4: an optional link to more depth; signals "I've done the work so you don't have to" (Ch 2, 9).

**Golden Rule of Bullets** — Split any 3+ related points or data into bullets; nobody wants to stare at a clump (Ch 9).

**Hierarchy** — Assigning importance to visual/text elements via scale, color, contrast to guide the eye (Ch 9, 14).

**Journalese** — Words no human would say (posit, obfuscate, ubiquitous); kill them (Ch 10).

**Kick-outs** — Anything that loses readers; top four: too much text, jargon, choices, long video (Ch 5).

**Lede** — The strong first sentence (Core 4 part 2); tell me something I don't know (Ch 2, 7).

**Literary handcuffs** — Imposed structural discipline (e.g., 10 numbered items) that halves word count and frees writer and reader (Ch 11).

**Muscular tease** — Core 4 part 1: six-or-fewer strong words to yank attention (Ch 2, 6).

**Nut graf** — Journalism's line telling you why you're reading; Axios pulled it up and bolded it as an Axiom (Ch 3, 8).

**1 big thing** — The lead newsletter item, signaling the single most important thing; paired with "1 fun thing" at the end (Ch 11).

**ONE Big Thing** — The one thing you want people to know, in one strong sentence (Ch 7).

**Short, not shallow** — Cut length without cutting essential facts, nuance, or depth (Ch 1, 2).

**Simplify to exaggerate** — Fewer words/slides/visuals; destroy anything that distracts from the essential point (Ch 13).

**Smart Brevity** — A system for creating, sharing, and consuming information: saying more with less (Ch 2).

**Smart Brevity count / Score** — Read-time estimate shown up top (~265 wpm); Axios HQ's numerical writing score (Ch 11, 16).

**Smart Jazz** — The Core 4 are guardrails, not unbreakable rules; deviate for the audience (Ch 2).

**Strong words** — Short, concrete, visible words (one-syllable nouns/verbs) that strike like lightning (Ch 10).

**Then stop.** — Say your point, then quit; the move that trips everyone up (Ch 4).

**Time on text** — Ronald Yaros's finding: ~26 seconds spent on a typical piece of content (Ch 5).

**Weak words** — Abstract or fancy "10-dollar" words; if you wouldn't say it at a bar, kill it (Ch 10).

**Why it matters** — The most common, effective Axiom; the context (Core 4 part 3) (Ch 2, 8).

**You are the chef** — Curate the reader's plate; selection is half the "smart" (Ch 11).
