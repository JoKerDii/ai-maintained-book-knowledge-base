## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**Smart Brevity's Core 4** — the system, in order:
1. **A muscular "tease"** — 6 or fewer strong words to yank attention (subject line/headline/tweet).
2. **One strong first sentence ("the lede")** — tell me something I don't know; one sentence.
3. **Context ("Why it matters")** — a bolded Axiom, 1–2 sentences on significance.
4. **The choice to "Go deeper"** — an optional link to more depth.
Do it on one phone screen. These are guardrails, not unbreakable rules ("Smart Jazz").

**Axioms** — the bolded lead-in signposts that guide skimmers (the sharpened "nut graf"). The workhorse is **Why it matters**. Menu: *The big picture · Between the lines · By the numbers · The bottom line · Go deeper · What's next · Reality check · Catch up quick · Zoom in/out · The backstory.* Swap weak framing ("In conclusion") for a strong Axiom ("The bottom line").

**Audience First** — the manifesto's opening words. Picture ONE specific reader; decide the ONE thing they must remember; write that first; then **stop.** Talk it out loud, then type. "Cowards hide in clauses."

**Be Worthy** — respect the reader's time (they give ~26 seconds — "time on text"). List points by importance, whittle to one or two, bullet the rest, then delete, delete, delete. Every word shaved is a gift.

**The Strong-Word Model** — one-syllable > two > three; strong words are concrete and visible; purge fancy "10-dollar" words and journalese; kill foggy could/may/might; active verbs always ("Who—doing—what"). "Tell me a story. Don't tell me about a story."

**Format for skimmers** — Golden Rule of Bullets (split 3+ points); bold what must be seen; 2–3 sentences per paragraph; when in doubt, just stop.

**In practice** — Newsletters (lead with "1 big thing," ≤200 words/item, <1,000 total, end with "1 fun thing"); Email (ask in subject + first sentence); Meetings (20–25 min, objective + 3-bullet agenda, summarize takeaways); Speeches (one Big Thought, ≤15 words, announced and restated); Presentations (one message per slide, pictures over text, always be closing); Social (one thing, give value, match the platform).

**Four guiding principles** — Authority (*be the expert, or find one*), Brevity (*short, not shallow*), Humanity (*write like you speak*), Clarity (*style text for impact*).

**Founding maxim** — "Brevity is confidence. Length is fear." Trust the data over the voice in your head: even elite readers want fewer words (only ~5% valued longer stories).

> Note: This book does not use a "MEE" acronym; its core method is the Core 4 and Axioms above.
