# Patterns — Smart Brevity Techniques

## The Core 4
**When to use**: Any one-to-many written communication.
**How**: (1) Muscular tease — 6 words or fewer. (2) One strong first sentence (lede). (3) "Why it matters" context. (4) "Go deeper" option. Fit it on one phone screen.
**Trade-offs**: Not for every circumstance (Smart Jazz); rigid application can feel curt — add human voice back.

## The Muscular Tease
**When to use**: Subject lines, headlines, tweets, openers.
**How**: Cap at 6 words; kill funny/ironic/cryptic; write the reason you're writing in ≤10 words; provocative yet accurate; active verbs; read aloud. Test: "Would you read it if you hadn't written it?"
**Trade-offs**: Provocation must stay accurate; a hot name/brand or emoji boosts opens but can look gimmicky if overused.

## The One-Sentence Lede
**When to use**: The opening line of any message.
**How**: Boil to your one most important point; skip anecdotes/jokes; one sentence; don't repeat the tease; cut adverbs/weak words. Trick: tell a friend what happened — that's your first sentence.
**Trade-offs**: Demands you actually know your point first ("Think, then type").

## The Axiom ("Why it matters")
**When to use**: Right after the lede, and as section signposts.
**How**: Bold "Why it matters:"; 1–2 sentences of significance (what changes, what it signals, larger context); direct, declarative, NOT redundant with the lede. Swap weak framing for strong Axioms.
**Trade-offs**: A lazy Axiom that restates the lede wastes the slot.

## Go Deeper
**When to use**: The parting line of an item.
**How**: Type "Go deeper," link to source/video/data. Most won't click — that's fine; it signals rigor and respect.
**Trade-offs**: Keeps the body short but requires you to have real depth to link to.

## The Strong-Word Model
**When to use**: Sharpening any sentence.
**How**: One-syllable > two > three; strong = concrete/visible; purge fancy + journalese; kill foggy "could/may/might"; active verbs (Who—doing—what); replace words with one-fewer-syllable versions.
**Trade-offs**: Over-simplifying can drop needed precision — keep essential nuance.

## The Bullet + Bold Format
**When to use**: Any body text a skimmer will scan.
**How**: Split 3+ related points into bullets; bold the word/figure/Axiom you most want seen; 2–3 sentences per paragraph, tops; mix bold/bullets/charts/Axioms.
**Trade-offs**: Overuse of bold dilutes emphasis.

## The "1 big thing" Newsletter
**When to use**: Recurring team/company/group updates.
**How**: Punchy name; state read time; lead with "1 big thing:"; number 5–10 items in order of importance; ≤200 words each; total <1,000; relevant image; end with "1 fun thing."
**Trade-offs**: Cadence is a commitment — skipping breaks the habit.

## Smart Brevity Email
**When to use**: Work email.
**How**: Urgent 6-word subject; news/ask in first sentence; "Why it matters" context; bullets; bold key figures/names; clean visual.
**Trade-offs**: None if you know your ask; fails if you bury it.

## Smart Brevity Meeting
**When to use**: Any meeting.
**How**: Confirm you need it; email objective (1 sentence) + agenda (3 bullets) the night before; 20–25 min limit; open with objective, then "Why it matters," then decisions; guide/interrupt tangents; be inclusive; at 2 min left summarize takeaways + owners; send bulleted follow-up.
**Trade-offs**: Requires a disciplined facilitator; start exactly on time.

## Smart Brevity Speech
**When to use**: Speeches, toasts, talks.
**How**: Authentic delivery (ditch crutches); short opening story ending in a chuckle; distill ONE Big Thought (≤15 words, word-for-word); announce it ("The one thing I want you to remember…"); verbal "Why it matters"; numbered supporting stats/stories; restate at end; say thank you. Or use Duarte's what is / what could be arc.
**Trade-offs**: One anecdote max — more kills momentum.

## Smart Brevity Presentation
**When to use**: Slide decks.
**How**: Write the outcome + 3–5 points; sharpen the ask to ~6 words; one message per slide (3-second billboard); pictures over text; McKinsey rule (for 20 slides you want, use 2); "always be closing" final slide.
**Trade-offs**: Cutting 90% of slides feels risky but improves recall.

## Smart Brevity Social
**When to use**: Twitter/Instagram/Facebook.
**How**: One thing per post; match register to platform; pair muscular text with an arresting image; give value rather than ask for a click.
**Trade-offs**: Platforms ration reach — forces brutal selectivity.

## Smart Brevity Visuals
**When to use**: Art, charts, illustrations.
**How**: Strong concept → edit out superfluous elements → judge from viewer's perspective; build hierarchy with scale/color/contrast; check legibility and fresh-eyes clarity.
**Trade-offs**: Good hierarchy is invisible; poor hierarchy distracts.

## The Take-It-for-a-Spin Drill
**When to use**: Converting any update to Smart Brevity (~2 min).
**How**: Define audience (WHO/WHAT/WHY) → 6-word headline → one-sentence "what's new" → bold "Why it matters:" → build your own Axioms → bullet the data → review (Accurate, Cohesive, Human).
**Trade-offs**: If the result feels curt, add voice back — the "Human" check.

## The Sacca Rewrite
**When to use**: Any long email you've already written.
**How**: Write the whole thing, then make the first 2–3 sentences say everything below — often the only part read.
**Trade-offs**: Front-loading requires you to know your conclusion.
