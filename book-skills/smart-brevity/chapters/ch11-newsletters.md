# Chapter 11: The Newsletter (Mike's Playbook + Art of the Newsletter)

## Core Idea
The Smart Brevity newsletter — numbered items, each in the Core 4 — is the single best way to communicate multiple important things and demand to be read.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The "1 big thing" newsletter format**:
  - When to use: Weekly team/company/group updates; recurring one-to-many comms.
  - How: (1) Give it a punchy one-or-two-word name. (2) State the read time up top (a "Smart Brevity count"; ~265 words/minute). (3) Lead with **"1 big thing:"** + a tight headline. (4) Write a few more items in order of importance. (5) Number them; say how many up front (5–10 ideal; >1,200 words is a book — trim; under 1,000 ideal). (6) Keep each item to 200 words, tops (big drop-off after 200). (7) Use a relevant image. (8) End with **"1 fun thing"** or "1 smile to go."
- **Mike's tricks**: You are the chef (pare the selection). Brevity is confidence. Being selfless is selfishly beneficial. Gamify it (a "word diet").

## Key Concepts
- **You are the chef**: Part of the "smart" is selection — don't make readers pick what's important; you pick.
- **Literary handcuffs**: Jim's discipline on Playbook — just 10 numbered items — cut Mike's word count in half and freed both writer and reader.
- **FOMO on your side**: A good newsletter makes people afraid to miss it (bury a wedding announcement at the end).
- **Smart Brevity Score**: Axios HQ scores each write-up to measure progress.

## Mental Models
- Writing is a **stacked buffet** — you choose what goes on the reader's plate.
- Trimming is a **word diet**: never easy, requires discipline, but yields a healthier, better-looking result.

## Anti-patterns
- **A hot mess that asks too much**: Old Playbook ran thousands of words, vaguely organized, no sense of what mattered.
- **Spraying graffiti**: Ignoring font, size, and layout so the package is unpleasant to the eye.

## Worked Example
**Mike's Big 6.** One slow-news Easter weekend, Mike said "no one's going to complain if their newsletter isn't long enough" and sent "Mike's Big 6" instead of his usual Top 10. When he returned to the normal format Monday, readers emailed: "Where can I sign up for the Big 6 version?" Humbling — and telling.

**Origins:** Mike's daily "How we can rock today" email to his Politico bosses became Politico Playbook after a third subscriber (Howard Wolfson) asked "Can I get it?" — and spread like wildfire. The NYT Magazine later called Mike "The Man the White House Wakes Up To."

## Key Takeaways
1. Lead with "1 big thing"; end with "1 fun thing."
2. Number items (5–10) and state read time up front.
3. Cap each item at 200 words; total under 1,000.
4. Selection is half the "smart" — curate ruthlessly.
5. Consistency + cadence builds a habit readers won't want to miss.
