# Chapter 8: Why It Matters — The Axioms

## Core Idea
After the lede, tell the reader why it matters — via a bolded signpost the authors call an "Axiom" that gives skimmers instant context.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Why It Matters** (Core 4, part 3):
  - When to use: Immediately after your first sentence.
  - How: (1) "Why it matters" is the most common, effective Axiom — bold it. (2) In one or two sentences, explain the significance: What will it change? What does it signal? What's the larger context? (3) Make it direct and declarative, NOT redundant with the lede. (4) Read lede + Axiom aloud — if that's all they hear, do they get the gist?
- **Axioms** (bolded lead-in signposts): "The name we've given the bolded 'Why it matters' headings" — crystal-clear street signs for skimmers.
  - How: Make them strong. Weak "Here's what's important to know" → strong **Why it matters**. Weak "A trend we've been observing" → **The big picture**. Weak "Let's take a look at the data" → **By the numbers**. Weak "In conclusion" → **The bottom line**.

## Key Concepts
- **The nut graf, sharpened**: Axioms are the stolen-and-improved journalism device (the line telling you why you're reading) — pulled to the top and bolded.
- **Axios = "worthy"** (Greek): the company is named for the idea of axioms — worthy of your time, trust, attention.
- **Street signs for the mind**: Axioms tell the reader where they are and where they're going, cueing a decide-to-continue moment.

## Mental Models
- Think of each Axiom as a **billboard exit sign** — a skimmer's eye lands on it and instantly knows the section's job.
- **Bold = "pay attention to this"**: darker and more detectable than italics.

## Anti-patterns
- **Weak framing**: Throat-clearing lead-ins ("Here's what's important to know…") instead of a sharp Axiom.
- **Redundant Axioms**: A "Why it matters" that just restates the lede adds nothing.

## Worked Example
Chief-of-staff resignation email:
- **BEFORE** — Subject: "Staff update to share with you when you're free…" then: "Sorry to bother you, but as you know, Janet Small has been doing a terrific job… she just informed me she'll be quitting… Ugh. Big blow… I guess I can step in…"
- **AFTER** — Subject: "Our chief of staff quit"
  - Lede: "Janet Small just informed me she's leaving in two weeks for a new job at our chief competitor."
  - **WHY IT MATTERS**: "Janet's leading two of our three most important strategic projects. I will step in to handle things while we quickly find her replacement."

## Favorite Axioms (menu)
The big picture · What's next · What we're watching · What we're hearing · Between the lines · The backstory · Catch up quick · Zoom in · Zoom out · By the numbers · The bottom line · Reality check · The other side · Go deeper

## Key Takeaways
1. Bold "Why it matters" and follow it with 1–2 sentences of significance.
2. Never make the Axiom redundant with the lede.
3. Read headline + lede + Axiom together — do they convey what matters most?
4. Build your own Axioms to fit your brand/voice.
5. "200 words done right can do more than most people do with 20,000."
