# Chapter 15: Run a Company on Smart Brevity + Communicate Inclusively

## Core Idea
Run an organization on transparent, crisp, weekly Smart Brevity updates — and make that communication inclusive, because if you're not communicating inclusively, you're not communicating effectively.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **5 Big Things** (company operating cadence):
  - When to use: Aligning a scattered/remote organization.
  - How: A weekly Smart Brevity newsletter detailing, in order of importance, what leadership is thinking/doing. Then every executive writes the same for their team and shares it with fellow execs — so everyone knows every big thing weekly. Eliminates canned status updates in meetings; frees 1:1s for real problems.
- **Jim's tips for CEOs/leaders**: (1) Mission matters — pull items back to purpose ("Why it matters" is perfect); repeat until it annoys you. (2) Tell a story — a year of newsletters should narrate what you were doing. (3) Don't be a fraud — write with candor. (4) Don't quit — connect weekly, no excuses. (5) Be humble — admit mistakes, poke fun at yourself. (6) Encourage copycats.
- **Inclusive writing best practices** (Roy's tips): Write in plain, clear language; use bullet points; keep it simple and short. Plus: be specific about identity (ask how people identify); cut stereotype-reinforcing descriptors; dig deeper on photo choices; **swap one identity for another** to test if the language stays nonjudgmental; omit irrelevant race/ethnicity/religion/disability details.

## Key Concepts
- **Communicator, not operator**: Your right-hand person should be a communicator — comms failures can cripple an organization.
- **Transparency kills gossip**: Crisp, transparent communication cuts the pot-stirring that comes from confusion.
- **Accessible by design**: Smart Brevity is direct and stripped-down — it helps people with dyslexia and non-native English readers (1 in 5 kids report learning disabilities; ~65M Americans).

## Mental Models
- **We're all communicators now** — be good at it or find someone who is.
- Inclusion is a **blind-spot audit**: know you have them, then run sensitive writing by someone with different life experience.

## Anti-patterns
- **Scoffing at culture/comms**: Early Politico did — high attrition earned it "a punishing place to work" (The New Republic).
- **Lawyered, constipated corporate nonsense**: People sniff out inauthenticity.
- **Offend me or confuse me and you've lost me** — not just this once, but for good.

## Worked Example
**GRAIL's The Rundown** (Matt Burns): To kill ad-hoc internal emails, Burns launched a weekly ~1,400-word (5-minute) newsletter — "It's Friday, GRAILERers!" — landing in 750+ inboxes like clockwork. Random internal emails were opened by <1/3 of recipients; the dependable newsletter hit ~90% open rates. Colleagues now lobby to get items included.

**Axios in-house newsletters**: Lights On (revenue), Cranes (Local), The TopLine (sales) — giving winners a forum, killing silos, and acting as an early-warning system so 1:1s start already caught up.

## Key Takeaways
1. Run the org on a weekly "5 Big Things" cascade, top to bottom.
2. Use "Why it matters" to keep hammering the mission.
3. Transparency plus brevity beats gossip and confusion.
4. Plain language + bullets is inherently more inclusive.
5. Swap-test identity language; omit irrelevant descriptors.
