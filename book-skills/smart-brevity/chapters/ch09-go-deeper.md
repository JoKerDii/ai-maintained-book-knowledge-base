# Chapter 9: Go Deeper — The Choice & the Format

## Core Idea
End with an optional "Go deeper" that lets readers choose depth, and package everything with bullets and bold so skimmers can scan.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Go Deeper** (Core 4, part 4):
  - When to use: As the parting line of a Smart Brevity item.
  - How: Type "Go deeper," then link to source material — an article, video, podcast, bio, map, poll crosstabs — anything for the reader who wants the rabbit hole.
  - Why it works: Most won't click. But offering it signals "I've done the work so you don't have to," showing thoroughness and respect.
- **The formatting toolkit**:
  - **Axioms rock**: bolded signposts pull attention and show direction.
  - **The Golden Rule of Bullets**: nobody wants to stare at a clump — split 3+ related points/data into bullets.
  - **Be bold**: bold the word/figure/Axiom you want to stand out.
  - **Mix it up**: two-to-three sentences per paragraph, tops; break up flow with bold, bullets, charts, Axioms.
  - **Just stop**: overtalking/overwriting is the biggest time-waster.

## Key Concepts
- **Depth without a word-glob**: "Go deeper" delivers nuance and context without losing the audience.
- **Elegant efficiency / hierarchy**: Bolding and bullets create a visual hierarchy the eye can scan.
- **Silence is communication**: "The most useful communication is often silence, grasshopper."

## Mental Models
- Think of "Go deeper" as a **door, not a hallway** — the reader chooses whether to walk through.
- Design for **the skimmer's eye**: bullets and bold are eye-traps that pull the gaze to what matters.

## Anti-patterns
- **Big blobs of bloviation**: Long consecutive paragraphs with no visual breaks.
- **Forcing all context inline**: Dumping every nuance into the body instead of linking it.

## Worked Example
**Mike's autonomous-vehicle item.** A newsletter item posed the ethics of a self-driving car's worst-case choice (hit the person ahead or veer toward the sidewalk?). Mike ended with two "Go deeper" links: the news article that inspired it, and the academic journal underlying that article. The reader's choice: get the big idea fast, read a little more, or dive into the debate's nuances.

**Jamie Dimon's letter.** JPMorgan's 32,000-word annual shareholder letter (longer than this book) was rebuilt by Axios HQ into a Smart Brevity newsletter capturing the key messages — 30,420 words shorter, and it linked to the full letter to "go deeper."

## Key Takeaways
1. Close with "Go deeper" + a link; most won't click, but it signals respect and rigor.
2. Split any 3+ related points into bullets.
3. Bold the Axiom/word/figure you most want seen.
4. Cap paragraphs at 2–3 sentences; break up the flow.
5. When in doubt, just stop.
