# Chapter 16: The Cheat Sheet & Take It for a Spin

## Core Idea
A do-it-yourself walkthrough: four guiding principles plus a fill-in-the-blank drill that turns any update into Smart Brevity in about two minutes.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Four Guiding Principles** (each with a one-line rule):
  1. **Authority** — Be a trustworthy expert who can judge what's new/important. *Be the expert, or find one.*
  2. **Brevity** — Give exactly what busy readers need, without leaving them unfulfilled. *Stay short, not shallow.*
  3. **Humanity** — Communicate with full human emotion, but familiar and conversational. *Write like you speak.*
  4. **Clarity** — Be frugal with words so the result is approachable and scannable. *Style text for impact.*
- **The Take-It-for-a-Spin drill**:
  1. **Define your audience** — WHO is the smart reader? WHAT do they need to know? WHY is it significant?
  2. **Write the HEADLINE** — 6 words or fewer? Clear and specific? Conversational, muscular words?
  3. **Write WHAT'S NEW** — one sentence only? What they must remember? Distinct from the headline?
  4. **Explain significance** — write "Why it matters:" (bold, colon), then one blunt sentence.
  5. **Build your own Axioms** — brainstorm branded versions of What's next / The bottom line / By the numbers / The big picture.
  6. **Break data into bullets.**
  7. **Review** — ACCURATE (didn't lose essential nuance), COHESIVE (still flows), HUMAN (voice intact — if it feels curt, breathe life back in).

## Key Concepts
- **Smart Brevity Score** (Axios HQ): An AI numerical score to optimize writing before sending; plus templates, guidance, collaboration, analytics, and a "living history" library.
- **Next Level Smart Brevity**: Scaling the method across an org with tooling.

## Mental Models
- Treat this as an **entry-level class** — do it a few times and your brain adapts to doing it intuitively.
- The final gut-check is **HUMAN**: over-cutting reads as curt; add a little life back.

## Anti-patterns
- **Over-editing to curt**: The first few times, if it feels clipped, you've gone too far.
- **Losing nuance for brevity's sake**: If a necessary detail got cut, bring it back.

## Worked Example
Fill-in drill applied:
- **BEFORE** — "Update on our work-from-home plans in 2021 / We continue to closely monitor the impact of COVID, and we are writing today to provide an update on our plans for the rest of the year."
- **AFTER** —
  - Headline: "Remote work option extended"
  - What's new: "Everyone will have the option—but not the requirement—to work from home for the rest of 2021."
  - **Why it matters:** [one blunt sentence of significance]

## Key Takeaways
1. Anchor on the four principles: Authority, Brevity, Humanity, Clarity.
2. Run the 6-word headline / one-sentence "what's new" / "Why it matters" drill.
3. Build Axioms in your own voice.
4. Final check: Accurate, Cohesive, Human.
5. Practice a few times — it becomes intuitive, and you'll "be heard again."
