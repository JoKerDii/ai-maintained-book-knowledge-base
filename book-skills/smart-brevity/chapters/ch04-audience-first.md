# Chapter 4: Audience First

## Core Idea
The two most important words in communication are "audience first" — picture one specific person and serve their needs, not your ego.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Audience First** (the Axios manifesto's opening words):
  - When to use: Before you write, speak, or record — every time.
  - How: (1) Picture ONE real, specific person you're trying to reach — a name, a face, a job. (2) Figure out the ONE thing you want them to remember. (3) Tailor the message tightly to them.
- **The just-say-it test**:
  - How: Ask a friend to read/hear your draft, then tell you the one big idea. If they can't spit it back almost verbatim, it's not clear yet.

## Key Concepts
- **Serve, don't self-serve**: We think too much about what we want to say vs. what others need to hear. Reverse it.
- **Then stop.**: The move that trips everyone up — say your point, then quit. "We could end every chapter with 'Then stop!'"
- **Cowards hide in clauses**: We bury feelings (apologies, feedback, asks) in unnecessary words.
- **Write like a human, for humans**: Be conversational; talk it out loud first, then write that.

## Mental Models
- If you try to speak to **everyone, you reach no one** — single out one person to clarify everything.
- **Talk, then type**: You speak more clearly and vividly than you write; capture the spoken version.

## Anti-patterns
- **Dumbing down for the least-informed** (the TV mistake): larding content with general context to reach the broadest audience.
- **Communicating selfishly**: Blurting every thought, over-explaining, hiding insecurity in extra words.

## Worked Example
Before/after rewrites that just say it:
- **Apology** — Bad: "I'm really sorry I said that, but here is what I was thinking… and what you did before that upset me…" → Better: "I'm sincerely sorry I said that."
- **Feedback** — Bad: "You do so many great things, and I know I have my flaws… but I really need you to show a little more effort…" → Better: "Here is one thing you need to improve fast: put more effort into your core tasks."
- **Plans** — Bad: long ramble about crazy life and virus mandates → Better: "Need to shift lunch to the corner bakery. My treat."
- **Work update** — Bad: "we decided after lots of meetings and endless deliberation to…" → Better: "UPDATE: Shrinking Monday meetings to core management team only."

Pastor Glade's nine words: "All you can do is the next right thing." (Could sharpen further: "Do the next right thing.")

## Key Takeaways
1. Focus on ONE person you are targeting.
2. Plot out ONE thing you want them to remember, and write it before anything else.
3. Shorten that one thing to fewer than a dozen words — declarative, not a question.
4. Talk it out loud first; then write it down; then stop.
5. Test comprehension on a real person — it's humbling and useful.
