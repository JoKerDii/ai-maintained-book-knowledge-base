# Chapter 12: Smart Brevity @Work — Email & Meetings

## Core Idea
Nowhere pays off faster than work: Smart Brevity emails and meetings make you visibly the person who values others' time and has something worth saying.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Email done right**:
  - How: (1) Short, direct, urgent subject line (why open NOW). (2) The news or ask in the first sentence, always. (3) Give the "Why it matters" context. (4) Bullets for skimmers and close readers. (5) Bold the words/figures/names you want seen. (6) Clean, intuitive visuals to amplify a point.
- **Smart Brevity meetings**:
  - **Pregame**: Confirm you need a meeting; the caller sets an objective (one sentence) + agenda (three bullets, max) in an email the night before; outline the decisions/actions.
  - **During**: (1) Set a time limit — 20 min usually suffices; try 25/50 (Slack) or 5–10-min micro-meetings. (2) Open with your one-sentence objective (headline). (3) Second sentence = "Why it matters" to this group now. (4) State the specific decisions needed. (5) Guide discussion ("Off-topic!" with a smile). (6) Be inclusive — draw out the quiet. (7) At 2 minutes left, summarize takeaways + next steps.
  - **After**: Send a bulleted follow-up while it's fresh.

## Key Concepts
- **BLUF**: "Bottom Line Up Front" — the military's version of Smart Brevity (Geoff Morrell brought it from the Pentagon to BP). Everyone reads the BLUF; few read the memo.
- **The presentation quick-fix**: Start with the big idea; each slide gets a taut headline + a few short bullets (>20 words on a slide → try again); restate the Big Idea; rarely exceed 5–6 slides.
- **Start exactly on time**: Bush had the door locked on a late Karl Rove — who was never late again.

## Mental Models
- A meeting is **won before it starts** — objective and agenda decide its quality.
- The way most emails are written **screams "blow me off"** — Smart Brevity it before hitting send.

## Anti-patterns
- **Chit-chat past start time**: Signals the meeting isn't important — so why hold it?
- **Too many people, too many topics, too much time**: Coworkers won't call it out, but they notice and remember.
- **Ad-hoc email overload**: 50 emails/day (2005) → 126 (2019, Cal Newport).

## Worked Example
**Morrell at BP.** He wanted his own BLUF, so he had Axios teach his team Smart Brevity and launched an internal newsletter, ITK ("In the Know"), to align management. It spread companywide; 500+ BP communicators were trained; open rates spiked. His five lessons: Smart Brevity is teachable, forces you to write like a normal person, can be cool, is contagious ("3 things you need to know" memos appeared), and is versatile (now in performance reviews and safety briefings).

## Key Takeaways
1. Put the ask/news in the subject line and first sentence.
2. 70% of employees want shorter work communications (Gallup); only ~half read leaders' notes.
3. Default meetings to 20–25 minutes with a one-sentence objective.
4. End meetings by summarizing takeaways and owners.
5. Send weekly updates Sunday/early Monday morning to maximize opens.
