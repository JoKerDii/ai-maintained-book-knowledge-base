# Chapter 14: Social Media & Visuals

## Core Idea
Social is hand-to-hand attention combat — you get one thing across in a blink — and visuals win when they establish clear hierarchy and say the point at a glance.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Smart Brevity for social**:
  - How: (1) Know your platform — Twitter likes facts/data/hot quotes/news breaks; Instagram wants an eye-catching image + slim muscular text; Facebook rewards a provocative spin. (2) Be image-conscious — clean, simple, arresting; words without art lose everywhere. (3) Bust out strong words + emojis. (4) Give something (an idea, score, laugh) rather than asking for a click — the algorithms reward giving.
  - The "one thing": On social you'll ONLY tell them one thing — tease a nugget, provoke with a quote, wow with a data point.
- **Smart Brevity visuals** (Sarah Grillo): Start with a strong concept → edit out superfluous elements → always judge from the viewer's perspective.
  - Establish **hierarchy** through scale, color, and contrast to guide the eye.
  - Ask: Does the concept make sense to fresh eyes? Are all elements legible? Does the layout express the content?

## Key Concepts
- **Elegant efficiency**: The cleanest, sharpest, most pleasing way to display something.
- **Hierarchy**: Assigning importance to visual elements so the eye lands where you want — the design twin of Smart Brevity writing. Good hierarchy goes unnoticed; poor hierarchy draws unwanted attention.
- **Ruthless rationing**: Platforms ration what gets seen, forcing selectivity.

## Mental Models
- A social feed is **Darwinian** — the blink of an eye, not a few seconds.
- A strong illustration can tell the story **in zero words** — the headline may be unnecessary.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ask-posts**: "Click, buy, do something" underperforms "here's something for you."
- **Tiny type / mixed messaging**: Cluttered visuals with unclear headlines.
- **Superfluous elements**: Anything that dilutes the concept or muddies hierarchy.

## Worked Example
- **Moon's wet.** A+ tweet: "🌙 –> Moon's wet." Blah tweet (the story's lede): "There is water on the moon's surface, and ice may be widespread in its many shadows, according to a pair of studies published Monday in the journal Nature Astronomy."
- **The elephant illustration** ("Texas Republicans Admit There's a Problem"): First version — an elephant waving a tiny Texas flag; the eye goes to the head, the flag is an afterthought, too small to see. Fix — scale up and crop out most of the elephant's body (the superfluous element); now there's balance, stronger hierarchy, and negative space that lets the flag read.

## Key Takeaways
1. One idea per post; give value instead of asking.
2. Match the register to the platform (Twitter facts, Insta image, Facebook provocation).
3. Pair muscular text with a clean, arresting image.
4. In visuals, build hierarchy with scale/color/contrast and cut the superfluous.
5. Always judge the work through the viewer's eyes.
