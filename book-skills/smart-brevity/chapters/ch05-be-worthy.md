# Chapter 5: Be Worthy

## Core Idea
People reward you when you respect their time and intelligence; be worthy of the seconds they give you by delivering only what's essential.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Be Worthy** (prioritize ruthlessly before sending):
  - When to use: Drafting any update, email, or message.
  - How: (1) List the points you must make, in order of importance — the first is most likely to stick. (2) Whittle to one or two; the rest become bullets, not blobs. (3) Gut-check each: Is it essential? Is there a simpler way? (4) Delete, delete, delete.
- **The Sacca test** (Chris Sacca): Write the whole email, then go back and make the first two-to-three sentences say everything below — often the only part that gets read.

## Key Concepts
- **Time on text**: Prof. Ronald Yaros's eye-tracking finding — people spend ~26 seconds on a story/update; anything after is usually wasted.
- **Continuous partial attention** (Linda Stone): Even when looking at your words, readers are thinking about the next alert — not truly reading.
- **Kick-outs**: Anything that loses readers. Top four culprits: too much text, too much jargon, too many choices, long video. Common thread: less is more.
- **"You pick"**: Since people remember at most one thing, don't make them choose — you choose the one thing.

## Mental Models
- Every word you shave is **a gift** — it saves the other person time.
- Respect is **reciprocal**: honor their time and they'll welcome your ideas; waste it and they'll find you annoying "for good."

## Anti-patterns
- **Busywork that costs money** (Megan Green, realtor): "Hi, hope you're having a good day" filler instead of "Here's how you set up your utilities."
- **Making the reader deduce your point**: Burying the one worthwhile paragraph deep in 1,200 words.

## Worked Example
Before/after — a youth soccer email:
- **BEFORE**: "The FIRST soccer tournament, the first of our year, will be held in Springfield, at the SoccerPlex, where our boys will kick off another great year together under Coach Smith—and hopefully win our first championship. The boys should arrive at 1 pm and bring food, water, etc., but I guess you probably know that since this is not our first tournament together. Thank you and Go RedDogs!!!"
- **AFTER**: "The FIRST soccer tournament will be held in Springfield, at the SoccerPlex. The boys should arrive at 1 pm. Go RedDogs!"

## Key Takeaways
1. Order your points by importance; lead with the stickiest.
2. Whittle to one or two must-make points; bullet the rest.
3. Gut-check every point for essentialness and simplicity.
4. Delete, delete, delete — then send.
5. In a world of noise, respecting time is a universal way to win favor.
