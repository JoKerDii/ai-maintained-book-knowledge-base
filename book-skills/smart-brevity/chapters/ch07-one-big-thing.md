# Chapter 7: ONE Big Thing — The Strong First Sentence

## Core Idea
If you take one thing from the book: identify and trumpet the ONE thing you want people to know, in ONE strong sentence — the "lede."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **ONE Big Thing / the lede** (Core 4, part 2):
  - When to use: The opening sentence of any email, note, story, or update.
  - How: (1) Boil down your most important point, audience in mind. (2) Skip anecdotes, jokes, showing off. (3) Stick to one sentence. (4) Don't repeat the tease verbatim. (5) Hack off adverbs and weak/extraneous words. (6) Ask: if this is the ONLY thing they see, is it exactly what you want to stick? Then move on.
- **The two reader questions**: Skimmers are answering "What the hell is this?" and "Is it worth my time?" — your lede must answer both.

## Key Concepts
- **The elevator shout**: If they were headed out the door, what's the one thing you'd shout and hope they don't forget? That's your opening sentence.
- **Tell-someone tip** (Mike): After an interview or event, call a friend/editor and tell them what happened — that's your first sentence, every single time.
- **"Just tell me something I don't f@$&ing know"** (John Bresnahan): what every first sentence must do.

## Mental Models
- Your brain **already knows the most interesting thing** — then typing makes it foggy. Say it the way you'd blurt it.
- The first sentence is your **one and likely only chance** — after a few seconds you lose the reader to a dozen other tabs.

## Anti-patterns
- **Making it complex, foggy, forgettable**: Over-crafting the natural, sharp thought into mush.
- **Burying the lede / repeating the tease**: Restating the headline instead of advancing.

## Worked Example
Before/after ledes:
- Party — Bad: "I know you are busy and have so much on your plate, but I was hoping to let you know that I am throwing a party and hoping to get a live band…" → "I'm throwing an epic bash with a live band."
- News — "President Joe Biden is relying on longtime advisers to guide him through tough foreign policy and domestic crises, and some Democrats worry…" → "Joe Biden is running the White House like his nemesis George W. Bush did: a small, secretive, like-minded oligarchy."
- Raise — "I have been here three years and work very hard and have this new house and car I need to finance and want to discuss, um, the possibility…" → "I know my value and want to discuss a raise."
- Assignment — long apology about shifting Teddy Roosevelt focus → "I will focus exclusively on Teddy Roosevelt's leadership style and deliver the assignment on Sunday."

## Key Takeaways
1. One sentence. Most memorable. New, wanted, or needed.
2. Answer both reader questions immediately.
3. Don't restate the tease; advance the story.
4. Write it as you'd say it to a friend on the elevator.
5. If this is all they read, it must be exactly what you want to stick.
