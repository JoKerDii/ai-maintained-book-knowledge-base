# Chapter 6: Grab Me! — The Muscular Tease

## Core Idea
The most important words you write are the subject line, headline, or first line — six or fewer strong words that win the split-second yes/no decision to keep reading.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Muscular Tease** (Core 4, part 1):
  - When to use: Every subject line, headline, tweet, or opening.
  - How: (1) Start by stopping — limit to 6 words, tops; stop being funny/ironic/cryptic; stop using SAT or business-speak. (2) In 10 words or less, write the reason you're writing at all. (3) Make it provocative yet accurate. (4) Short words are strong words; active verbs ALWAYS. (5) Read it aloud.
- **The would-you-read-it test**: "Would you read it if you hadn't written it?" The foolproof check for a good grabber.

## Key Concepts
- **6 words is optimal**: Axios's audience team found ~6 words is the optimal email subject line — short enough to show fully on a phone.
- **The decision point**: Headlines are bigger/darker because they're where the reader decides to click or scroll.
- **The "Hey listen" of Smart Brevity**: The tease says "I have something important, and I'll say it in a way worthy of your time."
- **Yes-or-no brain**: The brain makes a fast fight-or-flight, click-or-scroll call; a great word buys a few more seconds.

## Mental Models
- Treat everything you write like **a New York Times headline**: accurate, yet newsy enough to pull a reader in.
- Serving a great thought under a weak tease is **serving a gourmet meal in a dog bowl**.

## Anti-patterns
- **Timid, long-winded openers**: "Stop losing your reader at 'Hello.'"
- **Clever over clear**: Funny/ironic/cryptic teases confuse rather than entice.

## Worked Example
Before/after teases:
- Weekend plans — Bad: "Hey, there are some new plans for the weekend to discuss re: birthday party" → "🎉 New plan: trampoline park"
- Board update — Bad: "Board of Directors Update" → "We wowed our Board"
- Headline — "The coronavirus variant in California is possibly more infectious…" → "California COVID-19 strain is more infectious than the first"
- Headline — "Health-care jobs will be able to keep the US labor market growing—even if we see a recession" → "Health-care hiring is recession-proof"
- Subject — "Some follow-ups for Monday to discuss later today at meeting" → "TWO important updates"
- Tweet — "This is a great story and you should read it. Click below." → "SCOOP: Musk's next move."
- "Low-waste economy hits its groove" (lost me) vs. "Start-ups mining cash from trash" (got me).

## Key Takeaways
1. Cap the tease at 6 words.
2. Active verbs, one-syllable words, real and specific.
3. A hot name/brand in the subject line (Ferry, Buffett, Nike) boosts opens.
4. An emoji in a subject line makes it pop in a crowded inbox.
5. Apply the "would you read it?" test before sending.
