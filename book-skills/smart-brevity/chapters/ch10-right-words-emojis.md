# Chapter 10: The Right Words (and Emojis)

## Core Idea
Word choice is the difference between the lightning and the lightning bug: short, strong, concrete, active words strike; soft, foggy, fancy ones annoy.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Strong-Word Model**:
  - When to use: Writing or sharpening any sentence, especially teases and ledes.
  - How: (1) **Shorter is better** — one-syllable > two-syllable > three-syllable. (2) **Deploy STRONG words** — vivid, precise, something you can see/touch/photograph (fire, boat, chop, crush). (3) **Purge WEAK words** — if you wouldn't say it at a bar or the beach, kill it. (4) **Avoid foggy words** — "could/may/might" tell you nothing; say what is planned/considered/feared/expected. (5) **Use active verbs** — "Who—doing—what." (6) **Embrace strong phrases** — short, crisp, punchy. (7) **Check yourself** — replace any word with a one-fewer-syllable version.
- **Emojis** (used sparingly): Signal tone/topic instantly, save time, and make subject lines pop in the inbox — but overuse looks silly.

## Key Concepts
- **"Elongated yellow fruit" problem**: You'd never call a banana that — yet we do it constantly when writing.
- **10-dollar / spelling-bee words**: Fancy words meant to sound smart that make you sound like an ass (prevaricate → lie; conundrum → jam; elucidate → explain).
- **Journalese**: Words no human would say (posit → assume; obfuscate → hide; ubiquitous → everywhere).
- **"Tell me a story. Don't tell me about a story."**: The bottom line on active vs. passive.

## Mental Models
- Aim to **strike like lightning, not annoy like bugs** (Mark Twain: right word vs. almost-right word).
- **Subject. Verb. Object.**: Smart, taut writing is linear, not twisty.

## Anti-patterns
- **Business-speak**: "price point" for price; "core competency" for skill.
- **Passive fog**: "The situation in Afghanistan continues to deteriorate from a security perspective" vs. active "The Taliban captured Afghanistan."
- **Emoji abuse**: Too many and you look like "your old man in designer skinny jeans."

## Worked Example
- Weather — Never: "With record temperatures besetting the West and South… I'm going to avail myself of the nearby air conditioning." Instead: "It's hot. I'm going inside."
- Strong two-word phrases: "Japan surrenders." "Sales cratered." "Revenue boomed." "I quit." "Cubs lost." (Biblical model: "Jesus wept.")
- Substitutions: retribution → revenge.
- Paula LaRocque's fish passage: every word was one syllable — the power was in the simplicity.
- Emoji-as-headline: "📊 Data of the day" tells you instantly what's coming; a 🚨 before a breaking alert juices open rates.

## Key Takeaways
1. One-syllable words are stronger; use them in subject lines.
2. Strong = concrete and visible; weak = abstract.
3. Kill "could/may/might" fog — say what's actually happening.
4. Active verbs ALWAYS: Who—doing—what.
5. Emojis: sparingly, to signal tone and stand out.
