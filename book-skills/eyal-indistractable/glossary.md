# Indistractable — Glossary

**Attention residue** — The lingering focus on a prior task that makes refocusing after a switch harder (Sophie Leroy) (Ch 4).

**Autonomy** — One of three psychological nutrients: volition and freedom of control over one's choices (Ch 7).

**B = MAT (Fogg Behavior Model)** — A behavior occurs only when Motivation, Ability, and a Trigger converge simultaneously (Ch 4).

**Burn or burn** — A price pact: a $100 bill + lighter beside the calendar; exercise or burn the bill (Ch 5).

**Competence** — Psychological nutrient: the feeling of mastery, progress, and growth (Ch 7).

**Cycle of responsiveness** — Off-hours responsiveness trains others to expect it, escalating demands into an always-on norm (Leslie Perlow) (Ch 6).

**Distraction** — Any action that draws you away from what you planned to do (Ch 1).

**Effort pact** — A precommitment that makes an unwanted behavior harder to do (kSafe, Forest, Focusmate) (Ch 5).

**Effort-reward imbalance** — Work condition where little pay/recognition returns for hard effort; predicts depression (Ch 6).

**Ego depletion** — The largely debunked belief that willpower is a finite fuel that runs out (Ch 2).

**External trigger** — An environmental cue (ping, ding, person, object) that prompts action (Ch 1).

**Hedonic adaptation** — The tendency to return to a baseline of satisfaction no matter what happens; one of four factors making satisfaction temporary (Ch 2).

**"I don't" vs. "I can't"** — Identity framing; "I don't eat meat" resists temptation better than "I can't" (Ch 5).

**Identity pact** — A precommitment to a self-image ("I'm indistractable") so behavior follows identity (Ch 5).

**Indistractable** — Striving to do what you say you will do; the defining skill of the 21st century (Ch 1).

**Internal trigger** — A cue from within (boredom, anxiety, loneliness) that prompts action (Ch 1).

**Ironic process theory** — Suppressing a thought makes it rebound more strongly ("don't think of a white bear," Wegner) (Ch 2).

**Job strain** — High expectations combined with low control; predicts workplace depression and distraction (Ch 6).

**Liminal moments** — Transitions between activities where "just a second" balloons into lost time (Ch 2).

**Loss aversion** — We're more motivated to avoid losses than to seek equal gains; the engine of price pacts (Ch 5).

**Multichannel multitasking (cross-modal)** — Pairing one attention-heavy channel with a low-demand one (audiobook + walking) (Ch 4).

**Need density hypothesis** — The more needs go unmet in real life, the more they're sought in virtual realities (Ryan) (Ch 7).

**Negativity bias** — Bad events demand attention more powerfully than good; one of four dissatisfaction factors (Ch 2).

**Phubbing** — Phone + snubbing: ignoring a person by attending to a device (Ch 8).

**Precommitment** — A choice made in advance that binds your future self (Ulysses pact) (Ch 5).

**Price pact** — A precommitment putting money on the line, forfeited if you get distracted (Ch 5).

**Proximate vs. root cause** — Devices are the proximate cause we blame; escaping discomfort is the root cause (Ch 2).

**Pseudowork** — Tasks that look like work but aren't aligned with top priorities (Ch 3).

**Psychological safety** — Belief that one won't be punished or humiliated for speaking up (Amy Edmondson) (Ch 6).

**Relatedness** — Psychological nutrient: feeling important to others and that others matter to you (Ch 7).

**Reflect & Refine** — Weekly 15-minute review of what worked in your schedule and what to change (Ch 3).

**Residual beneficiary** — The one who gets only leftover time; don't let loved ones become this (Ch 3).

**Rumination** — Repeatedly dwelling on bad experiences; one of four dissatisfaction factors (Ch 2).

**Self-compassion** — Talking to yourself like a friend after a slip; predicts resilience and well-being (Ch 2).

**Self-determination theory** — Ryan & Deci: the psyche needs autonomy, competence, and relatedness to thrive (Ch 7).

**Social antibody** — A group norm that blocks the spread of a harmful behavior (Paul Graham) (Ch 8).

**Social contagion** — The spread of behaviors (device use, overeating) through a social group (Ch 8).

**Surf the urge** — Riding a craving like a wave — observing, neither suppressing nor acting — until it subsides (Ch 2).

**Sterile cockpit rule** — Aviation rule banning non-essential activity below 10,000 ft; model for interruption-free zones (Ch 4).

**Temptation bundling** — Only allowing a tempting activity alongside one you should do (audiobooks only at the gym; Milkman) (Ch 4).

**Ten-minute rule** — When an urge hits, allow it — but wait ten minutes first (Ch 2).

**Three life domains** — You, Relationships, Work — the framework for allocating time (Ch 3).

**Timeboxing** — Deciding what to do and when; eliminating white space via implementation intentions (Ch 3).

**TNT (T = n × t)** — Total email time = number of messages × time per message; reduce both (Ch 4).

**Traction** — Any action that draws you toward what you planned to do (Ch 1).

**Ulysses pact** — A freely made decision designed to bind oneself in the future (Ch 5).

**4 R's (smartphone)** — Remove, Replace, Rearrange, Reclaim (Ch 4).
