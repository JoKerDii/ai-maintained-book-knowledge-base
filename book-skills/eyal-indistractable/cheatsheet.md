# Indistractable — Cheatsheet

## The 4-Part Model (apply IN ORDER — don't skip)
| # | Step | Core move | Chapter |
|---|------|-----------|---------|
| 1 | Master internal triggers | Deal with discomfort (the root cause) | Ch 2 |
| 2 | Make time for traction | Timebox values into a calendar | Ch 3 |
| 3 | Hack back external triggers | Remove cues that don't serve you | Ch 4 |
| 4 | Prevent distraction with pacts | Precommit to stop yourself sliding | Ch 5 |

## Two decision rules that resolve most cases
- **Traction or distraction?** → "Did this move me toward or away from *what I planned*?" (You can't call it distraction unless you know what it distracts you *from*.)
- **The Critical Question** (external triggers) → "Is this trigger serving me, or am I serving it?" Serving me = keep. I'm serving it = hack back.

## When an urge hits (internal trigger)
1. Name the discomfort preceding it. 2. Curiosity, not contempt. 3. **10-minute rule** — allow it, but wait 10 min. 4. Surf the urge; it passes. → Never suppress (white-bear rebound). After a slip: self-compassion, not shame.

## Which pact for which job?
| Situation | Use | Because |
|-----------|-----|---------|
| Can add physical friction | **Effort pact** | Makes the bad behavior harder |
| Short task, removable trigger | **Price pact** | Loss aversion; moves pain to now |
| Long-term change of self | **Identity pact** | "I don't" > "I can't" |
> Price-pact pitfalls: skip if trigger is inescapable, task is long, or you beat yourself up.

## Email (TNT = n × t)
- Reduce **n**: office hours · delay-send · unsubscribe/black-hole.
- Reduce **t**: batch · touch twice · tag by *reply-time* ("Today"/"This Week"), not topic · answer in blocks.

## Smartphone in <1 hour — the 4 R's
**Remove** unused → **Replace** (social/YouTube → desktop; wear a watch) → **Rearrange** (home screen = Primary Tools + Aspirations only) → **Reclaim** (sound/sight notifications per app; Do Not Disturb).

## Meetings & chat tells
- **No agenda + proposed solution → no meeting.** Meetings = consensus, not thinking out loud. One laptop per room.
- Group chat: in/out at scheduled times; only necessary people; not a place to think aloud.

## Workplace diagnosis
Chronic distraction/tech-overuse = **symptom**. Disease = low **psychological safety** + high job strain (high expectations × low control). Fix: leaders model focus → pilot one small change with one team → open a safe forum.

## Kids — the smell test
- Overuse = symptom of unmet **Autonomy / Competence / Relatedness** (feed these offline first).
- Set limits *with* them, not *for* them (imposed limits → resentment + cheating).
- Tech-readiness test: can they use the device's own settings (Do Not Disturb, notifications) to turn off triggers? If not, not ready.
- Don't be the interrupting external trigger yourself.

## Relationships
- Phubbing → ask sincerely: **"I see you're on your phone. Is everything OK?"**
- Protect intimacy with all 4 steps: manage the urge → device-free bedtime (traction) → phones out of bedroom → router/timer shut-off (effort pact).

## Identity default
Call yourself **"indistractable."** It's a strived-for identity, not perfection — "strive" means struggle. You'll still slip; get back on track.
