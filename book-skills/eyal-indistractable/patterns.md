# Indistractable — Patterns & Techniques

## Reimagine the Internal Trigger (4 steps)
**When to use**: A recurring urge to distract (googling instead of writing, phone-checking).
**How**: (1) Look for the discomfort preceding it; (2) Write down the trigger — time, activity, feeling; (3) Explore the sensation with curiosity ("leaves on a stream"); (4) Beware liminal moments (transitions).
**Trade-offs**: Requires honest self-observation; takes trials to notice internal (vs. external) triggers.

## The 10-Minute Rule / Surf the Urge
**When to use**: An in-the-moment craving for a pacifying distraction.
**How**: Tell yourself you *can* give in — but not for ten minutes. Ride the urge like a wave without suppressing or obeying it. Reassess after ten minutes (the urge usually passes).
**Trade-offs**: Not a hard "no," so it relies on the urge genuinely subsiding — works because most do.

## Timeboxing the Three Life Domains
**When to use**: Whenever your days feel reactive or your calendar doesn't reflect your values.
**How**: Decide hours per week for You / Relationships / Work; build a "perfect week" template with zero white space; judge success by adherence, not output.
**Trade-offs**: Feels rigid at first, but constraints reduce decision fatigue and make distraction visible.

## Reflect & Refine (weekly review)
**When to use**: Every week, ~15 minutes.
**How**: Reflect ("when did I do what I said vs. get distracted?"), then Refine ("what calendar change would help me live my values?"). Treat the week as an experiment.
**Trade-offs**: Only works if you commit to the schedule between reviews.

## Schedule Syncing with Stakeholders
**When to use**: Any shared-time relationship — manager, partner, multiple bosses.
**How**: Share your timeboxed schedule; agree on it in regular increments (usually weekly; daily if it changes daily).
**Trade-offs**: Requires vulnerability/transparency; pays back in fewer imposed distractions.

## The Critical Question
**When to use**: On any external trigger.
**How**: Ask "Is this trigger serving me, or am I serving it?" Keep triggers that lead to traction; hack back the rest.

## Email TNT (T = n × t)
**When to use**: Email overload.
**How**: Cut **n** — office hours, delay-send, unsubscribe/black-hole. Cut **t** — batch; touch each email twice, tagging by *when* it needs a reply ("Today"/"This Week"), answer in scheduled blocks.
**Trade-offs**: Slower replies; usually a feature, not a bug (many issues self-resolve).

## Smartphone 4 R's
**When to use**: Phone feels distracting (one-time, <1 hr).
**How**: **Remove** unused apps → **Replace** where/when you use tempting ones (desktop, wristwatch) → **Rearrange** home screen to Primary Tools + Aspirations → **Reclaim** notifications (sound/sight per app; Do Not Disturb).

## Temptation Bundling + Multichannel Multitasking
**When to use**: To make a "should" (exercise) attractive or reclaim commute/chore time.
**How**: Only allow a tempting reward (audiobooks, saved articles) alongside the should. Pair one attention-heavy channel with a low-demand one (audio + walking).
**Trade-offs**: Only works across modalities — never two complex tasks at once.

## Effort Pact
**When to use**: A distraction you can add friction to.
**How**: Make it physically harder — app blockers (Forest, SelfControl, Freedom), kSafe, co-working/Focusmate, iOS Downtime.
**Trade-offs**: Determined self can override; friction just has to exceed the impulse.

## Price Pact
**When to use**: Short, well-defined task where you can remove the external trigger.
**How**: Put money on the line ("burn or burn"; forfeit to a friend). Exploits loss aversion.
**Trade-offs**: Four pitfalls — no escapable trigger, short tasks only, scary to start, not for the self-critical.

## Identity Pact
**When to use**: Long-term behavior change.
**How**: Adopt a noun ("I'm indistractable"); say "I don't," not "I can't"; tell others to solidify it.
**Trade-offs**: Slower to take hold, but self-reinforcing once it does.

## Create Psychological Safety (Edmondson's 3 steps)
**When to use**: Leading a team that overuses tech / can't raise concerns.
**How**: (1) Frame work as a learning problem; (2) Acknowledge your own fallibility; (3) Model curiosity, ask questions. Pilot small (one predictable night off, one team) and open a regular forum.

## Social Antibody Against Phubbing
**When to use**: Someone uses a device in a social setting.
**How**: Sincerely ask, "I see you're on your phone. Is everything OK?" — offering a graceful exit or a return to the group.
