# Chapter 34–35: Part 7 — How to Have Indistractable Relationships

## Core Idea
Distraction is socially contagious, and its cost is failing to be fully present with the people we love. Spread "social antibodies" against phubbing among friends, and apply all four Indistractable steps to reclaim intimacy with a partner.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Social Contagion & Social Antibodies** (Paul Graham): Behaviors spread through groups (one phone out at dinner triggers others). Societies develop "social antibodies" — new norms that make a harmful behavior taboo (as happened with indoor smoking in one generation). We can do the same for device use in social settings.
  - How: When someone phubs, don't embarrass them — ask the disarming question, *"I see you're on your phone. Is everything OK?"* It gives them two graceful options: attend to a real emergency, or put the phone away.
- **Be an Indistractable Lover (all four steps applied to a relationship)**:
  1. **Internal triggers** — use the 10-minute rule / surf the urge on the evening compulsion to check.
  2. **Traction** — schedule a strict bedtime; claim the bedroom as a sacred, device-free space.
  3. **External triggers** — remove phones from the bedroom.
  4. **Pacts** — an effort pact (timer outlets / router shut-off, e.g. Eero) that turns off the internet at night.

## Key Concepts
- **Phubbing**: Phone + snubbing — ignoring a person or your surroundings by busying yourself with a device. Naming it makes it callable-out.
- **Social contagion**: The spread of behaviors (device use, overeating) through a social group.
- **Social antibody**: A group norm that blocks the spread of a harmful behavior.

## Mental Models
- Removing devices isn't enough if you haven't dealt with the internal trigger — you'll just find a new "partner" (Eyal swapped his phone for a laptop and failed until he addressed the discomfort).
- TVs and even your own kids are external triggers in social settings too — scrutinize them with the critical question.

## Worked Example
**Reclaiming intimacy.** Eyal and his wife had a nightly ritual — she'd "fondle her cell phone" while he "stroked the screen" of his iPad — losing intimacy to "techno-infidelity." Their first fix (moving phones to the living room) failed: the anxiety of unanswered email drove them to "cheat" with a laptop, because they'd skipped the internal-trigger step. On the second attempt they used all four steps in order: managed the discomfort with a 10-minute rule and self-compassion, scheduled a strict device-free bedtime (traction), left phones outside the bedroom (external triggers), and wired the router and monitors to $7 timer outlets that cut power at 10pm — an effort pact so annoying to override that compliance became the default. The failed first attempt is the lesson: **pacts and trigger-removal only work on top of internal-trigger mastery.**

## Anti-patterns
- **Removing devices without addressing discomfort**: You'll substitute another distraction.
- **Publicly shaming a phubber**: Costs the friendship; use the sincere "Is everything OK?" instead.
- **Accepting background TVs / ambient screens** as harmless in social settings.

## Key Takeaways
1. Distraction is contagious; deliberately build social antibodies (norms) against it.
2. "I see you're on your phone. Is everything OK?" tactfully ends phubbing.
3. Being present is the whole point — connectivity to everyone can cost presence with the one beside you.
4. Apply all four Indistractable steps *in order* to protect intimate relationships.
5. Now it's your turn to become indistractable.
