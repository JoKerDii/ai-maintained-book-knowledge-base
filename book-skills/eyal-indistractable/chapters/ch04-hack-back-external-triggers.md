# Chapter 13–21: Part 3 — Hack Back External Triggers

## Core Idea
External triggers (pings, dings, people, feeds) only harm you when they don't serve your plan. For every trigger ask the critical question — then surgically remove the ones you're serving, across work, email, chat, meetings, phone, desktop, articles, and feeds.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Critical Question**: *"Is this trigger serving me, or am I serving it?"* If a trigger leads to traction, keep it; if it leads to distraction, hack it back.
- **Fogg Behavior Model (B = MAT)**: A behavior occurs only when **M**otivation, **A**bility, and a **T**rigger converge at once. Remove the trigger and the distracting behavior can't fire.
- **Hack Back Work Interruptions**: Signal "do not interrupt" with an unambiguous cue (screen sign: "I NEED TO FOCUS RIGHT NOW, BUT PLEASE COME BACK SOON"; the "concentration crown"). Inspired by the hospital "sterile cockpit" — bright vests cut nurse medication errors up to 88%.
- **Hack Back Email — TNT (T = n × t)**: Total email time = number received (n) × time per message (t). Reduce **n**: open office hours, delay-send replies, unsubscribe/black-hole senders. Reduce **t**: batch-process; only touch each email twice by tagging *when* it needs a reply ("Today" / "This Week") and answering in scheduled blocks.
- **Hack Back Meetings**: **No agenda, no meeting.** Require the caller to circulate a brief + proposed solution first. Meetings are for consensus-building, not thinking out loud. One laptop per room.
- **Hack Back Group Chat**: Get in and out at scheduled times; involve only who's necessary; don't use it to think out loud. It's "an all-day meeting with random participants and no agenda" (Jason Fried).
- **Hack Back Your Smartphone (4 R's)**: **Remove** unused apps → **Replace** where/when you use them (move social/YouTube to desktop; wear a watch) → **Rearrange** home screen to Primary Tools & Aspirations only (hide "Slot Machines") → **Reclaim** notifications (disable sound/sight per app; use Do Not Disturb). Takes <1 hour.
- **Hack Back Your Desktop**: Clear the visual clutter into one "Everything" folder; use search, not icons; disable all desktop notifications.
- **Hack Back Online Articles**: Rule — *never read articles in the browser.* Save to Pocket, then use **multichannel multitasking** (listen while walking/at the gym) and **temptation bundling** (only get the reward — the articles — while exercising).
- **Hack Back Feeds**: Kill the feed at the source with browser extensions (News Feed Eradicator, Newsfeed Burner, DF Tube) or navigate to a direct URL (e.g. LinkedIn.com/messaging) to bypass the infinite scroll.

## Key Concepts
- **Attention residue** (Sophie Leroy): Switching tasks leaves a residue that makes it harder to refocus.
- **Cycle of responsiveness**: Answering off-hours trains others to expect it, ratcheting up demands.
- **Multichannel / cross-modal multitasking**: You can pair one attention-heavy channel with a low-demand one (audio + walking), unlike two complex tasks.
- **Temptation bundling** (Katherine Milkman): Only allow a tempting activity alongside one you should do (audiobooks only at the gym → 51% more visits).

## Mental Models
- Not replying to a notification is *just as distracting* as replying — the mere presence drains attention. Get the phone out of sight.
- Triggers are **tools**: used right they keep you on track; used wrong they own you.

## Worked Example
**Email TNT in practice.** Eyal receives dozens of reader emails daily. Rather than reply to each (which spawns more replies via reciprocity), he cuts **n** with scheduled *office hours* — "I've held Tue/Thu 4–5pm; if this is still a concern, stop by." Many questions evaporate before the slot. He cuts **t** by giving his inbox one morning pass, tagging each message "Today" or "This Week" (never by topic — only by *when* it needs a reply), then answering "Today" tags in a dedicated block and reserving a 3-hour weekly box for "This Week." Result: nothing falls through the cracks, yet email stops blowing up the day.

## Anti-patterns
- **Ignoring vs. removing**: Trying to ignore a visible phone still drains attention — remove it.
- **Instant reciprocity**: Replying to every email the moment it lands trains everyone to expect it.
- **Meetings to think out loud**: Wastes a roomful of people; think on paper first.

## Key Takeaways
1. For every external trigger ask: "Is this serving me, or am I serving it?"
2. Remove the T in B=MAT and the distraction can't fire.
3. Email: send fewer to get fewer; batch and tag by reply-time, not topic.
4. Phone: Remove, Replace, Rearrange, Reclaim — under an hour.
5. Bundle temptations and use multichannel multitasking to turn reading/exercise into a win.
