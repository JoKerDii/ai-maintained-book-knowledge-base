# Chapter 22–25: Part 4 — Prevent Distraction with Pacts

## Core Idea
The last line of defense is a **precommitment** — a choice made now that binds your future self. Three kinds of pacts (effort, price, identity) keep you *in* once you've already mastered internal triggers, made time for traction, and hacked back external triggers.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Precommitment / Ulysses Pact**: "A freely made decision designed to bind oneself in the future" (Ulysses tied to the mast). Cements clearheaded intentions so you don't act against your interests later. **Only use pacts after the first three steps** — a pact without them will fail.
- **Effort Pact**: Make the unwanted behavior *harder* to do.
  - When to use: Any distraction you can add friction to.
  - How: kSafe locking container; app blockers (SelfControl, Freedom, Forest — kill the virtual tree if you leave); iOS Downtime / Android Digital Wellbeing; co-working with a friend or Focusmate (social pressure = a shared effort pact).
- **Price Pact**: Put money on the line — keep it if you follow through, forfeit it if you get distracted. Exploits **loss aversion** ("losing hurts more than winning feels good").
  - When to use: Short, well-defined tasks where you *can* remove the external trigger.
  - How: "Burn or burn" (a $100 bill taped to the calendar + a lighter: exercise or burn it); a $10,000 forfeit to a friend if the book draft misses its date.
- **Identity Pact**: Precommit to a *self-image* — change who you are, and behavior follows.
  - When to use: Long-term behavior change.
  - How: Adopt a noun ("I'm indistractable," "I'm a vegetarian"). Say "I don't" not "I can't." Tell others to solidify it (evangelizing reinforces the identity).

## Key Concepts
- **Loss aversion**: We're more motivated to avoid losses than to seek equal gains — the engine behind price pacts.
- **"I don't" vs. "I can't"**: "I don't eat meat" (identity) beats "I can't" (deprivation) — nearly 2× more people resisted temptation using "I don't."
- **The four price-pact pitfalls**: (1) useless when you can't escape the external trigger; (2) only for short tasks (long pacts feel like punishment); (3) scary to enter — do it anyway; (4) not for people who beat themselves up — learn self-compassion first.

## Mental Models
- A pact is the **last line of defense**, not the first — skipping the earlier steps guarantees failure.
- Identity is a **cognitive shortcut**: decide who you are once, and future choices get easier ("voters" vote more than people who merely intend "to vote").

## Worked Example
**"Burn or burn."** Eyal kept skipping the free gym in his own building — no excuse held up. So he made a price pact: he taped a crisp $100 bill to the wall calendar beside each workout date and set a 99-cent lighter next to it. Every day offered exactly two options — *burn the calories or burn the bill.* The pact works because it moves the pain of loss into the **present moment** (loss aversion), and the taped bill doubles as a daily external trigger reminding him of his commitment. Three-plus years later he'd gained twelve pounds of muscle and never once burned the money. He layered a second price pact for the book itself: $10,000 owed to his friend Mark if the first draft missed its date — enough to make him sick to lose, so he wrote two focused hours, six days a week.

## Anti-patterns
- **Pacts before the fundamentals**: Precommitting while internal triggers and unremoved external triggers still rule you → failure.
- **Long price pacts**: Bind too long and the task feels like punishment, breeding resentment.
- **Price pacts for the self-critical**: Without self-compassion, a forfeit spirals into shame.

## Key Takeaways
1. Precommitment binds your clearheaded self to protect your impulsive self.
2. Effort pacts add friction; price pacts add cost; identity pacts change who you are.
3. Use pacts *only after* the first three Indistractable steps.
4. Price pacts work best for short tasks where you can remove the trigger — and require self-compassion.
5. Call yourself "indistractable" — an identity pact makes the behavior self-reinforcing.
