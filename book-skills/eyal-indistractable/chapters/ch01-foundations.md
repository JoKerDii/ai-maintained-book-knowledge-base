# Chapter 1–2: Foundations — Your Superpower & the Indistractable Model

## Core Idea
Distraction isn't the opposite of focus — the opposite of distraction is **traction**. Being indistractable is the ability to do what you say you will do, and it is the defining skill of the 21st century.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Traction vs. Distraction**: Both come from the Latin *trahere* ("to draw/pull"). **Traction** = any action that pulls you *toward* what you planned. **Distraction** = any action that pulls you *away* from it.
  - When to use: To classify any action. The label depends entirely on intent — checking work email is traction if planned, distraction if it steals family time.
  - How: Ask "Did this move me toward or away from what I planned to do?"
- **Internal vs. External Triggers**: Every action is prompted by a trigger. **Internal triggers** cue from within (boredom, anxiety, loneliness). **External triggers** are environmental cues (pings, dings, other people, objects).
- **The 4-Part Indistractable Model**: The book's master framework, applied in order:
  1. **Master internal triggers** (deal with discomfort — the root cause)
  2. **Make time for traction** (turn values into a timeboxed schedule)
  3. **Hack back external triggers** (remove cues that don't serve you)
  4. **Prevent distraction with pacts** (precommit to stop yourself sliding)
  - When to use: As the complete operating system for controlling attention. Steps build on each other; the first is most foundational — don't skip ahead to pacts.

## Key Concepts
- **Indistractable**: Striving to do what you say you will do — being as honest with yourself as with others. "Strive" means struggle, not perfection.
- **Traction**: Action drawing you toward your intentions.
- **Distraction**: Action drawing you away from your intentions.
- **Trigger**: The prompt (internal or external) that precedes any behavior.

## Mental Models
- Think of distraction not as a tech problem but as an **attention-management skill** you can learn.
- Use the **Tantalus's curse** lens: the real curse isn't wanting things out of reach — it's being *blind* to the fact you didn't need them. We compulsively reach for things (email, news) we don't actually need right now.

## Anti-patterns
- **Blaming the tech**: Removing devices (flip phone, print newspaper, offline word processor) just makes you swap one distraction for another. The problem is deeper than tech.
- **Being a Luddite**: Indistractable ≠ anti-technology. It means using tech on your terms.

## Worked Example
Eyal's origin story: while playing an activity book with his young daughter, the question "If you could have any superpower, what would it be?" came up. He was staring at his phone, muttered "just a second," and when he looked up she had left. That blown moment launched five years of research. His first fixes all failed — the flip phone (missed GPS/audiobooks), the print newspaper (piled up unread while he watched TV news), the 1990s word processor (he flipped through unrelated books instead). Conclusion: **you can't remove your way out of distraction; you have to address why you seek escape in the first place.** Years later he re-asked his daughter the superpower question — she answered "to always be kind." The lesson mirrors the book's: the power to be indistractable, like kindness, is already within us.

## Key Takeaways
1. The opposite of distraction is traction, not focus — both are prompted by triggers.
2. You can't call something a distraction unless you know what it's distracting you *from*.
3. Distraction starts from within; tech is a proximate cause, not the root.
4. The Indistractable Model has four parts, applied in order — master internal triggers first.
5. "Indistractable" is a strived-for identity, not a state of perfection.
