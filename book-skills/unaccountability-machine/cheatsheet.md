# Cheatsheet — Decide & Diagnose

## The two laws everything reduces to
- **Fundamental law of accountability**: your ability to change a decision = your accountability for it. If you can't change it, you can't be blamed for it — and neither can anyone else.
- **Ashby's Law**: only variety can absorb variety. If regulator variety < environmental variety, the system *cannot* be controlled. No amount of effort, funding, or good intentions substitutes.

## Decision rules
| When | Do | Because |
|---|---|---|
| No information from the affected person could change the outcome | Name it an accountability sink; stop arguing with the operator | The operator is not a decision-maker; the decision was made elsewhere and is unappealable by design |
| A regulator's variety is plainly below the environment's | Kill the project or attenuate the environment — don't add staff | Requisite variety is a hard constraint, not a resourcing problem |
| A division bounces crisis to crisis | Give it its own internal Systems 2/3/4/5 | It's an unrecognised viable system, not a badly-supervised one |
| Leadership attention runs only to this quarter | Rebuild System 4 before anything else | The organisation is decerebrate and coasting on a stable environment |
| Algedonic signals are frequent | Look *down*, not at the signaller | Emergencies escalating means lower levels can't absorb their own variety |
| Algedonic signals are zero | Check the channel exists | Silence is more often a missing channel than health |
| Stated purpose ≠ delivered outcome | Apply POSIWID; ask what structure makes this systematic | Dissolves conspiracy-vs-cock-up, which is the wrong question |
| Someone says "however you look at the numbers" | Read it as "however *I* look at *these* numbers" | The modelling choices did the work, not the data |
| A model is settling a real decision | Run the four-condition check | Failing any one means you're watching the Ricardian Vice |
| Reusing a metric outside its origin | Ask what decision it was collected for | Learn the numbers by looking at the business, never the reverse |
| About to outsource or cut | Trace one signal across every boundary it crosses | If the contract carries only "OK"/"stopped", you've bought decerebration |
| Tempted to write a new rule | Locate where feedback is amplified, attenuated, or removed | Rules add variety to the regulated side; feedback adds it to the regulator |
| Designing any maximising system | Pair it with a higher-level supervisor and a red handle | The top level of an autonomous system must never itself be a maximiser |

## Diagnostic tree — "why does this system keep making terrible decisions?"
- **Does the information reach a decision-maker at all?**
  - No, no channel → missing channel. Build it.
  - No, channel exists but signal arrives unusable → **missing translation capacity**. It's data, not information.
  - Yes, but too late to act → arrival timing failure; same as no channel.
  - Yes, in time and actionable → continue.
- **Can the receiver absorb that much variety?**
  - No → requisite-variety mismatch. Attenuate incoming, amplify regulatory, or accept the system is uncontrollable.
  - Yes → continue.
- **Is there a System 4 watching what isn't in contact with operations yet?**
  - Only extrapolative forecasting → System 4 absent (forecasting ≠ intelligence).
  - Nothing → decerebrate organisation.
- **Does System 5 rule the relevant information out of scope?**
  - Yes → identity failure. The most common root cause; no amount of lower-level fixing survives it.

## Tells & smells
**An accountability sink**: "that's the policy" with no named owner · an exceptions process that exists but is never used · a crumple zone (the rule absorbs the blame that would have hit a manager) · the affected party has facts nobody is authorised to receive.

**A variety mismatch**: controls with fewer dimensions than the environment's degrees of freedom · observation frequency slower than the rate variety is generated · a regulator that keeps being "surprised" by things insiders already knew · "we just need better data" as the standing answer.

**A decerebrate organisation**: excellent quarterly execution, no one owns the five-year question · forecasts are the current model extended · System 4 was the first thing cut · performing beautifully right up until the environment changes structurally.

**Ideology wearing a lab coat**: a phrase cited to a source that doesn't contain it (the "fiduciary duty to maximise shareholder value" attributed to Friedman) · legal jargon used outside its legal context · "ad hoc" deployed to disqualify habit and rules of thumb · semantic drift from *increase* to *maximise*, from *profits* to *shareholder value*.

**A system about to fail**: targets demanding the impossible (you will get the unethical) · quarterly beat institutionalised as the primary filter · redundancy threat swamping middle-management judgement · costs prioritised over revenues because they're what management directly controls · volatility suppressed by using unemployment as the attenuator.

## Defaults the author commits to
- Prefer **viability over maximisation** as the corporate objective.
- **Management by exception**: push variety-handling to the lowest level that can absorb it; size the manager to the *residual* variety, not the total.
- **Resource bargain**: grant autonomy up to the point of jeopardising the wider system — no further, no less.
- Retain **limited liability** only for small, non-controlling investors; no further levels of it.
- The single private-equity reform worth making: **any entity taking control of an operating company must guarantee its debts**. Do *not* try to regulate "excessive" borrowing — requisite variety says that's doomed.
- Economists demoted to **competent technicians** (Keynes' dentists), toolkit retained.
- Accept unaccountable, robotic decision-making — give up the demand for a human to blame — and compensate by repairing feedback and attending to red handles. "I blame the system" becomes a literal statement.
