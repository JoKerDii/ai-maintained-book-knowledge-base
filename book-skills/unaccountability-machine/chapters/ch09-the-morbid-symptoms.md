# Chapter 9: The Morbid Symptoms

## Core Idea
Having cut away the middle management and civil service capacity that used to absorb volatility, the corporate and political systems reversed the old bargain and made the working class the shock absorber — and when that broke, the only channel left to the population was a one-bit scream, which is what populism is.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Reversing the polarity**: Jerome Levy's premise was that the investing class exists to serve the working class by insuring them against loss and providing desired goods, and that the working class does not exist to serve the investing class. The neoliberal era ran that relationship backwards, using unemployment itself as the variety attenuator.
  - When to use: To diagnose any system that has stopped absorbing volatility and started exporting it downward.
  - How: Ask who currently carries the variance. Marx's "reserve army of the unemployed", Kalecki's version of the Levy profit equations, Keynes' general theory and Friedman's "natural rate" (refined into the NAIRU) all point the same way — capital-owners will tolerate lower output as the price of keeping control of the wage bargain.
  - Why it works / failure mode: It produced the Great Moderation — external shocks could be accommodated or passed on so long as wage pressure and price inflation did not build. It fails because the corporate system, having reduced its own capacity, could no longer absorb shocks at all, and because the second escape route — insuring yourself through elected government — was closed off simultaneously by public-sector decerebration.
- **Translation and transduction**: Beer's criterion that careful attention must be paid to preserving information when a signal crosses a boundary — received in a form and at a time that lets it enter the decision-making process.
  - When to use: Before removing "redundant" middle management, or before outsourcing anything.
  - How: Identify every boundary the signal crosses; ask what processing capacity sits there to handle nuance, wobbles and flutters at the appropriate level. Much of the seeming redundancy in middle management was exactly this capacity, installed at communication boundaries.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Outsourcing swaps internal boundaries for external ones and narrows the channel to two states — "everything is going more or less as anticipated" or "it's stopped working and we need to find out why". Because the channel spends nearly all its time saying OK, nobody can estimate how much reserve bandwidth is needed for "conditions are changing" or for the crisis; with targets to hit, the spare capacity always looks cuttable.
- **The one-bit channel and the scream**: Where the population's communication channels are designed to carry very few bits, the only message they can carry is a scream — "Help! the Current State of Affairs is Intolerable to me."
  - When to use: When interpreting mass political movements.
  - How: Treat the medium as the message. Respond to the fact of mass distress, not to its content — which is why the endless quests for the true views of Real America reach such disappointing conclusions.
- **Time inconsistency (as an accountability sink)**: Prescott and Kydland's argument that governments know what to do long-term but are destabilised by election-cycle feedback, so control of money should pass to an independent, unaccountable body — Odysseus tied to the mast.
  - When to use: To recognise the general move of manufacturing an accountability sink to improve policy outcomes.
  - How: Read it cybernetically — the central bankers concluded that the accountability channels from those affected by unemployment and recession were working too well, transmitting too much information and impairing self-management.

## Key Concepts
- **Making the numbers**: Institutionalising the practice of always meeting or exceeding quarterly earnings forecasts, ignoring that a quarterly accounting figure is an extraordinarily crude information filter.
- **Costs over revenues**: Short-term targets privilege costs because management controls them; revenue requires finding a customer, winning the order, making the product and getting paid — impossible in a quarter.
- **Rank and yank**: Firing the bottom 10 per cent of managers annually regardless of absolute performance — retained even when conditions made it hard to justify, because the threat itself was the point.
- **Redundancy threat as debt-equivalent**: At middle management level, employment insecurity performs the same function debt performs higher up — swamping decision-making with the requirement that targets be met.
- **Decerebration**: Hacking away parts of a system's own regulatory and information-handling apparatus to see whether it can do without them — amateur brain surgery on the corporation and the state.
- **Great Moderation**: The sharp decline in the volatility of output and inflation, achieved by using the unemployment rate as a variety attenuator.
- **Deaths of despair**: Case and Deaton's rapid rise in suicide, alcoholism and opiate addiction — evidence that ignorance is not the only, or the most lethal, variety attenuator.
- **Status syndrome / the social gradient**: Marmot's Whitehall finding that civil service grade predicts serious mortality independent of income, smoking and family history — being out of control is physiologically harmful as well as emotionally intolerable.
- **El pueblo**: Allende's name for the System 5 box — the top-level, last-resort absorber of information, which hardly ever does anything and has no postal address; even in a dictatorship there is a collective veto if things become intolerable.
- **Blind spot in the control parameters**: Only government deficits were monitored; private-sector debt sat outside the control system entirely, and was regarded as positive by the post-Friedman system of corporate control.

## Mental Models
- Use "if you consistently demand the impossible, you will inevitably get the unethical" when a target regime produces fraud rather than performance.
- Think of an outsourcing contract as a channel deliberately built to carry one bit — fine while the bit is "OK", useless the moment it isn't.
- Think of accountability sinks as self-protection: to be accountable for something you can't change is exactly the out-of-control feeling the Whitehall studies suggest will kill you.
- Use "you can tell when a cybernetic system is overloaded because it breaks down" — at the level of a person, a firm, or an electorate.
- Think of a populist movement as a channel, not a programme: its content is noise; its existence is the signal.

## Anti-patterns
- **Managing the earnings line rather than the business**: GE offset a one-off $2bn gain by closing factories and making over 1,000 people redundant, to avoid a spike that would spoil future smooth growth.
- **Cutting the people who don't appear to do anything**: In an accounting system that targets overhead, thinning middle management looks like a no-brainer — and turns out to be exactly that.
- **Outsourcing core functions**: Corporations shed peripheral activities to protect core competence; states outsourced trains, hospitals and defence procurement, with more severe consequences.
- **Manufacturing arms-length agencies to bind successors**: Creating institutions and long-dated contracts that lock in priorities and shrink the share of expenditure attributable to identifiable politicians.
- **Treating elections and polls as horse races**: Nobody designs democratic channels from an information-engineering point of view, though a proportional system carries more information than first-past-the-post and a monthly poll has a shorter lag than an annual one.
- **Promising a simpler world**: The populists' offer is a fake solution — it is equivalent to claiming to reverse the direction of time, and their promise to restore broken communication channels comes with no method, since those channels were made of the middle managers and civil servants nobody wants back.

## Worked Example
Trace the Welch model end to end. GE institutionalises always making its quarterly number. Because costs are controllable within a quarter and revenues are not, the pressure lands on headcount: whole layers of middle management go, plus an annual rank-and-yank of the bottom 10 per cent, kept in place chiefly to preserve the threat. The removed managers were the processing capacity sitting at communication boundaries, so remaining signals cross those boundaries degraded — and outsourcing converts what boundaries remain into contracts carrying one bit. Meanwhile financial services grow until their subjective estimates of the future dominate group earnings; during the housing bubble the mortgage arm "jacked up the volume without controls" and misrepresented loan quality, ending in a $1.5bn Justice Department settlement. Corporate capacity to absorb shocks is now gone, so the economy is reorganised so that the working class absorbs them instead. It looked like the final triumph of capital. It was — until it wasn't.

## Key Takeaways
1. Before cutting "redundant" management, ask which boundary each role sits at and what it translates; the redundancy is usually the transduction capacity.
2. Ask who is currently absorbing volatility in your system; if the answer is the people with the least control, expect breakdown, not stability.
3. Treat any narrow contractual channel as unable to carry warning signals — build separate bandwidth for "conditions are changing" before you need it.
4. Watch what a control system does not monitor: the crisis arrived through private-sector debt, which no framework was tracking.
5. Read demands for the impossible as fraud generators, and check what your target regime is quietly commissioning.
6. When a population's only channel is one bit wide, respond to the fact of the scream rather than parsing its content.
7. Make recommendations, because if you don't make recommendations you won't know what to be disappointed by.
