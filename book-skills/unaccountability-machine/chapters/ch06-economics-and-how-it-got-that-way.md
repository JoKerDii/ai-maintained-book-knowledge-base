# Chapter 6: Economics and How It Got That Way

## Core Idea
Economics is best understood not as the study of the economy but as a variety-engineering technology: a style of rhetoric and modelling that attenuates the complexity of human society down to price and quantity so it can always give a definite answer. That attenuation is what made economics the identity-creating function of modern governance — and it generates three predictable blind spots in every system economists design.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Ricardian Vice** (Schumpeter's term): (a) model some feature of the economy, stripping away nearly all complexity; (b) make strong, often empirically questionable simplifying assumptions; (c) derive the conclusion from those assumptions — easy if they're strong enough; (d) act as if the conclusion has now been proved about the real world.
  - When to use: Whenever a policy or business case rests on a model result rather than an observation.
  - How: Ask which assumptions are merely technical (to make the maths tractable) and which are equivalent to assuming the conclusion; ask what a differently specified model would have shown; ask whether anyone has a systematic process for checking whether the model is still useful.
  - Why it works / failure mode: The vice is *necessary* to preserve economics' appeal — a straight logical line from justifiable assumptions to actionable conclusions, which is exactly what makes economists useful constructors of accountability sinks. In Beer's terms it translates to a weak System 4: information that could show the operating model has become unrealistic never enters the decision set. It also dulls response to red-handle signals, since screams of pain don't exist in the model.
- **Constrained Optimisation as the economic toolkit**: Convert the problem into a single numerical index of success or failure, express the controllable quantities and resource limits as equations, then solve to maximise or minimise the index.
  - When to use: Legitimately, where output really is describable by quantity and price and periods really are independent.
  - How: Check the four conditions the electricity example depends on — commodity good, observable marginal cost curve, genuinely independent spot markets, negligible strategic interaction. If any fails, the model is doing rhetoric, not measurement.
  - Why it works / failure mode: Requiring a single maximand forces every corporate value, regulatory relationship and long-term brand consideration to be re-expressed as beliefs about future profits — so ambiguity, perception and incompatible values become unsayable.
- **Markets as Computing Fabric**: Whenever there is a hole in an economic explanation, the market is credited with doing the information processing — a self-organising machine from which you get out more than you put in.
  - When to use: As a diagnostic. If an argument requires agents to have information nobody explains how they acquired, this assumption is doing the work.
  - How: Trace the four sources — Smith's invisible hand, Hayek's answer to the socialist calculation debate, post-war proofs about market equilibrium, and financial-economics market efficiency — and notice that none licenses the reverse inference that an observed market outcome is therefore optimal.

## Key Concepts
- **Economics as identity-creating function**: Economics performs System 5's job for policymakers — it tells rulers what they don't have to think about.
- **Attenuation via cost–benefit analysis**: Adopting growth and efficiency as philosophy lets thousands of policies and whole approaches to human life be rejected without consideration.
- **Comparative advantage**: Ricardo's result that gains from trade depend on relative not absolute costs — the crown jewel, and the template for the vice named after him.
- **Modelling strategy**: The postgraduate craft of choosing mathematical statements that make a model tractable and guarantee a single conclusion, rather than statements relevant to the actual economy.
- **Tractability**: The property of yielding a definite answer; historically visible in the open assumptions, now hidden inside simulations.
- **Microfoundations**: The demand that every large-scale phenomenon be justified as the cumulative result of individual optimising decisions — a commitment to opening the black box rather than analysing its behaviour.
- **Homo economicus**: An agent who cares about nothing but price and quantity, is perfectly informed about future supply and demand, and can read the minds of other agents.
- **Representative individual / representative firm**: The habit of assuming all preferences identical, or aggregating all producers into one, to dodge strategic interaction entirely.
- **Marginal cost curve**: The cost of producing one more unit, ignoring overheads and fixed costs — the one economic concept engineers independently invented (as a load balancing table).
- **Spot market**: A market for goods produced and consumed in a single short period, modelled as independent of all subsequent periods.
- **Ad hoc**: A grave insult among economists; disqualifies rules of thumb, habits and fashions from counting as explanations.

## Mental Models
- Think of economics' philosophy and methodology as variety engineering: attenuating the real world and amplifying the models.
- Use "whose numbers?" when hearing "however you look at the numbers" — it means "however *I* look at *these* numbers".
- Think of economists as Rubik's Cube people who have committed to Rubik's-Cube-shaped attenuations and won't give them up even when they cost vital information.
- Use "does this survive the fifth complication?" on any model result: add multiple commodities, multiple inputs, brands, firms, investment, strategy — and see whether the conclusion holds or just changes with the specification.

## Anti-patterns
- **Proving it in the model, then acting in the world**: The competitive-markets model of discrimination "shows" it can't survive competition, so competition gets treated as a substitute for anti-discrimination law — it works in the model only because information relevant to the actual problem was thrown away.
- **Reverse-inferring optimality from equilibrium**: If it looks like a market outcome it must be the best available — the reasoning behind reluctance to second-guess housing bubbles and financial deregulation. Market outcomes are a huge accountability sink.
- **Forcing safety information through cost–benefit**: "This aircraft has a faulty stall sensor which might cause it to crash" is fundamentally not a statement about expected costs, however comfortable you are pricing a life.
- **Treating a modelling strategy as a factual assertion**: Assumptions adopted to tame the maths quietly become claims about how people behave — invisible because economists don't do methodology.
- **"All models are wrong but some are useful" as a defence**: If you believed it, you would have built a systematic process for checking whether yours had stopped being useful.

## Worked Example
Ricardo's England and Portugal: cloth costs 100 labour-hours in England and 90 in Portugal; wine costs 120 in England and 80 in Portugal. Portugal is absolutely better at both. But relative prices differ — a bolt of cloth is worth nine-eighths of a barrel of wine in Portugal and only five-sixths in England — so Portugal does better making only wine and trading for cloth. Logically airtight and genuinely non-trivial; Samuelson could name no other proposition in the social sciences that was both. The vice appears at the next step. Add more commodities, more inputs, brands and quality, firms, investment, strategic planning, demand effects on world prices — or just observe that countries import goods much like the ones they export — and you can generate whichever conclusion your setup (or your boss) requires. The model cannot tell you which specification is right. What people resist is never the arithmetic; it is the leap from the arithmetic to a claim about international trade.

## Key Takeaways
1. Read any economic argument as an attenuation device: ask what it licensed you to stop thinking about.
2. Separate assumptions made for tractability from assumptions that smuggle in the conclusion — and say which is which out loud.
3. Before applying an optimisation model, verify commodity-likeness, independent periods and absence of strategic interaction.
4. Never infer that an outcome is optimal because it is a market outcome; that inference is where accountability disappears.
5. Treat information that cannot be expressed as expected cost (safety warnings, red-handle signals) as requiring a separate channel, not a monetary translation.
6. Watch for the filter between academic economics and applied economics: an idea isn't "economics" in practice until it reaches the undergraduate curriculum.
7. Judge economics on the practical deficiencies of the systems it builds, not on alleged moral deficiencies of its practitioners.
