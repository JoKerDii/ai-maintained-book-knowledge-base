# Glossary — The Unaccountability Machine (Dan Davies)

Cybernetics vocabulary first: get **variety**, **requisite variety**, **accountability sink**, and **System 1–5** exactly right; the rest hangs off them.

**Accountability sink** — A structure that severs the link between a decision's outcome and any human who could be answerable for it; its defining test is that no information from the affected person could change the result (Ch 1, 3). Not merely "nobody is responsible" — it is a *variety-amplification device*, "a solution as much as a problem" (Ch 4).

**Ad hoc** — In economics, a disqualifying insult; it rules habit and rules-of-thumb out as explanations (Ch 6).

**Algedonic signal / "red handle"** — A channel letting any level signal directly to the top, across intervening levels, that something threatens viability and needs immediate action; modelled on a train's emergency brake (Ch 5, 10).

**Ashby's Law (Principle of Requisite Variety)** — Only variety can absorb variety: a regulator must have at least as many states as the system it regulates (Ch 4).

**Ataxia / purpose tremor** — Feedback too weak (response trails the target) vs. too strong (oscillating overcorrection) (Ch 4).

**Attributability test** — A cost is attributable exactly to the extent it would be avoided by not producing that output; the accountants' version of the fundamental law of accountability (Ch 7).

**Black box** — A system defined by its behaviour, not its composition, because its internals cannot be safely or usefully known; respecting its integrity is management cybernetics' signature move (Ch 2, 3). Contrast **clear box** (directly controllable) and **muddy box** (analysable, but at disproportionate effort).

**Business hallucination** — Acting on a picture generated by reporting conventions rather than the real process (Ch 7).

**Complexity** — Defined operationally as that which must be treated as a black box (Ch 2); **complex** vs. **complicated** = treatable "only as a whole" vs. traceable by maths (Ch 3).

**Control fraud** (Black) — A criminal captures a bank and installs incentives that produce fraud via legitimate-looking cash transfers. **Self-organising control fraud** (Davies) — a system so criminogenic that banks organise themselves into fraud with no boss-to-crook link (Ch 1).

**Criminogenic organisation** — One whose incentives are structurally designed to ensure crimes (Ch 1).

**Crumple zone** — A policy's function of absorbing impact to shield a manager from a disastrous decision (Ch 1).

**Cybernetics** — The abandoned science of decision-making systems; **management cybernetics** is Beer's applied branch (Ch 0, 2).

**Cybersyn** — Chile's system: **Cybernet** (telex network, System 2), **Cyberstride** (statistical filter distinguishing blip / trend change / new normal), the **Operations Room** (System 3), **Checo** (System 4) (Ch 7i).

**Decerebrate organisation** — Still operates, but has lost intelligence and identity, so cannot act purposively; coasting on a stable environment (Ch 5i, 9).

**Decided-upon, the** — Those affected by decisions who have no person to address (Ch 0).

**Decision-making system** — Any arrangement producing decisions from standardised information rather than a human reacting to circumstances; Davies' replacement term for "artificial intelligence" (Ch 0, 3).

**Deep politics / parapolitics** (Scott) — The part of government where accountability is *intentionally* diminished and kept out of public discourse (Ch 1).

**Financialisation** — Not a parasite on the real economy but the growing weight of the financial aspects of the whole system (Ch 8).

**Fundamental law of accountability** — Your ability to change a decision equals your accountability for it. Corollary, the **principle of diminishing accountability**: the more decision-making is industrialised, the less accountability survives (Ch 1, 3).

**Homeostasis / homeostat** — The state a system returns to under feedback, with no implication that it is optimal; Ashby's **homeostat** was four veto-capable needles with 810,000 states that rapidly re-stabilised (Ch 2, 4). **Homeostatic forces** are the subsystem links pulling an organisation back to existing behaviour (Ch 2, 7).

**Management accounting vs financial reporting** — Process control and cost allocation tied to specific decisions, vs. a periodic convention-based summary for financiers that attenuates variety (Ch 7).

**Management by exception** — Push variety-handling to the lowest level that can absorb it; escalate only what that level cannot handle (Ch 5, 7i).

**Metasystem** — Beer's term for the part of a viable system responsible for its internal organisation (Systems 3–5) (Ch 5).

**Microfoundations / homo economicus / representative individual** — The requirement that phenomena be derived from individual optimising decisions; an agent valuing only price and quantity, perfectly informed; and the aggregation that removes heterogeneity and strategic interaction (Ch 6).

**POSIWID** — "The purpose of a system is what it does." Not cynicism: a description of how System 5 preserves viability (Ch 2, 5, 10).

**Polycrisis** (Tooze) — Structurally similar 21st-century crises whose common feature is loss of control by the existing hierarchy (Ch 1).

**Rank and yank** — Annual bottom-10% firing, retained mainly for its threat value (Ch 9).

**Recursion / embedding** — Every viable system contains all five systems and is itself a System 1 inside a larger one (Ch 5).

**Residual variety** — What is left after Systems 1 and 2 absorb theirs; the volume System 3 must be sized to (Ch 5).

**Resource bargain** — Autonomy granted up to the point of jeopardising the wider system: what a unit gets, and what it is accountable for delivering (Ch 2, 5, 10).

**Reversing the polarity** — The shift by which the working class came to insure the corporate sector against the business cycle instead of the reverse; **restoring the polarity** is Ch 10's core prescription (Ch 9, 10).

**Ricardian Vice** (Schumpeter) — Proving something in a stripped-down model, then acting as if it were proved about the world (Ch 6).

**Status syndrome / social gradient** (Marmot, Whitehall) — Loss of control over one's work is physiologically lethal (Ch 9).

**Survival set** — The output ranges corresponding to the regulated thing continuing to exist (Ch 4).

**Systems 1–5 (Viable System Model)** — 1 Operations (makes change in the world); 2 Regulation (stops operations clashing); 3 Integration (coordinates the 1s, strikes and polices resource bargains); 3* audit; 4 Intelligence ("there and then", watching the environment not yet in contact with operations); 5 Philosophy/identity (balances 3 against 4 by fixing what kind of organisation this is) (Ch 5).

**Technostructure** (Galbraith) — The managerial class maintaining overall system stability (Ch 10).

**Translation capacity / transduction** — Capability at each end of a channel to render a signal into a form the receiving box can act on; without it, variety stays "data" and never becomes information (Ch 4, 5, 9).

**Ultrastability** (Ashby) → **viability** (Beer) — The ability to find a steady state under conditions the designer never anticipated; viability is the potential to persist as an independent entity (Ch 4, 10).

**Variety** — The number of states a system can be in. Not intrinsic — a property of how you choose to describe it (Ch 4). **Variety engineering** = deliberately attenuating incoming variety and amplifying regulatory variety until Ashby's Law is satisfied.
