# Cheatsheet — Six Thinking Hats

## The Six Hats at a Glance

| Color | Mode | Focus | Signature question | Typical time |
|-------|------|-------|--------------------|--------------|
| **White** | Neutral / information | Facts & figures; what we have vs. need | "What do we know? What do we need?" | ~1 min/person; extend |
| **Red** | Feeling | Emotions, hunches, intuition — no justification | "What do I feel about this?" | Briefest; often 1 min total |
| **Black** | Caution | Risks, dangers, why it won't work, fit | "What could go wrong? Why won't it work?" | ~1 min/person; extend |
| **Yellow** | Benefit | Value, optimism, feasibility (with reasons) | "What are the benefits? Why will it work?" | ~1 min/person; extend |
| **Green** | Creative | New ideas, alternatives, provocation "po", movement | "What new ideas are possible?" | Set block (e.g. 10 min) |
| **Blue** | Control | Process, agenda, focus, summaries | "What thinking is needed next?" | Bookends + interjections |

Pairs: **white/red**, **black/yellow**, **green/blue**. Always name the **colour**, never the function.

## Recommended Sequences

- **Initial idea exploration**: Blue → White → Green → (Red) → Blue
- **Evaluating a proposal / assessment**: Blue → White → Yellow → Black → (Green to fix difficulties) → Red → Blue
- **Quick decision**: Blue → White → Yellow → Black → Red → Blue
- **Problem solving**: Blue → White → Black (define the problem) → Green (options) → Yellow + Black (assess) → Blue
- **Resolving conflict / charged topic**: Blue → Red (vent feelings) → White → Yellow → Black → Green → Red → Blue

Rules for sequences: blue always bookends; any hat may repeat; you need not use every hat; use **pre-set** (planned up front) not **evolving** until experienced.

## Decision Rules

- **When a meeting turns adversarial or ego-clogged** → call Red Hat (surface the feelings), then Blue Hat (reset structure).
- **When an idea is dismissed on first sight** → "Let's have some yellow hat on it" before any black hat.
- **When assessing a NEW idea** → Yellow before Black, so difficulties become problems worth solving.
- **When strong feelings pre-exist** → open with Red — UNLESS the boss's feeling would make everyone conform, or no feelings pre-exist.
- **When an objection is "I don't like it"** → that's Red, not Black; ask for the logical Black-hat reason.
- **When stuck in the same old ideas** → Green Hat, deliberate effort from everyone, judgement deferred.
- **When you need to break a fixed pattern** → put an idea forward as "po" and use it for movement, not judgement.
- **When green produces too many ideas** → fast Red Hat to pick those fitting a frame; park the rest.
- **When someone won't switch hats** → only the Blue-hat leader changes hats; staying critical after a switch = refusing to play the game.
- **When a decision won't come at the final Blue** → name why (missing info / clashing values), design a way covering both, or make an honest Red-hat decision — all decisions are finally red hat.

## Never

- Never use the **Black Hat to attack a person's idea to win** — it's caution, not argument.
- Never assign one person a **fixed hat** for the whole meeting — everyone wears every hat.
- Never **label people** by hat ("she's black hat") — hats are directions, not descriptions.
- Never demand **justification** under the Red Hat.
- Never state a **believed** fact as a checked one under the White Hat — frame the likelihood.
