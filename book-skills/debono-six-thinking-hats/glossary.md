# Glossary — Six Thinking Hats

**Assessment hats** — Red, yellow and black: the hats used to evaluate (feelings, benefits, cautions), not merely gather information — why a decision follows exploration (Ch 8).

**Backward effect** — Judging an idea by how well it fits past experience and existing patterns; the mode of the black and yellow hats, deliberately set aside under green (Ch 6).

**Black Hat** — Caution mode: risks, dangers, difficulties, why something won't work or doesn't fit; the most used and most valuable hat, but not for winning arguments (Ch 4).

**Blue Hat** — Process-control mode: thinking about thinking — orchestration, agenda, focus, discipline, summaries and conclusions; the conductor (Ch 7).

**Blue-hat bookends** — The rule that an initial blue hat opens and a final blue hat closes every structured session (Ch 1, Ch 7, Ch 8).

**Directions not descriptions** — A hat asks what is *about to* happen (a direction to think in), never labels what already happened or who a person is (Ch 0, Ch 1).

**Discipline** — Members stay with the indicated hat; only the leader changes hats; the rule that keeps parallel thinking from collapsing into argument (Ch 1, Ch 8).

**Ego, removal of** — In argument the ego is tied to being right; under the hats you show off by performing well as a thinker in every direction (Ch 0).

**Evolving sequence** — Choosing each next hat as you go; not recommended until experienced — wastes time and looks manipulative (Ch 1, Ch 8).

**Facts, first-class vs. second-class** — Checked/proven facts vs. facts believed true but not yet verified; kept distinct under the white hat (Ch 2).

**Focusing questions** — Narrowing an information request to draw out exactly what is needed and avoid being drowned in data (Ch 2).

**Green Hat** — Creative mode: new ideas, alternatives, possibilities, provocation and movement; deliberate scheduled creative effort (Ch 6).

**Lateral thinking** — A set of attitudes, idioms and techniques (movement, provocation, po) for cutting across patterns in a self-organising asymmetric patterning system to generate new concepts (Ch 6).

**Likelihood frame** — Labelling information by its degree of truth ("always," "by and large," "sometimes," "believed") so nothing is stated more firmly than known (Ch 2).

**Map-making** — Exploring and laying out the whole terrain before choosing a route, versus picking a conclusion first and defending it (Ch 0, Ch 7).

**Mismatch mechanism** — The brain's natural flagging of anything that doesn't fit expected patterns; the basis of easy black-hat caution (Ch 4).

**Movement** — Active green-hat idiom: using an idea for its forward effect (where it leads), not judging its fit; more than merely suspending judgement (Ch 6).

**No-justification rule** — Under the red hat, feelings are expressed as they are and never explained or justified (Ch 3).

**Operacy** — The "doing" skill of thinking — concern with effectiveness and making things happen, associated with the yellow hat (Ch 5).

**Parallel thinking** — Everyone thinks in the same direction (wears the same hat) at the same moment, laying views side by side, instead of adversarial argument (Ch 0).

**Po (provocative operation)** — A word marking an idea put forward as a provocation for its movement value; acts as a flag of truce protecting it from black-hat judgement (Ch 6).

**Provocation** — A deliberately illogical or reversed idea used as a stepping stone to jerk thinking out of fixed patterns (Ch 6).

**Red Hat** — Emotion mode: feelings, hunches, intuition, expressed without justification and on an individual basis (Ch 3).

**Reversal** — A simple provocation technique: state how something usually happens, then reverse it ("po, the store pays the customer") (Ch 6).

**Sensitisation** — The brain tunes chemically in one direction at a time (danger vs. benefit); you can't be optimally sensitised two ways at once — the basis for separating hats (Ch 0, Ch 4).

**Sequence, pre-set** — A hat order laid out in advance under the initial blue hat and then followed, with minor variations (Ch 1, Ch 8).

**Signalling** — A hat can request thinking of others, indicate a mode, signal your own mode, or (key for green) signal to yourself to make deliberate effort (Ch 1, Ch 6).

**Single use** — Invoking one hat on demand within an ordinary discussion to request a mode of thinking (Ch 1).

**Six hats / six directions** — White (facts), Red (feelings), Black (caution), Yellow (benefit), Green (creativity), Blue (control); paired as white/red, black/yellow, green/blue (Ch 0, Ch 1).

**Speculative-positive** — De Bono's name for the yellow hat: looking forward for value and feasibility as a deliberate choice (Ch 5).

**Value sensitivity** — A trained habit of being as alert to value as we naturally are to danger; needed because the yellow hat has no natural brain support (Ch 5).

**White Hat** — Information mode: neutral, objective facts and figures — what we have and what we need — like a computer (Ch 2).

**Yellow Hat** — Benefit mode: optimism, value, feasibility, logically-based; harder to wear than the black hat (Ch 5).
