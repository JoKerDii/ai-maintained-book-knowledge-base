# Chapter 7: The Blue Hat — Process Control and Orchestration

## Core Idea
Blue is the cool sky above everything: the hat for thinking about thinking. The blue hat organises the process — it defines the focus, sets the sequence of the other hats, enforces the discipline, announces switches, and produces summaries and conclusions. It is the conductor of the orchestra.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Blue-hat bookends**: An initial blue hat opens the session and a final blue hat closes it.
  - When to use: Every structured session, and whenever thinking needs direction (especially thinking alone).
  - How — **Initial blue** lays out: why we are here, what we are thinking about, the definition (and alternative definitions) of the situation, what we want to achieve, where we want to end up, the background, and the plan/sequence of hats. **Final blue** delivers: what we achieved, outcome, conclusion, design, solution, next steps.
- **Process control during the session**: The blue hat keeps discipline, holds people to the current hat, and announces every change of hat.
  - When to use: Continuously through the session.
  - How: Only the blue-hat role changes hats ("White hat thinking at this stage… now we need some proposals — yellow hat"); it stops argument and insists on map-making.
- **Summaries and monitoring**: The blue hat asks for overviews and conclusions periodically and at the end.
  - When to use: To checkpoint progress and harvest outcomes.
  - How: Request a summary; if little progress was made, say so, and define a new focus for a future session.

## Key Concepts
- **Thinking about thinking**: Wearing the blue hat you stop thinking about the subject and think about the thinking the subject needs.
- **The programming hat**: Blue lays out a plan for thinking — a sequence of operations — like a program, or steers moment-to-moment like a coachman guiding horses.
- **Focus**: Blue defines the subject, shapes the questions, and sets the thinking task; sharp focus is a blue-hat product.
- **Permanent role vs. open comment**: One person (facilitator/chair/leader) typically holds the blue-hat role throughout, but anyone may offer a blue-hat procedural suggestion.
- **Structure replaces argument**: In map-making thinking, attack-and-defence can no longer supply momentum, so the blue hat supplies the organising structure instead.
- **Discipline enforcement**: The blue hat stops the thinking degenerating back into free-flowing argument or a "thinking stew."

## Mental Models
- **Use the Blue Hat when** a discussion is drifting, waffling, or reacting moment-to-moment with no spelled-out objective — impose structure.
- **Open and close with blue**: define the focus and plan up front; harvest the outcome and next steps at the end.
- **Let only blue change the hat**: centralised switching is what keeps everyone thinking in parallel.
- **Use blue to interject a needed hat**: "Putting on my blue hat, I'd suggest some red hat thinking to clear the air."

## Anti-patterns
- **No structure (the "thinking stew")**: Suggestion, judgement, criticism, information and emotion all mixed together, guided only by negativity until someone stumbles on an answer.
- **Conclusion-first thinking**: Writing the conclusion in the first line and using the rest to defend it — the essay/courtroom/politics habit the blue hat exists to replace with exploration.
- **Distributed hat-switching**: Everyone declaring their own hat — this collapses parallel thinking back into argument.

## Worked Example
The initial blue hat runs a meeting like a program: it states why the group is meeting, defines the problem (and considers alternative definitions), fixes what success looks like, and lays out the hat sequence to be followed. Then during the session it steers — "White hat thinking at this stage… now we need some proposals, that means yellow hat, concrete suggestions please" — guiding the horses from moment to moment. At the close, the final blue hat asks for the outcome: a summary, conclusion or decision, plus the next steps — and if no decision was reached, it names why and defines the focus for the next session.

## Key Takeaways
1. The blue hat is process control — thinking about the thinking, not the subject.
2. Bookend every session: initial blue defines focus and sequence; final blue delivers outcome and next steps.
3. Only the blue-hat role changes hats and enforces discipline, though anyone may offer blue-hat suggestions.
4. Blue supplies the structure that replaces argument in map-making thinking.
5. Blue produces the summaries, overviews and conclusions along the way and at the end.
