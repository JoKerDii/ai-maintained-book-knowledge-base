# Chapter 3: The Red Hat — Emotions, Feelings, Intuition

## Core Idea
Red is fire and warmth. The red hat gives permission to express feelings, emotions, hunches and intuition about an idea — **without any need to explain or justify them**. Emotions enter thinking anyway; the red hat makes them visible instead of disguised as logic.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **No-justification rule**: Feelings are expressed as they exist at that moment, and justification is never permitted.
  - When to use: Every red-hat round — the moment you ask people to explain, they only offer feelings they can defend, and the real feelings stay hidden.
  - How: Go round the group, each person in turn stating a feeling; no "pass" allowed, but "neutral," "undecided," "mixed," "doubtful," "confused" are valid answers.
- **Applied to a specific idea**: The red hat is always aimed at a defined idea or situation; the thinker may not change the idea to make it likeable.
  - When to use: Whenever gauging reaction to a proposal.
  - How: State exactly what the feeling is about; if needed, the chair puts different versions of an idea and takes a red-hat reading on each.
- **Before-and-after readings**: Take a red hat early to surface pre-existing feelings, and another late to see whether they have shifted.
  - When to use: Emotionally charged subjects, or to check whether the thinking changed anyone's mind.
  - How: One brisk round at the start, another near the end; compare.

## Key Concepts
- **Two types of feeling**: Ordinary emotions (fear, dislike, suspicion) and complex, unjustified judgements (hunch, intuition, sense, taste, aesthetic feeling).
- **Intellectual feelings**: "I feel the idea has potential / is interesting / is unusual" are valid red-hat statements.
- **Intuition**: May rest on deep experience and be useful — but is not always right (even Einstein's intuition failed on Heisenberg's uncertainty principle).
- **Individual basis**: Each person present is asked in turn; red-hat feeling is personal, not a group vote.
- **Brisk timing**: Because no explanation is given, a red hat is fast — often one minute for everyone to speak.
- **Feelings as part of the map**: Making feelings visible lets them become part of the value system that finally chooses the route.

## Mental Models
- **Use the Red Hat when** unspoken emotion is clogging a discussion — put it on the table up front so it stops distorting the "logic."
- **Never demand a reason**: the value of the red hat is precisely that it bypasses justification; asking "why do you feel that?" destroys it.
- **Ask what's in the mix**: if someone reports "mixed," the facilitator may ask what goes into the mix — the one legitimate probe.
- **Remember all decisions are finally red hat**: you lay out facts and pros and cons, but the last choice is emotional.

## Anti-patterns
- **Requiring validation**: Forcing people to justify feelings filters out the honest, un-defendable ones — the very signal you wanted.
- **Letting the red hat drift into debate**: It is a snapshot of feeling, not an argument about whether the feeling is warranted.
- **Changing the idea to suit the feeling**: "I'd like it if contributions were voluntary" is not a red-hat response to a proposal about compulsory contributions.

## Worked Example
Before South Africa's first elections, de Bono taught the Six Hats to the heads of the Peace Accord Committees handling local problems. They would often **open** meetings with the red hat — giving everyone a chance to vent their feelings and emotions before any analysis. Getting the charged emotion out in the open, briefly and without argument, cleared the air so the substantive thinking could proceed in parallel rather than through confrontation. (De Bono cautions against an opening red hat when the boss's stated feeling would make everyone conform, or when no strong feelings pre-exist.)

## Key Takeaways
1. The red hat legitimises feelings and intuition as a real part of thinking.
2. Express feelings as they are, right now — never explain or justify them.
3. Go round individually; "pass" is not allowed, but neutral/mixed/doubtful are.
4. Keep it brisk; take a reading early and again late to catch shifts.
5. Intuition can carry deep experience but is not infallible — log it, don't obey it blindly.
