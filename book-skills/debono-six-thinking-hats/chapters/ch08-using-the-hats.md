# Chapter 8: Using the Hats — Sequences, Discipline, Outcomes

## Core Idea
The hats are used either singly (call a mode on demand) or in a sequence (run through in order to explore or solve). Done with discipline and short timeboxes, the method maps a subject so thoroughly that, at the final blue hat, **the decision often makes itself**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Pre-set vs. evolving sequences**: Lay the sequence out in advance under the initial blue hat (pre-set), rather than choosing each hat as you go (evolving).
  - When to use: Pre-set until you are experienced; evolving risks arguing over hats and looking manipulative.
  - How: Any hat may repeat; you need not use every hat; two to four or more hats is normal. Minor variations depending on output are allowed.
- **Guideline orderings**: There is no one right sequence — any that makes sense as a thinking strategy works.
  - When to use: Match the ordering to the task (exploration, problem-solving, dispute, decision).
  - How — Blue bookends always. **Opening red** when strong feelings already exist (get them out early). **Yellow before black** in assessment (find value first, so difficulties motivate rather than deter). **Final red** to reflect on the thinking performance ("Are we happy with the outcome?").
- **When NOT to open with red**: Skip the initial red hat if the boss's stated feeling would make everyone conform, or if there are no pre-existing feelings to surface.

## Key Concepts
- **Discipline**: Members stay with the indicated hat; only the leader changes it; hats indicate direction, not a licence to say what you wanted anyway.
- **Timing**: Set a short time — about one minute per person — and extend only while genuine points flow; the red hat is briefest.
- **Individual and group**: Benefits are greatest in groups, but individuals can use the sequenced framework to reduce confusion, and reports can be structured by the hats to cover every aspect (including caution, without offence).
- **The decision makes itself**: When the terrain is fully mapped, the best route becomes obvious to everyone — like choosing a road once you have the road map.
- **Assessment hats**: Red, yellow and black are used for assessment, not just information — this is why a decision follows the exploration.
- **All decisions are finally red hat**: You lay out the factors, but the last choice is emotional; if values can't be reconciled, make a red-hat decision.

## Mental Models
- **Use a pre-set sequence when** you want a subject explored without mid-meeting fights over what to do next.
- **Yellow-then-black in appraisals**: seeing value first turns difficulties into problems worth overcoming.
- **Open with red only when feelings pre-exist** and the boss won't skew them; otherwise start elsewhere.
- **Close with the final blue hat**: harvest outcome, conclusion, next steps — or, if stuck, define the new focus for another session.

## Anti-patterns
- **Fixed-role hats**: Assigning one person the black hat for the whole meeting — the opposite of the method.
- **Over-long timeboxes**: People sit around waffling; short-and-extend beats long-and-idle.
- **Forcing a decision when values genuinely conflict**: Better to design a way forward covering both choices, or make an honest red-hat decision, than to fake objectivity.

## Worked Example
A man wrote to de Bono after reading a short newspaper write-up of the method. He and his wife had argued for hours over whether to buy a large house in the country and could not decide. He suggested they use the Hats. Within ten minutes they reached a decision that satisfied both. Once feelings (red), facts (white), benefits (yellow) and cautions (black) were each laid out separately and in the open — the same process a mind runs privately, but done systematically — the choice became obvious. That is the recurring result: thorough parallel exploration, then a decision that seems to make itself.

## Key Takeaways
1. Use single hats for live discussion, pre-set sequences for structured sessions; stay pre-set until experienced.
2. Blue bookends always; open with red only when feelings pre-exist; put yellow before black in assessment.
3. Keep discipline and short timeboxes; only the leader switches hats.
4. The hats work for individuals and reports too, not just group meetings.
5. Map thoroughly and the decision tends to make itself — and all decisions are, finally, red hat.
