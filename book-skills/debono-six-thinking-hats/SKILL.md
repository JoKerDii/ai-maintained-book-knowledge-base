## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**Parallel thinking (the spine).** Instead of adversarial argument — where each party defends a fixed position and withholds anything that helps the "other side" — **everyone wears the same hat at the same moment and thinks in the same direction**. Contradictory views are laid side by side "in parallel," and a choice between them is made later only if it must be. The core value: do **one type of thinking at a time** instead of trying to handle emotion, information, logic, hope and creativity all at once. A hat is a **direction to think in**, never a label for a person.

**The six hats** — always referred to by colour, never function (the colour is neutral; "take off your black hat" is easy where "stop being cautious" is not):

- **WHITE = neutral facts & information.** What we have vs. what we need; play a computer, no interpretation. *"What do we know? What do we need?"* (Ch 2)
- **RED = emotions, feelings, intuition.** Expressed as they are, individually, with **no justification required**. *"What do I feel about this?"* (Ch 3)
- **BLACK = caution & critical judgement.** Risks, difficulties, why it won't work or doesn't fit; logically based. The **most used and most valuable** hat — but not for winning arguments. *"What could go wrong? Why won't it work?"* (Ch 4)
- **YELLOW = benefits, optimism, feasibility.** Value with reasons; **harder to wear than black** (no natural brain support). *"What are the benefits? Why will it work?"* (Ch 5)
- **GREEN = creativity & new ideas.** Alternatives, possibilities, provocation ("**po**"), and **movement** (use an idea for where it leads, not to judge it). *"What new ideas are possible?"* (Ch 6)
- **BLUE = process control.** Orchestration, agenda, focus, discipline, summaries — the conductor. *"What thinking is needed next?"* (Ch 7)

Pairs: white/red, black/yellow, green/blue.

**How to run them.** Deliberate **role-play** — putting the hat on gives permission to think that way (and to *signal* your mode to others and to yourself). The **Blue Hat orchestrates**: it bookends every session (initial blue defines focus and the planned hat sequence; final blue harvests outcome and next steps), enforces discipline, and is the only role that changes hats. Two modes of use: **single hats** called on demand in a live discussion, or a **pre-set sequence** run in order (use pre-set, not evolving, until experienced). Keep it **disciplined and timeboxed** — about one minute per person, extend while genuine points flow; red hat briefest.

**Key orderings.** *Use the White Hat when* you need the base map first. *Use the Red Hat when* unspoken emotion is distorting the discussion — surface it up front (unless the boss would skew it or no feelings pre-exist). *Use Yellow before Black* on new ideas, so difficulties become problems worth solving. *Use the Green Hat when* stuck in the same old ideas — defer judgement, use "po" provocations for movement. *Use the Blue Hat when* thinking drifts into waffle. Map thoroughly and **the decision often makes itself** — and, finally, all decisions are red hat.

Preserve exact names: **parallel thinking**, **lateral thinking**, **po**, **movement**.

## Scope & Limits
A practical method for directing thinking and running discussions, not a theory of psychology or a decision-analysis algorithm; the hats structure and separate thinking but the final choice remains a human (red-hat) judgement.
