# Patterns & Techniques — Six Thinking Hats

Reusable ways to deploy the hats in real discussions, meetings, and solo thinking.

## Single-Hat Request
**When to use**: A live discussion drifts into one register too long, or needs a specific mode injected right now.
**How**: Name the colour, not the function — "I think we need some green hat thinking here"; "Let's have some black hat on this"; "Yes, that's fine black hat; now let's try the yellow hat." You need not specify a hat every time you speak.
**Trade-offs**: Depends on everyone knowing the idiom; feeble if the group hasn't been trained. Great for steering without personal friction.

## Blue-Hat Bookends
**When to use**: Any structured session, and especially thinking alone (which needs structure most).
**How**: Open with an initial blue hat (why we're here, definition and alternative definitions, goal, background, planned hat sequence). Close with a final blue hat (outcome, conclusion, decision, next steps; or, if stuck, define a new focus).
**Trade-offs**: Adds a little ceremony up front; pays back in focus and a clean, harvestable outcome.

## Pre-Set Sequence
**When to use**: Group exploration or problem-solving you want mapped without mid-meeting arguments over what to do next.
**How**: Lay the whole hat order out at the start under the initial blue hat, then follow it (minor variations allowed by output). Any hat may repeat; no need to use every hat.
**Trade-offs**: Less adaptive than evolving on the fly — but evolving sequences waste time and look manipulative until the group is experienced.

## Opening Red Hat (Vent / Surface Emotion)
**When to use**: When strong feelings already exist on the subject and are distorting the "logic."
**How**: Immediately after the first blue hat, go round the group for a brisk red-hat feeling each — no explanations. Get the emotion into the open early.
**Trade-offs**: Skip it if the boss's stated feeling would make everyone conform, or if no strong feelings pre-exist (nothing to surface). Consider a second red hat later to check for shifts.

## Yellow-Before-Black (New-Idea Assessment)
**When to use**: Appraising a new idea or proposal.
**How**: Run the yellow hat first to find and reason out the value; only then run the black hat for difficulties. If no value appears under yellow, stop — no point proceeding.
**Trade-offs**: Feels counter-intuitive (we default to criticism), but seeing benefit first turns difficulties into problems worth overcoming rather than reasons to quit.

## Timeboxing Each Hat
**When to use**: Every hat, to force concentration and cut waffle.
**How**: Set a short time — about one minute per person present — and extend only while genuine points still flow. Keep the red hat briefest (often one minute total, since no justification is given).
**Trade-offs**: Under-allotting risks cutting a productive vein — hence "extend while real points come." Far better than long slots with people sitting idle.

## One-Minute Green-Hat Effort
**When to use**: To break "same old ideas" fixation, or to build creative confidence.
**How**: Deliberately put on the green hat and require a creative effort from everyone (or keep quiet). Even a barren effort counts — signalling to yourself sets aside the time and raises future yield.
**Trade-offs**: May produce nothing on a given round; the value is cumulative habit, not guaranteed instant ideas.

## Provocation with "Po"
**When to use**: Thinking is trapped in existing patterns and needs jerking out.
**How**: Put forward a deliberately illogical idea marked "po" ("po, a polluting factory should be downstream of itself"), or use reversal (state the norm, then reverse it). Then use it for *movement* — where does it lead? — not judgement. Po protects it from black-hat attack.
**Trade-offs**: Uncomfortable for judgement-minded people; requires discipline to defer criticism until after movement.

## Solo / Individual Thinking Within a Group
**When to use**: To get more and deeper ideas, especially under green, yellow or black.
**How**: The chair pauses a hat for two minutes of silent individual thinking, or goes round person by person for each one's thinking under the current hat.
**Trade-offs**: Slows the flow briefly; worth it when group cross-talk is crowding out real thought.

## Green → Red Triage
**When to use**: The green hat has produced more ideas than there's time to assess.
**How**: Use a fast red hat to pick the ideas that fit a frame ("low-cost," "easy to test"); park the rest for later. Keeps the creative energy usable.
**Trade-offs**: Feeling-based selection may drop a slow-burn idea — so park, don't discard.

## Final Red Hat (Reflect on the Thinking)
**When to use**: After the final blue hat, to appraise the session itself.
**How**: One quick round — "What do we feel about our thinking? Are we happy with the outcome? Did we do a good job?"
**Trade-offs**: Optional; useful for teams building the habit and for catching lingering unease before acting.
