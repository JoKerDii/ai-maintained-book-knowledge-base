## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**Decisions as bets (the book's spine).** Every decision is a bet on an uncertain future — it commits resources to one outcome and forecloses others; "not betting" is itself a bet. Most bets are against alternative future versions of ourselves. A bet is only as good as the belief behind it. *Use whenever a choice's outcome isn't fully in your control: name the alternatives, what's at stake, and your probabilities.*

**Decision quality ≠ outcome quality.** How life turns out = decision quality + luck. A good process can produce a bad result (bad luck) and a bad process a good one (good luck). Judge the bet, not the roll.

**Resulting — the core error.** Judging a decision by how it turned out (Pete Carroll's Super Bowl pass was a good bet with a ~2% bad result). *Countermove: ask "given what was knowable then, was this a good bet?" separate from the outcome.*

**Life is poker, not chess.** Poker = hidden information + real luck, so outcomes are a noisy signal of decision quality; chess = perfect information, outcomes track decisions. Sort your domain: the more hidden info and luck, the less a single result tells you.

**Beliefs: hear → believe → (maybe) vet.** We default to believing what we hear, then defend it with motivated reasoning. Being smart makes this worse (better at spinning data). *Use "Wanna bet?" as a vetting trigger whenever you feel certain.*

**Redefining "wrong" & calibration.** A non-0%/100% prediction can't be proven wrong by one outcome. Hold beliefs as probabilities; express confidence as a % or range; update in small increments instead of flipping right↔wrong. Declaring uncertainty makes you more credible and invites others' input.

**Be a truthseeker.** Want what's true over what flatters you (the red pill). Accuracy over confirmation.

**Fielding outcomes (luck vs. skill).** Learning requires sorting each result into a luck bucket or skill bucket, then feeding skill-driven results into belief-updating. Self-serving bias credits wins to skill and blames losses on luck — breaking the loop. *Use: "would the same decision predictably repeat this?" + perspective-taking.*

**The buddy system / truthseeking pod + charter.** Recruit ~3 people (two to disagree, one to referee) into a modified social contract chartered for **accuracy, accountability, and openness to diverse views**. Groups amplify bias by default (echo chambers); a charter forces *exploratory* over *confirmatory* thought.

**Communicating for feedback — dissent to win (CUDOS).** Merton's norms: **C**ommunism (share all data, "more is more"), **U**niversalism (judge the idea, not the messenger), **D**isinterestedness (outcome/belief blindness — don't lead with your conclusion or the result), **O**rganized Skepticism (civil dissent, "I'm not sure" not "you're wrong"). Outside your group: express uncertainty, lead with assent ("and" not "but"), ask permission, focus on the future.

**Mental time travel.** Recruit past- and future-selves as decision buddies to engage deliberative mind and escape the distorting zoom lens (tilt, ticker watching, temporal discounting). Toolkit: **10-10-10** (10 min/months/years), **backcasting** (work back from success), **premortem** (work back from failure), **scenario planning** (map futures + probabilities), **Ulysses contracts** (precommit to bind future-self), **decision swear jar** (flag irrational language). Life is one long game — take satisfaction in good bets, not guaranteed outcomes.

> Practitioner defaults: stop *resulting*, express beliefs as ranges, field outcomes into luck/skill honestly, recruit a CUDOS pod, hide the outcome when seeking feedback, and run 10-10-10 / premortems before consequential bets. See [patterns.md](patterns.md) and [cheatsheet.md](cheatsheet.md).
