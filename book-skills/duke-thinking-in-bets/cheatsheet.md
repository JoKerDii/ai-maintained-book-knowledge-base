# Cheatsheet — Thinking in Bets

Decision rules and tells to keep beside you while deciding, learning, and communicating under uncertainty.

## Core reflexes

- **Bad outcome? Ask first: bad decision or bad luck?** Judge the bet given what was knowable, not the roll. (Avoid *resulting*.)
- **A non-0%/100% prediction can't be "wrong" from one result.** A 2% event happening doesn't refute "2%." Judge calibration over many bets.
- **Replace "Am I sure?" with "How sure?"** State a % or a range; update in small increments, never a right↔wrong flip.
- **Nearly nothing is 0% or 100%.** If you're saying "always/never/certain," you're probably miscalibrated.
- **You're already guessing whenever you decide.** So make the probabilities explicit — a wide range beats no estimate.

## Tells → what's happening → countermove

| If you notice… | You're probably… | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| Grading a decision by how it turned out | Resulting | Reconstruct what was knowable; judge the bet, not the result |
| Crediting your wins to skill, blaming losses on luck | Self-serving bias / fielding error | Ask "would this repeat?"; take the other's perspective |
| "So unlucky," "I always get screwed" | Motivated outcome fielding | Field into luck *and* skill buckets; look for a controllable |
| Dismissing an idea because of who said it | Shooting the message (universalism fail) | Judge the claim on merit; imagine a source you respect |
| "I knew it all along," "it was bound to happen" | Hindsight bias | Rebuild the tree; the outcome was one branch of many |
| "Worst day ever," reacting to the last half hour | Ticker watching / zoom lens / tilt | Run 10-10-10; take the long view; step away |
| Everyone in the room agrees with you | Echo chamber / confirmatory drift | Seek dissent; add someone who disagrees |
| The detail you want to leave out | Communism fail (spinning a narrative) | Share it — hesitation is the signal it matters |

## CUDOS checklist (rules of engagement for a truthseeking group)
- **C**ommunism — is all the data on the table? "More is more."
- **U**niversalism — am I judging the idea, not the messenger?
- **D**isinterestedness — did I hide the outcome and my own opinion so I don't infect them?
- **O**rganized Skepticism — am I asking "why might this not be true?" civilly?

## Are you on tilt? (don't decide if yes)
Verbal tells: "Seriously? Again?", swearing, "why do I bother." Physical tells: flushed cheeks, racing heart, fast breathing.
→ Precommit to walk away, take ten breaths, sleep on it. Ask: "Will this matter in the long run?"

## Which time-travel tool?
| Situation | Tool |
|---|---|
| Hot, in-the-moment choice | 10-10-10 |
| Map the path to a goal | Backcasting (positive space) |
| Stress-test a plan before committing | Premortem (negative space) — pair with backcast; the two sum to 100% |
| Predictable future temptation | Ulysses contract (raise or lower a barrier) |
| Choosing among uncertain futures | Scenario planning + expected value |
| Catch yourself veering live | Decision swear jar |

## Talking to people who haven't opted in
1. **Express uncertainty** first ("I'm not sure, but…").
2. **Lead with assent**, then "**and**" — never "but."
3. **Ask permission**: "Want to vent, or looking for advice?"
4. **Focus on the future**: "What could you do so it turns out better next time?"

> Life is poker, not chess — and it's one long game. Take satisfaction in making good bets; outcomes will vary. Calibrate a little better each time.
