# Glossary — Thinking in Bets

**10-10-10** — A decision routine (Suzy Welch): weigh the consequences of each option in 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years to recruit future-self and defuse in-the-moment emotion (Ch 6).

**Accountability** — A willingness or obligation to answer to others for our beliefs and actions; a bet is a form of accountability that makes us hypervigilant about our confidence (Ch 4).

**All decisions are bets** — Every choice commits resources to one uncertain future while foreclosing others; "not betting" is itself a bet (Intro, Ch 2).

**Backcasting** — Imagining you've already achieved a goal ("We Achieved Our Goal!") and working backward to identify how you got there; reveals the positive path (Ch 6).

**Belief-formation flaw** — We don't hear→vet→believe; we hear→believe→(maybe) vet. Default is to believe what we hear (Gilbert) (Ch 2).

**Bet** — A decision about an uncertain future that risks resources on one outcome; the book's core reframe (Intro, Ch 2).

**Blind-spot bias** — Spotting bias in others but not ourselves; larger the smarter/more numerate we are. A group helps fill in our blind spots (Ch 2, Ch 4).

**Calibration** — Holding beliefs as probabilities and updating in small increments toward accuracy, instead of flipping between right and wrong (Ch 1, Ch 2).

**Confirmatory thought** — One-sided reasoning that rationalizes a held view; amplifies bias and breeds groupthink (Ch 4).

**CUDOS** — Merton's four norms for a truthseeking group: Communism, Universalism, Disinterestedness, Organized Skepticism (Ch 5).

**Communism (Mertonian)** — Data belong to the group; share everything, "more is more" (Ch 5).

**Decision-interrupt** — The stop-and-think moment a precommitment creates when you consider breaking it (Ch 6).

**Decision quality ≠ outcome quality** — A good process can yield a bad result (bad luck) and vice versa; judge the bet, not the roll (Intro, Ch 1).

**Decision swear jar** — A precommitment to flag words/thoughts signaling irrationality ("I'm sure," "so unlucky," "wrong"), triggering reflection (Ch 6).

**Devil's advocate** — An assigned role to argue against a position, raising alternative hypotheses (from the Church's canonization process) (Ch 5).

**Disinterestedness (Mertonian)** — Vigilance against conflicts of interest; use outcome/belief blindness so evaluators aren't infected (Ch 5).

**Dissent Channel / red team** — Institutional mechanisms (State Dept., CIA) that protect and reward dissenting views (Ch 4, Ch 5).

**Echo chamber** — A homogeneous group that repeats and confirms its own views; the natural drift of confirmatory thought (Ch 4).

**Expected value** — Probability-weighted worth of an option (a $50k grant at 70% beats a $200k grant at 10%) (Ch 6).

**Exploratory thought** — Even-handed consideration of alternative viewpoints; moves a group toward accuracy (Tetlock & Lerner) (Ch 4).

**Fielding outcomes** — Deciding whether a result came from skill (your decisions) or luck (outside your control); the mechanism of learning (Ch 3).

**Hindsight bias** — After an outcome, seeing it as inevitable ("I should have known"); the chainsaw that lops off unrealized branches of the tree (Ch 1, Ch 6).

**Learning loop** — Outcomes → field into luck/skill → update beliefs → better bets; fielding errors sever it (Ch 3).

**"Lettermanning"** — Offering a truthseeking challenge to someone who hasn't agreed to it, so it lands as an attack (Ch 4).

**Loss limit** — A preset stop-loss enforced by accountability to a group; a check against chasing losses while irrational (Ch 4, Ch 6).

**Luck** — The influence on outcomes of things outside our control (others' actions, weather, genes, hidden cards) (Intro, Ch 3).

**Mental contrasting** — Oettingen's finding that imagining obstacles (not just success) makes goals more achievable; basis of the premortem (Ch 6).

**Mental time travel** — Recruiting past- and future-selves as decision buddies to engage deliberative mind (Ch 6).

**Motivated reasoning** — Processing information to protect existing beliefs — seeking confirmation, discrediting the rest (Ch 2).

**Organized skepticism (Mertonian)** — Engaging dissent through civil discussion; asking "why might this not be true?" (Ch 5).

**Outcome blindness** — Presenting a decision up to the decision point, omitting the result, so it's judged on process not outcome (Ch 5).

**Path dependence** — Emotional response driven by the recent path, not the overall result (win $100 and feel sad, lose $100 and feel happy) (Ch 6).

**Poker vs. chess** — A model of decision environments: chess = perfect info, little luck; poker = hidden info, real luck (Ch 1).

**Premortem** — Imagining you've failed ("We Failed to Reach Our Goal") and listing why; reveals the negative space (Klein, Oettingen) (Ch 6).

**Rashomon Effect** — Different people give drastically different accounts of the same event; no one version is complete (Ch 5).

**Reconnaissance / scenario planning** — Mapping the range of possible futures of a decision and assigning probabilities (Ch 6).

**Redefining "wrong"** — A non-0%/100% prediction can't be proven wrong by a single outcome; judge calibration over many bets (Ch 1).

**Resulting** — Judging a decision's quality by its outcome; the core error to avoid (Ch 1).

**Self-serving bias** — Fielding wins into the skill bucket and losses into the luck bucket to protect self-image (Ch 3).

**SnackWell's Phenomenon** — Consuming more of something with less of a "bad" ingredient; a case of how hard it is to work backward from outcome to cause (Ch 3).

**Temporal discounting** — Overvaluing a reward now versus a bigger reward later (Night Jerry vs. Morning Jerry) (Ch 6).

**"They saw a game" effect** — Prior beliefs change how we experience the same event (Ch 2).

**Tilt** — Emotionally unhinged decision-making caused by recent outcomes; the limbic system shuts down the prefrontal cortex (Ch 6).

**Truthseeking pod / buddy system** — A small chartered group that helps you fight bias and field outcomes honestly (Ch 4).

**Truthseeking charter** — The explicit agreement for a productive group: accuracy, accountability, openness to diverse views (Ch 4).

**Truthseeker** — Someone who wants to know what's true regardless of whether it flatters existing beliefs (Intro).

**Ticker watching** — Reacting to moment-by-moment fluctuations instead of the long-term trend (Ch 6).

**Ulysses contract** — A precommitment by past-self to bind present-self against a predictable irrational impulse (Odysseus at the mast) (Ch 6).

**Universalism (Mertonian)** — "Don't shoot the message": judge a claim on its merits regardless of its source (Ch 5).

**"Wanna bet?"** — A challenge (real or imagined) that triggers vetting of a belief (Ch 2, Ch 3).

**"Yes, and"** — The improv rule for offering dissent: accept the construct, then add ("and" not "but") (Ch 5).
