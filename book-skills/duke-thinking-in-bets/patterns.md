# Patterns & Techniques — Thinking in Bets

Concrete, reusable practices Duke endorses for making smarter decisions under uncertainty.

## The "Wanna Bet?" Self-Challenge
**When to use**: Whenever you catch yourself stating something as certain.
**How**: Imagine a credible person says "Wanna bet?" Then run the vetting questions — How do I know this? How good is my source? What am I missing? What are the plausible alternatives? Why might I be wrong? — before committing to the belief.
**Trade-offs**: Slows down snap judgments; the payoff is beliefs calibrated closer to the truth and fewer over-confident bets.

## Express Confidence as a Percentage or Range
**When to use**: Any belief or prediction you state to yourself or others.
**How**: Replace "I'm sure" with a number ("I'm 60%") or a range ("Elvis died between 40 and 47"). Update in small increments as evidence arrives rather than flipping right↔wrong.
**Trade-offs**: Feels less authoritative, but it's more credible and *invites* others to add information and dissent.

## Field Outcomes into Luck vs. Skill
**When to use**: Any result you want to learn from.
**How**: Ask "Would the same decision predictably produce this result again?" If yes → skill; if it hinged on things you couldn't control → luck (most outcomes are a blend). Treat the fielding itself as a bet, and use perspective-taking ("What if this had happened to someone else?") to reach the truthful middle.
**Trade-offs**: Fights self-serving bias, which credits wins to skill and blames losses on luck; requires honesty that stings.

## Build a Truthseeking Pod with a CUDOS Charter
**When to use**: To sustain truthseeking that willpower alone can't; habits hard to change.
**How**: Recruit as few as three people (two to disagree, one to referee) who opt into a modified social contract. Charter it on **accuracy** (reward objectivity over confirmation), **accountability** (members answer for beliefs, with advance notice), and **diversity** (actively seek dissent). Operationalize Merton's **CUDOS**: Communism (share all data), Universalism (judge ideas independent of source), Disinterestedness (outcome/belief blindness), Organized Skepticism (civil dissent).
**Trade-offs**: Requires discipline against the natural drift toward clones and echo chambers; must be freely chosen, never imposed.

## Communicate for Feedback (Don't Lead with Your Conclusion)
**When to use**: Seeking honest input on a decision, inside or outside your group.
**How**: Describe the situation up to the decision point and **omit the outcome and your own opinion** ("It doesn't matter how it turned out"). Optionally omit the source too, so the group isn't biased for or against the messenger. Ask specific questions inviting reasons you might be wrong.
**Trade-offs**: Resisting the urge to reveal "how it ends" is hard, but it prevents infecting listeners with resulting and confirmation.

## Truthseek Gently Outside Your Group
**When to use**: With people (kids, coworkers, friends) who haven't agreed to the charter.
**How**: (1) Express your own uncertainty first; (2) lead with assent — state specific points of agreement, then add with "**and**," never "but"; (3) ask permission ("Do you want to vent, or are you looking for advice?"); (4) focus on the future ("What might you do so things turn out better going forward?").
**Trade-offs**: Slower and less direct, but avoids the defensiveness that shuts down the conversation (see Letterman/Conrad).

## Run 10-10-10
**When to use**: In-the-moment decisions where emotion is running high (tired, on tilt, a bad break).
**How**: Ask the consequences of each option in 10 minutes, 10 months, and 10 years — or the past-frame ("How would I feel today if I'd decided this 10 minutes / months / years ago?"). Draw on past experiences to engage deliberative mind.
**Trade-offs**: Won't make you perfectly rational, but keeps momentary events in proportion.

## Backcast (Then Premortem)
**When to use**: Planning toward a goal or committing to a strategy.
**How**: **Backcast** — imagine you've already succeeded ("We Achieved Our Goal!") and work backward to list the decisions and events that got you there. Then run a **premortem** — imagine you've failed ("We Failed to Reach Our Goal") and list every reason why. The positive and negative space must add to 100%.
**Trade-offs**: The premortem feels pessimistic in the moment, but negative visualization (mental contrasting) makes goals *more* achievable and shrinks over-optimism to realistic size.

## Scenario Planning / Reconnaissance of the Future
**When to use**: Before any consequential bet; budgeting, sales pipelines, strategy.
**How**: List the possible futures of a decision, then assign probabilities to each — even wide ranges (20%–80%) beat not guessing. Use expected value (probability × payoff) to prioritize. Memorialize the tree so you don't rewrite it with hindsight.
**Trade-offs**: People resist guessing probabilities, but you're already guessing whenever you decide; making it explicit reduces resulting and hindsight bias.

## Set a Ulysses Contract
**When to use**: Wherever you can predict a future irrational impulse.
**How**: Have past-you bind present-you. Raise barriers (throw out junk food, ride-share to the bar, preset settlement floor, home-buying budget) or lower them (carry healthy snacks, automatic retirement allocation). Even a soft, tamper-able version creates a decision-interrupt — a stop-and-think moment.
**Trade-offs**: Rarely 100% tamper-proof, but the interrupt prompts deliberative thought and reduces reactive decisions.

## Keep a Decision Swear Jar
**When to use**: To catch yourself veering from truthseeking in real time.
**How**: Predefine words/thoughts that signal irrationality — illusion of certainty ("I know," "always," "0%/100%"), irrational fielding ("so unlucky"), universalism violations ("idiot," "typical ___"), zoom-in language ("worst day ever"), motivated reasoning ("everyone agrees"), and especially the word "wrong." When you catch one, stop and reflect.
**Trade-offs**: Your list is personal and never complete, but each catch is a decision-interrupt back toward calibration.
