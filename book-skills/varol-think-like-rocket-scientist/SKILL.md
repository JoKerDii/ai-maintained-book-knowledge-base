## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

The book is a three-stage toolkit: **Launch** (ignite thinking) → **Accelerate** (propel ideas) → **Achieve** (learn from outcomes). "The bottleneck to giant leaps is a thought process, not raw intelligence or a science degree."

### Stage One — LAUNCH (Ch 1–4)

**1. Dance with uncertainty (Ch 1).** Progress happens only in uncertain conditions, yet we crave certainty (the **certainty trap** / "looking for keys under the street lamp"). Use X when Y: *when a scary leap paralyzes you*, name the worst *and* best case in writing to turn unknown unknowns into known unknowns; *when the environment is uncertain and irreversible*, engineer **redundancies** (independent backups) and **margins of safety** sized to the stakes; *when data doesn't fit*, chase the anomaly ("That's funny…") instead of discarding it. Prefer being "uncomfortably uncertain over comfortably wrong." Start walking before you see the path.

**2. Reason from first principles (Ch 2).** Strip a problem to its irreducible fundamentals and reason *up*, instead of *by analogy* (copying others). Use X when Y: *when "we've always done it this way" is the only justification*, you've found an **invisible rule** — question it ("What if this weren't true?"); *when you're too close to your own weaknesses*, run **kill-the-company / red teaming** (play the competitor destroying you, then defend); *when cutting clutter*, apply **Occam's razor** (prefer the simpler of two equal explanations — but "seek simplicity and distrust it"). Innovation often comes from subtracting.

**3. A mind at play (Ch 3).** Breakthroughs are ignited in "the laboratory of the mind." Use X when Y: *when testing an idea cheaply*, run a **thought experiment** ("What if?") — the word *experiment* lowers the stakes; *when a hard problem won't yield*, use **deliberate boredom** (walk, shower, Think Week) to switch to diffuse mode; *when you need novelty*, use **combinatory play** (compare apples and oranges; "What other industry has solved this?"); *when experts are stuck*, bring a **beginner's mind** (shoshin). Cycle solitude ↔ collaboration (the Medici effect).

**4. Moonshot thinking (Ch 4).** Aim **10x, not 10%** — a goal so big the status quo has to go, which *forces* first-principles solutions. Hunt antelopes, not mice. Use X when Y: *when generating*, be the **fly** (divergent, "What *could* we do?"), then the **bee** (convergent evaluation) — in that order; *when planning*, **backcast** from the imagined future (or happy customer); *when executing an ambitious project*, do the **monkey first** (hardest part) and set **kill metrics** before sunk cost distorts judgment. Pair the dreamer with the executor.

### Stage Two — ACCELERATE (Ch 5–7)

**5. Reframe the question (Ch 5).** Breakthroughs begin with a smart *question*, not a smart answer — "a problem well defined is a problem half solved." Beware the **Einstellung effect** (a familiar solution blinds you to a better one). Use X when Y: *when in love with a solution*, separate **strategy from tactics** ("What problem is this tactic here to solve?"); *when repurposing a resource*, see **function → form** to defeat functional fixedness; *when beating a stronger rival*, ask **"What if we did the reverse?"** (invert). ("What if we sent two rovers instead of one?")

**6. Prove yourself wrong (Ch 6).** Facts don't change minds — expect **confirmation bias**. Switch the default from proving right to proving wrong. Use X when Y: *at the outset of an investigation*, hold a **working hypothesis**, not an opinion (change your vocabulary); *to avoid falling in love with one idea*, breed a **family of conflicting hypotheses**; *to test a belief*, try to **falsify** it (every "no" beats a "yes"); *before a big call*, ask "What's missing?" and seek dissent (**steel-man** opponents). Goal: find what's right, not to be right.

**7. Test as you fly, fly as you test (Ch 7).** Make tests mirror real conditions, then operate as you tested — any gap crashes rockets, launches, and interviews. Use X when Y: *validating anything*, **test to fail** (hunt the breaking point, exaggerate flaws); *for a system of parts*, do **systems-level testing** (understand the "and" — Frankenstein's monster); *gauging opinion*, **observe behavior, don't self-report**; *trusting a measurement*, **calibrate** and use **multiple testers**.

### Stage Three — ACHIEVE (Ch 8–9)

**8. Nothing succeeds like failure (Ch 8).** Don't demonize *or* celebrate failure — **learn fast, not fail fast**. Use X when Y: *after a setback*, mine the **intelligent failure** (edge-exploring) as data, distinct from sloppy failure; *judging decisions*, focus on **inputs over outputs** (avoid "resulting"); *building a team*, create **psychological safety** (leaders advertise their own failures; "reward excellent failures, punish mediocre successes"). A moratorium on failure is a moratorium on progress.

**9. Nothing fails like success (Ch 9).** Success is "a lousy teacher" and a wolf in sheep's clothing — it breeds complacency and **normalizes deviance** (an "acceptable risk" until catastrophe). Use X when Y: *after a win*, treat it as a **permanent work in progress** (drop "routine") and run a **postmortem on the success**; *before acting*, run a **premortem** (assume failure, list causes, assign probabilities); *always*, mine **near misses**, fix the **cause behind the cause**, and distrust the safety net (**risk homeostasis**). "It remains Day 1" (Bezos).

> Practitioner spine: **Launch** — dance with uncertainty, first principles, a mind at play, moonshots. **Accelerate** — reframe, prove yourself wrong, test as you fly. **Achieve** — intelligent failure, guard against the success trap.
