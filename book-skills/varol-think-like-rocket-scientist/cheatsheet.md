# Cheatsheet — Think Like a Rocket Scientist

Decision rules and tells for reasoning under uncertainty, generating ideas, testing them, and learning from outcomes.

## If / Then decision rules

| If you're… | Then… |
|---|---|
| Paralyzed by a scary leap | Name the worst *and* best case in writing; turn unknown unknowns into known unknowns |
| Justifying something only with "we've always done it this way" | You've found an invisible rule — question it; the best way to expose a rule is to violate it |
| Copying a competitor's playbook | Stop — you're in a "smartness contest with everyone who came before"; reason from first principles or invert |
| Stuck on HOW to solve it | Question the WHAT — reframe the problem; ask "What problem is this tactic here to solve?" |
| In love with your first solution | Breed multiple conflicting hypotheses; you're probably solving the wrong problem |
| Brainstorming | Diverge first (be the fly, "What *could* we do?"), converge second (be the bee) — never reverse the order |
| Aiming for 10% improvement | Aim 10x instead — force a new approach, not an incremental tweak |
| Starting an audacious project | Do the monkey first (hardest part); set kill metrics *now*, while clearheaded |
| About to trust a test that "passed" | Test to *fail* — hunt the breaking point; exaggerate the flaw you found |
| Feeling the dopamine of being proven right | Try to prove yourself *wrong* — every "no" carries more information than a "yes" |
| On a winning streak | Assume you're slightly behind; run a postmortem on the *success* |
| About to act on a big decision | Run a premortem — assume it failed, list why, assign probabilities |
| Relying on a safety net (redundancy, deadline extension, ABS) | Pretend it isn't there (risk homeostasis) |

## Idea generation (separate from judgment)
- **Generate before you evaluate.** Idea generation and idea evaluation are different modes — don't let "we've tried that / no budget / won't be approved" kill ideas before generation finishes.
- Be the **fly, not the bee**: flutter freely first; the bee dies bumping the light.
- Ask **"What would a science-fiction solution look like?"** and **"What could we do?"** (not "should").
- **Compare apples and oranges** — collect diverse inputs, then connect them.
- **Get bored on purpose** — the answer arrives when you walk away.

## Tells of the certainty trap (Ch 1)
- You're "looking for keys under the street lamp" — choosing the familiar/safe because that's where the light is.
- You're forcing a definite conclusion onto genuinely ambiguous evidence.
- You feel a *story* clicking into place (narrative fills the vacuum of understanding).
- You're ignoring the anomaly that doesn't fit ("That's funny…" is the entrance to discovery — don't discard it).
- Antidote: say "I don't know"; be "uncomfortably uncertain over comfortably wrong."

## Tells of the success trap (Ch 9)
- "We got away with it last time, so we can lower the standard a bit" (normalization of deviance).
- A recurring flaw has quietly become an "acceptable risk."
- You're calling something "routine"; you've stopped running postmortems.
- Near misses feel like wins — you treat "no harm" as "no foul."
- Dissenters are being branded downers; the burden has shifted to *prove danger* rather than prove safety.
- Antidote: stay in "Day 1"; treat yourself as a permanent work in progress; fix the cause behind the cause.

## Testing (test as you fly)
- Make the test mirror flight; then fly as you tested. **Any gap can be catastrophic.**
- Test the **assembled system**, not just the parts ("understand the 'and'"); retest the whole after any change.
- **Observe behavior, don't self-report** — put the iPhone in their hands; watch people grimace.
- **Diversify** testing instruments and calibration targets — never rely on one.

## Failure (learn fast, not fail fast)
- Ask **both** "What went wrong?" **and** "What went right?" — keep the good inputs (Amazon's Fire → other tech; failed angina drug → Viagra).
- Judge **inputs, not outputs** — avoid "resulting" (a good decision can produce a bad outcome).
- **Reward excellent failures, punish mediocre successes.**
- Leaders **advertise their own failures** to build psychological safety.
- A single failure ≠ final defeat; breakthroughs are evolutionary ("solve one problem, then the next").

## The three stages (map)
| Stage | Move | Core principles |
|---|---|---|
| **Launch** | Ignite thinking | Dance with uncertainty · first principles · a mind at play · moonshots |
| **Accelerate** | Propel ideas | Reframe the question · prove yourself wrong · test as you fly |
| **Achieve** | Learn from outcomes | Intelligent failure · guard against the success trap |
