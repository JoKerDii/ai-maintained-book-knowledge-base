# Secret 6: Lighten Up

## Core Idea
Don't take yourself or your topic too seriously — the brain loves humor. Humor lowers defenses (endorphins turn "walls into windows"), makes you more likable, and makes people more willing to support someone they like. The key: be humorous without telling jokes.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Five Ways to Be Funny Without Telling a Joke**:
  1. **Anecdotes, observations, and personal stories** — short, aimed at a smile not a belly laugh; endear you to the audience (most of Sir Ken Robinson's humor). Best for business.
  2. **Analogies and metaphors** — compare complex topics to familiar things ("a semiconductor is like the brain of your computer"; "40 zetabytes = 57x the grains of sand in the world").
  3. **Quotes** — quote someone else who said something funny (family, anonymous, or famous); safest way to get a laugh (John McWhorter's escalating "young people can't write" quotes back to 63 A.D.).
  4. **Video** — takes the pressure off you to be funny (Kevin Allocca's viral clips; Apple Store comedian clips).
  5. **Photos** — place a complex topic in a context everyone gets (Juan Enriquez's black "The Economy" slide; the pool-radio leverage metaphor; the Ritz-Carlton "Joshi" the stuffed animal series).
- **George's formula (humor + shock + statistics)** — Rose George on sanitation: humor alone is fluff, shock alone repels, stats alone bore; blend all three (bikini "diarrhea" stock photo → the death of 9-month-old Maria Salie).

## Key Concepts
- **The brain is hardwired for humor** (Pradeep): brief, clear, interesting, *funny* conversations resonate and are acted upon.
- **Ingratiation tactic** (Rod Martin, The Psychology of Humor): humor gets you accepted in a group; explains why comedians often had difficult/outsider childhoods.
- **Affect-induction**: laughter captures attention, conveys emotion, and induces similar positive emotions in others, promoting a favorable attitude toward you.
- **Halo effect of humor**: humorous people are judged friendly, considerate, intelligent, perceptive, emotionally stable. (>80% of online daters rank "sense of humor" the most desirable trait.)
- **"Laughing all the way to the bank"** (Fabio Sala, HBR): outstanding executives used humor 17.8x/hour vs. 7.5x for average — bonus size correlated positively with humor use. It reflects emotional intelligence, not mere funniness.
- **Endorphins vs. adrenaline** (Chris Bliss): humor releases endorphins that lower defenses; fear/anger release adrenaline that raises walls. Comedy approaches guarded areas (race, religion, politics, sexuality) and turns walls into windows.

## Mental Models
- **Reveal the humor; don't manufacture it** — "All I had to do was reveal the humor in a situation." You don't have to *be* funny, just willing to do the homework.
- **Don't try to be funny** (Sala's paradox) — trying to be Chris Rock or Seinfeld will bomb; jokes only work for pros. Seinfeld spends *two years* per joke.
- **Humor as a mental break** — deflects controversy and relieves the audience from traumatic/heavy content (SNL after 9/11; Stephen Hawking's alien jokes).
- **Don't go for the big laugh out of the gate** — open with light observational humor; if you bomb too early, you may never recover.

## Anti-patterns
- **Telling jokes** (especially memorized internet jokes): inauthentic; "the moment you start the joke about the blonde or the rabbi and the priest, you're dead."
- **Crass, sexist, or tactless humor**: bombs and damages reputation instantly (the sales rep's sexist joke to a room of saleswomen distracted from a strong pitch).
- **Trying to be a comedian you're not**: "Your audiences don't expect you to be Chris Rock, so don't try to be."
- **Common/overused quotes pulled randomly from a quote library**: do the homework; use relevant, off-the-beaten-path quotes.

## Worked Example
**Sir Ken Robinson ("Schools Kill Creativity," TED 2006)** — the most-viewed TED talk ever (15M+ at the time), despite being about education reform, beating far more famous speakers. Its engine was *humor combined with novelty*. He opened: "if you work in education... you're not often at dinner parties, frankly." He wove self-deprecating anecdotes (Shakespeare as an annoying seven-year-old; his son's four-week "love of his life" Sarah, "the main reason we were leaving the country"; professors who treat their body as "transport for their head"). All were anecdotes and observations — never jokes. He earned a prolonged standing ovation from 1,200 billionaires, scientists, and thinkers.

## Key Takeaways
1. Never tell jokes (unless you're a professional comedian); be humorous instead.
2. Use one of the five techniques — anecdotes/observations, analogies, quotes, video, or photos.
3. Aim for a smile, not always a laugh; if something makes you laugh, it will likely make others laugh.
4. Blend humor with shock and statistics for heavy topics (George's formula).
5. Use humor to lower defenses on guarded subjects and to give the brain a break.
6. Keep humor authentic and appropriate — negative, disparaging, or tactless humor backfires.

## Connects To
- **Secret 4**: humor is novelty; the brain can't ignore either.
- **Secret 2**: most humor arrives via anecdote and story.
- **Secret 5**: humor + shock can be the jaw-dropping moment (Gates).
- **Secret 9**: humor must be authentic to *you* and your style.
