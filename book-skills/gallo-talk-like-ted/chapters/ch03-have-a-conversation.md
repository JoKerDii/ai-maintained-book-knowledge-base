# Secret 3: Have a Conversation

## Core Idea
Practice relentlessly and internalize your content so you can deliver it as comfortably as a conversation with a close friend. True persuasion requires emotional rapport and trust; if your voice, gestures, and body language are incongruent with your words, listeners distrust the message. Authenticity does not happen naturally — it takes hours of work.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three Ps: Passion, Practice, Presence** — identify your passion (Secret 1), then practice relentlessly; only then does genuine presence surface.
- **The Four Elements of Verbal Delivery**: Rate, Volume, Pitch, Pauses — the verbal "highlighter." Raise/lower volume, change speed, or set a key word off with a pause to emphasize it. If rate is wrong, nothing else matters.
- **The Ideal Rate of Speech (~190 wpm face-to-face)**: audio books = 150–160 wpm; in-person is slightly faster due to added sensory input (gestures, eye contact, expression). Benchmarks: Stevenson 190 wpm (the Goldilocks zone), Tony Robbins 240 wpm (auctioneer = 250), Kissinger 90 wpm (monotone). A ~3,400-word talk fits 18 minutes.
- **The Four-Tip Gesture System** (from David McNeil's research: complex thinkers use complex gestures): (1) Use gestures — hands out of pockets. (2) Use them sparingly/naturally — no canned gestures. (3) Use expansive gestures at key moments. (4) Keep gestures within the **power sphere**.
- **The Power Sphere**: a circle from the top of your eyes → tips of outstretched hands → belly button → back to eyes. Keep gestures and eye gaze inside it; hands below the navel lack energy and confidence.
- **Eager Nonverbal style** (Fennis & Stel): animated, broad, open movements; hands projected outward; forward-leaning posture. 71% of shoppers bought when exposed to it vs. a reserved style. Best for making a request/offer more attractive.

## Key Concepts
- **Command presence**: military term — presenting yourself as an authority to be respected and followed; the look begins with dress and carriage (FBI: prisoners judged officers by attire and posture).
- **Congruence**: words, tone, and body must match, or listeners sense deception (Morgan Wright: presenters manifest the same "tells" as lying suspects when they don't believe their message).
- **Behavior-analysis data (NSA 300 cases)**: audio-only truth detection 55%; video-only 65%; both 85%; plus case file 93%. Delivery matters — a lot.
- **Fake it till you become it** (Amy Cuddy): power posing (arms outstretched, held 2 minutes) raises testosterone ~15%, lowers cortisol; body changes mind changes behavior changes outcomes.
- **Mehrabian myth**: the "7% words / 38% tone / 55% body" statistic is misapplied — it only covers *emotional/incongruent* messages. Disregard the exact numbers; delivery still dominates impact.

## Mental Models
- **A Ferrari without knowing how to drive** — a magnificent story with poor delivery is wasted.
- **Nobody is born with a clicker in hand** — Steve Jobs was nervous and stiff early (1984 Macintosh, reading notes); he improved every decade through relentless practice. The 10,000-hour rule applies to speaking.
- **Don't deliver a presentation — have a conversation** — most people unnaturally *slow down* when presenting; match your natural conversational pace.
- **Your strength comes from within** (Janine Shepherd) — delivery enhances the message, but without passion and practice, presence collapses.
- **Pre-presentation physical ritual** (Tony Robbins: jumping, fists, trampoline for ~10 min) — movement and energy are intimately connected; adopt some physical warm-up.

## Anti-patterns
- **Fidgeting, tapping, jingling** (coins in pocket): looks unsure, nervous, unprepared. Fix: move with purpose; record and review yourself.
- **Standing rigidly in place**: looks stiff, boring, disengaged. Fix: walk, work the room; if you never leave the camera frame in 5 minutes, you're too rigid.
- **Hands in pockets**: looks bored, uncommitted. Fix: take them out; one hand acceptable only if the other gestures within the power sphere.
- **Slowing your rate and reading**: sounds unnatural; internalize content so you can match conversational pace.

## Worked Example
**Amanda Palmer ("The Art of Asking," TED 2013)** — a performance artist who still spent four months and consulted 105 people. Her process, a model of the practice principle: (1) crowdsourced the topic; (2) got early "brutal" feedback (Godin: "Stay vulnerable"), read it aloud to test; (3) rehearsed relentlessly — a diverse potluck audience, an art-school class (cameras off), scrolled the outline across the floor as a memory tool, practiced aloud on the plane, over Skype, and twice for the TED team. "If I'd done this alone it probably would not have been a good talk." Compare: one 20-minute Apple product launch consumed 250 total hours; Dr. Jill rehearsed her talk 200 times.

## Key Takeaways
1. Rehearse until content is internalized — then it sounds conversational, not delivered.
2. Get early, honest feedback from diverse people; record yourself on video (the single best improvement tool) and hunt for fillers, distracting habits, and pacing.
3. Aim for ~190 wpm; vary rate/volume/pitch/pause to "highlight" key words (Lisa Kristine punches words; Dr. Jill speeds to 220 wpm acting out "we lose half our minds").
4. Use natural gestures at key moments, kept in the power sphere; complex thinkers gesture.
5. Adopt eager-nonverbal posture; sit/stand tall (posture raises self-confidence).
6. Manage nerves, don't eliminate them — power pose beforehand and use a physical warm-up ritual.
