## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

Gallo analyzed 500+ TED talks and interviewed neuroscientists. A great talk is **EMOTIONAL** (Secrets 1–3), **NOVEL** (4–6), and **MEMORABLE** (7–9). Ideas are the currency of the 21st century; these nine secrets are how you spend it.

### EMOTIONAL — touch the heart (Secrets 1–3)

**Secret 1 — Unleash the Master Within (Passion).** Rule: build every talk on what makes your heart sing, because you can't inspire others unless you're inspired yourself and passion is *literally* contagious. How: ask three escalating questions — "What do you do?" → "What are you passionate about?" → "What is it that makes your heart sing?" Passion must be **core to your identity** (Cardon's Entrepreneurial Passion), and it's often adjacent to your day job (Mullins is passionate about human potential, not prosthetics). Perceived passion ranks #3 of 13 criteria investors use to fund ventures.

**Secret 2 — Master the Art of Storytelling.** Rule: tell stories to reach heads *and* hearts, because you cannot persuade through logic alone — stories cause **brain-to-brain coupling** that plants your ideas in the listener. How: balance Aristotle's **Ethos (credibility) / Logos (data) / Pathos (emotion)** — Stevenson's record-ovation talk was **10% / 25% / 65%**. Use one of the **three story types**: personal, about another person, or a brand success/failure. Give the audience a hero to root for.

**Secret 3 — Have a Conversation (Delivery).** Rule: rehearse relentlessly and internalize content so you deliver it like a conversation with a friend, because incongruent voice/body destroys trust. How: the **Three Ps** (Passion → Practice → Presence). Control the **four elements of verbal delivery** (rate ~190 wpm, volume, pitch, pauses); keep gestures in the **power sphere** (eyes → outstretched hands → navel); adopt the **eager nonverbal** style; power pose 2 minutes to manage nerves. "Fake it till you become it" (Cuddy). Record and review yourself — the single best improvement tool.

### NOVEL — teach me something new (Secrets 4–6)

**Secret 4 — Teach Me Something New.** Rule: reveal something new, freshly packaged, or a new solution to an old problem, because the brain cannot ignore novelty (it releases **dopamine**, the brain's "save button"). How: write a **Twitter-friendly headline** (≤140 chars — 0 of 1,500+ TED titles exceed it) as step one; show the big picture before details; connect abstract features to concrete daily-life benefit. Rosling made *statistics* go viral by animating them.

**Secret 5 — Deliver Jaw-Dropping Moments.** Rule: design one **emotionally charged event** per talk, because the amygdala tags emotionally arousing moments "Remember This!" (why you recall 9/11 but forget your keys). How: five options — props/demos (Gates's mosquitoes), shocking stats in context, images/video, memorable sound bites ("woolly mammoths back"), or personal stories. **Plan the story first; open PowerPoint last.** Make abstractions concrete.

**Secret 6 — Lighten Up (Humor).** Rule: don't take yourself or your topic too seriously, because humor lowers defenses (endorphins) and makes you likable — but **never tell jokes** (they only work for pro comedians). How: five joke-free techniques — anecdotes/observations, analogies/metaphors, quotes, video, photos. For heavy topics use **humor + shock + statistics** (Rose George). Robinson's humor made "Schools Kill Creativity" the most-viewed TED talk ever.

### MEMORABLE — never forget it (Secrets 7–9)

**Secret 7 — Stick to the 18-Minute Rule.** Rule: cap talks at **18 minutes** (Goldilocks 18–20), because longer creates **cognitive backlog** that overloads and even angers the audience (the brain is a glucose-hungry energy hog). How: build in **soft breaks** every ~10 min if longer; structure around the **Rule of Three** (working memory holds ~3 chunks); use a one-page **message map** (headline → 3 key messages → supporting stories/stats). Constraints breed creativity.

**Secret 8 — Paint a Mental Picture with Multisensory Experiences.** Rule: touch more than one sense, because **multitasking is a myth** and the **Picture Superiority Effect** lifts 3-day recall from ~10% (words) to ~65% (words + picture). How: pictures instead of text (≤40 words in first 10 slides, kill bullet points, one theme/slide); when you have no slides, paint pictures with vivid concrete words (the visual cortex can't tell real from imagined); add sound (delivery, video) and touch (props, demos). Slides complement — never replace — the narrative.

**Secret 9 — Stay in Your Lane (Authenticity).** Rule: be the authentic best version of *yourself*, never an imitation, because people spot a phony and won't trust an act. How: after crafting and rehearsing, let go and speak from the heart; **hold the space** (stay vulnerable at the emotional peak — Dr. Jill's Nirvana ending); rehearse to a friend/spouse first; reframe negative self-labels. Great communicators (Buffett, Branson, Osteen) all started terrified — confidence is built, not innate.
