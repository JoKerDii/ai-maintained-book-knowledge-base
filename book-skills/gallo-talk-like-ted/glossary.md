# Glossary — Talk Like TED

**18-Minute Rule** — the hard cap on TED talks; the ideal presentation length: long enough to be serious, short enough to hold attention, and the length of a coffee break so it goes viral (Secret 7).

**Affect-induction** — humor/laughter as a communication method that captures attention, conveys emotion, and induces similar positive emotions in others to win favor (Secret 6).

**Affective Communications Test (ACT)** — Howard Friedman's questionnaire measuring how well people transmit feelings to others (charisma); high scorers shift low scorers' moods without a word (Secret 1).

**Anaphora** — repeating the same opening words across successive clauses/sentences (MLK's "I have a dream"; Bono's "We are going to win because..."); an auditory rhetorical device best delivered without slides (Secret 8).

**Brain-to-brain coupling ("sync up")** — Uri Hasson's finding that telling a story makes speaker's and listeners' brains fire in the same regions, planting ideas and emotions (Secret 2).

**Cognitive backlog** — accumulating information that piles up like weights on the listener's mind; the core reason long talks fail and the 18-minute rule works (Paul King) (Secret 7).

**Command presence** — a military term for presenting yourself as an authority to be respected and followed; begins with dress and carriage (Secret 3).

**Congruence** — alignment of words, tone, and body language; incongruence signals deception and destroys trust (Secret 3).

**Curiosity gap** — the painful gap in knowledge that curiosity drives us to fill (Loewenstein/Heath); breaking a pattern grabs attention (Secret 2).

**Dopamine** — the brain's "save button" (Martha Burns); released by novelty and learning (and emotionally charged events), it tags information for memory (Secrets 4, 5).

**Dual-coding theory** — Allan Paivio's theory that pictures are encoded both visually and verbally (double-stamped) while words are verbal only, making pictures more memorable (Secret 8).

**Eager nonverbal** — a persuasive body-language style: animated broad movements, outward-projected hands, forward-leaning posture; best for making an offer more attractive (Fennis & Stel) (Secret 3).

**Emotionally charged event (ECS)** — the best-processed external stimulus ever measured; a heightened emotional moment that the amygdala tags "Remember This!" (John Medina); the "wow" or "holy smokes" moment (Secret 5).

**Emotionally enhanced vividness** — Rebecca Todd's finding that how vividly you perceive an event predicts how vividly you'll remember it; like a flashbulb (Secret 5).

**Entrepreneurial passion (EP)** — Melissa Cardon's definition: "a positive, intense feeling you experience for something profoundly meaningful to you as an individual"; core to self-identity (Secret 1).

**Ethos** — Aristotle's persuasion via credibility (title, experience, achievements) (Secret 2).

**Flashbulb memory** — a vivid memory of an emotionally arousing event (e.g., 9/11) vs. forgotten mundane events (Secret 5).

**Flynn Effect** — the generational rise in IQ scores, attributed to constant mental workouts; TED provides such a workout (Secret 4).

**Goldilocks zone** — the "not too fast, not too slow" range: ~190 wpm delivery (Secret 3); 18–20 minutes in length (Secret 7).

**Holy smokes moment** — Gallo's term for the emotionally charged/wow moment when the audience's jaw drops and they "get it" (Secret 5).

**Hook** — the journalism term for the wow moment/rhetorical device that grabs attention and makes people share (Secret 5).

**Logos** — Aristotle's persuasion via logic, data, and statistics (Secret 2).

**Message map** — a one-page visual of your idea: Twitter headline → three key messages → supporting stories/stats/examples (Secret 7).

**Mood contagion** — the transfer of a leader's positive emotions to the audience, lifting their mood and perceived effectiveness (Bono & Ilies) (Secret 1).

**Multimedia principle** — retention improves through words plus pictures rather than words alone (Richard Mayer) (Secret 8).

**Multitasking myth** — the brain is biologically incapable of processing attention-rich inputs (listening + reading a wordy slide) simultaneously (John Medina) (Secret 8).

**Neuroplasticity** — the brain grows and rewires with repetition; speaking often physically develops the brain's language areas (Secret 1).

**Novelty recognition** — the hardwired survival instinct that makes the brain seek out what is new and stands out; the single most effective attention-grabber (Secrets 4, 6).

**Pathos** — Aristotle's persuasion via emotional appeal; delivered through stories (Stevenson's talk was 65% pathos) (Secret 2).

**Perceived passion** — the audience/investor-detectable trait of genuine enthusiasm; ranked #3 of 13 criteria angels use to fund ventures (Secret 1).

**Picture Superiority Effect (PSE)** — concepts shown as pictures are recalled far better than words: ~10% recall for words alone vs. ~65% with a picture, three days later (Secret 8).

**Power sphere** — the zone from the top of your eyes to your outstretched hands to your navel; keep gestures and eye gaze inside it (Secret 3).

**Power posing** — holding an expansive posture (arms outstretched) ~2 minutes to raise testosterone and lower cortisol; "fake it till you become it" (Amy Cuddy) (Secret 3).

**Rule of Three** — people reliably remember ~3 chunks of information; structure talks around three key messages (Secret 7).

**Sound bite** — a short, provocative, repeatable phrase built to be retweeted/reposted ("We will get woolly mammoths back") (Secret 5).

**Soft breaks** — stories, videos, demos, or extra speakers inserted every ~10 minutes to reset cognitive load in longer talks (Secret 7).

**Stay in your lane** — Oprah's principle of being the authentic best version of yourself rather than imitating another speaker (Secret 9).

**Three A's of Awesome** — Neil Pasricha's rule-of-three talk: Attitude, Awareness, Authenticity (Secret 7).

**Three Ps** — Passion, Practice, Presence — the sequence to genuine, conversational delivery (Secrets 1, 3).

**Three types of stories** — personal stories, stories about other people, and brand/product success-or-failure stories (Secret 2).

**Twitter-friendly headline** — the one message you want the audience to remember, in ≤140 characters; Step One of building any talk (Secrets 4, 7).

**Willpower/glucose depletion** — Baumeister's finding that self-control and attention draw on a finite, glucose-fueled energy; long talks deplete the audience's fuel (Secret 7).
