# Cheatsheet — Talk Like TED (Decision Rules & Numbers)

## The Master Rules
- **When building any talk, start with "What makes my heart sing?"** — because you can't inspire others unless you're inspired yourself (Secret 1).
- **When you have a great story but flat delivery, rehearse until it's conversational** — because a Ferrari is useless if you can't drive it (Secret 3).
- **When you must persuade, lead with story before data** — because you cannot persuade through logic alone; break the wall first (Secret 2).
- **When drafting content, write a ≤140-character headline first** — because it forces clarity and the audience needs the one big idea (Secrets 4, 7).
- **When the topic is complex, simplify until a 7th-grader gets it** — because simplicity signals mastery and takes courage (Secret 7).
- **When you're tempted to imitate a star speaker, don't — stay in your lane** — because audiences spot a phony and won't trust an act (Secret 9).

## Hard Numbers & Thresholds
| Metric | Value | Secret |
|---|---|---|
| Max talk length | **18 minutes** (Goldilocks 18–20) | 7 |
| Soft break interval (if longer) | every **~10 minutes** | 7 |
| Ideal face-to-face speaking rate | **~190 wpm** (audio book 150–160; Robbins 240; Kissinger 90) | 3 |
| Words in ~18 min | **~3,400** | 3 |
| Stevenson's persuasion ratio | **Ethos 10% / Logos 25% / Pathos 65%** | 2 |
| Headline / talk title length | **≤140 characters** (0 of 1,500+ TED titles exceed it) | 4, 7 |
| Working-memory chunks | **3** (Miller's 7±2, modernized to 3–4) | 7 |
| Words per average PowerPoint slide | **40** (TED slides rarely near it) | 8 |
| Target words, first 10 slides | **≤40 total** | 8 |
| Recall: words alone (3 days) | **~10%** | 8 |
| Recall: words + picture (3 days) | **~65%** (6x more) | 8 |
| Power pose duration | **2 minutes** (arms outstretched) | 3 |
| Perceived passion rank (angel funding) | **#3 of 13 criteria** | 1 |
| Rehearsal reference points | Palmer 4 months/105 people; Dr. Jill **200x**; Apple launch **250 hrs** | 3 |

## The 9 Secrets (3 Pillars)
| Pillar | Secret | One-line rule |
|---|---|---|
| EMOTIONAL | 1 Unleash the Master Within | Speak on what makes your heart sing |
| EMOTIONAL | 2 Master Storytelling | Aim ~65% pathos; tell stories |
| EMOTIONAL | 3 Have a Conversation | Rehearse until it sounds conversational |
| NOVEL | 4 Teach Me Something New | Reveal something new / freshly packaged |
| NOVEL | 5 Jaw-Dropping Moments | Design one emotionally charged event |
| NOVEL | 6 Lighten Up | Be humorous without telling jokes |
| MEMORABLE | 7 18-Minute Rule | Cap at 18 min; rule of three |
| MEMORABLE | 8 Multisensory Picture | Pictures > words; touch >1 sense |
| MEMORABLE | 9 Stay in Your Lane | Be authentically yourself |

## The Three Story Types (Secret 2)
1. Personal stories tied to the theme.
2. Stories about other people the audience relates to.
3. Brand/product success or failure stories.

## Five Ways to a Jaw-Dropping Moment (Secret 5)
Props/demos · Shocking statistics (in context) · Pictures/images/videos · Memorable sound bites · Personal stories.
**Rule**: plan the story first, open PowerPoint last.

## Five Ways to Be Funny Without a Joke (Secret 6)
Anecdotes/observations · Analogies/metaphors · Quotes · Video · Photos.
- **When you want a laugh, never tell a joke** — jokes work only for pro comedians; be humorous instead.
- **When the topic is heavy, use humor + shock + statistics** — all three, balanced (Rose George).
- **When opening, don't go for the big laugh** — start with light observational humor; bombing early is unrecoverable.

## Delivery Fixes (Secret 3)
| Problem | Quick fix |
|---|---|
| Fidgeting / tapping / jingling | Move with purpose; record & review |
| Standing rigidly | Walk the room; leave camera frame several times / 5 min |
| Hands in pockets | Take them out; keep gestures in the power sphere |
| Speaking too slowly / reading | Internalize content; match natural conversation |
| Nerves | Power pose 2 min + physical warm-up ritual |

## Tells & Smells (what betrays you) (Secret 3)
Incongruence between words and body = perceived deception (you show the same "tells" as a lying suspect). Truthfulness detection: audio-only 55% · video-only 65% · both 85% · + context 93%. **Believe what you're saying**, or the body can't fake it.

## Message Map (Secret 7)
Headline (≤140 chars) → 3 key messages → stories/stats/examples under each. One page.

## Slide Design (Secret 8)
Pictures instead of text · ≤40 words in first 10 slides · kill bullet points · one theme/stat per slide · break data with photos/stories · slides complement (never replace) the narrative · "use visuals to enhance words, not duplicate."
