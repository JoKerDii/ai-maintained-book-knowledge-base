## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

### Trauma is not what happens to you — it's what happens inside you
The event is not the trauma. The trauma is **the wound and the adaptations built around it**. Two people can survive the same event and only one is traumatized; one person can be traumatized with no nameable event at all. Ask **"what happened to you?"** rather than "what's wrong with you?"

- **big-T Trauma** — overt catastrophe: abuse, violence, war, disaster.
- **small-t trauma** — chronic unmet need and absent attunement. Pervasive, easily missed, and consequential. Its invisibility is why it goes untreated.
- **Developmental trauma** — trauma arising from the conditions of early development itself, not from discrete events.

### The myth of normal
A toxic culture normalizes conditions that are in fact **pathogenic**. Widespread does not mean healthy; "normal" is a statistical statement, never a standard of health. When a rate of illness rises sharply within decades, genetics cannot be the explanation — the environment is. Use this whenever "normal" is offered as reassurance: *normal for whom, by what standard?*

### Mind-body unity and interpersonal biology
Reject the Cartesian split. Emotional, psychological, social, and spiritual life are inseparable from physiology, so **the social is always also biological**. **Biopsychosocial** medicine treats these as one system. **Interpersonal biology**: human nervous, immune, and hormonal systems are regulated by *other people* — we are not self-contained organisms.

### Authenticity vs. attachment — the central developmental trade-off
A child needs both **attachment** (proximity to caregivers, a survival requirement) and **authenticity** (contact with their own feelings and needs, also a long-run survival requirement). When a child cannot have both, **attachment wins every time** — authenticity is suppressed. The suppression was intelligent and adaptive then. It becomes the adult pathology now: compulsive niceness, inability to say no, repressed anger, chronic self-abandonment. Nearly every framework in the book routes through this one.

### Disease as process, not thing
Illness is not an entity invading an otherwise separate self; it is a process arising from the whole organism in its lifelong context. Ask *"what process is unfolding, and what conditions sustain it?"* rather than *"what does she have?"* — and always alongside, never instead of, medical treatment.

### Coping style and illness *(Maté's position — contested)*
Maté argues that habitual **self-suppression**, **compulsive niceness**, and **repressed anger** are significant contributing factors in autoimmune disease and cancer. The stress–immune–inflammation pathways are real and researched; **the strength and directionality Maté assigns them go beyond current scientific consensus.** Present as his position, not settled fact.

### Responsibility is not blame — the caveat that matters most
Maté **explicitly and repeatedly rejects** victim-blaming and the "you caused your cancer" reading of his work. This is the most frequently misread part of the book. Causation is multifactorial and contextual, never individual fault. *Responsibility* is the capacity to respond now; *blame* is fault for what happened. If anything you say lands as "I did this to myself," harm has been done and must be corrected plainly.

### Addiction: "not why the addiction, but why the pain"
Addiction is an **adaptation to pain**, not a disease of choice and not merely a brain disease. Any behavior that provides temporary relief and is craved despite negative consequences qualifies. The question is never why the drug — it is what pain the drug relieved and how that wound was sustained. This dissolves the us/them line between "addicts" and everyone else.

### ADHD as a stress/attunement phenomenon *(Maté's position — diverges from mainstream psychiatry)*
Maté argues ADHD is not a genetically determined brain disease but a developmental response to stress and disrupted attunement, citing the sharp rise in diagnoses, the December-birthday effect, and trauma–dopamine research. He is **not** anti-medication, and nothing here is guidance to alter treatment.

### Children's needs and the toxic culture
**Attunement** — accurate reading of and resonance with a child's inner state — is a need, not a luxury; its chronic absence is itself traumatic. **Rupture and repair**: repair, not the absence of rupture, builds security. **Epigenetics** explains how environment and experience, including prenatal stress, switch gene expression on and off — the counter to genetic determinism. The culture undermines birth, parenting, and childhood while blaming the individuals inside it.

### Healing is not cure
**Healing** is a *natural movement toward wholeness* — a direction, not a destination; a line on a map, not a dot. **Cure** is the absence of disease. Either can occur without the other. Healing is closer to **self-retrieval** than to self-improvement: recovering exiled parts of self, not bettering them.

### The A's of healing
**Authenticity, Agency, healthy Anger, Acceptance** — guideposts, not steps. Healthy anger flares, defends the boundary, and subsides; it is not rage, venting, or nursed grievance. Acceptance is allowing what is, not resignation. Ch 33 adds **Activism** and **Advocacy** for collective healing.

### Compassionate Inquiry
Maté's own method: open-ended, humble, non-judgmental exploration of what doesn't serve us — tracking bodily sensation and asking how *familiar* it is, which dates the pattern to its origin. Compassion is an **attitude, not a feeling**, and it brooks no "should." Where self-compassion is absent, get curious about the absence rather than commanding it.

### Disillusionment as a healing step
Losing an illusion is a **gain**. It costs something real — you relinquish a refuge — but the cost of refusing is higher and less visible. Disillusionment is also what makes denial, and therefore an unhealthy status quo, negotiable.

### Anti-patterns (Maté's critiques)
- **Treating symptoms while ignoring context** — correct treatment, incomplete intervention.
- **Genetic determinism** — genes load possibilities; environment and experience largely decide expression.
- **The "normal" that isn't healthy** — mistaking prevalence for health.
- **Toughening children out of their emotions** — the culture's script for producing self-suppression.
- **Mistaking coping-by-niceness for virtue** — "she never got angry," "no ego," "so easy to care for" describe a cost, not a character.

## Scope & Limits

**This skill is a knowledge base about a book. It is not medical, psychiatric, or therapeutic advice, and it is not a substitute for care from a qualified professional.** Nothing here should be used to diagnose anyone, and nothing here is a reason to decline, delay, or discontinue medical or psychiatric treatment — Maté's own cases pair conventional treatment with inner work.

**Contested claims are Maté's positions, not scientific consensus.** This applies especially to the personality-to-cancer and coping-style-to-autoimmunity links, the ADHD account, the critique of the chemical-imbalance model, and the psychedelics material. The underlying stress–immune–inflammation research is real; the strength and directionality Maté assigns it goes beyond what is established. Attribute accordingly.

**Maté explicitly rejects victim-blaming.** Any use of this material that implies someone caused their own illness misreads the book. Hold responsibility and blame apart, always.

**Handle with care.** This is a book about trauma and illness, read by people who are personally affected. Its healing practices are inquiries, not instructions, and they lose their meaning when compressed into self-help checklists. Where a reader appears to be in distress or crisis, point them toward real support rather than toward a framework.
