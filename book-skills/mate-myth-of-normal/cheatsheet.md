# Cheatsheet — The Myth of Normal

Diagnostic questions and decision rules, not a symptom checklist. **Not medical, psychiatric, or therapeutic advice.**

---

## The four questions that reframe almost anything

| Instead of asking | Ask | Because |
|---|---|---|
| What's wrong with you? | **What happened to you?** | Trauma is not what happens to you, it's what happens inside you — the wound, not the event |
| Why the addiction? | **Why the pain?** | Addiction is an adaptation to pain, not a disease of choice or a moral failure |
| What does she *have*? | **What process is unfolding, and what sustains it?** | Disease is a process in a life, not a thing that invades a separate self |
| How do I get rid of this? | **What is this for? Why is this here?** | Everything within us exists for a purpose; it was once protective |

## Decision rules

- **When a diagnosis explains the symptoms but not the person** → ask what was happening in that life in the 2–5 years before onset. The answer is data.
- **When someone is described admiringly as "never angry," "no ego," "always thinking of others," "so easy to care for"** → treat that as a *coping style*, not a virtue. Maté's position: habitual self-suppression and repressed anger are physiologically consequential.
- **When a behavior looks irrational** → assume it was once an adaptation. Ask what it solved, and when.
- **When a child's behavior is the problem** → look first at the state of the attachment relationship and the adults' capacity to attune, not at the child's will.
- **When "normal" is offered as reassurance** → ask *normal for whom, and by what standard?* Widely shared does not mean healthy. That is the myth.
- **When a cause is attributed to genes** → genes load possibilities; environment and experience largely decide expression. Ask what turned it on.
- **When a symptom is being treated and the context is untouched** → the intervention is incomplete, however correct the treatment.
- **When self-compassion is absent** → do not command it. Notice the absence and get curious about it. Compassion brooks no "should."
- **When healing is discussed** → separate it from cure. Healing is a *direction*, a movement toward wholeness; cure is absence of disease. You can have either without the other. Never promise the second by way of the first.

## Tells & smells

| If you see | Suspect |
|---|---|
| "I never get angry" / conflict avoidance as identity | Repressed anger, authenticity traded for attachment |
| Illness onset shortly after a loss, betrayal, or role collapse | Chronic process made acute by interpersonal stress |
| "Idiopathic" on the chart | No one asked about the life |
| A patient apologizing for taking up your time | Self-suppression pattern, likely lifelong |
| Compulsive niceness, inability to say no | The childhood adaptation still running |
| Strong self-condemnation in familiar bodily sensations | The critic is old and was once protective |
| A parent describing a toddler's needs as manipulation | Culture's toughening script; attunement is being withheld |
| "You must have brought this on yourself" | Victim-blaming — the distortion Maté explicitly rejects |

## The critical distinctions (get these wrong and you get Maté wrong)

| Not this | But this |
|---|---|
| You caused your illness | Illness is multifactorial and contextual; **responsibility is not blame** |
| Personality causes cancer | Maté's *position* is that emotional repression is a significant contributing factor — a contested claim, not consensus |
| Blame the parents | Parents are themselves shaped by their own trauma and by a culture that undermines parenting |
| Trauma = the terrible event | Trauma = the internal wound and the adaptation it forced |
| Big-T Trauma is the only real trauma | **small-t trauma** — chronic unmet need, absence of attunement — is pervasive and consequential |
| Acceptance = resignation | Acceptance = allowing what is, which is what makes change possible |
| Healthy anger = venting | Healthy anger flares, defends the boundary, and subsides |
| Healing = self-improvement | Healing = **self-retrieval** |
| Disillusionment = loss | Disillusionment = gain; the cost of refusing is higher |

## The authenticity/attachment audit — fast version

Signal appears (tension, anxiety, irritability, regret, depression, fatigue) → ask:
1. What inner guidance am I defying or avoiding?
2. What truth am I withholding, and what am I afraid of losing by saying it?
3. Where, recently, did I abandon myself?
4. What story made that feel necessary — and where did I learn it?

The chronic pattern matters, not the isolated lapse. Deliberate, chosen sacrifice is not the target; **habitual, unwilled** selflessness is.

## Safety rails

- Anything that lands as "you did this to yourself" has done harm. Say so plainly and correct it.
- Never frame Maté's work as a reason to decline, delay, or discontinue medical or psychiatric treatment. His own cases pair conventional treatment with inner work.
- Contested claims (personality→cancer, ADHD as a stress/attunement phenomenon, psychedelics) are **Maté's position and emerging research**, not settled science. Attribute them.
