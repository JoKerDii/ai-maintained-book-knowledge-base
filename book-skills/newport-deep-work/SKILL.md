## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

**The whole thesis — The Deep Work Hypothesis**: The ability to do deep work is becoming *rarer* at the same time it is becoming *more valuable*. Cultivate it and make it the core of your work, and you'll thrive. This is an arbitrage: depth is a widening competitive edge.

**The two definitions (memorize these):**
- **Deep Work** — "Professional activities performed in a state of distraction-free concentration that push your cognitive capabilities to their limit. These efforts create new value, improve your skill, and are hard to replicate."
- **Shallow Work** — "Noncognitively demanding, logistical-style tasks, often performed while distracted. These efforts tend not to create much new value and are easy to replicate."

**The one formula — Law of Productivity**: `High-Quality Work Produced = (Time Spent) × (Intensity of Focus)`. To produce more, raise *intensity*, not hours. Fewer, fiercer hours beat many distracted ones.

**Attention Residue**: Switching tasks (even a 10-second inbox glance) leaves part of your attention stuck on the previous task, degrading the next — "potentially devastating" to performance. → Work on one hard task in long, uninterrupted pulses (batch).

**Why it's valuable (Ch 2)**: The new economy rewards two abilities — *quickly mastering hard things* and *producing at an elite level in quality and speed*. Both require deliberate practice, which is impossible under divided attention.

**Why it's meaningful (Ch 4)**: What you focus on *is* your experienced life (Gallagher); flow — mind stretched to its limit — is the reliable source of satisfaction (Csikszentmihalyi); depth is how knowledge workers earn a craftsman's meaning.

### The Four Rules (Part 2 — the practice)

**Rule #1 — Work Deeply**: Willpower is finite, so *ritualize* depth instead of relying on resolve.
- **Pick a Depth Philosophy**: Monastic (eliminate shallow) · Bimodal (deep days/seasons) · Rhythmic (same time daily, keep the streak — the default) · Journalistic (any gap, on demand — advanced).
- **Ritualize**: pre-decide where/how long, how you'll work (rules + metrics), how you'll support it.
- **Execute Like a Business (4DX)**: wildly important goal → lead measure (deep-work hours) → compelling visible scoreboard → weekly cadence of accountability.
- **Be Lazy — Shutdown Ritual**: end each day by confirming every open task has a trusted plan or is captured, then say a closing phrase ("Shutdown complete") to defeat the Zeigarnik effect.

**Rule #2 — Embrace Boredom**: Concentration is a trainable muscle; don't feed distraction addiction.
- **Schedule internet blocks**; stay offline between them. Never jump online on impulse — reschedule the *next* block a few minutes out instead.
- **Roosevelt dashes** (max intensity under a tight deadline) and **productive meditation** (one work problem on a walk) stretch the focus ceiling.

**Rule #3 — Quit Social Media**: Reject the **any-benefit** mindset ("some benefit justifies use"). Use the **Craftsman Approach**: keep a tool only if it *substantially* serves your core goals. Apply the **Law of the Vital Few** (~20% of activities drive ~80% of results). Test tools with a quiet **30-day quit**.

**Rule #4 — Drain the Shallows**: Contain shallow work ruthlessly.
- **Schedule every minute** (thoughtfulness, not rigidity).
- **Quantify depth**: "How many months to train a smart recent college grad to do this task?" (few = shallow, years = deep).
- **Shallow-work budget** (~30–50% cap) · **Fixed-Schedule Productivity** (set a hard endpoint, prune backward) · **Become hard to reach** (sender filters, process-centric email, the right not to respond).
