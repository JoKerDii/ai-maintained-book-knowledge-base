# Glossary — Deep Work (Cal Newport)

**Any-Benefit Approach** — The flawed rule that a network tool is justified if it offers *any* possible benefit or you might miss *anything* by not using it; approves everything because every tool has some benefit (Ch 7).

**Attention Residue** — Sophie Leroy's finding that after switching from Task A to Task B, part of your attention stays stuck on A, degrading performance on B; worse when A is left unfinished (Ch 2).

**Attention Restoration Theory (ART)** — Directed attention is a finite resource that depletes; time in nature (or restful downtime) replenishes it, aiding subsequent deep work (Ch 5).

**Bimodal Philosophy** — Divide time into clearly defined deep stretches (days or a season) and open shallow periods; minimum deep unit usually a full day (e.g., Jung, Adam Grant) (Ch 5).

**Busyness as a Proxy for Productivity** — Doing lots of visible stuff as a stand-in for value, used when knowledge work lacks clear productivity metrics (Ch 3).

**Cadence of Accountability** — 4DX Discipline #4: a regular (weekly) review confronting your scoreboard and committing to next actions (Ch 5).

**Compelling Scoreboard** — 4DX Discipline #3: a physical, visible tally of your lead measure (deep-work hours); "people play differently when they're keeping score" (Ch 5).

**Craftsman Approach to Tool Selection** — Adopt a tool only if its positive impact on your core success/happiness factors *substantially* outweighs its negatives (Ch 7).

**Cult of the Internet** — Treating any new technology as inherently good/necessary, branding skeptics Luddites, so tools spread unchecked (Ch 3).

**Deep Work** — "Professional activities performed in a state of distraction-free concentration that push your cognitive capabilities to their limit. These efforts create new value, improve your skill, and are hard to replicate" (Intro).

**Deep Work Hypothesis** — The ability to do deep work is becoming rarer *and* more valuable; the few who cultivate it and center their work on it will thrive (Intro).

**Deliberate Practice** — Ericsson's mechanism of skill acquisition: focused, feedback-driven strain on a specific ability just beyond current reach; incompatible with divided attention (Ch 2).

**Depth Philosophy** — Your chosen pattern for integrating deep work: Monastic, Bimodal, Rhythmic, or Journalistic (Ch 5).

**Eudaimonia Machine** — David Dewane's conceptual building whose architecture channels occupants from social space into progressively deeper chambers ending in isolated deep-work rooms; a metaphor for engineering depth (Ch 5).

**Fixed-Schedule Productivity** — Fix a firm workday endpoint, then work backward, ruthlessly pruning shallow obligations to fit deep work into the constrained hours (Ch 8).

**Flow** — Csikszentmihalyi's state of full absorption when mind/body is stretched to its limits on a worthwhile task; the reliable source of satisfaction (Ch 4).

**Grand Gesture** — A radical change to environment or investment that raises a task's perceived importance and mobilizes focus (e.g., Rowling's hotel suite) (Ch 5).

**Hub-and-Spoke** — Architecture of collaboration: expose yourself to "hub" spaces for serendipitous idea exchange, then retreat to isolated "spoke" spaces for deep execution (Ch 5).

**Journalistic Philosophy** — Fit deep work into any open gap on demand; requires trained concentration and confidence (e.g., Walter Isaacson); hard for beginners (Ch 5).

**Lag Measure** — A metric describing the outcome you ultimately want (papers published, revenue); arrives too late to steer daily behavior (Ch 5).

**Law of Productivity** — `High-Quality Work Produced = (Time Spent) × (Intensity of Focus)`; raise intensity to produce more per hour (Ch 2).

**Law of the Vital Few** — ~80% of a desired effect comes from ~20% of possible causes; concentrate effort on the high-impact minority (Ch 7).

**Lead Measure** — A metric for the controllable behavior that drives the lag measure (for depth: hours spent in deep work); movable day-to-day (Ch 5).

**Metric Black Hole** — The impact of distraction/depth on productivity is real but hard to measure, so it goes unmanaged (Ch 3).

**Monastic Philosophy** — Maximize depth by radically eliminating or minimizing shallow obligations (e.g., Knuth, Stephenson) (Ch 5).

**Myelination** — Growth of insulating myelin around repeatedly-fired neural circuits; the physical basis of skill, triggered by isolated intense focus (Ch 2).

**Principle of Least Resistance** — Without clear feedback on bottom-line impact, we default to whatever behavior is easiest in the moment (Ch 3).

**Process-Centric Email** — A reply that resolves the whole underlying project/loop in one message to minimize future back-and-forth (Ch 8).

**Productive Meditation** — Focusing attention on a single well-defined professional problem during physical-but-not-mental activity (walking, driving); redirect from distraction and looping (Ch 6).

**Rhythmic Philosophy** — Make deep work a daily habit at a fixed time, sustained via a streak/chain ("don't break the chain") (e.g., Brian Chappell) (Ch 5).

**Ritual (Deep Work Ritual)** — A preset answer to where/how long you'll work, how you'll work (rules/metrics), and how you'll support the work — to minimize willpower cost of starting (Ch 5).

**Roosevelt Dash** — A burst of maximal-intensity focus on one task under a deadline far shorter than normal, used to stretch your concentration ceiling (Ch 6).

**Sender Filter** — Published expectations shifting the burden of contact to the sender, reducing inbound email you must handle (Ch 8).

**Shallow Work** — "Noncognitively demanding, logistical-style tasks, often performed while distracted... tend not to create much new value and are easy to replicate" (Intro / Ch 8).

**Shallow Work Budget** — An explicit agreed percentage of time (often 30–50%) allowed for shallow work, defended against overrun (Ch 8).

**Shutdown Ritual** — An end-of-day algorithm confirming every incomplete task has a trusted plan or is captured, ending in a set phrase ("Shutdown complete") (Ch 5).

**The Great Restructuring** — Economic split by intelligent machines/connectivity into those who work with depth (winners) and those who don't (Intro).

**Willpower Depletion** — Willpower is finite and drains with use; hence rely on rituals, not resolve, to sustain deep work (Ch 5).

**Zeigarnik Effect** — Incomplete tasks dominate our attention; the shutdown ritual neutralizes it by capturing/planning open loops (Ch 5).

**4DX (Four Disciplines of Execution)** — Framework applied to individual depth: focus on the wildly important → act on lead measures → keep a compelling scoreboard → create a cadence of accountability (Ch 5).
