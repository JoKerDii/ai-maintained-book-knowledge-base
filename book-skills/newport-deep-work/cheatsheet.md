# Deep Work — Cheatsheet (decisions, defaults, tells)

## The one formula
`High-Quality Work Produced = (Time Spent) × (Intensity of Focus)` → to produce more, **raise intensity**, not just hours.

## Pick your depth philosophy (decision tree)
- Can you shed almost all shallow duties? → **Monastic**
- Can you clear whole days/seasons but need open time too? → **Bimodal** (min unit ≈ 1 day)
- Regular job, need a reliable daily habit? → **Rhythmic** (same time daily, keep the streak) ← *default for most*
- Already have trained, on-demand focus and an unpredictable schedule? → **Journalistic** (not for beginners)

## Is this task deep or shallow?
Ask: **"How many months to train a smart recent college grad (no field training) to do it?"**
- Few months → **shallow** → batch it, minimize it, fit it in the budget
- Years of expertise → **deep** → protect a dedicated block for it

## Keep or kill a tool? (craftsman test)
1. List the few goals that define success (work + life).
2. Does the tool *substantially* help the key activities behind them?
   - Yes → keep · "some benefit" / "might miss out" → **kill** (that's any-benefit reasoning)
3. Unsure? Run the **30-day quit**: gone 30 days — would it have been *notably* better? Did anyone *care*? Both must be a clear yes to readopt.

## Willpower rule
Willpower is finite → **never rely on resolve**; rely on rituals, fixed times, and pre-decisions. If starting deep work requires a burst of willpower, your system is wrong.

## 4DX for depth (run all four)
| Discipline | Individual version |
|---|---|
| Wildly Important | 1–2 ambitious goals; say *yes* to the compelling goal, not *no* to distractions |
| Lead Measure | Track **deep-work hours** (not papers/revenue — those are lag) |
| Compelling Scoreboard | Visible tally; circle the hour that produced a result |
| Cadence of Accountability | Weekly review confronting the scoreboard |

## Defaults & thresholds
- **Shallow-work budget**: aim ~30–50% max; anything over → decline with the budget as reason.
- **Fixed endpoint**: set when you *stop* (e.g., 5:30, no nights/weekends), then prune backward.
- **Internet blocks**: schedule allowed windows; offline between them; need it early → move the *next* block a few min out, never jump online on impulse.
- **Schedule every minute**: ~30-min blocks; rebuild the remaining day when derailed (thoughtfulness > obedience).

## Email: become hard to reach
- **Sender filter** → put the burden on them ("reply only if it's a good match").
- **Process-centric reply** → answer the whole project in one message; kill the back-and-forth.
- **Don't respond** → ambiguous / uninteresting / disproportionately costly = professionally ignorable.

## Tells & smells (you're in trouble if…)
- You "just quickly check" your inbox mid-task → **attention residue** is taxing every hour.
- Your calendar is full but you can't name what you *produced* → **busyness as proxy** for value.
- You reach for your phone at every micro-boredom (line, elevator) → training **distraction addiction**; your focus muscle is atrophying.
- You justify a tool with "it has *some* benefit" → **any-benefit trap**.
- Work thoughts loop at night → no **shutdown ritual** (Zeigarnik effect).
- You default to the easiest task available → **Principle of Least Resistance** running your day.

## End-of-day shutdown (algorithm)
inbox check → capture loose tasks → skim all lists + next few days' calendar → rough plan for tomorrow → say **"Shutdown complete."**
