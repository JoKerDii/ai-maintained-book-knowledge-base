# Glossary — Trust Me, I'm Lying

**Abandoned shells** — Words like *exclusive, sources, leaked, developing* that keep their old aura of authority while emptied of meaning; George W.S. Trow's term ("in the age of no-authority, these are the authorities") (Ch 25).

**Advertising as content** — Turning ads into "news" by leaking them to blogs or getting them "banned," gaining free coverage companies normally pay for (Ch 21).

**Agenda manufacturing** — Blogs creating candidates, celebrities, or controversies (to fill infinite space/profit) that mass media then legitimizes (Ch 2).

**Backfire effect** — Nyhan & Reifler: a correction makes readers believe the original falsehood *more* strongly and confidently by reintroducing the claim (Ch 20).

**Blog (broad definition)** — All online publishing — Twitter to newspaper sites to group blogs — anything subject to the same attention incentives (Ch 2).

**Blog Con** — Blogs are built not to be profitable but to be *sold* to a larger buyer for a multiple of traffic; scoops and marquee bloggers inflate the exit (Ch 4).

**Cognitive dissonance / rigidity** — The mind's refusal to abandon an accepted premise; the conclusion persists even after its supporting facts are removed (Ch 20).

**Coverage about coverage** — Worthless meta-filler ("how the story broke") that borrows relevance from real news (Ch 21).

**Degradation ceremony** — Ritualized public destruction that expels a target from the group; works by reframing known facts, not new evidence (Ch 23).

**Delegation of trust** — Old-media habit of building on another outlet's facts, valid only when all share high verification standards (Ch 17).

**Douchebag insult** — Dismissing someone entirely without reasons or work, depriving them of being taken seriously (Ch 22).

**Experience technology** — A medium (the web) that earns more trust the more it's used — a trust the culture abuses (Ch 16, 24).

**Feeding the monster** — The controlling metaphor: new media is a cute monster you feed for attention until it grows dangerous and turns on you (Ch 1).

**Fiat over culture** — The influence gained by mastering the rules that govern blogs (Ch 2).

**Frovocation** — Jack Shafer's portmanteau: manufactured "faux provocation" / online controversy (Ch 3).

**Genetically modified entertainment** — Content engineered around search terms, tested titles, and calibrated length to maximize dependence (Ch 16).

**HARO (Help a Reporter Out)** — Service connecting "self-interested sources" to reporters; manufactures self-promotion disguised as research (Ch 6).

**Illusion of control** — The manipulator's fatal belief he can feed the monster without being devoured (Ch 1).

**Indirect bribe** — Feeding the levers a blogger is paid on (traffic, speed, access, free product) instead of paying cash (Ch 5).

**Iterative journalism** (process/beta journalism) — Publish first, verify later; sold as a "learning process" but really a profit model (each correction = another post) (Ch 19).

**Laurel leaf illusion** — A credibility marker (festival laurels, an html link) that survives after its underlying scarcity/meaning is gone (Ch 25).

**Lead steer** — Michael Sitrick: the one Level-1 site that starts the media stampede (Ch 3).

**Link economy** — Recursive trading of traffic/links between blogs; links *look* like citations but rarely are (Ch 17).

**"May becomes is becomes has"** — How speculation hardens into asserted fact as a story climbs the linked chain (Ch 13, 17).

**Narcotizing dysfunction** — Lazarsfeld & Merton: mistaking media busyness for knowledge; consuming as a substitute for action (Ch 16).

**One-Off Problem** — When news is sold story-by-story, publishers must create/sensationalize news to sell each unit anew (yellow press then, blogs now) (Ch 9).

**Pageview compensation** — Paying writers by traffic (Gawker's "big board"), making every post a conflict of interest (Ch 5).

**Pseudo-event** — Daniel Boorstin: anything staged solely to attract media attention (press release, launch, "leak," award show) (Ch 24).

**Pseudo-exclusive** — Nick Denton: "take ownership of a story even if it isn't a strict exclusive" — pretend you have a scoop (Ch 4).

**Pseudo-metrics** — Faked traffic/comments/coverage signals that trick blogs into treating you as newsworthy (Ch 11).

**Snark** — Cheap, unanswerable, click-friendly mockery; a defense mechanism of the weak that annihilates without effort ("cultural vandalism") (Ch 22).

**Specious present** — The window in which news has "news interest"; once read it becomes fact, so it can never truly be iterative (Ch 19, 20).

**Stacking** — Reverse-chronological "new stuff at the top"; makes the time stamp an expiration date, forcing short, constant posts (Ch 12).

**Substance words** — Weasel-word signals ("developing," "sources") that lend flimsy stories borrowed status (Ch 25).

**Suppression by transmission** — Misinforming the public not by omission but by what the sharing filter selects to spread (dead Detroit photos) (Ch 7).

**Three-level media chain** — Level 1 (small blogs) → Level 2 (legacy-media blogs) → Level 3 (national); the pathway of trading up (Ch 3).

**Trading up the chain** — Plant a story with a small low-standards blog and let it climb to major outlets "until the unreal becomes real" (Ch 1, 3).

**Traffic by any means** — The single dictum ruling every publisher decision; Advertisement × Traffic = Revenue (Ch 4).

**Unreality** — The netherworld where fake and real build on each other and can't be told apart — the cumulative endpoint of the system (Ch 24).

**Valence** — The intensity of positive or negative emotion; both extremes drive sharing, the moderate middle doesn't (Ch 7).

**Warnock's Dilemma** — Publishers can't tell whether silence means satisfaction or apathy, so they fear silence and chase any feedback (Ch 11).

**Yellow press** — 1830s–1900s sensational street-sold papers (Day, Bennett, Pulitzer, Hearst) whose incentives blogs recreate (Ch 9).
