# Patterns — Manipulation Tactics & Defenses

Book One = manipulation tactics. Book Two = the critique and how to read defensively. Each technique is a "pathetic vulnerability in our media system."

---

## Trading Up the Chain
**When to use**: Inject a narrative into mass culture with no money, credentials, or connections.
**How**: Seed a fabricated/exaggerated story at Level 1 (small, high-trust, understaffed blogs) → gather 3–4 links → present the cluster to Level 2 (legacy-media blogs) as a "trend" → soft-sell Level 3 (national) by targeting what their reporters read, plus anonymous multi-address tips ("How have you not covered this?").
**Trade-offs**: Once started, it "takes on a life of its own" and can't be controlled; the same machinery causes riots and deaths, not just publicity.

## The Indirect Bribe (Tactic #1)
**When to use**: Buy a blogger's coverage without cash.
**How**: Feed the levers their pay depends on — free product (rarely disclosed), affiliate commissions, exclusives, set visits, access, paid tweets, or invested-early influence in a rising blogger.
**Trade-offs**: Builds sources you can direct, but ties you into the same conflicted economy; the blogger knows and doesn't care.

## Tell Them What They Want to Hear (Tactic #2)
**When to use**: Get a message published without verification.
**How**: Fabricate a "leaked" internal document and email it as an anonymous insider; put out press releases for everything (SEO'd, auto-syndicated to finance sites); edit Wikipedia framing then cite it; use HARO to become a quoted "expert" and bootstrap upward.
**Trade-offs**: The same unverified-source pipeline can end careers (or your own) with one anonymous tip.

## Give Them What Spreads (Tactic #3)
**When to use**: Make content go viral.
**How**: Engineer for **valence** — crank anger first, then awe/excitement/titillation; strip out sadness and complexity. Make the ad itself the story via provocation.
**Trade-offs**: What spreads is systematically false to reality; provocation invites blowback and pulled ads (a "slap on the wrist" the author deems worth it).

## Help Them Trick Their Readers (Tactic #4)
**When to use**: Get click-driven coverage.
**How**: Feed rhetorical/loaded questions and leave issues open; the blogger converts them into question-mark headlines (a lie with legal cover — answer is always "no"). Avoid giving them anything final, useful, or reasonable.
**Trade-offs**: Escalation cycle — each headline must out-inflame the last; occasional exposure, but low consequences.

## Make It All About the Headline (Tactic #6)
**When to use**: Win the "Pick Me!" fight for a one-off story.
**How**: Pre-write the headline (or hint at options) in your email/release so the blogger steals it; make it extreme, literal, SEO-friendly ("Google doesn't laugh").
**Trade-offs**: Truth is irrelevant to the headline's job; the reader gets the buyer's remorse.

## Kill 'Em With Pageview Kindness (Tactic #7)
**When to use**: Hijack a metric-driven newsroom.
**How**: Give bloggers content that moves them toward pageview quotas; fake the pseudo-metrics — buy traffic (StumbleUpon/Outbrain), post to your own social channels, leave fake positive/negative comments to simulate a "hot debate" (exploiting Warnock's Dilemma).
**Trade-offs**: "The beast bites back" — once a site sees traffic in you, it won't stop and will fabricate scandal to keep the topic alive.

## Use the Technology Against Itself (Tactic #8)
**When to use**: Exploit the format's structural constraints.
**How**: Meet bloggers on-format (short, skim-friendly, spoon-fed into itty-bitty bites); fill their pre-shaped story with your terms. They cut expertise, so they can't check you.
**Trade-offs**: Blame the format, not the writer — but the same constraints distort everything, including in your favor and against you.

## Just Make Stuff Up (Tactic #9)
**When to use**: Create news from nothing.
**How**: Manufacture urgency (fake ~20-min "exclusive," given to multiple blogs; arbitrary deadlines). Rely on the self-feeding two-blog hustle where each site reifies the other's speculation up the chain.
**Trade-offs**: Runs with or without you; you can't reason or litigate a "kook" out of an invented angle.

## Weaponize the Backlash (Book Two)
**When to use**: Turn critics into distributors.
**How**: Make critics irrationally angry ("Bring It On" / "Blow Me" releases) so they spread your message everywhere; assume the persecuted-underdog cloak so exposure only re-famous-es you.
**Trade-offs**: Ethically corrosive; produces real collateral-damage victims (Sherrod, ACORN, Assange).

## The Implicit Shakedown / Illusion of a Lawsuit (Book Two)
**When to use**: Extract a payout or force behavior.
**How**: Veiled threats ("it'd be a pity if…"); leak a lawyer's *intention-to-sue* letter (not a real suit) to gossip blogs.
**Trade-offs**: Starts an arms race; frictionless enough that "it was almost difficult not to do so."

## Encourage Snark to Deflect (Book Two)
**When to use**: Change the nature of coverage away from a serious untrue claim.
**How**: Make the easiest story the mocking one — file a countersuit stuffed with hilarious trivia, email it with jokes hinting "make fun of this."
**Trade-offs**: Works for untrue facts as well as true ones, but "impossible to truly control" — snark burns everyone eventually.

## DEFENSIVE PATTERNS (how to read / protect against the above)

## Decode the Substance Words
**When to use**: Reading any blog post.
**How**: Translate "abandoned shells" — *tipster* = someone tricked the blogger; *leaked* = an email, likely fake; *BREAKING* = unconfirmed; *Updated* = pasted at the bottom, not reworked; *EXCLUSIVE* = a coverage arrangement or stolen story; *reached out for comment* = emailed two minutes before publishing.
**Trade-offs**: Skepticism alone "is not even enough to be a starting point" — the reliable markers are gone.

## Refuse the Published-First Trap
**When to use**: You're the subject of an iterative story.
**How**: Understand the post is already live; a response becomes a buried "update" that won't change minds (corrections backfire). Don't over-respond to snark (jujitsu — energy used against you). Ask "what do I plan to do with this information?"
**Trade-offs**: Speaking validates the story; silence leaves it standing — an impossible position by design.

## Change the Incentives
**When to use**: Fixing the system, not a single story.
**How**: Move from one-off/pageviews to subscription/paywall (NYT/Ochs → "more than twenty must-read articles a month"); demand quality over quantity as a reader; hold publishers to "Is this information true?"
**Trade-offs**: There is no tidy "solution" — the word itself takes the broken assumptions for granted.
