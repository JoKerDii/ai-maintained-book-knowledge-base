# Cheatsheet — Trust Me, I'm Lying

## The one rule that explains everything
**Advertisement × Traffic = Revenue → "traffic by any means."** When a blog's behavior confuses you, ask: *what did their business need?* The answer predicts the coverage.

## Decode a blog post (tells & smells)
| You see… | It really means… |
|---|---|
| "According to a tipster" | Someone (like a manipulator) tricked the blogger |
| "We're hearing reports" | Random tweets / message-board posts |
| "Leaked" / "official documents" | An email; docs likely fake or fabricated |
| "BREAKING" / "developing" | Published before facts confirmed |
| "Updated" | Pasted at the bottom; story not reworked |
| "EXCLUSIVE" | A favorable-coverage deal, or a stolen/multi-given story |
| "Sources tell us" | Unvetted, uncorroborated, attention-hungry |
| "Said in a press release" | Probably just spam email |
| "According to a report by" | Summarizer exaggerating with poor comprehension |
| "We've reached out for comment" | Emailed 2 minutes before hitting publish at 4 a.m. |
| Headline ends in "?" | Answer is "No, of course not" — a lie with cover |
| "which means / will result in" | Blogger has zero expertise, no consequences |

## Virality decision rule
**When you need something to spread, engineer for VALENCE. Rank emotions:**
1. **Anger** (strongest — ≈ 3 hrs as NYT front-page lead)
2. Awe / excitement / titillation / laughter (arousing = shareable)
3. Positive (≈ 1.2 hrs) — decent
- **Avoid: sadness** (low-arousal, kills sharing) and **reasonableness/complexity** (the unshareable middle).
- Rule of thumb: "Things must be negative but not too negative." Despair/pity → inaction; anger/fear → spread.

## Trading up the chain (the play)
Level 1 (small, high-trust, understaffed blog) → **get 3–4 links** → Level 2 (legacy-media blogs, look legit) → **soft sell** → Level 3 (national). Don't pitch the big reporter; **seed what they read.** A cluster of links = a "trend piece."

## Which tactic fits the goal?
| Goal | Tactic |
|---|---|
| Coverage without cash | Indirect bribe: free stuff, access, paid tweets (#1) |
| Publish without verification | Fake leak / press release / Wikipedia / HARO (#2) |
| Go viral | Crank valence, lead with anger (#3) |
| Click-driven coverage | Feed rhetorical questions → question headline (#4) |
| News from nothing | Manufacture urgency, fake multi-exclusive (#9) |
| Turn critics into distributors | Make them irrationally angry; underdog cloak |
| Extract a payout | Implicit shakedown / leak an intention-to-sue letter |
| Deflect a serious claim | Encourage snark |

## If you are the target (defense)
- **The post is already live.** A response = a buried "update." **Corrections backfire** (backfire effect) — they entrench the false belief.
- **Don't fight snark** — reason and appeals to humanity are used against you (jujitsu). "Nothing you can say."
- **Speaking validates / silence leaves it standing** — the iterative trap has no clean exit.
- Ask: **"What do I plan to do with this information?"** Usually: nothing → it's distraction.

## Red flags a story is manufactured
- Appears simultaneously across many sites with near-identical wording → link-economy propagation.
- "May" on site 1 → "is" on site 2 → "has" on site 3.
- Question-mark headline; CAPS tags (EXCLUSIVE, UNPRECEDENTED); no named/verified source.
- A short, conveniently edited clip prefit to one group's biases (doctored-clip play).
- A pseudo-event: press release, launch, "leak," award show, banned ad, contrite post-scandal interview.

## Structural truths to remember
- **One-off distribution forces sensationalism; subscription enables nuance.** RSS died on purpose.
- **A blog isn't small if its readers are media elite.** Influence = who reads, not how many.
- **Blogs are built to be sold**, so short-term traffic desperation is maximal — and desperation is the manipulator's best asset.
- **"If the news is important it will find me" is false** — what finds you rose by slickness, titillation, and polarity, not value.
- **Skepticism is not enough** to defend yourself; the reliable markers of authority are gone.
