# Chapter 13 (XII): Tactic #9 — Just Make Stuff Up (Everyone Else Is Doing It)

## Core Idea
Bloggers *must* find an angle on every topic, so they always do — even where none exists. Manufacture urgency and they'll write whatever you want. And you don't even need to be the source: the system fabricates angles on its own, then reifies them up the chain.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Manufacture Urgency for the Fake Exclusive**: give a blogger an illusory ~20-minute head start and they'll write anything, however you want it. Give the *same* "exclusive" to multiple blogs (the plural is deliberate) — they race to publish first. Add an arbitrary deadline ("we go live in the morning") and even big blogs skip fact-checking.
- **Fictive Interpolation Up the Chain**: an invented angle on one site becomes the source for another, "until the origins are forgotten." It moves vertically to more reputable sites, seeming more real at each level. "The products of our imagination become the solid facts of society" (Cooley).
- **The Two-Blog Hustle (self-feeding)**: Site A ("reporting") needs Site B ("controversy") to justify itself, and vice versa — each reifies the other's baseless speculation, feeding fashion/news sites downstream. Posting a comment or filing a lawsuit doesn't interrupt the cycle; it feeds it.

## Key Concepts
- **"There's more bullshit than legitimate information"** — MG Siegler (TechCrunch) admits most of what he and competitors write is bullshit, "a lot" of it.
- **Shamelessness as a virtue**: enables "Amy Winehouse's Untimely Death Is a Wake Up Call for Small Business Owners" and "Has American Apparel Gone too Far?" sourced to "Some Tweeters."
- **Always wrong, never in doubt**: the earnest conviction of a "kook" (BNET's Jim Edwards) becomes fodder other sites disingenuously "report on what the kook reported" — CBS logo lends it official status.

## Mental Models
- **"No topic too mundane that you can't pull a post out of it"** (Blogger Boot Camp) — angle-hunters never come up empty because they can't afford to.
- **It goes in every direction**: do something innocent → wrenched out of context; complicated → simplified beyond recognition; nothing → turned into something; wrong → spun beyond comprehension.
- **Pride goeth before the fall**: bloggers pride themselves on finding the angle — "shit becomes sugar" — and that pride is the lever to use against them.

## Anti-patterns
- **Reasoning or litigating with a "kook" blogger**: it "would just give Edwards more to talk about." You can't argue someone out of an angle they're paid to invent.
- **Assuming there's a manipulator behind every fabricated angle**: sometimes "the system was manipulating itself" — the author was even hired to *mitigate* self-generated manipulation with more manipulation.

## Worked Example
BNET's Jim Edwards repeatedly asked, across escalating headlines, why American Apparel didn't roll a 6%-interest personal loan into a 15%-interest investor loan (i.e., he didn't grasp that 6% < 15%). The exchange:
- Author: "You issued a correction on this story in 2009."
- Edwards: "But I'm quoting the status of Charney's loans directly from the proxy. Is the proxy wrong?"
- Author: "YOUR BASIC UNDERSTANDING OF MATH IS WRONG!"

His nonexistent "loan scandal" then fueled conspiracy theories, and — bearing the CBS logo — became "fodder for fashion websites at the national level." Jezebel needed his "reporting" to snark on; Edwards needed Jezebel's "controversy" to justify his analysis; both fed the fashion sites downstream. Edwards was later rewarded with a job at Business Insider. "All the infamous American Apparel controversies were made up. Either I made them up or bloggers did."

## Key Takeaways
1. Bloggers must produce an angle, so manufactured urgency reliably gets your version published.
2. Fake "exclusives" to multiple blogs at once trigger a publish-first race.
3. Invented angles reify as they climb — mistakes pile on mistakes, becoming "an amalgam of an amalgam."
4. The self-feeding two-blog hustle runs with or without a manipulator; you can't reason it away.
