# Chapter 15 (XIV): There Are Others — The Manipulator Hall of Fame

## Core Idea
The author wasn't the only one exploiting blog incentives. Others (Andrew Breitbart, James O'Keefe) wielded the same tactics over national politics, ruining real lives — proving media manipulation is not fringe but a repeatable craft with ominous ends.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Prefit Narrative + Doctored Clip**: produce a short, cheap, heavily/disingenuously edited video prefitted to a vocal group's biases (e.g., angry Republicans). Its speed overwhelms the media's "atrophied verification and accountability muscles" before anyone checks. A hollow shell blogs use "as a substitute for the real thing."
- **Three Stories from One (the profitable backlash)**: outlets get the accusation, then the reversal, then "the discussion about the controversy" — three stories instead of one, lasting a week and good for follow-ups. "The media doesn't mind being played, because they get something out of it."
- **Feeding the Media Like Training a Dog** (Breitbart): "You can't throw an entire steak at a dog… give it little bits of steak over and over until it learns."
- **Weaponize the Backlash**: "the best way to make your critics work for you is to make them irrationally angry." Blinded with rage, they spread your message everywhere (Breitbart: "Bring It On"; author: Tucker's "Blow Me" release).

## Key Concepts
- **Manipulator, not ideologue**: Breitbart was "an expert on what spreads — a provocateur," first employee of Drudge and founding employee of HuffPost (built both dominant conservative *and* liberal blogs). Not a racist; a media manipulator.
- **The persecuted-underdog cloak**: assuming victimhood means the inevitable exposure *helps* rather than hurts. Being caught as a manipulator "can only help make you more famous."
- **Collateral damage**: Shirley Sherrod (edited to look racist, forced to resign; Obama apologized), gutted ACORN, framed NPR — real people fired or branded before the doctored footage was exposed six months later.

## Mental Models
- **Design for consumption**: Breitbart split the Sherrod clip into 2:30 and 1:06 pieces — quick to consume, easy to republish — knowing no one would sit through the 43-minute original.
- **The dog still responds to the same commands**: even after a master (Breitbart) dies, the trained media keeps obeying the same stimuli.
- **A bullshit correction is still a win**: Breitbart's technically-true "correction" put his name back in the news, as did O'Keefe's exposed edits.

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming exposure deters the manipulator**: nearly all O'Keefe's stories were later shown doctored (the pimp hat/fur coat edited in — he actually wore a suit), yet by then the image was "indelibly stuck" and the exposure only re-boosted his fame.
- **Mistaking the players for ideologues**: treating them as partisan kooks misreads them as ruthless "seekers of attention" monetizing fame into books, speaking gigs, and ad revenue.

## Worked Example
Breitbart's Shirley Sherrod video traded up the chain "perfectly." Per Media Matters: FoxNews.com and Gateway Pundit picked it up first, then Hot Air and dozens of blogs (embedding the YouTube clip, repeating "racist"), then a CBS NYC affiliate, then Drudge, then lead stories on nearly every nighttime cable and morning show. Titled "Video Proof: The NAACP Awards Racism," the post spent most of its 1,300 words fighting an imaginary foil rather than explaining the video's origin. Sherrod — actually giving a speech about *how not to be racist* — was forced to resign. For Breitbart the wide debunking "was not a failure"; it put him on every outlet's lips. "Sherrod was just collateral damage."

## Key Takeaways
1. Doctored, prefitted clips exploit the shortage of original reporting — they're cheap substitutes blogs eagerly run.
2. The backlash is a feature: it yields three stories and irrational amplification.
3. Exposure doesn't punish the manipulator; it re-famous-es them.
4. These are attention-seekers, not ideologues — and the machine keeps obeying after they're gone.
