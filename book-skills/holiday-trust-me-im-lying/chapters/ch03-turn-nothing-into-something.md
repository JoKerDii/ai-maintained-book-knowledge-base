# Chapter 3 (II): How to Turn Nothing Into Something — Trading Up the Chain

## Core Idea
"Trading up the chain" manipulates the media through *recursion*: place a story with a small low-standards blog, which becomes the source for a bigger blog, which becomes the source for major outlets — "a self-reinforcing news wave." Content now filters *up* as much as down.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Three-Level Media Chain**:
  - **Level 1 — The Entry Point**: small blogs, hyperlocal sites, a friend's site. High trust, cash-strapped, traffic-hungry, understaffed. Easiest to place a loosely-relevant story that sets up the next level.
  - **Level 2 — The Legacy Media**: newspaper/TV-station blogs and branded sister sites (SmartMoney, BNet). They *look* legitimate (share the URL, get aggregated in Google News) but have looser editorial standards. A Wired.com blog mention lets you put "'A revolutionary device' — Wired" on the box.
  - **Level 3 — National**: NYT, Today Show, CNN. Less pushing, more "massaging." The lower sites now *want* national links, so they promote your story to aggregators (Reddit, Digg) themselves. 3–4 links = the makings of a "trend piece."
- **The Soft Sell (Level 2 → 3)**: Never pitch a Washington Post columnist directly; instead seed the media-centric sites they read (Gawker, Mediabistro) so outrage reaches them indirectly. When direct, send links from several fake Gmail/Yahoo addresses: "How have you not done a story about this yet?"

## Key Concepts
- **Self-reinforcing news wave**: coverage that legitimizes and amplifies itself at each level.
- **Lead steer** (Michael Sitrick): the media gallops as a herd; you only need one Level-1 site to start the stampede, then point everyone in its direction.
- **Frovocation** (Jack Shafer): "faux provocation" — manufactured online controversy.
- **89% of journalists use blogs for research** (Cision/GWU); most admit online sources are less reliable but use them anyway due to "lack of fact-checking, verification, or reporting standards."

## Mental Models
- **Content filters up, not just down** (Lindsay Robertson): small sites are "farm teams" for larger ones — pitch the lower tier deliberately.
- **The media is dancing with itself**: everyone (except the top layer) is under crushing deadline pressure and "desperately, desperately need[s] to buy." The flimsiest excuse suffices.
- **Create the perception the meme already exists**; the reporter thinks they're merely popularizing it and rarely looks past first impressions.

## Anti-patterns
- **The unverified anonymous source**: standard journalism requires editors to know a source's identity. The author has been an anonymous source "dozens of times," never once verified — the safeguard is dead.
- **Intention laundering**: charity and violence use the identical chain (Terry Jones's Koran burning traded up perfectly, killing ~30 people in Afghanistan).

## Worked Example
A friend's Kickstarter charity campaign, executed on the author's instructions: (1) made a YouTube video exaggerating exotic-but-least-beneficial work; (2) wrote it up on a small Brooklyn blog whose stories get picked up by HuffPost NY; (3) HuffPost featured it as NYC/LA local news; (4) sent a fake-address email with the links to a CBS LA reporter, who did a TV piece using the edited clips; (5) having pre-seeded Reddit connections for weeks, posted the bundle to Reddit → front page → "cool stuff" blogs (BoingBoing, Laughing Squid) picked it up. Result: ~500k views, ~$10k raised, national registration — "from nothing to something," with no budget or publicist.

## Key Takeaways
1. Break the chain into three levels; each level's coverage is "evidence" that legitimizes the next.
2. Getting from Level 2 to 3 is a soft sell — target what the big reporters read, not the reporters.
3. A cluster of 3–4 links reads as a trend; that's all national outlets need.
4. The system is so primed it manufactures news even with no one pushing (fake Favre meme).
