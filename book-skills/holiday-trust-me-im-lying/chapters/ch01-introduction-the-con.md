# Chapter 1: Introduction — The Con (Trading Up the Chain)

## Core Idea
Marketers manufacture news by exploiting the economics of online media: plant a story at the bottom of the media food chain and let it climb, "until the unreal becomes real." The author is a self-described media manipulator "paid to deceive" — and the whole system invites it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Trading Up the Chain**: The master strategy. Plant a fabricated or exaggerated story with a tiny low-standards blog → it becomes the source for a larger blog → then for major outlets → then cable news — "and back again, until the unreal becomes real."
  - When to use: whenever you need to inject a narrative into mass culture without credentials, money, or connections.
  - How: seed at Level 1 (small blogs), gather 3–4 links, present the cluster as a "trend" to Level 3 outlets via anonymous tips.
- **Feeding the Monster**: The controlling metaphor. New media is a cute monster you feed for attention; the more you feed it the bigger, more demanding, and more dangerous it grows — until it turns on you. Applies to the manipulator, the publisher, AND the reader.

## Key Concepts
- **Media manipulator**: someone who lies to the media so the media lies to the public — for pay.
- **The illusion of control**: the manipulator's fatal belief that he can feed the monster without being devoured ("each hopes that if he feeds the crocodile enough, the crocodile will eat him last").
- **Manufactured firestorm**: coordinated fake outrage (fake tips, fake complaints, fake tweets, Photoshopped ads) that becomes real once real people react to it.
- **What begins online ends offline**: online narratives filter into TV, print, conversation, and real-world decisions.

## Mental Models
- Think of blogs as **today's newswires** — "The News, Poisoned At Its Source."
- Use the **two-book structure**: Book One = how to manipulate (tactics); Book Two = what it means (consequences). The tactics only make sense alongside their costs.
- Treat **desperation** as the manipulator's greatest asset in a victim — publishers and bloggers "desperately, desperately need to buy" content.

## Anti-patterns
- **Believing intentions justify the outcome**: "intentions are not a justification I'm going to hide behind." Trading up the chain for charity uses the identical machinery as trading it up to cause riots and death.
- **Assuming fabricated stories only waste time**: they move markets, end careers, and (Terry Jones / Koran burning) get people killed.

## Worked Example
The *I Hope They Serve Beer in Hell* campaign (Tucker Max). With no budget, the author: designed and bought offensive billboards, then vandalized them himself at 2 a.m. and emailed photos to blogs under the fake name "Evan Meyer." He filed anonymous complaints about his own ads and leaked them; baited LGBT and women's groups to protest screenings (guaranteeing local-news cameras); started a fake Facebook boycott; posted fake comments and tweets; won a contest for photographing a "defaced" ad (using a photo from the wrong city); and manufactured stories about Tucker's behavior. He closed the loop with a press release answering his own manufactured criticism: "TUCKER MAX RESPONDS TO CTA DECISION: 'BLOW ME.'" Result: FoxNews front page, Page Six, Washington Post and Chicago Tribune editorials, a CTA ad ban — and a #1 New York Times bestseller. "The entire firestorm was, essentially, fake."

## Key Takeaways
1. The vulnerability is structural: because bloggers are paid by the pageview and write to catch attention, "the system eats up the kind of material I produce."
2. Exposing the tactics is the point — "once in the open they'll no longer work as well." This skill is media literacy, not a playbook to admire.
3. The manipulator, the blogger, and the reader are all feeding the same monster; no one is purely a victim or purely in control.
4. The most precious resource being stolen is the reader's **time and credulity**.
