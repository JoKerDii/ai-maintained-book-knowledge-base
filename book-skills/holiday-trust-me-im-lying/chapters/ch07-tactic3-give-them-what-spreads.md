# Chapter 7 (VI): Tactic #3 — Give Them What Spreads, Not What's Good

## Core Idea
"If it doesn't spread, it's dead" (Henry Jenkins). Blogs publish what will go viral, not what's accurate or important — so design what you sell them around the emotions that drive sharing. The most powerful predictor of virality is **anger**.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Valence Formula**: virality is driven by *valence* — the intensity of positive OR negative emotion. Both extremes beat anything moderate. Reasonableness, complexity, and mixed emotions never spread.
  - Levers to crank valence: scandal, conflict, triviality, titillation, dogmatism, arousal, indignation.
- **The Emotion Hierarchy (Wharton study of 7,000 NYT Most-Emailed articles)**:
  - **Anger** = the strongest predictor. One SD increase in anger ≈ 3 extra hours as the NYT front-page lead story.
  - **Positive/awe** ≈ 1.2 hours — significant but weaker.
  - **Sadness = unviral** — a low-arousal emotion that *depresses* sharing.
- **Arousal, not just negativity**: hopelessness/despair/pity make people do nothing or act offline; anger, fear, excitement, laughter make people *spread*. "Things must be negative but not too negative."

## Key Concepts
- **Economic disaster porn**: the viral Detroit-ruins slideshows contain *no people* — deliberately dead, because signs of life (homeless, stray dogs) evoke unshareable sadness. Slideshows also multiply pageviews (20 per user vs. 1 for an article).
- **Suppression by transmission** (not omission): realistic Detroit photos exist, but only the "cool"/dead ones spread — the public is misinformed not by censorship but by what the sharing filter selects.
- **Humiliation should be monetized** (Rob Walker on the Rick Santelli clip): outlets post their own embarrassing footage to capture the traffic rather than suppress it.

## Mental Models
- **Package for the emotional spectrum**: information "just *is*" — neither clearly good nor bad — so distort it into something that registers as high-valence.
- **"They push your buttons so you'll press theirs"**: negative footage makes viewers more aroused, attentive, and likely to hit "share."
- **~96% of viral articles spike once and vanish** — but the *consequences* (a toxic political party, an attention addiction) persist.

## Anti-patterns
- **Trying to spread accurate, complex, or sad truth**: it's structurally disadvantaged — "the web has only one currency… and it adds up to false perception."
- **Believing controversy is created "for the sake of controversy"**: for the author it was ROI — chatter correlated with sales spikes.

## Worked Example
The author ran fully nude (NSFW) ads featuring porn star Sasha Grey on two tiny blogs for ~$1,200 total. Formula: a naked woman + a major U.S. retailer + blogs = a massive online story. The ads got picked up by Nerve, BuzzFeed, Fast Company, Jezebel, Refinery29, NBC New York and more — reaching print as far as Rolling Stone Brazil. Almost none of the millions of views were on the paid sites. Some wrote in anger, some in disgust, some loved it — "the important part was that they wrote about it at all." The leveraged-advertising strategy helped take American Apparel online sales from $40M to ~$60M in three years on a minuscule budget.

## Key Takeaways
1. Design content for sharing emotions, led by anger; avoid sadness and moderation.
2. Both emotional extremes (valence) beat the reasonable middle every time.
3. What spreads is systematically *false to reality* — the sharing filter selects against truth.
4. Provocation is a cheap substitute for paid ad inventory: make the ad the story.
