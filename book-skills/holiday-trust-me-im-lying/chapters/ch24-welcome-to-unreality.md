# Chapter 24 (XXIII): Welcome to Unreality

## Core Idea
Individually, blogging's bad stories look like manipulator opportunities. *Collectively* — millions of posts building on each other — they produce **unreality**: "a netherworld between the fake and the real where each builds on the other and they cannot be told apart."

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The News Funnel**: the media systematically *limits* the information the public sees. Layers from top to bottom:
  1. ALL THAT HAPPENS
  2. ALL THAT'S KNOWN BY THE MEDIA
  3. ALL THAT IS NEWSWORTHY
  4. ALL THAT IS PUBLISHED AS NEWS
  5. ALL THAT SPREADS
  "The news… is just the content that successfully navigated the media's filters" — a *constructed* reality, not a summary of what happened or even of what's important.
- **Only the Unexpected Makes the News**: "News is only news if it departs from the routine" (Park; Denton: "the job of journalism is to provide surprise"). Since most of life is normal (and omitted), the basic need for novelty "inherently puts our newsmakers at odds with reality."
- **Pseudo-Events** (Daniel Boorstin, *The Image*, 1962): anything planned deliberately to attract media attention — press releases, award shows, premieres, product launches, "leaks," the contrite post-scandal interview, controversial ads. Real enough to happen, but staged solely to generate press and inject narratives.
- **From the Fake, the Real**: create a pseudo-event → trade it up the chain → elicit real responses and action → "you have altered reality itself."

## Key Concepts
- **Experience technology**: the more the web is used, the more users trust it — "the longer a user engages with it, the more they believe in the world it creates." That trust has been "utterly abused."
- **Embracing the fake**: Blodget's "Dear PR Folks" post inviting publicists to write directly for Business Insider — "he became the purveyor of unreality and its publisher."
- **Pseudo-events are a news subsidy**: clean, clear, unconstrained by what happens naturally — "typically much more interesting to publishers than real events."
- **Pseudo-environment → actual behavior** (Lippmann, 1922): the news is a pseudo-environment, "but our responses to that environment are not pseudo but actual."

## Mental Models
- **Unreality doesn't stay unreal**: pseudo-events "don't stay unreal" — "the domain in which they are consumed and acted on is undoubtedly real." Counterfeit events are "laundered and passed to the public as clean bills — to buy real things."
- **The dominant medium determines culture**: as TV once turned everything into entertainment (Postman), the web turns everything into *conflict, controversy, and crap* — its god is Traffic, its question "Will this spread?"
- **Cite your own leak**: Cheney leaked bogus WMD info to the NYT, then cited "a story in the New York Times this morning" on *Meet the Press* — "He used his own pseudo-event to create pseudo-news."

## Anti-patterns
- **Believing the news informs you**: it's "a mechanism for systematically limiting the information seen by the public." The funnel is the opposite of a summary.
- **Trusting because it's written/aggregated**: rising trust in an experience technology is exactly what makes the abuse possible.

## Worked Example
Dick Cheney, 2002: leaked unverified information about Iraqi WMD to an attention-hungry NYT reporter, then went on *Meet the Press* and said, "There's a story in the New York Times this morning, and I want to attribute the Times," citing his own plant as now-"public," accepted fact — to help drive the country to war. "I use unreality to get free publicity. Cheney used his media manipulations to drive the public toward war… By the time they [knew], the facts had been established, the fake made real by media chatter, and a real war had been waged. From the pseudo-environment came actual behavior." An emblematic TechCrunch headline: "Rumors of Apple Rumors Now Leading to Rumors of Counter-Rumors."

## Key Takeaways
1. The news is a funnel that constructs reality, not a mirror that reflects it.
2. Pseudo-events are staged fictions that become the news the one-off medium craves.
3. Fake never stays fake — real responses to pseudo-events alter the real world.
4. The web's ruling metric is Traffic, so it renders the world as conflict and crap.
