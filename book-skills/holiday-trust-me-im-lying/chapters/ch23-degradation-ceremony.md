# Chapter 23 (XXII): The 21st-Century Degradation Ceremony — Blogs as Machines of Hatred and Punishment

## Core Idea
"In the old days men had the rack. Now they have the Press" (Wilde). The online media cycle is not a process for developing truth but for performing cultural catharsis — a modern, "civilized" way to tear someone to pieces.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Degradation Ceremony** (anthropology): ritualized destruction that lets the public single out and denounce a member, lower their status, and expel them — "a we-versus-you scenario with deep biological roots." By the end the disgraced person is cemented as "not one of us"; everything about them is "torn down and rewritten."
- **Reframing, Not New Facts**: the target hasn't changed — the *role blogs need them to play* has. "Almost all the evidence blogs used… was available and known before the charges came to light." Quirks that were "endearing" become "creepy"; secrecy becomes "paranoid"; a mission becomes "ego."
- **Testing the Narrative**: before committing to destruction, blogs try angles. Gawker on Assange went from "Is WikiLeaks' Julian Assange a Nerdy Sex God?" and "Weird Hair?" to "Megalomaniac" within two weeks — "it wasn't the allegations that marked the point of no return; they were just a convenient cover."

## Key Concepts
- **On the Pleasure of Hating** (Hazlitt): the passion "carries us back to the feuds, the heart-burnings, the havoc… of a barbarous age"; blogs love "the excitement of hunting and the rush of the kill without any of the danger."
- **Build-up enables tear-down**: blogs turn on people "just as we've begun to build them up," often having created the celebrity themselves under equally frivolous pretenses. "No wonder only morons and narcissists enter the public sphere."
- **Rage of the creative underclass**: overqualified, underpaid reporters covering the rich and talentless "grind with the rage of the creative underclass" — bitterness that fuels the ceremonies.

## Mental Models
- **Digital blood sport**: unlike Greek tragedy (which taught humility and how misfortune could befall anyone — de Botton), blog degradation teaches nothing. It's "mere spectacle… to make us feel better by hurting others," stressing that the target is a freak while "we are normal."
- **"This can happen to you too"**: Assange "got to experience personally what he had, through WikiLeaks, helped do to many others."
- **Directable mob**: the author gets "frantic calls from sensitive millionaires" to fend off a mob — or to "discreetly direct this mob toward one of their enemies." "Once I start, I don't stop."

## Anti-patterns
- **Assuming guilt from reframed evidence**: the people who "roast[ed] Assange alive" knew no more about his guilt than the author did; suspect allegations against someone who angered every powerful government warranted caution, not a ceremony.
- **Expecting a satirical message to land amid celebrity drama**: the Woody Allen "Highest Rabbi" billboards were *intended* as a statement against hysterical media destruction — but blogs ignored the message for the $10M-lawsuit celebrity drama.

## Worked Example
Julian Assange: in under a year, "from intriguing web hero to ominous pariah." He "did not suddenly become an awful person overnight. He had not changed. But tempers had." When suspect sexual-misconduct allegations surfaced, Gawker launched WikileakiLeaks.org soliciting embarrassing info, ran "Accused then Immediately Un-Accused of Rape" (note: "Un-Accused" — blogs didn't), and pivoted every prior endearing trait into a damning one. "Again, Assange hadn't changed. Someone had just reframed him. The role blogs needed him to play had shifted." His work's redeeming value — the very thing that made him worth covering — became "utterly irrelevant." The billboard the author helped run against this exact phenomenon (defending even Woody Allen's contributions, quoting Dershowitz on losing good people to "obsessive focus on private lives") was itself swallowed by the ceremony.

## Key Takeaways
1. The cycle exists to punish and expel, not to find truth — cultural catharsis, not journalism.
2. Destruction works by reframing known facts, not discovering new ones.
3. Blogs build people up specifically so they can tear them down for spectacle.
4. The mob is directable; a manipulator can aim it — and it teaches its audience nothing.
