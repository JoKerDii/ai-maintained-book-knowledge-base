# Chapter 22 (XXI): The Dark Side of Snark — When Internet Humor Attacks

## Core Idea
Snark is the perfect device for people with nothing to say who must talk for a living: cheap, click-friendly, and impossible to answer. It's not "frustrated outrage" seeking change — it's a defense mechanism that annihilates without effort and destroys culture.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Snark, defined**: Denby — "the nasty, insidious, rug-pulling, teasing insult, which makes reference to some generally understood shared prejudice." The author's simpler test: "you're dealing with snark when you attempt to respond and realize there is nothing you can say." The remark means nothing (though it hurts), and the speaker doesn't care enough about anything to be criticized back. "If I call you a douche, how would you defend yourself without making it worse?"
- **Snark as Jujitsu**: a victim's instinct is to appeal to reason ("that's not true!") or humanity ("why are you doing this?"). Both fail — "the energy you'd exert in your defense will be used against you to make the embarrassment worse." A squeaky "why is everyone making fun of me?!" invites more mockery.
- **Encourage Snark to Deflect**: make the easiest story the one where blogs mock the whole mess. The author filed a countersuit stuffed with trivial-but-hilarious details and emailed it with jokes — hinting: make fun of the lawsuit instead of debating a serious untrue claim. "Snark worked just as well for untrue facts as for true ones."

## Key Concepts
- **The douchebag insult**: "a way to dismiss someone entirely without doing any of the work or providing any of the reasons… deprives the recipient of the credentials of being taken seriously."
- **Snark is intrinsically destructive**: "It breaks things; it does not build." No politician ever reformed because of a joke about his hairline. Real change requires *believing* what you say beneath the humor and being *consistent* (like Jon Stewart) — snark isn't.
- **Cultural vandalism** (Ebert): snark "makes the conditions that make culture possible impossible." Its targets are earnestness, honesty, vulnerability — "everyone is being snarked into line."
- **Who benefits from snark**: only people with nothing to lose (reality stars) thrive under it; genuine contributors "lurk in the back of the room, hoping not to get noticed."

## Mental Models
- **The weak are drawn to snark; the strong say what they mean.** Snark shields insecurity and gives a feeling of control the writer doesn't possess: "It can criticize, but it cannot be criticized."
- **You lived by the sword of online attention, now you may die by it** — the author's advice to a snark victim: there's nothing you can do.
- **They didn't mean any of it**: at the Fleshbot Awards the bloggers who'd called Dov a rapist and monster were "smart and friendly in person" — "It had all been a game… If Dov hadn't been a convenient target, they'd have said the same stuff about someone else."

## Anti-patterns
- **Defending yourself against snark**: Scott Adams deleted his post (drew more attention), then repeatedly clarified — turning himself from a generation-defining cartoonist into "a cross between a buffoon and a misogynistic rape apologist," a hole he can't dig out of.
- **Posting your cell number to "talk it out"** (Dov): created new material to mock and ~1,000 prank calls.

## Worked Example
Nikki Finke's "live snarking" of the Academy Awards: she repeatedly called it the "GAYEST OSCARS EVER" for too much singing/dancing — then minutes later railed against honoring Jerry Lewis for "antigay slurs," writing "Humanitarian my ass" about a man who raised $60M+ for muscular dystrophy. "This is snark in its purest form: aggressively, self-righteously full of shit." She'd made her own gay jokes minutes before yet felt superior. A producer couldn't respond ("Finke's attack came from a place of self-loathing") without being called "whiny," "humorless," or "old." Even Ebert, who called snark "cultural vandalism," couldn't resist tweeting about the death of *Jackass* star Ryan Dunn ("Friends don't let jackasses drink and drive") — and "the snarker is snarked," his Facebook page hacked and deleted. The cycle embodied.

## Key Takeaways
1. Snark is unanswerable by design — reason and humanity both backfire (jujitsu).
2. It's a defense mechanism of the weak, not a "call to change"; it builds nothing.
3. It destroys culture by punishing earnestness, and rewards only the shameless.
4. Encourage snark to deflect coverage — but it "cannot be controlled" and burns everyone eventually.
