# Chapter 12 (XI): Tactic #8 — Use the Technology Against Itself

## Core Idea
"The medium is the message" (McLuhan). The technical constraints of blogging — reverse-chronological stacking, short posts, constant updates, skimming readers — predetermine what bloggers can publish. Understand the constraints and you can turn the technology against the people who depend on it.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Stacking (reverse-chronological order)**: Tim Berners-Lee's "new stuff goes at the top" became the web's default. The time stamp is "like an expiration date," creating relentless pressure to be short, immediate, and constantly refreshed. "Always Be Blogging" — daily, if not hourly.
- **The Length Ceiling**: the medium suppresses length regardless of subject. HuffPost rule: readers quit if they can't see the end by ~800 words. Denton's ideal Gawker item: "One hundred words. Two hundred, max. Any good idea can be expressed at that length." (80% of cancer blog posts were <500 words; avg 335.)
- **The Skim Constraint**: avg time on Jezebel ~1 minute, Lifehacker <10 seconds; ~1 second to hook; bounce rate >50%. Eyes hit the headline (first few words), descend the left column, and leave. Form (short paragraphs, bold decks, bullets) buys a few seconds — so knowing anything gets cut.
- **Nielsen's Rule**: cut 40% of every article; supposedly it loses "only 30%" of its value — "the kind of math publishers go through every day."

## Key Concepts
- **The medium behaves like a dictator** (Alterman on TV): a visual medium can't show abstract ideas, so it gives conflict, talking heads, b-roll. Blogs are equally logical — just a different logic.
- **What gets left on the cutting-room floor is expertise**: "actually knowing anything about what they're talking about" is what bloggers cut — making it easier to spin or lie.
- **Duty to reality, not to platforms** (Jack Fuller): bloggers believe the opposite — they serve the platform, and the world doesn't shape itself to any platform.

## Mental Models
- **Meet them on their terms, fill it with yours**: "I'll come to them with the story… their story will be filled with my terms." They won't check with anyone else.
- **The format is the problem — or the perfect opportunity, depending on how you look at it.** Blogs must distort the news for the format to work.
- **Spoon-feed the medium**: break long, thorough material into "itty-bitty bites."

## Anti-patterns
- **Blaming individual writers or editors**: "where there is little volition, there should be little bitterness or blame." The constraints explain almost everything blogs do — which is why they're predictable and exploitable.
- **Fact-checking by tweeting into the void**: a Mediagazer blogger tried to "verify [the author's] credibility" by asking strangers on Twitter rather than emailing him — because waiting for a reply was harder than speculating.

## Worked Example
Promoting a book that had spent five weeks on the NYT bestseller list (people paid for it in book form), the author found the material "just too long" for blogs. So he "got rid of the thoroughness and the supporting arguments and reduced it down to the most basic, provocative parts." One chapter — enjoyed fully in book form — "had to be split up into eight separate posts." To get attention he had to "cut it up into itty-bitty bites and spoon-feed it to readers and bloggers like babies." The medium's length constraint dictated the mangling.

## Key Takeaways
1. Stacking, length ceilings, and skim behavior are structural, not choices — so they're predictable.
2. The first thing cut is expertise, which makes spinning and lying easy.
3. Bloggers serve the platform over reality; exploit that by meeting them on-format.
4. Blame the format, not the writer — then use the format against them.
