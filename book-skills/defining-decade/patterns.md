# Patterns & Techniques — The Defining Decade

Concrete moves Meg Jay uses (or teaches) to help twentysomethings act. Each is a repeatable technique, not just a concept.

## Take the Job With the Most Capital
**When to use**: Choosing between twentysomething options (jobs, programs, moves).
**How**: Rank options by identity capital gained — skills, connections, door-opening stories — not by comfort or short-term pay. Reframe "paying dues" as "investing in your dream."
**Trade-offs**: The high-capital option is often less fun/lower-paid now; the payoff is compounding and delayed.

## Assemble Your Six-Flavor Table
**When to use**: When you (or someone) claim to have "no idea" what to do.
**How**: List your *actual* viable options (usually ~6, shaped by your history), not the mythical infinite menu. Then choose among those. Ask the real question: *What could I do well enough to support the life I want, and enjoy enough to keep at for years?* — not the lottery question.
**Trade-offs**: Choosing opens you to work, failure, and heartbreak — but not-choosing has worse, delayed consequences.

## Surface the Unthought Known
**When to use**: Vague "I just want things to work out" talk that avoids what the person already knows.
**How**: Have them recount real experiences and history; reflect back the patterns; let them "stumble onto" the choice they were avoiding. Name the vagueness as a defense.
**Trade-offs**: Requires patience (listening first); the surfaced truth may be scary to act on.

## Ask a Weak Tie for a Specific Favor (the Franklin method)
**When to use**: Seeking a job, intro, reference, or informational interview.
**How**: Research the person's expertise → make yourself interesting and relevant → ask for a *clearly defined* favor (not "coffee???"). Doing a favor makes them like you (Ben Franklin effect); one favor begets larger ones.
**Trade-offs**: Some will say no; vague or oversized asks (deciding your whole career) overwhelm and fail.

## Sort Shoulds From Goals
**When to use**: Speech full of "should," "supposed to," "less-than."
**How**: For each ambition, ask: does this direct me from inside (goal) or judge me from outside (should)? Trace each should to its source (parents, friends, Facebook) and test it against what's real and authentic for you.
**Trade-offs**: Dropping a prestigious "should" can feel like failing others' expectations.

## Build From Person, Place, and Thing
**When to use**: Overwhelmed about how to construct an adult life.
**How**: Start with whichever of who-you're-with / where-you-live / what-you-do you already know something about, and build outward.
**Trade-offs**: Committing to one anchor (e.g. a city) can feel like foreclosing options; usually it opens them.

## Tell a Coherent Story (Customized Life)
**When to use**: Applications, interviews, coffee dates.
**How**: Make yourself the protagonist with an arc — past → present → intended next. Balance complexity and cohesion; it must *make sense* (not necessarily be linear). Move from "what I'm not" to "what I'm for."
**Trade-offs**: A too-simple story reads inexperienced; too-complex reads disorganized. Aim for the middle.

## Don't Slide — Decide (avoid the cohabitation effect)
**When to use**: Considering moving in together.
**How**: Before combining lives, have an explicit conversation about each person's commitment level and what it means. Ideally don't cohabit before engagement; if you do, anticipate and regularly re-evaluate the setup/switching costs that could lock you in. Test the relationship other ways (variety, travel).
**Trade-offs**: The convenient/economical path (sliding) is easy now but raises later switching costs.

## Run the Nicaragua Test
**When to use**: Assessing a serious relationship's real compatibility.
**How**: Do something stressful and inescapable together (third-world travel is "the closest thing to being married and raising kids") and watch whether you become better or *more extreme* versions of yourselves.
**Trade-offs**: Reveals mismatches you'd rather not see — which is the point.

## Assess the Big Five (and check Neuroticism)
**When to use**: Evaluating a partner's fit beyond obvious similarities.
**How**: Rate both of you high/medium/low on Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism. Personality similarity smooths the early years; be especially wary of high Neuroticism (it drives criticism and contempt). Be picky about 20-year things (values, goals, personality, love), not daily discrepancies.
**Trade-offs**: Some difference is inevitable; over-filtering on minor traits (a high-N habit) sabotages good relationships.

## Edit Your Life Story (stop dating/working down)
**When to use**: Choices that contradict who the person actually is now; secrecy/shame tells.
**How**: Get the untold story out of the back of the mind by telling it aloud; treat it as a first draft from adolescence and revise it through new conversations and experiences. Replace old internal voices with new ones. Shift from being wanted to wanting.
**Trade-offs**: Naming a shame story is painful; it's the precondition for change.

## Reappraise ("What about the facts?")
**When to use**: A work/relationship jolt triggers catastrophizing ("I'll be fired and waiting tables").
**How**: Answer the emotional brain (amygdala) with the frontal lobe — reevaluate based on facts. Jobs/relationships usually aren't that fragile; a feeling isn't a command to act. Avoid the failure modes: flight (quitting), chronic worry, suppression, over-borrowing an ego.
**Trade-offs**: Reappraisal is a learned skill — slower and harder than calling mom, but it builds self-soothing.

## Bank Mastery Experiences (confidence outside-in)
**When to use**: Low confidence at a new job.
**How**: Seek challenging work done without too much help; count hours toward ~10,000; take stock of hours already earned; adopt a growth mindset (failures are feedback); seek feedback instead of avoiding it; stop self-deprecating talk.
**Trade-offs**: Requires tolerating not-going-well days — but easy successes build only fragile confidence.

## Prescribe a Life Transplant
**When to use**: Feeling stuck, anxious, "at loose ends," waiting to feel like an adult before acting.
**How**: Make concrete adult commitments (a job, a steady place, a goal you actually want) — "getting along and getting ahead." Even *working toward* good work/love raises happiness; goals alone boost well-being.
**Trade-offs**: Feels like it will "add to your problems"; the evidence says the opposite.

## Do the Math With a Timeline
**When to use**: The future feels far off and abstract; goals get tossed "decades away."
**How**: Work backward from what you actually want (career milestones, marriage, kids), counting the required steps and years. Bring the future close (like seeing your future self). Distinguish *having* a life at thirty from *starting* one then.
**Trade-offs**: Timelines provoke anxiety — that discomfort is what converts distance into action.
