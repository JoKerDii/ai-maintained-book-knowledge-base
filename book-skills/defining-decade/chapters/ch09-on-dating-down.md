# Chapter 9: On Dating Down

## Core Idea
"Dating down" is usually about dating down to an old, low, inaccurate *story* about yourself — often a shame-filled one left over from adolescence. Those untold stories loop silently and shape whom you date and what you accept, until you get them out of your head and *revise* them.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **Dating Down**: Choosing partners (or letting them choose you) that reflect a diminished self-image. "I'm not talking about looks. I'm talking about dating down to an old, low, inaccurate version of yourself."
  - When to use: When someone's romantic choices contradict who they actually are now.
  - How: Find the untold story driving it; make it explicit; then edit it.
- **Self-Defining Memories & the Life Story (narrative identity)**: High school and the twenties are when we form our most self-defining memories and first life stories. "The stories we tell about ourselves become facets of our identity." Untold personal stories are most often about **shame**, and they "loop silently in our minds without anyone, sometimes even ourselves, knowing."
  - Key: These stories are the bits of identity with the *greatest potential for change* — faster and more dramatically than personality itself. "Life stories with themes of ruin can trap us. Life stories that are triumphant can transform us."
- **The Self-Cure (Masud Khan)**: "The most difficult thing to cure is the patient's attempt at self-cure." Old coping solutions (music, hookups, cutting, numbing) outlive their usefulness and become the new problem. **"Every problem was once a solution."** *"A raft is a good thing when crossing a river. But when you get to the other side, put it down."*

## Key Concepts
- **Conversations create your world**: "Conversation is to be thought of as creating a social world just as causality generates a physical one" (Rom Harré). Old stories "originated in old conversations and change only through new conversations and new experiences."
- **The tell of shame**: Secrecy. Cathy never even *thought* to tell her best friend about her men — Jay "flagged shame." Stories hide "in the gaps between what we plan to do and what we actually do."
- **From being wanted to wanting**: The shift from letting others choose you ("I wanted to be wanted") to doing the wanting and taking charge of your love life.

## Mental Models
- **Edit your story like a draft.** "The story you're telling yourself now is a first draft left over from adolescence." A story that made sense to you when you wrote it may not make sense to anyone reading it now — including you.
- **New voices in your head.** Change comes from replacing old internal conversations (bullies, critical parents, an angry iPod playlist) with new ones (therapist, best friend, students, your own).
- Ask: **When you look back objectively, does this story still make sense?** Usually it doesn't.

## Anti-patterns
- **"The twenties are a dress rehearsal / just practice"**: Dismissing what you're actually rehearsing — "Consider what part you're rehearsing to play."
- **Confiding only in "music" (or any non-responsive substitute)**: "It can't talk back. It's just confirming every bad thing you already think about yourself." A friend turned bad, keeping you in the corner.
- **Taking whatever attention comes**: Treating each person who wants you as "an oasis," fearing they're "the last person to want me" — driven by an outdated shame story, not by choice.

## Worked Example
Cathy, 27, was a beloved elementary teacher and budding writer by day; by night she "let them choose her," responding to 2 a.m. booty texts, sometimes unprotected, thinking "this could work" about any man. The **tell** was secrecy — she'd only reveal a guy after her heart broke, and it "never occurred to her" to tell her best friend (shame). Her only "honest conversations" were with an iPod full of "angry, hurt songs" — an iPod-commercial split self: calm teacher in front, dancing rage in shadow. The origin story: relentless high-school teasing (as a Korean American girl told she was "too much" and "less-than"), still a virgin three years out of college, then drunk sex with a band's lead singer — "I felt like I joined the world that night… I wanted to be wanted." Jay named the self-cure ("put the raft down"), had Cathy *tell the story* rather than hand over the soundtrack, then used Cathy's own expertise as a children's writer: "What do you know about editing stories?" — "you're blinded by the feelings of the moment; later you can be more objective." The turning question Cathy finally dared: *"How do you see me?"* — a sign she was ready to be re-seen and to rewrite. She slowed down, moved from being wanted to wanting, and got new voices in her head. "Her story is being revised."

## Key Takeaways
1. Dating down usually means dating down to an outdated, low story about yourself.
2. Self-defining stories form in adolescence/twenties and often carry hidden shame.
3. Secrecy is the tell of a shame story — look in the gap between intention and action.
4. Every problem was once a solution; retire self-cures that have outlived their use.
5. Stories change through new conversations and experiences — replace the old voices.
6. Shift from being wanted to wanting: take charge of what you seek in a partner.
