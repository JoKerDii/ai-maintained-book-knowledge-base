# Chapter 4: My Life Should Look Better on Facebook

## Core Idea
Comparing your real, messy life to everyone else's curated highlight reel breeds the "tyranny of the should" — paralyzing outside judgments that pull you away from what's actually true and good for you. Reaching your potential means investing in what's in front of you, not chasing an ideal image.

## Frameworks Introduced
- **The Tyranny of the Should (Karen Horney)**: "Shoulds" masquerade as high standards but are *paralyzing judgments from the outside*. Goals direct us from the inside and feel like authentic dreams; shoulds feel like oppressive obligations and set up a false either/or of perfection-or-failure.
  - When to use: Whenever your sentences fill with "should," "supposed to," "less-than."
  - How: Name each "should," trace where it came from (parents, friends, Facebook, culture), and test it against what's *real* and *authentic for you*.
- **The Search for Glory (Horney)**: Striving toward an *ideal* rather than your *real* potential — "when we learn more about what is ideal than about what is real." You become alienated from what's true about yourself and the world.
  - Tell: Any life driven by shoulds/supposed-to's, chasing how life is "supposed to look."
- **Person, Place, and Thing**: An adult life is built not out of "eat, pray, love" but out of *who you're with, where you live, and what you do for a living.* Start with whichever one you already know something about (for Talia: place — going home to Nashville).

## Key Concepts
- **Upward social comparison**: Treating peers' manufactured Facebook posts as real, underestimating how much others struggle, so your not-perfect life looks "low" against everyone's "high life."
- **Social surveillance**: Frequent users spend more time *examining* others' pages than posting — "checking up," judging, evaluating.
- **Realizing potential is not a twentysomething event**: It usually happens in your 30s–50s, and starting "often means doing what doesn't look so good" (carting granola, a starter job).

## Mental Models
- **Goals vs. shoulds**: Goals come from inside and energize; shoulds come from outside and paralyze. Sort every ambition into one bucket.
- **"The best is the enemy of the good"** (Voltaire, chapter epigraph) — chasing the ideal wrecks the workable.
- **Bigness comes from investing in what you have** — from taking part in what's in front of you, not from the biggest thing you see others doing.

## Anti-patterns
- **Grading your twenties like school** ("How do I get an A in my twenties?") — there is no formula; the friction between you and hard work is where potential sparks.
- **Borrowed shoulds**: "Shouldn't I be having my own *Eat, Pray, Love* / traveling in France for three years?" — ideals you don't actually want and can't afford.
- **Treating "going home" or a sensible choice as "giving up"** because friends invalidate finding over searching.

## Worked Example
Talia, ~24, arrived mid-"nervous breakdown," sure these were "supposed to be the best years." Her speech was littered with shoulds: work should be "Wow!", she should be in grad school, she should be "saving orphans like everybody else" (though she had zero interest in humanitarian work). Jay separated ideal from real: actual unemployment rates, ~$30k median salaries, what her friends' lives were *actually* like. Talia took a marketing-analyst job — not glamorous, but she thrived, gaining humility and competence. Later the shoulds returned around *place*: she secretly wanted to move home to Nashville near her sisters, but her father and friends framed it as "settling." Jay validated it (person/place/thing — she'd found her *place*). Talia called about a "closed" Nashville job anyway ("it could be closed because they're sitting on faceless résumés — and find out if you know anybody there"), got the interview, and moved home to build the life she actually wanted.

## Key Takeaways
1. Sort ambitions into goals (inside, authentic) vs. shoulds (outside, oppressive) — act on goals.
2. Facebook shows highlight reels; nearly everyone hides their troubles. Stop the upward comparison.
3. Potential is realized later, and starting often looks unimpressive — that's normal.
4. Build a life from person, place, and thing — begin with whichever you already know.
5. "Finding" is not inferior to "searching"; going home can be the braver, harder choice.
6. Reaching potential means investing in what's in front of you, not chasing an ideal.
