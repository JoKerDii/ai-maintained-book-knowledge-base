# Glossary — The Defining Decade

**Age Thirty Deadline** — The nagging twentysomething worry "I'd better not be alone at thirty" (Ch 6).

**Age Thirty Bait and Switch** — At thirty, the deadline flips to panic; commitment goes from "something for later" to "something for yesterday," driving rushed choices (Ch 6).

**Availability heuristic** — Judging how likely something is by how easily an example comes to mind; e.g. overestimating late-40s fertility from anecdotes and headlines (Ch 15).

**Big Five** — The five personality factors: Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, Neuroticism; ~50% inherited, relatively stable; the real "match maker" (Ch 10).

**Borrowing an ego** — Reaching out so someone else's frontal lobe does your emotional calming; fine occasionally, harmful if overused (Ch 12).

**Claiming (vs. conforming)** — Committing to a concrete interest/career; feels like conforming but is what makes a life distinctive (Ch 5).

**Cohabitation effect** — Couples who live together *before engagement* are, on average, less satisfied and more likely to divorce (Ch 8).

**Customized life** — An authentic, unique life assembled from standard "parts" over time, like a custom bike (Ch 5).

**Deal breakers, not match makers** — Obvious similarities (age, religion, politics) filter out mismatches but don't create happiness (Ch 10).

**Elaborated speech** — Fuller, more organized communication required with weak ties who don't share your assumptions (Ch 2). See *restricted speech*.

**Falling up** — Being successful young as a survival strategy that hides real pain (Ch 7). See *YAVIS*.

**Fixed mindset** — Believing abilities are innate ("you have it or you don't"), so mistakes become verdicts (Ch 13). See *growth mindset* (Carol Dweck).

**Flashbulb memory** — A vivid, "frozen" memory of a surprising or emotional moment; the twenties produce more than any stage (Ch 12).

**Forward thinking** — Frontal-lobe function of anticipating consequences and planning under uncertainty; comes with practice, not just age (Ch 11).

**Frontal lobe** — The brain's "executive functioning center," last to mature (between ~20 and 30) (Ch 11).

**Getting along and getting ahead** — Making adult commitments (jobs, partners, leases) that drive positive personality change in the twenties (Ch 14).

**Growth mindset** — Believing people can learn and grow, so failures are opportunities, not verdicts (Ch 13).

**Helper's high** — The happiness/health boost from being generous; underlies goodwill toward twentysomethings (Ch 2).

**Homophily** — "Love of the same"; similarity breeds connection, which makes strong-tie clusters homogeneous (Ch 2).

**Identity capital** — Your collection of personal assets (résumé and personal) that you use to "purchase" jobs and relationships (Ch 1).

**In like** — Being alike in ways that matter *and* genuinely liking who your partner is (Ch 10).

**Lock-in (consumer lock-in)** — Decreased likelihood of switching to a better option after an investment; built from setup and switching costs (Ch 8).

**Mastery experience** — An actual lived success at something difficult; the true source of confidence (Ch 13).

**Memento vivi** — A reminder that you are *going to live* — that life continues, and can be great, past the twenties (Ch 16). Cf. *memento mori*.

**Microtrauma** — An everyday jolt of the twentysomething workweek (a public "screwup") taken intensely personally (Ch 12).

**Negative / positive identity** — Knowing yourself by what you're *not* vs. the braver, affirmative sense of what you *are* (Ch 5).

**Neuroticism** — Big Five factor (anxious, moody, critical); high Neuroticism is "toxic" — more predictive of breakup than dissimilarity (Ch 10).

**Person, place, and thing** — An adult life is built from who you're with, where you live, and what you do — start with whichever you know (Ch 4).

**Positivity effect** — With age, greater interest in positive information and weaker reaction to the negative (Ch 12).

**Present bias** — Discounting the future in favor of today's rewards; strong in twentysomethings (Ch 16).

**Restricted speech** — Encoded in-group shorthand used among similar strong ties (Ch 2). See *elaborated speech*.

**Search for glory** — Striving toward an *ideal* rather than your *real* potential (Karen Horney) (Ch 4).

**Self-cure** — An old coping solution that outlives its usefulness and becomes the new problem; "the most difficult thing to cure" (Ch 9).

**Self-defining memory / life story** — The narratives (often shame-laden) formed in adolescence/twenties that shape identity and choices; highly changeable (Ch 9).

**Self-efficacy** — One's trusted ability to produce a desired result; the precise term for confidence (Ch 13).

**Setup cost** — The initial investment (furniture, lease, pets) that contributes to lock-in (Ch 8).

**Six-flavor table** — Your real, limited set of viable options (vs. the mythical "24-flavor table" of infinite choice), from the jam experiment (Ch 3).

**Sliding, not deciding** — Drifting into cohabitation (or a shared life) gradually, without a conscious decision (Ch 8).

**Social recovery** — Relearning frontal-lobe skills through regular routines (Phineas Gage driving a stagecoach) (Ch 11).

**Strength of weak ties** — Acquaintances (not close friends) most often bridge us to new jobs and opportunities (Mark Granovetter) (Ch 2).

**Survival of the busiest** — "Neurons that fire together, wire together"; the brain keeps connections you use, prunes those you don't (Ch 11).

**Switching cost** — The time/money/effort to change course; underestimated upfront, larger up close; rises with age (Ch 8).

**10,000-hour rule** — Expertise comes mostly from time in (~5 yrs focused full-time); "naturals" are largely myths (K. Anders Ericsson) (Ch 13).

**Tyranny of the should** — Paralyzing outside judgments ("shoulds") that masquerade as goals but oppress (Karen Horney) (Ch 3–4).

**Tyranny of the should-not** — The mirror image: rejecting every conventional path in the name of being different (Ch 5).

**Unthought known** — Something you know about yourself but forget or won't say out loud (Christopher Bollas) (Ch 3).

**Urban tribe** — The makeshift family / weekend friends; supportive but insular, helps us survive not thrive (Ch 2).

**Weak ties** — People you've met or are connected to but don't currently know well (Ch 2). See *strength of weak ties*.

**YAVIS** — Young, Attractive, Verbal, Intelligent, Successful; high-functioning clients whose polish can hide real pain (Ch 7).
