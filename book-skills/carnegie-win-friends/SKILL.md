## Core Frameworks & Mental Models

Carnegie's entire system rests on one premise: **people are creatures of emotion, driven by pride and the hunger to feel important — not by logic.** Every technique below points attention *away from you* and *toward the other person's wants and dignity.*

### The Three Fundamentals (Part 1 — always start here)
1. **Don't criticize, condemn, or complain.** Criticism wounds pride and provokes defense; it almost never changes behavior. "Any fool can criticize — and most fools do."
2. **Give honest and sincere appreciation.** Feed the universal *importance hunger*. Appreciation is specific and sincere ("from the heart out"); flattery is generic and insincere ("from the teeth out") — and eventually detected.
3. **Arouse in the other person an eager want.** *"Bait the hook to suit the fish."* Nobody cares what you want. Frame every request around what *they* want and show them how to get it.

### Six Ways to Be Liked (Part 2)
Be **interested, not interesting**: (1) become genuinely interested in others, (2) smile, (3) use their name — "the sweetest sound in any language," (4) be a good listener; let them talk, (5) talk in terms of their interests, (6) make them feel important — sincerely.

### Twelve Ways to Win People to Your Thinking (Part 3)
The master rule: **you cannot win an argument** — win it and you humiliate the person; lose and you lose. So: never say "You're wrong"; if wrong, admit it fast; begin friendly; use the **Secret of Socrates** (get "yes, yes" early); let them talk and let the **idea feel like theirs**; **see their viewpoint**, sympathize, and **appeal to nobler motives**; dramatize; throw down a challenge.

### Nine Ways to Lead & Change People (Part 4)
Correct without breeding resentment: begin with praise; criticize **indirectly** (say "**and**," never "**but**"); admit your own faults first; **ask, don't order**; let them **save face**; praise every improvement; give a **reputation to live up to**; make the fault seem easy to fix; make them glad to comply.

### The One-Line Test
Before any interaction: **"What does the other person want, and how do I make them feel important — sincerely?"**

> ⚠️ **Sincerity gate**: every technique here becomes manipulation — and fails — the moment it's applied *at* people rather than *for* them. Carnegie insists the appreciation and interest must be real.
