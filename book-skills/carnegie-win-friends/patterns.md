# Patterns — Carnegie's Techniques in Practice

## Bait-the-Hook Reframe
**When to use**: any request or "ask" (email, delegation, sales, parenting).
**How**: delete every "I/we want" framing; rewrite the ask around what the other person already wants and show the path from their want to your request.
**Trade-offs**: requires genuinely knowing their interests — guessing wrong reads as manipulation.

## Sincere Appreciation (not Flattery)
**When to use**: opening a relationship, motivating, before a hard conversation.
**How**: find something specific and genuinely admirable; name it concretely ("the way you handled X" not "good job"). Must be true.
**Trade-offs**: fails instantly if insincere or generic; over-use dilutes.

## The "Yes, Yes" Ladder (Secret of Socrates)
**When to use**: overcoming resistance or an early "no"; onboarding, sales, negotiation.
**How**: begin with questions the other person cannot help but agree to; chain agreements toward the conclusion you want. Never open on the point of dispute.
**Trade-offs**: transparent if the questions are leading or gimmicky; keep them honest and relevant.

## Admit-Your-Own-Fault-First
**When to use**: correcting a peer or junior; defusing a critic.
**How**: name your own similar mistake before mentioning theirs ("I got this wrong for years myself..."); or beat a critic to the punch by condemning yourself first.
**Trade-offs**: requires humility; loses power if the shared-fault claim is fabricated.

## "And" Instead of "But"
**When to use**: any praise-then-correct message (feedback, reviews, coaching).
**How**: give sincere praise, then join the improvement with "and" — never "but," which cancels the praise.
**Trade-offs**: subtle; still needs the correction phrased as a forward-looking possibility, not a veiled criticism.

## Order-as-a-Question
**When to use**: delegating, directing a team, changing behavior.
**How**: convert commands into questions — "Would it work to...?", "What do you think about trying...?" — so the person keeps initiative and ownership.
**Trade-offs**: slower than a direct order; unsuitable in true emergencies where speed > buy-in.

## Reputation-to-Live-Up-To
**When to use**: motivating improvement, building trust, turning someone around.
**How**: publicly attribute a virtue to the person ("you've always been reliable"), then hand them the task that lets them prove it.
**Trade-offs**: manipulative if the virtue is fictional; works only when the person can plausibly own it.

## Idea-Ownership Transfer
**When to use**: pushing a proposal that meets resistance.
**How**: plant the seed via question or partial suggestion; let the other person develop and voice the conclusion so it becomes *their* idea.
**Trade-offs**: requires ego restraint — you don't get public credit for the idea.

## The Face-Saving Exit
**When to use**: someone is wrong, is losing, or must reverse course.
**How**: give them a dignified way out — attribute the error to circumstances, offer a graceful reframe, correct privately.
**Trade-offs**: means forgoing the satisfaction of being visibly right.

## Interested-Not-Interesting Listening
**When to use**: any first meeting, networking, relationship-building.
**How**: ask about the other person's favorite subject and listen with full attention; talk about yourself as little as possible.
**Trade-offs**: none, provided the interest is genuine; feigned interest eventually shows.
