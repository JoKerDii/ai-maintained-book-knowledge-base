# Cheatsheet — How to Win Friends & Influence People

## The 30 Principles at a Glance

**Part 1 — Handling People**
1. Don't criticize, condemn, or complain.
2. Give honest and sincere appreciation.
3. Arouse in the other person an eager want.

**Part 2 — Make People Like You**
1. Become genuinely interested in others. 2. Smile. 3. Use the person's name. 4. Be a good listener; let them talk about themselves. 5. Talk in terms of their interests. 6. Make them feel important — sincerely.

**Part 3 — Win to Your Thinking**
1. Avoid arguments. 2. Never say "You're wrong." 3. If wrong, admit it fast. 4. Begin friendly. 5. Get "yes, yes" early. 6. Let them talk most. 7. Let the idea be theirs. 8. See their viewpoint. 9. Sympathize with them. 10. Appeal to nobler motives. 11. Dramatize ideas. 12. Throw down a challenge.

**Part 4 — Be a Leader**
1. Begin with praise. 2. Criticize indirectly. 3. Admit your own faults first. 4. Ask, don't order. 5. Let them save face. 6. Praise every improvement. 7. Give a reputation to live up to. 8. Make the fault seem easy to fix. 9. Make them happy to comply.

## Decision Rules

| Situation | Do this | Because |
|---|---|---|
| Tempted to point out a fault | Stop — say nothing, or correct indirectly | Criticism triggers defense, never change |
| Need someone to act | Reframe around *their* want | No one is motivated by *your* want |
| Someone says "You're wrong" is on your lips | Say "I may be wrong — let's check" | Contradiction locks the mind shut |
| You *are* wrong | Admit it loudly, first, before they do | Disarms the critic completely |
| Facing resistance / an early "no" | Open with questions earning "yes" | Momentum carries them to your view |
| Must give feedback | Praise + "and" (never "but") + easy fix | "But" cancels the praise |
| Delegating | Phrase as a question | Preserves initiative and pride |
| Someone is losing/wrong publicly | Give them a face-saving exit | Humiliation costs the relationship |
| Want the idea adopted | Let them think they thought of it | People back their own ideas |
| First meeting | Ask about them, listen, use their name | Interested beats interesting |

## Tells & Smells
- You feel the urge to say "Actually..." → you're about to trigger ego defense. Stop.
- Your praise contains "but" → it will read as a set-up. Swap for "and."
- You're explaining what *you* need → you've lost them. Reframe to their interest.
- You "won" the argument → check whether you just lost the person.
- Praise feels generic → it will read as flattery. Name the specific thing or don't say it.

## The One-Line Test
Before any interaction: **"What does the other person want, and how do I make them feel important — sincerely?"** If you can't answer, you're not ready to persuade.
