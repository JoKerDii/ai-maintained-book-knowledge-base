# Glossary — How to Win Friends and Influence People

**Appeal to the nobler motives** — persuade by attributing a fine, unselfish reason to the other person and letting them live up to it (Ch 3, P10).

**Appreciation (honest and sincere)** — genuine, specific recognition of what is admirable in someone; the second Fundamental Technique. Opposite of flattery (Ch 1, P2).

**Arouse an eager want** — frame any request around the other person's desires so they *want* to act (Ch 1, P3).

**Bait the hook to suit the fish** — metaphor for speaking to the other person's self-interest, not your own (Ch 1).

**Begin in a friendly way** — open persuasion with warmth, not confrontation; "a drop of honey" (Ch 3, P4).

**Creatures of emotion** — Carnegie's premise that people are driven by pride and vanity, not logic (Ch 1).

**Dramatize your ideas** — make points vivid and showmanlike so they land and stick (Ch 3, P11).

**Face-saving** — protecting another's dignity even when they're wrong; a core leadership move (Ch 4, P5).

**Flattery** — insincere, generic praise; "from the teeth out." The counterfeit of appreciation (Ch 1).

**Fundamental Techniques (the three)** — don't criticize; give sincere appreciation; arouse an eager want (Ch 1).

**Importance hunger** — the deepest human craving to feel important and appreciated; root of most behavior (Ch 1).

**"...and" not "...but"** — replace "but" with "and" after praise so correction reads as encouragement, not a set-up (Ch 4, P2).

**Indirect criticism** — calling attention to a mistake by inference rather than direct rebuke (Ch 4, P2).

**Nobler motives** — the reason that "sounds good" (vs. the real reason); appeal to it to move people (Ch 3, P10).

**Praise every improvement** — reinforce the slightest progress specifically and lavishly (Ch 4, P6).

**Reputation to live up to** — assign someone a virtue so they strive to embody it (Ch 4, P7).

**Save face** — see *Face-saving* (Ch 4, P5).

**Secret of Socrates** — chain of small "yes" agreements that builds momentum toward your conclusion (Ch 3, P5).

**Show respect for opinions** — never say "You're wrong"; invite a joint look at the facts (Ch 3, P2).

**Six Ways to Make People Like You** — interest, smile, names, listening, their interests, feeling important (Ch 2).

**Smile** — low-cost signal of gladness; "act as if" until the feeling follows (Ch 2, P2).

**Spotlight rule** — aim attention at the other person, not yourself (Ch 2).

**Throw down a challenge** — spur people via competition and the desire to excel (Ch 3, P12).

**"Yes, yes" response** — see *Secret of Socrates* (Ch 3, P5).
