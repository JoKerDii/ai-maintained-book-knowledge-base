# Don't Believe Everything You Think: Quiz Book

*Source note: this quiz reflects the chapter notes in the "nguyen-dont-believe-everything" skill (a summary of Joseph Nguyen's book), not the book's own wording. Anything the notes do not say is left out. Nguyen's claims are experiential and spiritual (Three Principles tradition), so "correct" below means "consistent with the book's own model as the notes describe it".*

## How to use this quiz

1. Work one section at a time (the 18 chapters are grouped in threes). Cover the answers, write or say your answer first, then check.
2. Score each question 0 (missed), 1 (partly), 2 (solid). Re-do any section below 70% after a day.
3. Scenario questions are the most valuable: they test whether you can *apply* the concept, not just recite it.
4. The 20-question final exam at the end mixes the whole book, including cross-chapter synthesis.
5. Question types: SA = short answer, TF = true/false, MC = multiple choice, EX = explain in your own words, SC = scenario/application.

# SECTION A: Chapters 1-3 (Pain vs. suffering, the root cause, why the mind thinks)

**A1 (SA).** What are the "two arrows," and which one is optional?
**Answer:** The first arrow is the event (pain); the second arrow is your reaction to it (suffering). The second is always optional. The first can't always be dodged. (Ch 1, Buddhist teaching.)

**A2 (TF).** True or false: Nguyen's claim is that bad events and physical pain are not real.
**Answer:** False. Pain is "real, and not denied by this book." He scopes "suffering" strictly to psychological and emotional distress, not physical pain or the reality of bad events. (Ch 1.)

**A3 (MC).** What did Nguyen conclude about the many things he tried (hundreds of books, psychology, therapy, shadow work, meditation, retreats, diet and schedule overhauls)? (a) They each cured him; (b) They gave incremental gains but never stopped the suffering because they treated symptoms; (c) They were all harmful; (d) Only the diets helped.
**Answer:** (b). He still had daily anxiety, irritation and heaviness, and felt he had lost more than when he began. The turning point was a mentor pointing him to how experience is generated from thinking, an understanding rather than another technique. (Ch 1, "searching trap".)

**A4 (EX).** In your own words, what is the "inside-out" view of experience?
**Answer:** Event, then your thinking/meaning, then your feeling. Reality is the bare event with no meaning attached; every interpretation is added by you, so you can only ever feel what you are thinking. Circumstances never push a feeling in from outside. (Ch 2.)

**A5 (SA).** What diagnostic question does Ch 2 give to test whether a situation "makes" you feel something?
**Answer:** Ask whether *every* person in that situation would feel identically. If not, the feeling is produced by thinking, not by the situation. Supporting images: money, politics or a job described 100 ways by 100 people; two people in the same coffee shop, one in crisis and one at peace; fuming about work while relaxing at home (the "living-room test"). (Ch 2.)

**A6 (SA).** Retell the Empty Boat story in two or three sentences and give its lesson.
**Answer:** A young monk cannot meditate for noise, flees to a lakeshore, then rows to the middle of the lake. On day three a boat rams his; he yells at the pilot, gets no reply, and rises in fury, only to find the boat empty, drifted by the breeze. His anger vanishes instantly: nothing external can anger you without your reaction. He returns to the noisy monastery and meditates in peace. (Ch 2, Zen story.)

**A7 (MC).** The "who would you be without that thought?" inquiry is adapted from whom, and what does it usually reveal? (a) Sydney Banks; the thought is false; (b) Byron Katie; the feeling underneath is usually light, free, peaceful; (c) Thich Nhat Hanh; the mind is empty; (d) Joe Bailey; you need a mentor.
**Answer:** (b). It is a one-minute inquiry. Caveat from the patterns file: it removes the second arrow; it does not remove real-world problems. (Ch 2.)

**A8 (TF).** True or false: Ch 3 says the mind's job is to make you fulfilled and it is simply failing at it.
**Answer:** False. The mind's job is survival/safety; consciousness/soul's job is fulfillment. Conflict comes from confusing the two. (Ch 3, "Survive vs. Thrive Split".)

**A9 (SC).** A manager notices that she snaps at her own anxious mind: "Why can't I just stop catastrophizing before the board meeting?" Which concept applies and what should she do instead?
**Answer:** Ch 3's "innocent misunderstanding" and the survive-vs-thrive split. Her mind is doing its job (threat scanning, replaying memories to forecast dangers). Getting angry at it adds a second layer of thinking. The practical stance is to recognise the mind is doing its job innocently, thank it, relieve it of duty, and stop taking its alarms as truth, rather than fighting it. A useful check: "is this a bush with a predator, or a thought about one?" (Ch 3.)

**A10 (SC).** An employee says, "My job makes me anxious, so I need a new one." Using Ch 1-2, what would you ask, and what is the risk of simply switching jobs?
**Answer:** Apply the living-room test and the "would everyone feel this?" check: if they fume about work while relaxing at home, the generator is thinking, not the job. Anti-pattern: "trying to fix the outside to fix the inside". Also ask "who would you be without that thought?" Nuance from the notes: this does not deny real problems; it targets the second arrow. (Ch 2, Ch 1.)

**Bonus (SA).** What does "one thought away" mean?
**Answer:** Because feelings follow thinking, you are always one thought, or one moment of no thought, from a different experience. It returns in Ch 17 as the single thing to remember. (Ch 2, 17.)

# SECTION B: Chapters 4-6 (Thoughts vs. thinking, positive thinking, the Three Principles)

**B1 (SA).** State the book's central distinction in one sentence per term.
**Answer:** A *thought* (noun) is effortless raw material that arrives unbidden from beyond you; light, expansive, creative. *Thinking* (verb) is your effortful engagement with a thought (judging, analyzing, criticizing); it costs willpower and is heavy and restrictive. Thoughts create; thinking destroys. (Ch 4.)

**B2 (TF).** True or false: Nguyen says the goal is to stop having thoughts.
**Answer:** False. You cannot control which thoughts arrive; you can stop thinking about them. Thoughts are not the root of suffering; thinking is. (Ch 4 takeaway 1; Ch 2 is refined by Ch 4.)

**B3 (EX).** Walk through the 5x income experiment and say what it proves.
**Answer:** Ask your dream yearly income; an answer surfaces in seconds (a thought: quick, neutral, maybe exciting). Multiply it by 5 and sit a minute; the cascade ("no way," "nobody in my family earns that," "wanting that is greedy") is thinking and carries self-doubt, unworthiness, guilt. Repeat with "how could I make that?": ideas pop in effortlessly until you start evaluating them, and heaviness returns. Nothing hurt until the thinking began; the only useful thing was the first spontaneous answer. (Ch 4.)

**B4 (MC).** In Ch 4's reconstructed chart, thinking is paired with which of these? (a) Source: Universe; (b) Effort: effortless; (c) Weight: heavy, restrictive; (d) Belief: infinite possibilities.
**Answer:** (c). Thinking has the ego as source, heavy/restrictive weight, limited/destructive nature, fear/stress as feeling, confining belief, separateness as sense, and laborious effort. Thought is the mirror image. (Ch 4.)

**B5 (SA).** How are feelings used as "radar", and what is the warning attached?
**Answer:** Heaviness or negative emotion means you are thinking; lightness or aliveness means you are receiving thoughts. Use feelings as a dashboard, not as facts about the world. (Ch 4, Ch 18.)

**B6 (SA).** What is the refined principle in Ch 5, and what is the thought-o-meter?
**Answer:** "We only feel negative emotion when we are thinking." Joy, love and peace are the default and need no thought. The thought-o-meter is a mental speedometer in thoughts-per-minute; negative intensity rises with thinking volume and positive intensity with 1/volume; the red zone is burnout, fury or panic. (Ch 5.)

**B7 (EX).** Explain "it is not WHAT you think but THAT you think" and why positive-thinking regimens fail by this logic.
**Answer:** Stress tracks the volume of thinking regardless of topic, so adding pleasant thinking keeps the meter high. The remedy is to reduce thinking so the natural state surfaces (sun and clouds: you don't manufacture sunshine, you wait for clouds to clear). Support: the paired-recall exercise (2-3 memories of extreme stress show heavy thinking; 2-3 of peak joy or love show near-silence). (Ch 5.)

**B8 (MC).** In the DVD analogy, what is Universal Mind? (a) The DVD; (b) The TV and player; (c) The electricity; (d) The remote.
**Answer:** (c). Universal Mind is the invisible power connecting everything and the source of thoughts. Universal Thought is the DVD (the raw material/information); Universal Consciousness is the TV plus player that brings it to life. (Ch 6, Sydney Banks's Three Principles.)

**B9 (SC).** A founder feels lonely and cut off from her team after a bad quarter and concludes she must be fundamentally isolated. Which idea from Ch 6 applies?
**Answer:** Separation is an illusion produced by thinking; connection/wholeness is the default signal of Mind flowing, and feeling separate or lonely signals that thinking is blocking it. Anti-pattern: "believing the separation" as if the sense of being cut off were fact. (Ch 6.)

**B10 (SC).** A friend says, "I need to say affirmations every morning to counter my negativity." Respond using Ch 5 and Ch 4.
**Answer:** Affirmations add more thinking and keep the thought-o-meter high; positive feeling comes from the natural state, not from positive thoughts. Suggest reducing thinking, noticing "this is thinking," and letting the default surface. Maltz's effortful positive-imagery approach is flagged in the notes as a contrast. (Ch 5, Ch 4 takeaway 3.)

**Bonus (TF).** The three principles are techniques to practise daily.
**Answer:** False. They are descriptions of how experience works, not practices; understanding them is itself the intervention. (Ch 6.)

# SECTION C: Chapters 7-9 (Stopping thinking, flow, goals)

**C1 (SA).** What is the one tool for reducing thinking in Ch 7, and what are its four steps?
**Answer:** Awareness. (1) Notice the feeling, (2) recognise "I'm thinking," (3) don't fight it, (4) let it settle. No effort or technique beyond that. (Ch 7.)

**C2 (EX).** Explain the murky water principle and the quicksand image.
**Answer:** Cloudy water clears if left alone; filtering or boiling (analysing, problem-solving) stirs it up, so the mind's natural state is clear and disturbance is thinking. In quicksand, struggling tightens the grip while stillness lets natural buoyancy lift you; fighting thinking amplifies it. (Ch 7.)

**C3 (TF).** True or false: success in this model means never relapsing into thinking.
**Answer:** False. Oscillation between thinking and non-thinking is normal. The aim is to shorten time in thinking; progress is measured by recovery speed, and guilt about relapse is more thinking. (Ch 7, 11, 16.)

**C4 (SA).** Retell the Heaven and Hell parable.
**Answer:** A samurai demands the Zen master explain heaven and hell. The master insults him (a slob, a worm, unworthy of an answer); the samurai draws his sword in rage. "That's hell." He freezes, sees his own mind manufactured the rage, and bows in gratitude. "And that's heaven." The distance between them was one moment of awareness. (Ch 7.)

**C5 (SA).** What is non-thinking, and what is it not?
**Answer:** Allowing thoughts to flow through without engaging them; it is not the absence of all thoughts. (Ch 7.)

**C6 (EX).** What does Ch 8 claim about peak performance, and what is mushin?
**Answer:** Peak performance is the state of non-thinking: flow, "the zone," mushin. Mushin ("no mind") is a mind free of random thoughts, anger, fear and ego, acting from trained instinct without hesitation. Thinking only adds hesitation, doubt and fear after training is complete. Train hard, then execute without thinking. (Ch 8.)

**C7 (MC).** Which quote does Ch 8 use as a diagnostic of which state you're in? (a) "Whether you think you can or can't, you're right"; (b) "Anxiety is thought without control. Flow is control without thought."; (c) "Thought is not reality"; (d) "Empty your cup."
**Answer:** (b), attributed in the notes to James Clear (quoted). (Ch 8.)

**C8 (SA).** Which evidence does Ch 8 say is the real argument, not the athlete anecdotes?
**Answer:** The guided inquiry: recall your absolute best work (captivated, time and space gone), sit 30-60 seconds, and ask what thoughts were running. Nearly everyone reports none, or thoughts flowing through without engagement. First-person evidence; test it in your next task rather than adopting it as belief. (Ch 8.)

**C9 (EX).** Distinguish inspiration goals from desperation goals on at least five attributes, and give Nguyen's three tests.
**Answer:** Desperation: from thinking, scarcity, urgency, heaviness, imposter syndrome, "HAVE to," a means goal, built from analysing the past and what seems realistic; achievement brings emptiness within hours and a bigger desperate goal follows. Inspiration: from thought, light, expansive, "WANT to," a calling, an end in itself, from abundance, feels like it comes through you. Tests: did it arrive as a thought or get built by thinking? light or heavy? means or end? (Ch 9.)

**C10 (SC).** A sales director hits her target, feels empty within days, and immediately sets a bigger one. Name the pattern and a way out.
**Answer:** The desperation loop (Ch 9): realistic goal from past data, scarcity and urgency, hunting for faster methods, achieving, emptiness, seeing others doing the same, a bigger desperate goal. Way out: audit the goal's source with the three tests and ask the Infinite-Resources Question, acting on the first answer before critiquing it. (Ch 9.)

**C11 (SA).** Quote the Infinite-Resources Question and say what each clause removes.
**Answer:** "If I had infinite money, had already traveled the world, had no fear, and would receive no recognition for what I do, what would I do or create?" It is worded to strip out scarcity, novelty-seeking, fear and external motives so only inspiration answers. Notes say answers rarely look practical, which is the design. (Ch 9.)

**C12 (SC).** An engineer is mid-presentation and starts mentally replaying and judging each slide as he gives it. What does Ch 8 say, and what is the fix?
**Answer:** Anti-pattern: over-analysing mid-performance; thinking at execution time is interference, like a driver grabbing the wheel from a trained autopilot. Practice is where learning belongs; performance is where thinking must stop. Notice "this is thinking," let it settle, and return to the work. (Ch 8, Ch 7.)

# SECTION D: Chapters 10-12 (Unconditional love and creation, after peace, nothing good or bad)

**D1 (EX).** Why are reasons for loving someone a problem, per Ch 10?
**Answer:** Each reason ("her laugh," "her kindness") is a condition under which the love would stop, like a clause in a contract that could be breached. Unconditional love has no listed reasons. (Ch 10.)

**D2 (SA).** Tell the Makenna story and its lesson.
**Answer:** Nguyen asks his partner why she loves him; she says "I don't know, I just do, a lot." He lists fifty reasons for loving her and is bothered for years that she can't reciprocate. Then he asks whether he would stop loving her if she stopped laughing or helping people; obviously not, so the reasons were never the cause. Her inability to give reasons was the signature of unconditional love; he removes the conditions from his own love, and the logic transfers to creation. (Ch 10.)

**D3 (SA).** What is the "feelings are the real goal" claim, with examples?
**Answer:** We want money for security, family time for love, meaningful work for fulfilment; all are feelings, generated internally and never delivered by the object. (Ch 10.)

**D4 (TF).** True or false: creating for no reason delays the feelings you are chasing.
**Answer:** False. The paradox is that unconditional creation delivers them immediately; creating for a reason postpones them, possibly forever. (Ch 10.)

**D5 (EX).** After first tasting peace, why do worry and doubt return (Ch 11) and what is the cure?
**Answer:** The ego reasserts itself (after "ego death" it fights to regain control) and there is surplus energy formerly spent on thinking. Cure: shorten recovery time rather than prevent relapse, and channel the freed energy into pre-written inspiration goals, never desperation goals. (Ch 11.)

**D6 (SA).** What is the activation ritual and what must it never include?
**Answer:** A morning routine that puts you into non-thinking or flow first thing so momentum carries through the day ("object in motion stays in motion"). Never start the day with phone or email, which launches fight-or-flight momentum. Ch 18 adds it should leave space for a spiritual practice. (Ch 11, 18.)

**D7 (MC).** The snake-and-rope reflex means: (a) fear can be prevented with practice; (b) the initial fear reaction is unpreventable, but seeing through the illusion is always available; (c) ropes are dangerous; (d) the ego never returns.
**Answer:** (b). (Ch 11, glossary.)

**D8 (EX).** Explain the 88 piano keys.
**Answer:** No key is inherently wrong; it only seems wrong relative to a song someone assumes is being played. Likewise there are no wrong decisions, only more or less pleasant sequences. Replace "was that wrong?" with "what does this vantage point let me see?" Opposing political parties are different keys, not wrong keys. (Ch 12.)

**D9 (SA).** State the Universal Truth Test.
**Answer:** If something is "true" for one person but not another, it is not truth; it is thinking. Truth is what holds for every conscious human regardless of origin. Seek what is universally true instead of who is right. (Ch 12.)

**D10 (SC).** Two department heads are in a bitter dispute, each trying to prove the other wrong. Which concepts apply?
**Answer:** Duality (right/wrong generating conditions and thus feelings), the piano keys (different vantage points on the mountain trail, none wrong), and the universal truth test. Anti-pattern: proving you're right locks you into duality and its emotions. Negative emotion here is a flag of "misunderstanding here", belief in one's own thinking. (Ch 12.)

**D11 (SC).** A freelance designer who loves her work starts choosing projects only for status and income and now dreads the work. Diagnose using Ch 10.
**Answer:** Conditional creation: making something as a means to something else yields a joyless process and brief satisfaction. Remedy: identify what she would create for no reason and do that; the brain calling purposeless creation pointless is the thinking that blocks it. (Ch 10, Ch 9.)

# SECTION E: Chapters 13-15 (Knowing what to do, intuition, creating space)

**E1 (SA).** State the three parts of "know that you already know."
**Answer:** (1) Recognise you already know what to do and fear is hiding it; (2) if genuinely unsure with no fear present, trust inner wisdom will supply the answer; (3) "if you don't know, know that you can know." First check for fear masking a known answer. (Ch 13.)

**E2 (EX).** What is the "inner GPS"?
**Answer:** Intuition guarantees the destination but not the route; detours and blockages are rerouted in real time. Trust the destination, release the route. (Ch 13.)

**E3 (SA).** What does the weight-loss test show, and what does regret signify?
**Answer:** You already know the formula, so not-knowing is really fear or self-doubt. Regret is the signature of having overridden intuition with someone else's advice. (Ch 13.)

**E4 (TF).** True or false: if your intuition is right, society will confirm it early.
**Answer:** False. "Society never confirms early"; seeking outside validation produces backlash and conflicting opinions, which is expected and not a signal you're wrong. Pros/cons lists and polling everyone (including the pets) manufacture anxiety. (Ch 13.)

**E5 (SA).** What is "what, not how"?
**Answer:** Hold the *what* you want and enter non-thinking; the *how* (and when) belongs to the Universe/Infinite Intelligence. Trying to work out the how is the source of "hard work and suffering." (Ch 14.)

**E6 (EX).** List the four marks of recognising intuition.
**Answer:** It arrives as a spontaneous thought with a sense of knowingness; it rarely seems logical; it points toward the unknown; it feels light despite the fear that follows. It is available only in the present, only in non-thinking. (Ch 14.)

**E7 (SA).** Give the fear-dissolution sequence.
**Answer:** Intuition, then the personal mind panics (unknown), then recall "it's just thinking creating the fear," then fear falls away, courage surfaces, act. Fear marks the edge of the unknown, where new outcomes live ("to get what you don't have, do what you haven't done"). Caveat: it doesn't remove risk, it removes the thinking that magnifies it. (Ch 14.)

**E8 (MC).** The Divine Download Process, step 2, is: (a) Analyse the problem harder; (b) Create space, surrender manual thinking, have faith inner wisdom will answer, surrender how and when; (c) Ask friends; (d) Write a pros/cons list.
**Answer:** (b). Step 1 is becoming aware thinking is the root cause of the negative emotion; step 3 is noticing feelings as you surrender, magnifying love, peace and joy, and meeting the problem with love. (Ch 15.)

**E9 (SA).** Retell the empty-your-cup story and the Edison and Einstein illustrations.
**Answer:** A Zen master overfills a scholar's teacup to show that a mind full of its own opinions has no room for insight. Edison dozed holding steel balls; when they dropped and woke him, the solution was there (engineered non-thinking). Einstein stopped working on the problem, played the violin, and let the answer arrive. (Ch 15, as given in the notes; rest-as-results: athletes gain strength in recovery, not the workout.)

**E10 (SC).** An executive faces a hard strategic problem, has been grinding on it for days, and demands an answer by tomorrow. Which concepts apply and what are the errors?
**Answer:** Divine download process and empty your cup. Errors: efforting with the same thinking that produced the problem, and withholding surrender on the timing (demanding the answer now keeps the cup full). Also use questions that challenge current thinking as space-makers. Caveat from the notes: no timeline control. (Ch 15, Ch 14.)

**E11 (SC).** A woman senses she should leave her stable job to start something, her family disagrees, and she makes a pros/cons list that feels inconclusive. Apply Ch 13-14.
**Answer:** First check for fear masking a known answer; intuition looks irrational and points to the unknown; society won't confirm it early; list-making and polling manufacture anxiety; regret is the record of overriding intuition. Use what-not-how and the fear-dissolution sequence, act on the next revealed step. Note the notes also say Duke's *Thinking in Bets* offers a contrasting analytical approach for calibrating when analysis is warranted. (Ch 13, 14.)

# SECTION F: Chapters 16-18 (Obstacles, "Now what?", guides appendix)

**F1 (EX).** What is the unfamiliar-peace trap?
**Answer:** After enough non-thinking, problems vanish and serenity feels foreign; the brain treats unfamiliar as unsafe, so the mind manufactures worry ("you've lost your edge," "you're lazy," "something must be wrong, I feel too good") to restore the safety of familiar suffering. Label it, "this is my mind trying to make me think again," and choose the unknown. (Ch 16.)

**F2 (SA).** What is "The Choice"?
**Answer:** Free and happy in the unknown, or confined and suffering in the familiar. It is always this binary. (Ch 16; echoes the Thich Nhat Hanh epigraph in Ch 1.)

**F3 (TF).** True or false: peace reduces productivity.
**Answer:** False per the notes: humans are most productive when happy and not thinking; tasks feel easier and people gravitate to you. "Stay long enough to see the results." (Ch 16.)

**F4 (SA).** Name the three obstacles in the Ch 18 appendix.
**Answer:** (a) Clinging to thinking because "it got me here" (what got you here won't get you there); (b) insufficient faith (you must first believe daily peace is possible and that you're part of something larger); (c) fear, a sign something matters, with thinking as its root; the way out is through. (Ch 18.)

**F5 (EX).** What does "irreversible insight" mean and how should the book be read?
**Answer:** Once seen, how experience is created can't be unseen; consciousness once expanded doesn't contract. You will forget and think again; remembering is all that is required. Read for insight, not information (the Introduction's line as cited in the notes). (Ch 17.)

**F6 (SA).** What is "simplicity as truth-test"?
**Answer:** If the message seems too simple, that objection is the mind restarting thinking; complexity moves away from truth. (Ch 17.)

**F7 (SA).** Give the three steps of the moment-level "Framework for How to Stop Thinking."
**Answer:** (1) Realise thinking is the root of suffering; don't hunt for another root cause. (2) Create space for persistent negative thinking: allow it, acknowledge it; you are the sacred space holding the feeling; have courage to be alone with it; every feeling contains a seed of truth. (3) Acknowledge, allow it to pass, just be; positive emotion arises naturally; if negativity persists, return to step 1. (Ch 18.)

**F8 (SA).** What are the four categories in the trigger audit and what is the rule on quantity?
**Answer:** Physical Health (foods, stimulants), Physical Environment, Digital Environment (phone, computer, TV), Digital Consumption (media/content). Audit, categorise, rank, pick the top items and write a removal action; start small, because removing too much at once creates more stress. (Ch 18.)

**F9 (MC).** The work-ledger target is: (a) 50% of time on 7s; (b) 80% of work time on 9-10 activities; (c) 100% on 10s; (d) 20% on 9-10 activities.
**Answer:** (b). Rate activities 1-10 (1 most draining, 10 most alive); each week eliminate 1-3 drainers and do more 9s and 10s. Caveat: assumes latitude over tasks; delegate or negotiate the rest. (Ch 18, cheatsheet.)

**F10 (SC).** A team lead keeps stress-eating at 3 p.m. and "has to" do it. Walk through the habit inquiry.
**Answer:** Confirm she wants to change; document frequency and timing; name the feeling just before and the self-talk; surface the belief that she HAS to; ask what she thinks happens if she doesn't and "is it absolutely 100% true?"; see the cost of that thinking; consult inner wisdom for why she actually wants to change; feel the release (physically lighter, sees the habit differently) and journal it; repeat if it returns. It is inquiry into the belief, not willpower. (Ch 18.)

**F11 (SC).** A founder two weeks into regular calm suddenly feels "too good" and worries she is losing her edge, and that evening skips her morning ritual and checks email first. Diagnose.
**Answer:** Unfamiliar-peace trap and ego return (Ch 16, 11): the "edge" worry is the mind selling thinking. Starting with email launches fight-or-flight momentum; return to the activation ritual, channel energy into pre-written inspiration goals, relapse without guilt, and measure recovery speed. (Ch 16, 11, 18.)

**F12 (SA).** What is the single thing Ch 17 says to remember when life gets hard?
**Answer:** You are always one thought, or one moment of non-thinking, away from peace, love and joy. (Ch 17.)

# FINAL EXAM: 20 questions across the whole book

**1 (SA).** Define suffering as Nguyen scopes it and name its cause.
**Answer:** Psychological/emotional distress, caused by thinking (the reaction to an event), never by the event itself. Pain is the unavoidable first arrow. (Ch 1-2.)

**2 (SA).** Which three-part sequence expresses "inside-out"?
**Answer:** Event, your thinking/meaning, your feeling. (Ch 2.)

**3 (TF).** Thoughts are the root cause of suffering.
**Answer:** False; thinking (the effortful engagement) is. Thoughts are effortless raw material and create. (Ch 4.)

**4 (MC).** Which is a tell of non-thinking? (a) Heaviness; (b) Peace, joy, passion, inspiration, loss of time/space/self, feeling one with the activity; (c) Self-criticism; (d) Planning.
**Answer:** (b). (Ch 18, cheatsheet.)

**5 (EX).** Why does adding positive thinking fail by Nguyen's logic?
**Answer:** Stress tracks the volume, not the content, of thinking; positive states are the default and thinking is the only obstruction, so reduce thinking rather than add pleasant thinking. (Ch 5.)

**6 (SA).** Name the Three Principles and the DVD analogy.
**Answer:** Universal Mind (electricity), Universal Consciousness (TV/player), Universal Thought (DVD). From Sydney Banks. (Ch 6.)

**7 (SA).** What is the one intervention in Ch 7?
**Answer:** Awareness: notice "this is thinking," don't fight, let it settle (murky water). (Ch 7.)

**8 (SA).** Give the three tests for the source of a goal.
**Answer:** Thought or thinking? Light or heavy? End or means? (Ch 9.)

**9 (EX).** How do Ch 10's unconditional creation and Ch 9's inspiration goals connect?
**Answer:** Inspiration goals are unconditional creation in practice: ends in themselves, from abundance, no reason beyond wanting to. They deliver the sought feelings immediately, whereas means goals postpone them. (Ch 9, 10.)

**10 (MC).** The Ch 12 principle that no decision is inherently wrong is illustrated by: (a) murky water; (b) the 88 piano keys; (c) the empty boat; (d) mushin.
**Answer:** (b). (Ch 12.)

**11 (SA).** Which two ideas from Ch 13-14 together answer "what should I do and how will I get there?"
**Answer:** "Know that you already know" (the what; check fear first) and "what, not how" with the inner GPS (hold the destination, release the route; the path reveals itself as you walk). (Ch 13, 14.)

**12 (EX).** Explain how fear and intuition are related.
**Answer:** Intuition points into the unknown, so fear appears as the tell you're at the edge where new outcomes live; recalling that the fear is just thinking dissolves it and courage surfaces. (Ch 14.)

**13 (SA).** List the three Divine Download steps.
**Answer:** Aware thinking causes the negative emotion; create space and surrender manual thinking, how and when; magnify love/peace/joy and meet the problem with love. (Ch 15.)

**14 (SC, synthesis).** A manager is furious after a peer takes credit. Sequence the concepts from the book you would apply in the first hour.
**Answer:** Two arrows (the event is the first, fury the second); inside-out check ("would everyone feel this?", "who would I be without that thought?"); thought-or-thinking check (first spontaneous idea vs the judgment cascade; heaviness as radar); awareness and murky water (don't analyse, let it settle); empty boat (anger needs no one in the boat). Then act from the first light idea, not the resentment cascade. (Ch 1, 2, 4, 7.)

**15 (SC, synthesis).** A startup founder sets a $1M target "so I can finally be free", is grinding, and keeps opening email first thing. Identify at least four issues and fixes.
**Answer:** Means/desperation goal (Ch 9: HAVE to, escape, emptiness on success); feelings are the real goal (Ch 10: freedom is a feeling no object delivers); conditional creation (Ch 10); email first launches fight-or-flight (Ch 11, 18).
