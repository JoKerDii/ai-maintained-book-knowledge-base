# Don't Believe Everything You Think Quiz Book
*Don't Believe Everything You Think: Why Your Thinking Is the Beginning & End of Suffering* by Joseph Nguyen

**How to use:** Cover the answers, answer from memory (out loud or in writing), then check. Anything you miss is a section to reread. Questions are short-answer (SA), true/false (T/F), or multiple-choice (MC). The book has 17 short chapters plus a guides appendix (Ch 18), so chapters are grouped two at a time, with six questions per group. A cumulative final exam is at the end.

---

## Chapters 1-2: The Journey to the Root Cause / The Root Cause of All Suffering

**Q1 (SA).** What are the "two arrows," and which one is optional?
**A:** The first arrow is the event (pain), which is often unavoidable. The second arrow is your reaction to it (suffering), which is always optional.

**Q2 (T/F).** Nguyen claims that physical pain and bad events are not real. **A:** False. The book does not deny pain. "Suffering" is scoped strictly to psychological and emotional distress, produced by reaction rather than by the event.

**Q3 (SA).** Why did Nguyen's long list of self-improvement efforts (books, therapy, shadow work, meditation, retreats, diets, routines) fail to end his suffering?
**A:** Each gave incremental gains but treated symptoms, not the root. What changed things was an understanding of how experience is generated from thinking, not another technique.

**Q4 (MC).** Which sequence describes "inside-out" experience?
a) Feeling, then event, then thinking  b) Event, then your thinking/meaning, then your feeling  c) Event, then feeling, then thinking  d) Thinking, then event, then feeling
**A:** b. Reality is the bare event with no meaning; every interpretation is added by you.

**Q5 (SA).** What two questions does Chapter 2 give you to test whether a situation is really "making" you feel something?
**A:** First, would every person in that situation feel identically? If not, the feeling comes from thinking. Second, "Who would you be without that thought?" (adapted from Byron Katie), which usually reveals something light, free, and peaceful underneath.

**Q6 (SA).** In the Zen story of the empty boat, what does the monk discover and what is the lesson?
**A:** After fleeing noise to the middle of a lake, a boat rams his. He yells at the pilot, then finds the boat empty and drifting, and his anger evaporates. Nothing external can anger you without your reaction; every disturbance is an empty boat.

## Chapters 3-4: Why Do We Even Think? / Thoughts vs. Thinking

**Q1 (SA).** What is the "survive vs. thrive" split?
**A:** The mind's job is survival and safety; consciousness/the soul's job is fulfillment. Inner conflict arises from confusing the two, and from not realizing the mind is only doing its job.

**Q2 (T/F).** Chapter 3 recommends fighting and correcting your anxious mind. **A:** False. The practical stance is to relieve the mind of its duty (thank it, stop taking its alarms as truth). Getting angry at your mind adds a second layer of thinking.

**Q3 (SA).** Define "thought" and "thinking" as Nguyen uses them.
**A:** A thought (noun) is effortless raw material that arrives unbidden from beyond you; it is light, expansive, and creative. Thinking (verb) is your effortful engagement with a thought (judging, analyzing, criticizing); it costs willpower and is heavy, restrictive, and destructive.

**Q4 (MC).** In the 5x income experiment, which part is "thinking"?
a) The first answer to "what's your dream income?"  b) The idea that pops up for how to make it  c) The cascade of "no way," "nobody in my family earns that," and "that's greedy"  d) The number itself
**A:** c. The first answers are thoughts; the doubt cascade is thinking, and it brings self-doubt, unworthiness, and guilt.

**Q5 (SA).** How do you use feelings as a "radar"?
**A:** Negative emotion or heaviness means you are thinking; lightness and aliveness mean you are receiving thoughts. Feelings are a dashboard, not facts about the world.

**Q6 (T/F).** According to Chapter 4, you can learn to control which thoughts arrive. **A:** False. Thoughts are not yours to control. You cannot stop thoughts, but you can stop thinking about them.

## Chapters 5-6: Don't We Need to Think Positively? / The Three Principles

**Q1 (SA).** What is the refined principle of Chapter 5?
**A:** We only feel negative emotion when we are thinking. Peace, love, and joy are the natural default and need no thought to produce them.

**Q2 (SA).** Explain the thought-o-meter. Does content or quantity of thinking matter more?
**A:** It is a mental speedometer of thoughts per minute; the red zone is burnout, fury, panic. Negative emotion intensity rises with the volume of thinking, and positive emotion intensity rises as thinking volume falls. It is not what you think about but that you are thinking.

**Q3 (SA).** Describe the paired-recall exercise and what it is meant to show.
**A:** Recall 2-3 memories of extreme stress and note how much thinking was running, then recall 2-3 memories of peak joy or love and note the same. Typically thinking is heavy in the first set and near-silent in the second, which is the experiential proof the chapter relies on.

**Q4 (MC).** Name the Three Principles.
a) Mind, Body, Spirit  b) Universal Mind, Universal Consciousness, Universal Thought  c) Thought, Feeling, Action  d) Past, Present, Future
**A:** b. They come from Sydney Banks.

**Q5 (SA).** Using the DVD analogy, what does each principle correspond to?
**A:** Thought is the DVD (the information), Consciousness is the TV and player (it brings the thought to life), and Universal Mind is the electricity (the invisible power connecting everything).

**Q6 (T/F).** Chapter 6 presents the Three Principles as techniques to practice. **A:** False. They are descriptions of how experience works, not practices to perform. Any felt separation from anything is an illusion produced by thinking.

## Chapters 7-8: How Do We Stop Thinking? / Thriving Without Thinking

**Q1 (SA).** What is the only tool Chapter 7 says you need to reduce thinking?
**A:** Awareness: notice that you are thinking and that thinking is the cause of the feeling. That detaches you from it and it settles on its own.

**Q2 (SA).** Explain the murky water and quicksand images.
**A:** Murky water clears by being left alone, not by filtering or boiling it (analyzing, problem-solving, working on the thoughts). Fighting thinking is like struggling in quicksand: resistance tightens the grip, stillness lets you rise.

**Q3 (T/F).** The goal of non-thinking is to have no thoughts at all. **A:** False. Non-thinking means allowing thoughts to flow through without engaging them. Oscillation between thinking and non-thinking is normal, and making "never think" the goal creates more thinking.

**Q4 (SA).** In the Heaven and Hell parable, what is the point?
**A:** A Zen master insults a samurai, who draws his sword in rage. The master says "That's hell." The samurai sees that his own mind manufactured the rage and bows, and the master says "And that's heaven." The whole distance between the two states was one moment of awareness.

**Q5 (MC).** What is *mushin*?
a) A breathing technique  b) "No mind": a mind free of random thoughts, anger, fear, and ego, acting from trained instinct  c) A form of positive thinking  d) The Japanese word for patience
**A:** b.

**Q6 (SA).** What is the guided inquiry of Chapter 8, and what does it demonstrate?
**A:** Ask, "When you're doing your absolute best work, fully captivated with time and space gone, what thoughts are running through your head?" Nearly everyone reports none, or thoughts flowing through without engagement. That first-person evidence is the argument. Practice is where learning belongs; performance is where thinking should stop.

## Chapters 9-10: Goals, Dreams & Ambitions / Unconditional Love & Creation

**Q1 (SA).** Contrast desperation goals and inspiration goals.
**A:** Desperation goals come from thinking: scarcity, urgency, heaviness, "HAVE to," always a means to something else, built from analyzing the past; success brings emptiness and a bigger desperate goal. Inspiration goals come as thought: light, expansive, "WANT to," an end in themselves, from abundance, and feel like they come through you.

**Q2 (SA).** What three questions test the source of a goal?
**A:** Did it arrive as a thought or get built by thinking? Does it feel light or heavy? Is it a means or an end?

**Q3 (SA).** State the Infinite-Resources Question.
**A:** "If I had infinite money, had already traveled the world, had no fear, and would receive no recognition for what I do, what would I do or create?" It strips out fear, scarcity, and external motives. Act on the first answer, not the critique of it.

**Q4 (T/F).** Nguyen says that if you adopt non-thinking you should give up your ambitions. **A:** False. Keep your ambitions and change their source.

**Q5 (SA).** Why does listing reasons make love "conditional"? How does Nguyen test this in his own relationship?
**A:** Each reason is a condition under which love could be withdrawn. Nguyen listed fifty reasons for loving his partner Makenna and was bothered that she could not list reasons for loving him ("I don't know, I just do"). Asking whether he would stop loving her if she stopped laughing or helping people showed the reasons were never the cause; her inability to list reasons was the signature of unconditional love.

**Q6 (MC).** The paradox of unconditional creation is that:
a) it is only for professionals  b) creating with no conditions delivers the feelings you were chasing immediately, while creating for a reason postpones them, possibly forever  c) it guarantees money  d) it removes the need for love
**A:** b. What you want is always a feeling (security, love, fulfillment), generated internally and never delivered by an object.

## Chapters 11-12: What Do You Do After Experiencing Peace? / Nothing Is Either Good or Bad

**Q1 (SA).** Why do worry and doubt often return after a first taste of peace, and what does Chapter 11 recommend?
**A:** It is the ego reasserting itself plus a surplus of energy formerly spent on thinking. Recommendation: channel the energy into pre-written inspiration goals and shorten recovery time rather than try to prevent relapse.

**Q2 (T/F).** Progress is measured by how rarely you fall back into thinking. **A:** False. Success is faster remembering ("this is just thinking") and returning, measured by recovery speed, not zero relapse.

**Q3 (SA).** What is an activation ritual and what must it never include?
**A:** A morning routine that puts you into non-thinking/flow first thing so momentum carries through the day. Never start the day with phone or email, which launches fight-or-flight momentum.

**Q4 (SA).** Explain the 88 piano keys idea.
**A:** No key is inherently wrong; a key only seems wrong relative to a song someone assumes is being played. Likewise there are no wrong decisions, only more or less pleasant sequences. Replace "was that wrong?" with "what does this vantage point let me see?"

**Q5 (SA).** What is the Universal Truth Test?
**A:** If something is "true" for one person but not another, it is not truth, it is thinking. Truth is what holds for every conscious human regardless of origin.

**Q6 (MC).** According to Chapter 12, what does being gripped by negative emotion signal?
a) The situation is truly bad  b) You have been wronged  c) You currently believe your thinking and have forgotten where experience comes from  d) You need a new plan
**A:** c. Treat it as a flag reading "misunderstanding here," not a verdict on the situation.

## Chapters 13-14: Knowing What to Do / How to Follow Your Intuition

**Q1 (SA).** What is "know that you already know"?
**A:** You almost always already know what to do, and fear or self-doubt hides it. First check for fear masking a known answer; only if you are genuinely unsure with no fear present, trust that inner wisdom will supply the answer ("if you don't know, know that you can know").

**Q2 (SA).** What does the inner GPS analogy say about intuition?
**A:** Intuition guarantees the destination, not the route. Detours and blockages are rerouted in real time, so trust the GPS for the destination and release the route.

**Q3 (T/F).** If your intuition is right, society will confirm it early. **A:** False. Society never confirms early; seeking outside validation produces backlash and conflicting opinions, which is expected and not a sign you are wrong. Regret is the signature of having overridden intuition with someone else's advice.

**Q4 (SA).** What does "what, not how" mean?
**A:** Hold the what you want (decided with imagination) and enter non-thinking; the how and when belong to the Universe/Infinite Intelligence. Trying to figure out the how is the source of "hard work and suffering."

**Q5 (SA).** List the identifying features of intuition and what to do when fear appears.
**A:** It arrives as a spontaneous thought with a sense of knowingness, rarely seems logical, points toward the unknown, and feels light despite the fear that follows. Fear signals you are at the edge of the unknown; recall that the fear is only thinking, it dissolves, courage surfaces, and you act.

**Q6 (MC).** Why does the path reveal itself only as you walk?
a) The Universe is playful  b) Lighting the whole route in advance would negate faith, which is structurally required  c) Planning is impossible  d) Nobody can see the future
**A:** b. The unknown is also the only place new outcomes can come from ("to get what you don't have, do what you haven't done").

## Chapters 15-16: Creating Space for Miracles / Potential Obstacles

**Q1 (SA).** What does "empty your cup" teach?
**A:** In the Zen story the master overfills the scholar's teacup; a mind full of its own opinions has no room for insight. Stop generating, surrender, and ask questions that challenge your current thinking.

**Q2 (SA).** State the three steps of the Divine Download Process.
**A:** (1) Become aware that your thinking is the root cause of the negative emotion. (2) Create space: surrender manual thinking, have complete faith inner wisdom will answer, and surrender the how and when. (3) Notice the feelings that arise as you surrender, magnify love, peace, and joy, and meet the problem with love. The answer comes.

**Q3 (SA).** What do the Edison steel-ball trick and Einstein's violin illustrate?
**A:** Deliberately engineered non-thinking. Edison dozed holding steel balls and the solution was there when they dropped and woke him; Einstein stopped working on the problem, played the violin, and let the answer arrive.

**Q4 (SA).** What is the unfamiliar-peace trap?
**A:** Sustained peace feels unfamiliar and the brain treats unfamiliar as unsafe, so the mind (the "greatest salesman") sells you the idea that something is wrong ("you've lost your edge," "you're lazy"). Label it as the mind trying to make you think again and choose the unknown.

**Q5 (T/F).** Relapsing into thinking calls for self-punishment so you learn. **A:** False. Guilt is more thinking. Falling back is human: notice, return, no guilt.

**Q6 (MC).** What is "the Choice" of Chapter 16?
a) Work or rest  b) Free and happy in the unknown, or confined and suffering in the familiar  c) Thought or feeling  d) Plan or improvise
**A:** b. It is always this binary, and faith (the Universe works for you; no failures, only lessons) is the operating requirement.

## Chapters 17-18: Now What? / Summary & Practical Guides

**Q1 (SA).** What is "one thought away" and what is the single thing to remember when life gets hard?
**A:** At any moment you are one thought, or one moment of non-thinking, from peace, love, and joy. Forgetting is normal and remembering is the whole practice.

**Q2 (T/F).** If the message seems too simple, that is a reason to doubt it. **A:** False. Chapter 17 treats that objection as the mind restarting thinking; simplicity is the truth-test and complexity moves away from truth.

**Q3 (SA).** Give the order of the four-category trigger removal and name the categories.
**A:** Audit what makes you prone to thinking/fight-or-flight, categorize into Physical Health, Physical Environment, Digital Environment, and Digital Consumption, rank within each category by impact, then pick the top items and write a removal action. Start small, because removing too much at once creates more stress.

**Q4 (SA).** Describe the work energy ledger and its target.
**A:** List draining and energizing work activities, rate each 1-10 (1 most draining, 10 most alive), eliminate 1-3 drainers each week and do more 9s and 10s. The target is 80% of work time on 9-10 activities.

**Q5 (SA).** What is the key move in the destructive-habit inquiry?
**A:** Surface the belief that makes you feel you HAVE to do it and ask what you think happens if you don't, then "Is it absolutely 100% true?" Habit change is inquiry into the belief, not willpower against the behavior; you know it worked if you feel physically lighter and see the habit differently.

**Q6 (MC).** Which are the two reflection prompts the guide offers?
a) "What did I accomplish?" and "Who let me down?"  b) "1-10, how much did I think today?" and "What percent of today was fight-or-flight versus calm?"  c) "What went wrong?" and "What next?"  d) "Am I right?" and "Am I wrong?"
**A:** b.

## Final Exam (cumulative, 10 questions)

**Q1 (SA).** State the book's central claim in one sentence.
**A:** Thinking is the root cause of all psychological suffering; the event (pain) is unavoidable, but you can only feel what you are thinking, and peace, love, and joy are the default when thinking settles.

**Q2 (SA).** Distinguish thoughts from thinking and give the fast tell.
**A:** Thoughts arrive effortlessly, are light and creative (thoughts create); thinking is effortful engagement that is heavy and restrictive (thinking destroys). Lightness signals thought, heaviness signals thinking.

**Q3 (T/F).** Nguyen's recommended remedy for a bad mood is to replace negative thoughts with positive ones. **A:** False. Positive states are the default; reduce thinking rather than add pleasant thinking, since stress tracks the volume of thinking, not its content.

**Q4 (SA).** What are the Three Principles and which analogy explains them?
**A:** Universal Mind, Universal Consciousness, Universal Thought; the DVD analogy (Thought = DVD, Consciousness = TV and player, Mind = electricity).

**Q5 (SA).** Why can't you just fight thinking, and what is the alternative?
**A:** Fighting is quicksand and filtering or boiling stirs the murky water. Awareness that "this is thinking" lets it settle; oscillation is normal and progress is measured by recovery speed.

**Q6 (MC).** A goal that feels heavy, urgent, "HAVE to," and aimed at something beyond itself is:
a) an inspiration goal  b) a desperation (means) goal  c) an unconditional creation  d) a divine download
**A:** b.

**Q7 (SA).** Why does Nguyen say reasons make love and creation conditional, and what do you gain by dropping them?
**A:** Every reason is a condition under which love or creation could stop. Create or love with no reason and the feelings you were chasing (security, love, fulfillment) arrive immediately rather than being postponed.

**Q8 (SA).** Walk through the chain from deciding to acting on intuition.
**A:** Own the what, surrender the how and when, enter non-thinking, receive a thought with knowingness (usually irrational and pointing into the unknown), meet the fear by recalling it is only thinking, act, and take the next revealed step.

**Q9 (SA).** Name two Zen stories from the book and the point each makes.
**A:** The empty boat (nothing external angers you without your reaction) and Heaven and Hell (the distance between rage and peace is one moment of awareness). Also acceptable: empty your cup (a full mind has no room for insight).

**Q10 (T/F).** After first experiencing peace, doubts like "I've lost my edge" mean the approach is failing. **A:** False. This is the ego and the unfamiliar-peace trap; label it, stay, pre-load inspiration goals, and use an activation ritual. Remembering faster, not never relapsing, is the measure.
