# Inner Excellence (Jim Murphy): Quiz Book

> **Source note.** This quiz reflects the *skill's chapter notes* for Jim Murphy's *Inner Excellence: Train Your Mind for Extraordinary Performance and the Best Possible Life*, not the book's own wording. Anything the notes do not state is left out. Where an answer cites a story or number, it is the one recorded in the notes.

## How to use this quiz

1. Read a chapter (or its section in the companion file `murphy-inner-excellence-arguments-and-evidence.md`), then close it and answer that section's questions aloud or on paper.
2. Cover the answers with a sheet of paper. Reveal them only after you commit to an answer.
3. Question types are labelled: **[SA]** short answer, **[T/F]** true/false, **[MC]** multiple choice, **[Explain]** in your own words, **[Scenario]** application.
4. Score yourself: 1 point per question, half a point if you had the idea but missed a key detail. Re-study any section where you score under 80%.
5. Finish with the 20-question Final Exam (end of file). Do it a few days later for best retention.

# Chapter 0: Preface, Presuppositions and Introduction (The Foundations)

**Q0.1 [SA]** What are the three elements of Quality of Life in the notes?
**A:** (1) The inner world of thoughts, feelings, beliefs and desires; (2) your frame of reference (mindset); (3) your relationships. Murphy's claim is that quality of performance and quality of life run through the same inner world.

**Q0.2 [SA]** What does BFF stand for, and which of the three resources produces each element?
**A:** Belief, Focus, Freedom. Wisdom produces belief, courage produces focus, love produces freedom. Belief is who you are and what is possible; focus is a fully engaged heart, mind and body in the moment; freedom is playing like a kid, curious and unafraid of what others think.

**Q0.3 [SA]** Complete the Inner Excellence mindset statement.
**A:** "I compete to raise the level of excellence in my life, to learn and grow, in order to raise it in others." It frames competition as growth and service rather than as beating people.

**Q0.4 [T/F]** The book's order of priority is performance first, heart second.
**A:** False. "Heart first, performance second." The pursuit of extraordinary performance and of the best possible life are the same path, and both run through the heart.

**Q0.5 [MC]** According to the presuppositions, the root cause of fear is: (a) lack of talent, (b) lack of preparation, (c) self-centeredness, (d) bad luck.
**A:** (c). Presupposition 3: self-centeredness is the root cause of fear, which leads to self-consciousness, then overanalysis, then self-rejection. The notes stress it is a limiting default, not a moral flaw.

**Q0.6 [Explain]** Put presupposition 8 ("the problem is not the problem") in your own words and say why it matters for performance.
**A:** The situation itself is not what hurts you; how you are thinking about it is. Because you can train your thinking (presupposition 7: you are not your mind, it is a part of you to train), you keep power even when circumstances are fixed. The notes link this to Nguyen's *Don't Believe Everything You Think*.

**Q0.7 [SA]** Give three of the four contrasts between the world's mindset and the Inner Excellence mindset.
**A:** Any three: winning is everything vs. inner strength and growth last longer; you are your results vs. results are an inconsistent measure (you can play well and lose); opponent = enemy vs. opponent = partner in the dance; failure not an option vs. failure is the key to growth; fierce competitors get mad vs. the best develop emotional control.

**Q0.8 [SA]** What does the story of Bunpachiro the samurai illustrate?
**A:** Murphy's great-great-grandfather built his identity on a role that could be lost; when he lost it, he fell into alcoholism. Lesson: build identity on something no circumstance can remove.

**Q0.9 [SA]** What did the Ryan Dodd story show about training versus perspective?
**A:** Dodd's training volume was the same before and after his skull fracture. What changed, and produced a world record, was how he saw the world. The point: the lens matters more than the workload.

**Q0.10 [Scenario]** A sales director feels worthless in any month she misses quota and tells herself "I am my numbers." Which presuppositions and foundational ideas apply, and what would you advise?
**A:** Presupposition "my value = my results" is one of the beliefs to drop; the replacement is that results are an inconsistent measure and her worth is inherent (the notes' "created for glory"/infinite inherent worth). Also apply "no failure, only feedback" and the finite-vs-infinite game question: she is playing a zero-sum game of comparison instead of an infinite game of growth and connection.

**Q0.11 [Scenario]** An engineer says "Anyone at the top was just born that way, so why bother?" Which presupposition is being violated, and how does the book reframe it?
**A:** The notes say to drop "the best were born that way." The reframe uses the ten presuppositions as a lens: everything is here to teach and help you, and the primary skill is how to believe (presupposition 2), which can be trained.

# Chapter 1: Maslow and the Maserati (The Pursuit of More)

**Q1.1 [SA]** Describe the two paths.
**A:** The wide, popular road offers praise, admiration, less risk and instant gratification. The narrow road is difficult and rocky but leads to deep contentment, joy, confidence and an undivided heart. Real freedom is costly: it means facing fears and connecting with your true self.

**Q1.2 [SA]** What does PALMS stand for, and is it bad to have these things?
**A:** Possessions, Achievements, Looks, Money, Status. Having them is fine; the "affluenza virus" is placing your trust and identity in them. Its engine is not "more" but "more than others."

**Q1.3 [SA]** List as many of Maslow's nine selfless-actualizer characteristics (as relabeled) as you can.
**A:** Total absorption; personal growth (the means is the end); self-awareness; Gemeinschaft (community); fresh gratitude; authenticity/resistance to enculturation; comfort in solitude; purpose beyond self; lack of ego defenses. Use as a self-audit of whether you live from your true self.

**Q1.4 [SA]** What are the three diagnostic questions for what you love most, and what does the answer predict?
**A:** What do I dream about? What do I worry about? What do I get upset about? The answer is what your life is built on, so your stability equals its stability.

**Q1.5 [Explain]** Explain "ordered loves" from Augustine.
**A:** We are shaped by what we love rather than by what we think or do; discontent comes from loves that are out of order. The advice is to love most what is most powerful.

**Q1.6 [MC]** Michelangelo's David illustrates: (a) adding skills until you are great, (b) revealing your true self by removing what is not you, (c) copying a master, (d) the cost of fame.
**A:** (b). The true self is uncovered by subtraction, a theme that returns in self-mastery (Ch 4).

**Q1.7 [T/F]** The notes say winning is a pure good with no downside.
**A:** False. "Winning is part solution, part problem." You can win as an imposter without being good, and a personal best in a loss may be the better race. The person you become is the real prize.

**Q1.8 [SA]** What do David Foster Wallace and Joe Ehrmann each contribute?
**A:** Wallace: everybody worships; the only choice is what, and worshipping money, body, power or intellect "eats you alive." Ehrmann: the ladder on the wrong building, outward NFL success with inner emptiness, leading him to redefine masculinity as relationships plus a cause bigger than self.

**Q1.9 [SA]** What does Murphy observe in Costa Rica?
**A:** A dentist host invites the tow-truck driver in for dinner after a tow and gets visibly excited when the driver points out his hometown on a map. Lawyers, cab drivers and shop owners socially level. People who "needed little and appreciated much" seemed far happier than his own culture. It is a contrast case for a PALMS-free social world.

**Q1.10 [Scenario]** A manager notices she is intensely upset whenever a peer is praised in meetings and her own reputation is questioned. Which concepts apply?
**A:** Entitlement, a symptom of the affluenza virus: deep disturbance when reputation or status is threatened. Run the three questions (dream/worry/anger) to see what she has built identity on; the engine is "more than others," i.e., comparison.

**Q1.11 [Scenario]** A coach defines success as the league title, and the team tightens up in close games. Which ideas from the chapter explain this and offer a fix?
**A:** Dean Smith and Krzyzewski treated winning as a by-product of process; defining success by what you cannot control undermines the process. Obsession with winning makes you afraid of losing and steals freedom and joy. Fix: swap the lollipop (medal) for the whole candy store (fullness of life); the notes also cite Thoreau's price test (the price of anything is the life you exchange for it).

# Chapter 2: Where the Wild Things Are (Pride, Fear and the Center of the Universe)

**Q2.1 [SA]** Give the Path to Fear in order.
**A:** Self-centeredness, attachment to past failures, self-consciousness, ego amplified, comparison (via PALMS), pride and fear; doubts escalate from external ("I won't achieve it") to personal ("I'm not able") to identity ("I'm a failure"). Use it by tracing any anxiety back along the chain.

**Q2.2 [SA]** What is the Attachment Cascade?
**A:** Identity placed in uncontrollable goals leads to attachment to circumstances (to monitor progress), then to attachment to thoughts, then frustration when what "has to" happen does not.

**Q2.3 [Explain]** Distinguish self-centeredness from self-awareness.
**A:** Self-centeredness is nearsightedness that cannot see past failures and limits. Self-awareness observes thoughts unattached, sees the bigger picture and treats setbacks as feedback. Self-centeredness is not selfishness or morality; it is a preoccupation with self that limits options.

**Q2.4 [T/F]** Fear is future-oriented and self-focused, while love connects and integrates.
**A:** True. Fear projects past failures forward and separates and isolates; love connects.

**Q2.5 [SA]** How does C.S. Lewis describe pride, and who adds what about competitiveness?
**A:** Pride is "unsmiling concentration on the self"; it gets no pleasure from having something, only from having more than the next person. Keller adds that competitiveness is at the heart of pride.

**Q2.6 [MC]** The ego is best described as: (a) the part of the mind always comparing, threatened and never satisfied; (b) the seat of courage; (c) another word for confidence; (d) the subconscious.
**A:** (a). It relies on external validation and cannot handle losing (Dean Smith: leaders must be able to handle losing).

**Q2.7 [SA]** What is the free-throw "moment it starts"?
**A:** The instant "I've gotta make this" replaces the routine: heart on one side, ego on the other. It marks the pivot from play to self-concern.

**Q2.8 [Explain]** Tell the two-colleagues story and its lesson.
**A:** After the same bad meeting, the self-centered colleague concludes "I'm a poor speaker, they're being nice, they'll gossip." The self-aware one notices the well-prepared did better, accepts the compliment and prepares more. Same event; only the lens differs.

**Q2.9 [SA]** Describe the "Me, Myself and I" exercise and the 3x5-card day.
**A:** At a social event with strangers, go all evening without saying anything favorable about yourself, without talking about yourself except answering direct questions, and without using "me," "myself" or "I." It exposes how hard non-self-occupation is. The 3x5-card day pairs with it: mark each time your mind is in the past or future.

**Q2.10 [SA]** Which mindset is the ego's home, and what is its worst fear?
**A:** The fixed mindset (Dweck). Its worst fear is trying hard and failing, because a failure could define you permanently.

**Q2.11 [Scenario]** A new team lead keeps checking her peers' performance numbers and feels worse each time. Which ideas from the chapter diagnose the loop?
**A:** Comparing to feel better: there is always someone with more, so identity is threatened and she grasps more. This is the PALMS/comparison link in the Path to Fear. Diagnostic question: "was it me or something mine that was threatened?" Placement of security in what she cannot control is the issue, not the achievement itself.

**Q2.12 [Scenario]** An analyst replays a failed pitch all night and concludes he is "not able." Which stage of the doubt escalation is he at, and what is the exit?
**A:** Personal ("I'm not able"), heading toward identity. The notes say nobody can make you feel inferior unless your own mind thinks, accepts and affirms it. The exit is self-awareness: observe the thoughts unattached and treat the pitch as feedback; also choose each morning to step away from self-centeredness.

# Chapter 3: The Greatest Opponents You'll Ever Face (Critic, Monkey Mind, Trickster)

**Q3.1 [SA]** Name the three adversaries and the one-line job of each.
**A:** The Critic judges and reacts; the Monkey Mind clutters and overanalyzes; the Trickster lies and accuses. Each gains power from attachment to results.

**Q3.2 [Explain]** What is the difference between judging and discerning?
**A:** To judge is to deliver a negative verdict (often without full information); to discern is to use wisdom to choose the best course. Judgment and curiosity cannot coexist, and creativity dies with curiosity.

**Q3.3 [SA]** What does Cal Botterill say about overanalysis?
**A:** It is the number one obstacle for performers; number two is caring too much. The Monkey Mind crowds out the space needed to dream and thrives in a heart with no single central purpose.

**Q3.4 [SA]** What are the Trickster's two tools and its core message?
**A:** Deception and accusation. Core message: your worth depends entirely on your results. Typical lines: "you're a fraud," "there's not enough to go around," "you'll get rejected," "who are you to be great?" It sounds logical because it cites real past failures.

**Q3.5 [SA]** What is the D-slide, and whose is it?
**A:** Dr. David Coppel's: Disappointed, Devastated, Defeated, Defective. Many go straight from disappointed to defective.

**Q3.6 [T/F]** Thoughts are facts with emotion added.
**A:** False. Circumstances are facts free of opinion or emotion; thoughts are opinions with emotion attached. Thoughts are not facts, and they must be separated from circumstances before seeking a solution.

**Q3.7 [SA]** What is "the Great Trick," and what is "self-rejection"?
**A:** The Great Trick is self-deception, holding too high or too low a view of yourself (the Trickster also tricks you into settling for lesser goals, the lollipop). Self-rejection (Nouwen) is the greatest trap in life, greater than success, popularity or power: feeling you *are* a failure rather than that you failed.

**Q3.8 [SA]** What is Charlie Maher's "fusing with language"?
**A:** "What if...?" thoughts create a reality that is not there. The real reality is just a hitter, a catcher and runners.

**Q3.9 [Explain]** Walk through the pandemic example in terms of circumstances, thoughts and truth.
**A:** Circumstance: schools closed, events cancelled, people ill. Thoughts: "everything's falling apart, it's hopeless." Truth: life is different; we will all die; the real question is whether we will fully live; this might be the best season to rethink your path.

**Q3.10 [Scenario]** You lose a big sale. Walk the D-slide and apply the intervention.
**A:** Disappointment is natural; attachment makes you devastated; the Critic says "this is really bad"; the Trickster recalls every failure and projects more, so you feel defective. Intervention: write circumstances (sale went to a competitor, the facts) in one column and thoughts in another. Nobody can argue with column one; only then consider the solution.

**Q3.11 [Scenario]** A team member says in every meeting, "I'm hopeless at presenting, everyone notices." Which adversary is speaking, and which presuppositions are the antidote?
**A:** The Trickster (accusation, past failures cited as proof) and the Critic. Antidotes: "All things are here to teach me and help me" and "The problem is not the problem, it's how I'm thinking about it." Set an alarm asking "Where's your mind right now?" and reset with two long breaths, exhaling longer than inhaling.

# Chapter 4: The Daring and the Twilight (Three Pillars of Extraordinary Performance)

**Q4.1 [SA]** State the three pillars, what each is called as an action, and what each brings.
**A:** Love: lead with your heart; brings freedom; becomes passion. Wisdom: expand your vision; brings belief; becomes purpose. Courage: be fully present; brings focus; becomes poise.

**Q4.2 [SA]** What is zoe?
**A:** Absolute fullness of life, the full expression of love, wisdom and courage together; selfless-actualization.

**Q4.3 [SA]** Name the three marks of a mastered ego.
**A:** Unembarrassable (no mistake or foolishness reduces your sense of self), unoffendable (no words or actions push your buttons; self-control, not self-protection), unirritatable (no person or circumstance steals your presence).

**Q4.4 [SA]** List the five ego reality-checks.
**A:** Worried what people think; nervous about embarrassment; more concerned with being right than learning; obsessed with winning over experiencing; angry when humiliated.

**Q4.5 [SA]** List the signs of mastery.
**A:** More space in your rhythm; less caught up in your mind; less attached to results; more concerned with empowering others; listen more than you speak; strict daily routines.

**Q4.6 [Explain]** Distinguish dreams from goals, using Dawn Staley.
**A:** Goals are external outcomes not fully in your control (win, hit .300, become CEO). Dreams are feelings; you set goals in order to feel the dream. Staley: the gold medal is the goal; playing to win against the best is the dream.

**Q4.7 [SA]** What is resonance?
**A:** A physics term, frequencies aligning; effortless excitement; fully engaged with a clear mind and unburdened heart. Great results are its by-product; extraordinary performance is a subset of extraordinary experience.

**Q4.8 [SA]** State the Fearless Four and the success rule that goes with them.
**A:** (1) Give my best, 100% of what I have today; (2) be present; (3) be grateful; (4) focus on my routines and only what I can control. Giving 100% of today's 40% counts as success.

**Q4.9 [SA]** Explain the three types of best (Frosty Westering) and the double win.
**A:** *The* best is a by-product and cannot be controlled; *your* best is hard to know; your *best shot* (prepare, go out, love doing it even when it is not good) is always available. The double win is bringing out the best in teammates and yourself.

**Q4.10 [Explain]** Retell Lewis Pugh's 2007 North Pole swim and what changed between failure and success.
**A:** Days before, two test swims failed (cells in his hands burst; 600 m max in warmer water), scientists said it was lethal, and his doctor's hands shook. His fear did not change. What changed: a connection to a purpose beyond himself (global warming, the coral reefs he had seen), and his coach planted one of 29 teammates' national flags every 100 m so he thought only about one nation's flag at a time, never the kilometer. Fear turned to aggression the moment he stepped on the ice. Recipe: purpose, chunking, team.

**Q4.11 [SA]** What did Georges St-Pierre say about fear?
**A:** It never disappears; it gets worse. What changes is acceptance ("a suffering process I go through before a fight").

**Q4.12 [Scenario]** Before a major client pitch a director thinks, "I have to win this." Which concepts convert the pressure, and how?
**A:** Want-to versus have-to: shift to "get to share." Outcome-oriented focus makes courage hard; focus on sharing something beautiful. Use the Fearless Four as the day's scorecard and unconditional love (the only fearless kind) plus a purpose beyond self.

**Q4.13 [Scenario]** A founder sets an ambitious goal and notices fewer friends understand her. Which note applies?
**A:** "The bigger your goals, the smaller your peer group": prepare emotionally for fewer people relating to you.

# Chapter 5: Code of the Samurai

**Q5.1 [Explain]** State the Samurai Paradox.
**A:** The samurai were fearless because they prepared to die each day: they learned to fight to the death by focusing on love (honor, service) more than self. "Die anew every morning"; if you cling to your life you will lose it.

**Q5.2 [SA]** What are the two forces in this chapter, and the three-part answer on staying in love under stress?
**A:** Self-protection mode (fear) versus self-expression mode (love). Staying on love: fuel your heart daily with the words and stories of those who lived in love so that is what comes out in crisis; return via the presuppositions and reset tools.

**Q5.3 [SA]** List the five steps of Beauty Before Bed.
**A:** (1) Review the day; (2) recall three small, specific beautiful moments; (3) reprogram mistakes (see yourself doing it as you wish), then let go of the day; (4) preview tomorrow's routines; (5) visualize tomorrow's feelings. Never carry worries to bed, since the subconscious consolidates whatever you bring.

**Q5.4 [SA]** Happiness versus joy?
**A:** Happiness is temporary and attached to circumstance; joy is deep well-being, freedom and gratitude independent of circumstance, possible in suffering. Chasing happiness is "The Great Mistake."

**Q5.5 [SA]** What chain does the chapter give for suffering, and what blocks learning from it?
**A:** Suffering leads to perseverance, then character, then hope. Barriers: believing you should not suffer (which doubles it) and fear (of self, future, unknown). Wim Hof lesson: focus on the cold rather than ignore it.

**Q5.6 [T/F]** The most dangerous addictions are the obvious ones such as meth.
**A:** False per the notes. All addiction is avoidance of emotional pain, and hidden addictions (pride, needing recognition, comfort) are hardest because they are socially acceptable.

**Q5.7 [Explain]** What do the Four Horses (Suzuki) teach?
**A:** The best horse runs before the whip's shadow; the worst only when pain reaches the marrow, yet the "clever" often plateau and those who suffered learn deepest. Victory is the most dangerous position (it tempts pride and slack); failure is dangerous too (self-rejection).

**Q5.8 [SA]** List the eight attachments.
**A:** How others see you; money and possessions; what you want (goals); comfort; your past; how things are (status quo); expectations; your self. Self-mastery is largely dropping these.

**Q5.9 [Explain]** What is the lollipop exchange?
**A:** You cannot take a lollipop from a kid without resistance unless you give a bigger one. Trade winning (lollipop) for fullness of life (the whole candy store) as the highest goal; you get the best shot at the medal as a bonus.

**Q5.10 [SA]** What do the 12 Steps and the UBC golf team illustrate?
**A:** 12 Steps: the illness in every group is self-centeredness; the cure is dying to the old nature, self-awareness, surrendering control and vision beyond self (Serenity Prayer). UBC golf: their mantra "The score is for the fans; we play for the love of the game" was lost when they were attached; they won two national titles when they let go.

**Q5.11 [SA]** Give the Kalanithi and Apolo Ohno lessons.
**A:** Kalanithi: "wouldn't it be great if it [death] were more painful?" The pain of loss measures the size of the gift, so do not refuse gifts for fear of losing them. Ohno at 3:30 a.m. in a parking lot with a miner's lamp: "I'm willing to do what nobody else is willing to do"; "the conversation doesn't end when you fail; that's when it starts."

**Q5.12 [Scenario]** Murphy's Scenario A versus B: what were they, and what did they test?
**A:** A: marching in the Sydney 2000 Opening Ceremony minutes after a talk from Nelson Mandela, elation on top of anxiety about an uncertain life. B: sitting broke and in debt in a friend's tiny LA apartment while the friend played a gratitude song on guitar, feeling fully known and loved. Extraordinary circumstances produced happiness over anxiety; nothing circumstances produced joy. Use it to test what you are optimizing for.

**Q5.13 [Scenario]** After a record quarter, a sales leader stops preparing and starts coasting. Which concept applies?
**A:** "Victory is the most dangerous position" (Four Horses): it tempts pride and slack. Also "slacking after victory" is a listed anti-pattern; keep process goals and the Fearless Four.

# Chapter 6: Change Your State, Change Your Life

**Q6.1 [SA]** Give the Results Chain.
**A:** Circumstance, filtered through memories and beliefs, becomes thoughts, then feelings, then actions, then results (which become new circumstances).

**Q6.2 [SA]** What are the four pattern processes of the brain?
**A:** Assumptions (fills in the elephant from a leg), generalizations (one bad presentation becomes "I always struggle"), deletions (necessary filtering that also hides possibilities), distortions ("always/never"). Selfless-actualizers still do all four but recognize and manage them.

**Q6.3 [Explain]** What does "what gets your attention gets you" mean, and what evidence supports it?
**A:** (E. Stanley Jones) attention drives thoughts, feelings, actions and results, so organize your environment. Priming research (hot coffee leading to warmer judgments and more generosity) shows subtle cues steer behavior without awareness.

**Q6.4 [SA]** What is "The Choice," and its point?
**A:** A $10M ocean house with normal stresses versus an apartment paycheck-to-paycheck with guaranteed deep contentment for life. Go directly for what the house is supposed to give you.

**Q6.5 [SA]** Explain the three focus/relax skills.
**A:** (1) Focus softly (between plays, relaxed readiness) and sharply (key moments); (2) relax moderately (between sets, off-days) and completely (naps, sleep; delta waves, whereas anxiety is beta too fast); (3) move back and forth. Practice with peripheral vision and Open Focus (Fehmi).

**Q6.6 [SA]** State the Basic Reset steps and box breathing.
**A:** Stop; pick a spot above the horizon; stare and stop thoughts; inhale through the nose (~4); exhale longer (~6), relaxing jaw and face and letting go of desires and concerns; twice (once is fine). Box breathing: inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4 (jaw loose), hold 4.

**Q6.7 [Explain]** What does "I expect nothing" mean, and does it kill confidence?
**A:** Inhale "I expect," hold, exhale "nothing" (samurai: expect nothing, prepare for anything), optionally "I can handle anything." Expectation is anticipation of circumstances (avoid); confidence is faith in the future (keep). Expect abundance but do not attach to it.

**Q6.8 [SA]** Explain anchoring and the Float-Up.
**A:** Anchoring: pair a peak feeling with a unique sensory cue (smell is strongest, e.g., peppermint oil; or a fist clench, a song) at the feeling's height, then trigger the cue later. Float-Up: centered, float to the ceiling, above the building, city, continent, the whole earth, leaving concerns below, then descend with cares gone.

**Q6.9 [T/F]** Giving in to every desire is harmless because desires are motivating.
**A:** False. Desire is a marvelous motivator and terrible master; discipline is delaying gratification. Give in to everything and you soon cannot reject what you once could. "Have-to" feelings are ego; "get-to" feelings are mastery.

**Q6.10 [SA]** Cite two supporting stories: Gareth Bale, Freddie Roach, or the bike kid.
**A:** Bale: sleeps 9-10 hours plus a 1-2 hour nap, waking exactly four hours before kick-off (homeostasis of routines). Roach: "I don't want him angry. We have a plan; I want him to follow the plan." Bike kid: you move toward what you stare at (the rock).

**Q6.11 [Scenario]** Driving to an important event, your heart is racing. Apply the chapter's worked example.
**A:** Pull over; center (spot above the horizon, two long exhales); mind settles, heart rate normalizes; still nervous but no longer scattered. Then run "I expect nothing... I can handle anything" and drive on with more peace and presence. (Contrast the ballet dancer Erica K., who breathes at center stage hours early and imagines hugging the audience.)

**Q6.12 [Scenario]** A pitcher has used narrow, intense focus all game and is burning out. Which concept helps?
**A:** The anti-pattern "gas pedal always down." Use soft focus and moderate relaxation between actions and sharp focus only during, practicing the switch with peripheral vision and Open Focus.

# Chapter 7: The World Is Flat (Developing Beliefs in Line with Your Dreams)

**Q7.1 [SA]** What is the X factor in the Super Bowl kick example?
**A:** Beliefs, thoughts and feelings in the months before, more than form, wind or snap: "are you the type of person who kicks game-winners?"

**Q7.2 [Explain]** Explain homeostasis and why it matters.
**A:** The subconscious keeps performance inside what you believe is right for you, dragging down over-performance and lifting under-performance. You attract confirming experiences, which create more such experiences. Results will not exceed beliefs for long.

**Q7.3 [SA]** How do beliefs form?
**A:** An experience with strong feeling (rejection when you sang; the "spider" that was a ball of yarn) leads the brain to file the feeling to protect you, producing a belief, often for life and in an instant. Stronger negative feeling means a likelier limiting belief.

**Q7.4 [SA]** What is the belief audit?
**A:** Ask: What are the three main beliefs I have about achieving this goal? Is each useful now? What would I have to believe to achieve it?

**Q7.5 [Explain]** Explain "speak the truth about the past" with an example.
**A:** Say everything you do not want to continue in past tense. Not "I'm struggling with my putting" (the subconscious hunts for proof) but "In the past I struggled with X and I'm getting better every day."

**Q7.6 [SA]** What is the One-Second Tingle?
**A:** Visualize some aspect of your biggest dream every day until you feel tingling excitement for at least one second. Daily beats long.

**Q7.7 [SA]** List the negative-thought overrides.
**A:** Dismiss immediately (a thought has power only if you let it linger; surfer letting waves pass); replace with the opposite affirmation; shout "STOP!" with a giant red stop sign; snap a rubber band on your wrist.

**Q7.8 [T/F]** You must believe an affirmation before you use it.
**A:** False. You are not expected to believe it at first; that is why you affirm. Make it positive ("I am..."), short, present tense; model someone who holds the belief; try silent phone pop-ups; act as if (stand tall, look up; Delancey Street with ex-felons).

**Q7.9 [Explain]** What is the "final piece" congruence check?
**A:** Subconscious sabotage arises when achieving the goal conflicts with a value or threatens something you would lose (smokers' social bond; wealth distrusted by a strict upbringing). Ask what achieving the dream will give you and whether any of it conflicts with who you want to be. Repeated injury or illness before big events can be a subconscious veto.

**Q7.10 [Explain]** Retell the Callista Balko story and what made it work.
**A:** She had struck out 9-of-9 against Cat Osterman all season. In preseason she wrote affirmations ("I am an incredible two-strike hitter," "I love pressure," "I control my destiny"), scripted the post-championship interview in detail and visualized the exact scenario daily for months: Osterman pitching, winning run on second, World Series on the line. In the real game they intentionally walked a batter to face her; she felt confidence, and on first base thought only that she had rehearsed this and it had just come true. Ingredients: affirmations, scripted scene, daily feeling, hard practice.

**Q7.11 [SA]** Name two supporting examples: Andy Lopez, Muggsy Bogues, or the Olympian's hurdle.
**A:** Lopez played College World Series footage in the Pepperdine locker room all season; the small school won it all. Bogues: shot at five, 5'3", 14 NBA seasons, imagination fed daily. The hurdle: an Olympian cleared a 42-inch hurdle in the living room 25 times a day to plant the image.

**Q7.12 [Scenario]** A salesperson says "I'm struggling with cold calls" every morning, and his slump persists. What do you tell him?
**A:** Present-tense struggle talk prolongs slumps; switch to past tense ("In the past I struggled with cold calls and I'm getting better every day"), run a belief audit and add a daily one-second tingle visualization.

**Q7.13 [Scenario]** A manager aims for a big promotion but keeps falling ill before key reviews. Which concept applies?
**A:** The congruence check: the subconscious may be vetoing a goal that conflicts with a value or threatens something she would lose. Ask what the promotion would give her and what it might cost.

# Chapter 8: A Clear and Present Beauty (Five Ways to Be Fully Present)

**Q8.1 [SA]** List the five practices of presence.
**A:** (1) Get out of your head, into your heart and soul; (2) focus on routines and only what you can control; (3) be grateful; (4) focus on a mantra; (5) ruthlessly eliminate hurry.

**Q8.2 [Explain]** Desire versus passion.
**A:** Desire is future-focused and judges whether the outcome will come; passion is in the present. Visualize the putt dropping before; during execution have zero thoughts of outcome (Bruce Lee: the great mistake is anticipating the outcome).

**Q8.3 [Explain]** Why does a cluttered mind mean a heart problem, and what is the fix?
**A:** Anxiety is too many thoughts from too many concerns; a cluttered mind is a heart without one unifying devotion (Bonhoeffer). Simplify to one thing (Phelps: "what could I remove from my life?"); do one dish well, one rep, one true sentence. Sound tool: loudest sound, quietest, both, add one body part.

**Q8.4 [SA]** What is the simple mind and its 80% check?
**A:** (Ronn Svetich) get the sign, see the pitch, throw it. Between innings ask "did I keep a simple mind at least 80% of the time?"

**Q8.5 [SA]** Give the GPS Tool timings and components.
**A:** 3 min Gratitude (small, specific, last 24-48 h) + 3 min Presence (eyes open, let go of desires) + 3 min Showtime (visualize the big moment under pressure) + 1 min Funnel (outside, barefoot, arms in a Y, receive abundance with gratitude).

**Q8.6 [T/F]** Perfection helps presence because it focuses you.
**A:** False. Perfection is the enemy of presence (Reid Hoffman's embarrassing first version; Lamott's messy first drafts). Wanting control and wanting perfection are the same fear; surrendering control is how you gain it.

**Q8.7 [SA]** Why is gratitude central, and what is the SEAL instructor quote?
**A:** You cannot be anxious and grateful at once, or judgmental and grateful; gratitude and entitlement are opposite poles. Practice on small, specific moments; gratitude expands vision, creativity and pattern recognition. SEAL instructor: "humility can defeat any opponent; pride busts down the front door and gets you killed; humility sees the back door."

**Q8.8 [SA]** Give two mantra stories.
**A:** Harrington's "I'm going to win" in the Open playoff; Murphy's trail-riding trio (sing aloud, visualize ESPN covering you, repeat "I am the best athlete in the world"); Wim Hof retreat: "My body is a sauna... my backpack is a heater" on a two-hour shirtless hike at -8 C, with the lesson that your conscious mind does not know what is possible.

**Q8.9 [SA]** What is hurry sickness, how common, and what is the prescription and rhythm?
**A:** Chronic time urgency (Friedman and Rosenman); symptoms include passing cars for no reason, impatience with cashiers, poor sleep, constant stress, pressing the elevator close button. 95% of executives have it (Jolly). Prescription (Willard to Ortberg): "You must ruthlessly eliminate hurry from your life. There is nothing else." Rhythm: gaps about every 90 minutes, one full day in seven, one year in seven.

**Q8.10 [Explain]** Why is hurry "a disordered heart"?
**A:** Hurry is not a schedule problem; it is the cause of stress, not its cure, and it kills joy and compassion (compassion interrupts plans). Busyness is a compulsive search for approval (Nouwen).

**Q8.11 [Scenario]** A PGA client in 2019 began meeting his caddie before each round. What did he do, what followed, and how can a manager copy it?
**A:** He reviewed the Fearless Four for the day with the caddie, then took one silent minute to visualize pursuing them; several weeks in, he won an event worth over $1M. As a work ritual: name today's four process goals with a partner, then spend a minute visualizing.

**Q8.12 [Scenario]** A team is overloaded, everyone multitasks and keeps rushing to "reduce stress." What concepts apply?
**A:** Anti-patterns: multitasking as productivity (the mind processes one thought at a time) and hurrying to reduce stress (the hurry is the stress). Apply one devotion, one basic element, 90-minute gaps and a weekly day off.

# Chapter 9: Unstoppable (Overcoming Mental Blocks, Fears and Phobias)

**Q9.1 [SA]** What are the subconscious's three jobs?
**A:** (1) Sift sensory input for physical or emotional danger, filed in the same folder; (2) run life in the background (shoes, driving); (3) line up circumstances and results with your beliefs (the 70s golfer on pace for the low 60s is pulled back).

**Q9.2 [Explain]** Why is a mental block a sign the mind is working perfectly?
**A:** The subconscious has filed an emotionally painful context as danger and protects you from it. Think of a flawless bodyguard with a wrong threat list: update the list, do not fight the guard.

**Q9.3 [SA]** Describe the Sax sequence of how a block forms.
**A:** Error, felt bad, looked at stats and projected the future, more errors, obsession (idee fixe), career-threatening block. Generalized: painful event leads the subconscious to lock in the context, add it to the warn/protect list, embed it as deep as the emotion, and replay it whenever the context recurs.

**Q9.4 [SA]** Why do easy tasks block?
**A:** Social pressure not to miss the easy thing: extra points rather than 55-yard field goals; two-foot putts.

**Q9.5 [SA]** List the four removal principles.
**A:** (1) Take the emotion out; (2) find the smallest change that would have made a difference; (3) insert a positive, high-energy feeling plus the small change into the block's context; (4) anchor that feeling to the original painful context for a new, stronger association.

**Q9.6 [Explain]** Describe Hollywood Helper.
**A:** Imagine a theater; see a black-and-white still of yourself on screen; float up into the projection booth, hands on cool glass, watching yourself watch; run the event in black-and-white at 2x, 4x, 8x; then step into the film at the end and run it backwards first-person at 2x, 4x, 8x, 16x. Third-person rule: the farther the viewpoint, the weaker the emotion.

**Q9.7 [SA]** What is the smallest change, with examples?
**A:** Sax calls his dad immediately; Ankiel calls time after the first wild pitch and recalls a moment of perfect accuracy. It need not be the solution, only one the subconscious can accept so it can release the pain. Sax's dad: "one day you'll wake up and it's gone; it happened to me too."

**Q9.8 [SA]** How do you insert and anchor positive feelings, and how many repetitions?
**A:** Recall a time you felt calm and confident with all senses; locate it in the body; ask the subconscious for two more such memories; stack them; re-enter the movie first-person, in color, with the small change and stacked feeling; play 5-7 times.

**Q9.9 [SA]** Describe Power APP and countdown breathing.
**A:** Place the memory on a floor spot, step in (feel it), step out and shake off; build a peak state with countdown breathing (inhale counting 10 to 7, pause, exhale 6 to 1, for 1-2 min); walk toward the spot only when calm; step in, turn a full circle, stay 3-4 min, step out.

**Q9.10 [SA]** What is the pain rating rule?
**A:** Rate the memory 1-10 before (look for 9s and 10s) and after (target 0-2); repeat if not near zero. No details need to be shared.

**Q9.11 [Explain]** Retell Rick Ankiel's story and its lesson.
**A:** Wild pitches in the playoffs led to nightmares, vodka before games and years in the minors; he quit pitching and returned as an outfielder throwing 300-foot lasers. Same arm; a different anchored context.

**Q9.12 [Scenario]** A top-10 golfer hits into the water on a par 3 on day one and rates tomorrow's confidence "very low." What does the chapter's example do?
**A:** Fifteen minutes on the phone run through dissociate, smallest change, positive-feeling anchor; he plays that hole effortlessly the rest of the week. Similar results: Masters-eve client birdied or parred his doubtful holes (top-10 finish); an abuse survivor rated memories 8-10, then 0-2, ranked No. 1 within six months.

**Q9.13 [Scenario]** A violinist keeps freezing on one passage and responds by drilling it harder. Which anti-pattern applies and why?
**A:** Working harder at the blocked skill (Sax threw perfectly blindfolded in an empty stadium; effort was not the problem) and analyzing/worrying about the block, which convinces the subconscious the danger is real. Update the anchored association instead. (Notes caution that serious trauma may need more.)

# Chapter 10: The Hero and the Goat (Poise Under Pressure)

**Q10.1 [SA]** What are the six elements of performance, and which matter most?
**A:** Circumstances, beliefs, thoughts, state, performance, results. The three that matter most (beliefs, thoughts, state) are all unseen.

**Q10.2 [SA]** Define presence, resonance and goat; and state resonance's characteristics and obstacles.
**A:** Presence: clear mind, unburdened heart. Resonance: excitement and presence from being fully engaged in your natural state. Goat: the one blamed for the loss. Resonance: clear mind, unburdened heart (free of how the performance affects your worth), non-judgmental awareness; obstacles: overanalysis, self-consciousness, constant judging of circumstances/results.

**Q10.3 [SA]** List the four keys.
**A:** Share your heart, not your ego; pursue mastery, not the score; love your opponent; visualize presence, not perfection.

**Q10.4 [Explain]** Key 1: why does sharing beauty build toughness (Sinek) and what does Bonnie Blair show?
**A:** Playing a piece perfectly cannot produce mental toughness; giving the audience something beautiful can, because you can always do that. Dreams (feelings) are in your control; goals are not. Blair valued a 4th-place personal best over a gold.

**Q10.5 [Explain]** Key 2: what does "from ability to availability" and "be willing to be the goat" mean?
**A:** The score is past/future and not in your control, so chasing it is an obstacle to a good score. Mastery means endless self-awareness, self-discipline and growth, embracing failure; availability is willingness to sacrifice, face fears and surrender self-concern. To be the G.O.A.T. you must be willing to be the goat. Phil Jackson: when the trophy is handed over, "we are no longer a success."

**Q10.6 [SA]** Key 3: what were the client's four shifts before the world title?
**A:** Gratitude for opponents; respect as people; wanting the best for them; recognizing the greatest rival is necessary to reach your potential. Derin McMains: flow comes from loving the people you are in the dance with. GSP prays for his opponent. Keep want-to greater than got-to.

**Q10.7 [SA]** Key 4: list the visualization techniques and the 80/20.
**A:** Preview (5 min before every sleep incl. naps), confidence memories (stack three), presence under pressure (exaggerate to the ridiculous), relax, reframe (replay mistakes as intended), scene of success (the 20 seconds then 20 minutes *after*, not the performance), role models/mentors (the best asking you for advice; ivy-covered room with MLK, Wooden, Mandela, anchored to one song). GSP: ~80% what he will do, ~20% overcoming adversity.

**Q10.8 [SA]** What are Newburg's five questions?
**A:** How do I want to feel? What does it take to get that feeling? What keeps me from it? How can I get it back? What am I willing to work for?

**Q10.9 [Explain]** Explain the adversity ladder (Kyla Inaba).
**A:** Anything you cannot control is how it is supposed to be; "bad" weather is good for Inner Excellence athletes. Staying neutral puts you one notch above the field; embracing it as sent for you puts you two notches up, before the event starts. She won.

**Q10.10 [Explain]** Stewart Cink at the 2009 Open: what happened and what does it teach?
**A:** He said "nah, don't got it": no confidence, but no fear of the feeling of failing, gratitude to be there; resonance without confidence; he won. Aim for resonance rather than confidence.

**Q10.11 [Scenario]** Dr. Tribble's resident, during a failing cardiac operation, tries an unorthodox procedure. What does he say, and how do you apply it to a presenter?
**A:** Asked if he had done it before: "No, but I've thought about it a lot." He had rehearsed contingencies with poise. A presenter lists worst-case interruptions (no projector, notes lost, hostile question) and visualizes handling each with a clear mind until nothing real can exceed the rehearsal.

**Q10.12 [Scenario]** A salesperson hopes a rival bids badly. Which key applies, and why is the hope self-defeating?
**A:** Key 3: love your opponent. Wanting the opponent to fail creates negative energy and bad strategy; a strong opponent is your best chance to play your best. Use the ski-race story told to Mickelson: two best friends race, photo finish, high-five; you would not storm off, so you were not skiing for the race.

# Chapter 11: Three Hallmarks of Extraordinary Leaders (and Conclusion)

**Q11.1 [SA]** What are the three hallmarks?
**A:** Redefine success; connect individuals with a vision beyond themselves; seek self-mastery and help others do the same.

**Q11.2 [Explain]** How do Wooden, Tressel and Westering redefine success?
**A:** Wooden: peace of mind from knowing you made the effort to become the best you are capable of; Tressel adds "for the group." Westering: winning is a by-product of learning to live decently; his player: "They're here to beat us; we're here to be us." Wooden neither scouted opponents nor recruited.

**Q11.3 [SA]** What do Collins and Blue Ocean add?
**A:** Collins' myth #10: visionary companies beat themselves, not the competition. Blue Ocean: stop trying to beat the competition. Also the Warriors' four values: joy, compassion, mindfulness, competition.

**Q11.4 [SA]** Purpose versus vision, with examples.
**A:** Purpose is who you are (meaning, inspiration); vision is a goal serving the greater good (direction, focus). A SEAL in Hell Week: "I focused on my team; quitting wasn't an option." Brother Lawrence: a dishwasher whose presence is studied 400 years later.

**Q11.5 [SA]** State pace, pace, pace, lead and the 55/38/7 split.
**A:** Match posture/physiology (55%), tone (38%) and words (7%) before leading; e.g., walk with the angry employee, match volume, then guide to sit.

**Q11.6 [Explain]** Explain values elicitation.
**A:** Ask "What's most important to you about your job?" then "What does that give you?" and repeat until the answer stops changing (e.g., security). Connect the group's vision to that root value.

**Q11.7 [Explain]** What is the pink elephant doing backflips?
**A:** The brain does not picture "don't." Say "hold on to the ball," not "don't fumble"; give the image and feeling of what you want. Yelling at one player is yelling at everyone (Davey Johnson).

**Q11.8 [SA]** What does Jordan's practice love teach, and what is the renewal list?
**A:** He loved practice: "pure basketball," challenged in what he was good at, no crowd, no score, making practice and games the same chance for resonance. Renewal list: focus on what is True; fill your mind daily with love/wisdom/courage (books, podcasts, sermons; memorize key ideas); train the heart's appetites; spend time with the much wiser and those in greater need; review the day before bed then let go; monitor the ego; one day in seven; get honest feedback.

**Q11.9 [SA]** What do "a person first" (Vermeil), "growth is the end," and "culture at scale" mean?
**A:** A football player is a person first, then coach the activity. Asking why pursue growth with no end is like asking why live when you will die. When you cannot meet everyone, build a culture of purpose, vision and self-mastery so people connect with each other.

**Q11.10 [Scenario]** A manager walks with an employee who is angry about a reorg. Apply the chapter.
**A:** Pace, pace, pace, then lead: match posture and volume, then guide to sitting. Elicit the root value ("what does that give you?") and frame the team's vision in terms of it (e.g., security); discretionary effort follows.

**Q11.11 [Scenario]** A coach tells his kicker, "Don't miss this." Rewrite it and explain.
**A:** "Hold on to the ball"-style: give the image and feeling of the desired action (e.g., a clean, relaxed strike); the brain skips "don't," so a "don't" instruction pictures the failure.

**Q11.12 [Scenario]** A leader of a 400-person division cannot meet everyone. Which idea applies?
**A:** Culture at scale: build a culture of purpose, vision and self-mastery; also model renewal (leaders must know when they are not present).

**Q11.13 [Explain]** What does the Conclusion add?
**A:** The seesaw between past and future with the present as the balance point; sacred moments felt unsettling because they revealed potential and the suffering required; the reader now has a mindset, skill set and tool set; seek first to develop the inner world and relationships, and the rest is added.

# FINAL EXAM (20 questions, whole book)

**F1 [Explain]** Summarize the book's central thesis in two sentences.
**A:** Extraordinary performance and the best possible life are the same path, through the heart; heart first, performance second. Self-centeredness is the root of fear, the chief obstacle, and love, wisdom and courage overcome it. (Ch 0, 2, 4)

**F2 [SA]** Map the three pillars to BFF.
**A:** Love/lead with your heart: freedom (passion). Wisdom/expand your vision: belief (purpose). Courage/be fully present: focus (poise). (Ch 0, 4)

**F3 [SA]** Give the full Path to Fear and name which later chapter's three adversaries operate inside it.
**A:** Self-centeredness, attachment to past failures, self-consciousness, amplified ego, comparison, pride and fear, doubt escalating external, personal, identity. The Critic, Monkey Mind and Trickster (Ch 3) are the ego's tools along this path.

**F4 [T/F]** The Critic has power regardless of whether you are attached to the goal.
**A:** False. The Critic has power only when you are attached to an uncontrollable goal, since the ego reads every event as progress toward or away from it. (Ch 3)

**F5 [MC]** Which is *not* one of the Fearless Four? (a) Give my best, (b) be present, (c) win the event, (d) be grateful.
**A:** (c). The four are give my best, be present, be grateful, focus on routines and only what I control; none is an outcome. (Ch 4)

**F6 [SA]** What are the eight attachments, and which chapter's exchange helps drop them?
**A:** How others see you, money/possessions, goals, comfort, your past, status quo, expectations, your self. The lollipop exchange (Ch 5): trade winning for fullness of life.

**F7 [Explain]** Compare the "I expect nothing" mantra (Ch 6, 10) and the idea of expecting abundance.
**A:** Expectation is anticipation of circumstances and is future-bound; confidence is faith in the future. Expect abundance (beauty is always there) while attaching to nothing: "I expect nothing; I can handle anything" (Dodd's dock mantra).

**F8 [Explain]** How do homeostasis (Ch 7) and the subconscious's third job (Ch 9) fit together?
**A:** Both describe the subconscious lining up results with beliefs: it drags down over-performance and lifts under-performance. A mental block is the same mechanism guarding an anchored emotional danger, so updating beliefs and anchors changes results.

**F9 [SA]** What is the Results Chain, and where does the belief layer sit?
**A:** Circumstance, filtered through memories and beliefs, then thoughts, feelings, actions, results. Beliefs are in the filter (Ch 6); Ch 10's six elements put beliefs second and call beliefs, thoughts and state the unseen three that matter most.

**F10 [SA]** State "speak the truth about the past" and one related tool from Ch 3.
**A:** Put unwanted patterns in past tense ("In the past I struggled with X and I'm getting better every day"). Related: replace Trickster lies with truth, and the circumstances/thoughts split.

**F11 [Explain]** Why can't you be both anxious and grateful, and how does that tie to the five presence practices?
**A:** The notes state it as fact: gratitude and anxiety (or judgment) cannot coexist; gratitude expands vision. It is practice 3 of Ch 8, linked to the Fearless Four's "be grateful" and the GPS tool's first block.

**F12 [Scenario]** A rising executive hates a rival and quietly hopes he stumbles at the board presentation. Diagnose using at least three chapters.
**A:** Ch 10 key 3 (love your opponent; wishing failure creates negative energy and bad strategy), Ch 2 (comparison/pride: "more than others"), Ch 1 (PALMS: status). Tell: "you want your opponent to slip" means ego comparison and presence lost. Remedy: gratitude, respect, want their best, see them as necessary to your potential.

**F13 [SA]** Give the mastery-versus-ego pairs that most contrast "best shot" and "process."
**A:** Best shot vs. win-or-lose; process vs. outcome; also willing to look foolish vs. afraid of humiliation; few needs vs. needy; sees beauty first vs. sees flaws first; transcends circumstances vs. attached to them. (Ch 5)

**F14 [Explain]** How do Ch 4's want-to/have-to, Ch 6's get-to/have-to feelings and Ch 10's "want-to greater than got-to" relate?
**A:** All describe the same shift from ego-pressure to freedom: "have to win" versus "get to share." Pressure is lessened when want-to exceeds got-to.

**F15 [SA]** List the numbers: Fearless Four, simple mind, visualization split, GPS timing, pain rating, rest rhythm, rapport.
**A:** 100% of today's capacity (even 40%); simple mind at least 80%; ~80% execution / ~20% adversity, 5 min before every sleep; GPS 3+3+3+1; pain 8-10 needs work, done at 2 or below; gap ~90 min, one day in seven, one year in seven; rapport 55% posture, 38% tone, 7% words, pace three times.

**F16 [Scenario]** A VP says: "I'll be happy when we hit the target; I'm anxious until then." Diagnose with Ch 1, 5 and 6.
**A:** Ch 5: chasing happiness (circumstance-dependent) is The Great Mistake; target joy. Ch 1: identity in PALMS/achievement. Ch 6: reactionary living, chasing better circumstances for better feelings; feelings come from thoughts. Use Beauty Before Bed, gratitude and the dream-vs-goal distinction (Ch 4).

**F17 [Explain]** Contrast what is "protected" in a mental block (Ch 9) with the Critic's verdicts (Ch 3). Why are both fixable?
**A:** Both are learned associations rather than facts: the Critic's verdicts are opinions attached to circumstances; the block is an anchored association filed as danger. Separate thoughts from facts; update the association with dissociation, a small change and a stacked confidence feeling.

**F18 [SA]** Which leader practices connect to personal practices?
**A:** Redefine success (Ch 1, 4's three bests); vision beyond self (Ch 4 purpose beyond self); self-mastery and renewal (Ch 5 Beauty Before Bed and day off; Ch 8 rhythm). Coaching in pictures of what you want links to Ch 9's finding that you move toward what you think about.

**F19 [Scenario]** You will give a high-stakes keynote next week. Build a one-week plan from the book.
**A:** Daily GPS (Ch 8); Fearless Four with a partner; visualization suite (Ch 10): preview 5 min before sleep, presence under pressure exaggerated, mistakes reframed, scene of success 20 s then 20 min after; rate any 8-10 memory and run block removal (Ch 9); belief audit and affirmations (Ch 7); on the day, Reset and "I expect nothing; I can handle anything" (Ch 6), love the audience as in Erica K.'s example.

**F20 [Explain]** In your own words: why does the book say pursuing fullness of life also gives the best shot at the medal?
**A:** Winning is a by-product of process (Smith, Krzyzewski, Westering), results are an unreliable measure, and chasing the score pulls the mind into the future and away from presence. Trading the lollipop for the candy store keeps the heart free, so you are most likely to perform at your best. (Ch 1, 5, 10)
