# Inner Excellence Quiz Book
*Inner Excellence: Train Your Mind for Extraordinary Performance and the Best Possible Life* by Jim Murphy

**How to use:** Cover the answers, answer from memory (out loud or in writing), then check. Anything you miss is a section to reread. Each chapter has short-answer (SA), true/false (T/F), and multiple-choice (MC) questions. A cumulative final exam is at the end. Chapter numbering follows the `murphy-inner-excellence` skill (Ch 0 is the Preface, Presuppositions and Introduction; Ch 11 includes the Conclusion). The questions test the skill's summary of the book, not the book's own wording.

---

## Chapter 0: Preface, Presuppositions & Introduction

**Q1 (SA).** What is Murphy's central claim about extraordinary performance and the best possible life?
**A:** They are the same path, and both run through the heart. The biggest obstacle on that path is self-centeredness. Order matters: heart first, performance second.

**Q2 (SA).** What does BFF stand for, and which resource produces each element (as mapped later in the book)?
**A:** Belief, Focus, Freedom, the three performance variables. Wisdom produces belief, courage produces focus, love produces freedom.

**Q3 (SA).** State the Inner Excellence mindset in Murphy's words.
**A:** "I compete to raise the level of excellence in my life, to learn and grow, in order to raise it in others."

**Q4 (MC).** Which is NOT one of the ten presuppositions?
a) The map is not the territory  b) You are not your mind  c) There is no failure, only feedback  d) Winning is everything
**A:** d. Winning is everything is the world's mindset. The Inner Excellence view is that inner strength and growth last longer.

**Q5 (SA).** Presupposition 8 says "the problem is not the problem." What is the rest of it?
**A:** The problem is how you're thinking about it.

**Q6 (T/F).** In the Inner Excellence mindset, the opponent is an enemy to be beaten. **A:** False. The opponent is a partner in the dance. The world's mindset (opponent = enemy) is the contrast.

**Q7 (SA).** What did Murphy's great-great-grandfather Bunpachiro the samurai illustrate?
**A:** Identity built on a role that can be lost. When the role was lost he turned to alcoholism. Build identity on something no circumstance can remove.

## Chapter 1: Maslow and the Maserati

**Q1 (SA).** What does PALMS stand for, and what is the actual problem with it?
**A:** Possessions, Achievements, Looks, Money, Status. The virus is not wanting or having them; it is placing trust and identity in them. Its engine is not "more" but "more than others."

**Q2 (SA).** What are the three diagnostic questions for finding what you love (worship) most?
**A:** What do I dream about? What do I worry about? What do I get upset about? Your life is built on the answer, and your stability equals its stability.

**Q3 (MC).** The two paths in this chapter are:
a) the wide road of praise and comfort vs. the narrow road of facing fears and connecting to your true self  b) the road of work vs. the road of rest  c) the road of goals vs. the road of dreams  d) the road of ego vs. the road of money
**A:** a. The narrow road is difficult but leads to deep contentment, joy, confidence and an undivided heart.

**Q4 (SA).** What do the lollipop and the whole candy store stand for?
**A:** Gold medals and CEO titles are lollipops; fullness of life is the candy store.

**Q5 (T/F).** Murphy says it is wrong to have possessions, achievements or status. **A:** False. They are fine to have and fatal to build identity on.

**Q6 (SA).** Name at least four of the nine characteristics of the selfless-actualizer (Maslow, relabeled).
**A:** Any four of: total absorption; personal growth (the means is the end); self-awareness; *Gemeinschaft* (community); fresh gratitude; authenticity / resistance to enculturation; comfort in solitude; purpose beyond self; lack of ego defenses.

**Q7 (SA).** What did the Costa Rica observation (the dentist and the tow-truck driver) show?
**A:** A PALMS-free social world: a dentist invited the tow-truck driver in for dinner and got excited when the driver pointed out his hometown on a map. People who "needed little and appreciated much" seemed far happier than people in Murphy's own culture.

## Chapter 2: Where the Wild Things Are

**Q1 (SA).** What is the root cause of fear of failure, according to this chapter?
**A:** Self-centeredness: the hard-wired preoccupation with self and attachment to the past. It breeds self-consciousness, feeds the ego and ends in self-rejection.

**Q2 (SA).** Walk through the Path to Fear.
**A:** Self-centeredness, attachment to past failures, self-consciousness, ego amplified, comparison (via PALMS), pride and fear. Doubts then escalate from external ("I won't achieve it") to personal ("I'm not able") to identity ("I'm a failure").

**Q3 (T/F).** Self-centeredness means being morally selfish. **A:** False. It is not selfishness or a moral flaw but a preoccupation with self that limits options and stunts growth. It has been the default since infancy.

**Q4 (MC).** C.S. Lewis described pride as:
a) a sense of superiority over rivals  b) "unsmiling concentration on the self," taking pleasure only in having more than the next person  c) joy in accomplishment  d) fear of public speaking
**A:** b.

**Q5 (SA).** Contrast self-centeredness and self-awareness.
**A:** Self-centeredness is nearsightedness that can't see past failures and limits. Self-awareness observes thoughts unattached, sees the bigger picture and treats setbacks as feedback.

**Q6 (SA).** Describe the "Me, Myself and I" exercise and its purpose.
**A:** At a social event with strangers, go the whole evening without saying anything that puts you in a favorable light, without talking about yourself except to answer direct questions, and without using "me," "myself," or "I." It exposes how hard it is to be non-self-occupied and how acceptance-driven the ego is.

**Q7 (SA).** In the two-colleagues story, what differs between them after the same bad meeting?
**A:** Only the lens. The self-centered one concludes "I'm a poor speaker, they're being nice, they'll gossip." The self-aware one notices the well-prepared did better, accepts the compliment and prepares more.

## Chapter 3: The Greatest Opponents You'll Ever Face

**Q1 (SA).** Name the three adversaries and what each does.
**A:** The Critic delivers a negative verdict and reacts emotionally. The Monkey Mind clutters and overanalyzes. The Trickster lies and accuses ("your worth depends on your results").

**Q2 (SA).** What are the Trickster's two tools, and why is it convincing?
**A:** Deception and accusation. It sounds logical because it cites real past failures.

**Q3 (SA).** What is the D-slide?
**A:** Failure going Disappointed, Devastated, Defeated, Defective (Dr. David Coppel). Many go straight from disappointed to defective.

**Q4 (MC).** The difference between judging and discerning is:
a) none  b) judging delivers a negative verdict, discerning uses wisdom to choose the best course  c) discerning is harsher  d) judging is only about other people
**A:** b.

**Q5 (T/F).** Thoughts are facts if they feel true. **A:** False. Circumstances are facts free of opinion and emotion; thoughts are opinions with emotion attached; thoughts are not facts.

**Q6 (SA).** What is the first defense against all three adversaries?
**A:** Separate circumstances from thoughts before seeking a solution (for example, two columns; nobody can argue with the circumstances column).

**Q7 (SA).** According to the chapter, when does the Critic have power and when does the Monkey Mind thrive?
**A:** The Critic has power only when you are attached to an uncontrollable goal. The Monkey Mind thrives in a heart without one central devotion.

## Chapter 4: The Daring and the Twilight (Three Pillars)

**Q1 (SA).** Name the three pillars with what each leads to.
**A:** Love: lead with your heart, brings freedom, becomes passion. Wisdom: expand your vision, brings belief, becomes purpose. Courage: be fully present, brings focus, becomes poise.

**Q2 (SA).** What are the three marks of self-mastery (mastering the ego)?
**A:** Unembarrassable, unoffendable, unirritatable.

**Q3 (SA).** Distinguish goals from dreams.
**A:** Goals are external outcomes not fully in your control (win, hit .300, become CEO). Dreams are the feelings you set goals in order to have. Live the dream daily while pursuing the goal.

**Q4 (SA).** List the Fearless Four.
**A:** (1) Give my best (100% of what I have today); (2) be present; (3) be grateful; (4) focus on my routines and only what I can control.

**Q5 (MC).** Frosty Westering's three types of "best" are:
a) first, second, third  b) *the* best, *your* best, your *best shot*  c) good, better, best  d) physical, mental, spiritual
**A:** b. Only the best shot (prepare, go out, love doing it even when it isn't good) is always available.

**Q6 (T/F).** Resonance is mainly a by-product of great results. **A:** False. It is the reverse: great results are the by-product of resonance, the effortless excitement of being fully engaged with a clear mind and unburdened heart.

**Q7 (SA).** What changed between Lewis Pugh's failed test swims and his successful North Pole swim?
**A:** His fear did not change. What changed was his connection to a purpose beyond himself (global warming, the coral reefs he'd seen) and his coach's chunking: a teammate's national flag every 100 m, thinking only about one nation at a time, never the kilometer.

## Chapter 5: Code of the Samurai

**Q1 (SA).** What is the Samurai Paradox?
**A:** The samurai learned to fight to the death by focusing on love (honor, service) more than self. They prepared to die each day: "die anew every morning."

**Q2 (SA).** Name the five steps of Beauty Before Bed.
**A:** (1) Review the day; (2) recall three small, specific beautiful moments; (3) reprogram mistakes (see yourself doing it as you wish) and let go of the day; (4) preview tomorrow's routines; (5) visualize tomorrow's feelings.

**Q3 (SA).** Contrast happiness and joy.
**A:** Happiness is temporary and attached to circumstance. Joy is deep well-being, freedom and gratitude independent of circumstance, and possible in suffering. Chasing happiness is "The Great Mistake."

**Q4 (SA).** List the eight attachments.
**A:** How others see you; money and possessions; what you want (goals); comfort; your past; how things are (status quo); expectations; your self.

**Q5 (T/F).** Victory is the safest position for a competitor. **A:** False. Victory is the most dangerous position (it tempts pride and slack). Failure is dangerous too, because of self-rejection.

**Q6 (SA).** What is the Lollipop Exchange?
**A:** You can't take a lollipop from a kid without resistance unless you give a bigger one. Trade "winning" for "fullness of life" as the highest goal, and you get the best shot at the medal as a bonus.

**Q7 (MC).** According to the chapter, all addiction is ultimately:
a) weak willpower  b) avoidance of emotional pain  c) genetic  d) a lack of discipline
**A:** b. The hardest are hidden, socially acceptable ones such as pride, needing recognition and comfort.

## Chapter 6: Change Your State, Change Your Life

**Q1 (SA).** Give the Results Chain.
**A:** Circumstance, filtered through memories and beliefs, then thoughts, feelings, actions, results (which become new circumstances).

**Q2 (SA).** What are the steps of the Reset (Reboot)?
**A:** Stop what you're doing; pick a spot above the horizon and stare, stopping all thoughts; inhale through the nose (about 4); exhale longer (about 6), relaxing jaw and face and letting go of all desires and concerns; do it twice (once is fine).

**Q3 (SA).** What is box breathing?
**A:** Inhale 4, hold 4, exhale 4 (jaw loose), hold 4.

**Q4 (MC).** "I expect... nothing" rests on which distinction?
a) Expectation is anticipation of circumstances (avoid); confidence is faith in the future (keep)  b) Expectation is good, confidence is bad  c) They are the same  d) Expectation is physical, confidence is mental
**A:** a. The longer version is "I expect nothing... I can handle anything": expect abundance, attach to nothing.

**Q5 (SA).** What are soft/sharp focus and moderate/complete relaxation?
**A:** Focus softly between plays (relaxed readiness) and sharply at key moments; relax moderately (between sets, off-days) and completely (naps, sleep). The skill is moving back and forth, practiced with peripheral vision and Open Focus (Fehmi).

**Q6 (T/F).** Anchoring works best with a visual cue. **A:** False. Smell is the strongest cue (for example peppermint oil), paired with the peak feeling at its height; a fist clench or a song also works.

**Q7 (SA).** What does "what gets your attention gets you" imply, and what research supports it?
**A:** Attention leads to thoughts, feelings, actions and results, so organize your environment. Priming research (hot coffee leading to warmer judgments and more generosity) shows subtle cues steer behavior without awareness.

## Chapter 7: The World Is Flat

**Q1 (SA).** Why does Murphy call beliefs a thermostat?
**A:** The subconscious keeps performance inside what you believe is right for you (homeostasis), dragging down over-performance and lifting under-performance.

**Q2 (SA).** How do beliefs form?
**A:** From repeated thoughts and especially feelings. An experience with strong feeling is filed by the brain to protect you, often in an instant and often for life. The stronger the negative feeling, the more likely a limiting belief.

**Q3 (SA).** What does "speak the truth about the past" mean? Give the example form.
**A:** Say everything you don't want to continue in past tense: not "I'm struggling with my putting" but "In the past I struggled with X and I'm getting better every day."

**Q4 (T/F).** You must believe an affirmation before you use it. **A:** False. You are not expected to believe them at first; that is why you affirm. Make them positive, short and present tense.

**Q5 (MC).** The One-Second Tingle asks you to:
a) visualize some aspect of your biggest dream daily until you feel excitement for at least one second  b) meditate one second a day  c) exercise one second  d) write a goal once
**A:** a. Daily beats long.

**Q6 (SA).** What is the "final piece" congruence check?
**A:** Subconscious sabotage occurs when achieving the goal conflicts with a value or threatens something you'd lose (smokers' social bond; a wealthy outcome a strict upbringing distrusts). Ask what achieving the dream will give you and whether any of it is incongruent with who you want to be.

**Q7 (SA).** How did Callista Balko prepare for Cat Osterman, and what happened?
**A:** She had struck out 9-of-9 against Osterman. In preseason she wrote affirmations, scripted the post-championship interview and visualized the exact scenario daily for months. In the game they walked a batter to face her with the World Series on the line; she felt confidence, and the game-winning hit came as rehearsed.

## Chapter 8: A Clear and Present Beauty

**Q1 (SA).** List the five ways to be fully present.
**A:** (1) Get out of your head (one devotion); (2) focus on routines and only what you can control; (3) be grateful; (4) focus on a mantra; (5) ruthlessly eliminate hurry.

**Q2 (SA).** What are the four parts of the GPS morning tool and their lengths?
**A:** 3 minutes Gratitude (small, specific, last 24-48 hours), 3 minutes Presence (eyes open, let go of desires), 3 minutes Showtime (visualize the big moment under pressure), 1 minute Funnel (outside, barefoot, arms in a Y, receive abundance with gratitude).

**Q3 (T/F).** Hurry sickness is mainly a scheduling problem. **A:** False. It is a disordered heart. Hurry is the cause of stress, not its cure, and it kills joy and compassion.

**Q4 (SA).** What rest rhythm does the chapter prescribe?
**A:** Gaps about every 90 minutes, one full day in seven, one year in seven.

**Q5 (MC).** Why is gratitude a route to presence?
a) it is polite  b) you can't be anxious and grateful at once, or judgmental and grateful  c) it makes you work harder  d) it raises expectations
**A:** b. Gratitude and entitlement are opposite poles.

**Q6 (SA).** What does "perfection is the enemy of presence" mean, and which examples support it?
**A:** Wanting control and wanting perfection are the same fear; surrendering control is how you gain it. Reid Hoffman's embarrassing first version and Lamott's messy first drafts illustrate it.

**Q7 (SA).** What is the "simple mind" check?
**A:** Define the one basic element (get the sign, see the pitch, throw it, per Ronn Svetich) and between innings ask "did I keep a simple mind at least 80% of the time?"

## Chapter 9: Unstoppable (Mental Blocks)

**Q1 (SA).** Why does Murphy say a mental block or phobia is the subconscious working perfectly?
**A:** The subconscious files emotional danger in the same folder as physical danger and protects you from the feeling it has filed as danger. The block is an anchored association between a context and that protection.

**Q2 (SA).** What are the subconscious's three jobs?
**A:** (1) Sift sensory input for physical or emotional danger; (2) run life in the background (shoes, driving); (3) line up circumstances and results with your beliefs.

**Q3 (SA).** State the four removal principles.
**A:** (1) Take the emotion out; (2) find the smallest change that would have made a difference; (3) insert a positive, high-energy feeling plus the small change into the block's context; (4) anchor that feeling to the original painful context for a new, stronger association (re-run first-person, in color, 5-7 times).

**Q4 (SA).** What is the Hollywood Helper?
**A:** A movie-theater dissociation: see a black-and-white still of yourself on screen, float up into the projection booth and watch yourself watching, run the event in black-and-white, replay at 2x, 4x, 8x, then step into the film at the end and run it backwards first-person at 2x, 4x, 8x, 16x.

**Q5 (MC).** On the pain-rating scale, the work is done when the memory rates:
a) 5  b) 3-4  c) 0-2  d) 10
**A:** c. Rate 1-10 before (look for 9s and 10s) and repeat if not near zero. No details of the memory need to be shared.

**Q6 (T/F).** The right response to a block is to work harder at the blocked skill. **A:** False. Sax threw perfectly blindfolded in an empty stadium; effort wasn't the problem. Analyzing and worrying about the block also convinces the subconscious the danger is real.

**Q7 (SA).** What is Power APP?
**A:** Anchor Peak Performance: place the painful memory on a floor spot, step in first-person, step out and shake off, build a peak state with countdown breathing (inhale counting 10 to 7, pause, exhale 6 to 1), walk to the spot only when calm, step in, turn a full circle, stay 3-4 minutes, step out.

## Chapter 10: The Hero and the Goat

**Q1 (SA).** Name the four keys to resonance.
**A:** Share your heart, not your ego; pursue mastery, not the score; love your opponent; visualize presence, not perfection.

**Q2 (SA).** Give the six elements of performance and say which matter most.
**A:** Circumstances, beliefs, thoughts, state, performance, results. The three that matter most (beliefs, thoughts, state) are all unseen.

**Q3 (SA).** What are resonance's three characteristics and three obstacles?
**A:** Characteristics: clear mind; unburdened heart; non-judgmental awareness. Obstacles: overanalysis, self-consciousness, constant judging of circumstances and results.

**Q4 (MC).** In the visualization advice, the recommended split (from Georges St-Pierre) is roughly:
a) 50/50  b) 80% what he'll do, 20% overcoming adversity  c) 20% execution, 80% adversity  d) 100% perfection
**A:** b. Also exaggerate pressure beyond anything reality can bring.

**Q5 (T/F).** Visualizing a flawless performance is the best preparation. **A:** False. Visualizing perfection sets up judgment; visualize presence and readiness for anything.

**Q6 (SA).** What is the Scene of Success?
**A:** Visualize the 20 seconds and then the 20 minutes after the performance, not the performance itself.

**Q7 (SA).** How did Stewart Cink win the 2009 Open without confidence?
**A:** He said "nah, don't got it," but he had no fear of the feeling of failing and was grateful to be there. Resonance without confidence; he won.

## Chapter 11: Three Hallmarks of Extraordinary Leaders (+ Conclusion)

**Q1 (SA).** Name the three hallmarks of extraordinary leaders.
**A:** Redefine success; connect individuals with a vision beyond themselves; seek self-mastery and help others do the same.

**Q2 (SA).** Distinguish purpose from vision.
**A:** Purpose is who you are (meaning, inspiration). Vision is a goal serving the greater good (direction, focus).

**Q3 (SA).** What does "pace, pace, pace, lead" mean, and what weights does the chapter give to rapport channels?
**A:** Match the person before leading them: posture/physiology (55%), tone (38%), words (7%). Example: walk with the angry employee, match volume, then guide them to sit.

**Q4 (SA).** Describe values elicitation.
**A:** Ask "What's most important to you about your job?", then "What does that give you?", and repeat until the answer stops changing (for example, security). Connect the group's vision to that root value.

**Q5 (MC).** The "pink elephant" lesson for coaching is:
a) praise often  b) the brain doesn't picture "don't," so say "hold on to the ball," not "don't fumble"  c) never give instructions  d) yell at one player to teach all
**A:** b. Yelling at one player is yelling at everyone (Davey Johnson).

**Q6 (T/F).** Wooden scouted opponents heavily and recruited aggressively. **A:** False. Wooden neither scouted opponents nor recruited, an example of the view that visionary companies beat themselves, not the competition.

**Q7 (SA).** Why did Michael Jordan love practice, per the chapter?
**A:** It was "pure basketball": challenged in what he was good at, no crowd, no score. Practice and games became the same opportunity for resonance.

## Final Exam (Cumulative)

**Q1 (SA).** State the book's thesis in one sentence.
**A:** Extraordinary performance and the best possible life are the same path, through the heart, and self-centeredness (the root of fear) is the chief obstacle to both.

**Q2 (SA).** Walk the chain from self-centeredness to the thought "I'm a failure."
**A:** Self-centeredness, attachment to past failures, self-consciousness, ego amplified, comparison (via PALMS), pride and fear, then doubts escalating external, personal, identity. The Trickster's D-slide (disappointed, devastated, defeated, defective) is the same slide in the mind.

**Q3 (MC).** Which pairing is correct?
a) Love, belief  b) Wisdom, belief  c) Courage, freedom  d) Love, focus
**A:** b. Love brings freedom, wisdom brings belief, courage brings focus (BFF).

**Q4 (SA).** Why "goals are not dreams," and why does it matter under pressure?
**A:** Goals are outcomes not fully in your control; dreams are the feelings you set goals to have. Under pressure you can always share something beautiful and give your best shot (feelings and process are yours), while the score is past/future and uncontrollable.

**Q5 (SA).** What is the first thing to do when frustration or a "what if..." thought shows up, and which Ch 6 tool re-centers you when you are not present?
**A:** Separate circumstances (facts) from thoughts (opinions). To re-center, use the Reset: spot above the horizon, nose inhale about 4, longer exhale about 6 with jaw loose, release all desires, twice.

**Q6 (T/F).** The subconscious that produces a mental block is malfunctioning. **A:** False. It is working perfectly to protect you from a feeling it has filed as danger, which is why the fix is to update the association (remove emotion, smallest change, anchor a stacked confidence feeling) rather than fight it.

**Q7 (SA).** What are the two mechanisms of belief change in Ch 7, and how does the Ch 8 GPS tool relate?
**A:** Speak the truth about the past (past tense for what you don't want to continue) and affirm/model/act-as-if with a daily one-second feeling of the dream. The GPS tool (3 gratitude, 3 presence, 3 showtime visualization, 1 funnel) is a morning ritual that builds presence and rehearses the pressure moment.

**Q8 (SA).** Contrast happiness and joy, and the lollipop and the candy store. How do they connect?
**A:** Happiness is circumstance-dependent; joy is independent of circumstance and survives suffering. Winning is the lollipop; fullness of life (zoe) is the store. Trading the lollipop for the store makes joy the target and also gives the best shot at the medal.

**Q9 (SA).** Which two ideas from Ch 4 and Ch 10 produce "poise," and why does Murphy say to love the opponent?
**A:** Resonance (clear mind, unburdened heart) and the Fearless Four/best-shot mindset. The opponent is a partner in the dance; their best brings out yours, and wishing them to fail creates negative energy and bad strategy.

**Q10 (SA).** Give three leadership behaviors from Ch 11 and tie one back to an earlier chapter.
**A:** Redefine success (effort, growth, for the group); pace three times then lead, and elicit root values; coach in pictures of what you want rather than "don't"; renew daily and keep one day in seven. Tie-back examples: redefining success connects to Ch 1 (lollipop vs. candy store) and the double win in Ch 4; renewal connects to Beauty Before Bed in Ch 5.
