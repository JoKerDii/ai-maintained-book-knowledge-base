# Psycho-Cybernetics Quiz Book
*The Essential Psycho-Cybernetics: The Science of Success* by Maxwell Maltz

**How to use:** Cover the answers, answer from memory (out loud or in writing), then check. Anything you miss is a section to reread. The book is an omnibus of five volumes, and the skill numbers its 24 chapters sequentially, so related chapters are grouped two to a section here (each question names the chapter it comes from). Questions are short-answer (SA), true/false (T/F), or multiple-choice (MC). A cumulative final exam is at the end.

---

## Section 1: Chapters 1-2 (Frustration, the Tomorrow Illusion, Conscience)

**Q1 (SA).** What does "Psycho-Cybernetics" mean, and where does the word come from? (Ch 1)
**A:** *Psycho* = mind; *cybernetics* comes from the Greek *kybernetes*, "helmsman." Together: steering your mind toward a productive, useful, worthwhile, predetermined goal. The point of the image is that the engine always runs; the question is who has the wheel and where the port is.

**Q2 (T/F).** In Chapter 1, frustration is mainly an event caused by circumstances, so the first fix is to change your circumstances. (Ch 1)
**A:** False. Frustration is a habit of self-relation, broken by changing your opinion of yourself. Maltz says no one can make you feel frustrated "without your consent," and he warns against waiting for conditions to change first (there are "no ifs" to happiness).

**Q3 (MC).** Which of these is NOT one of the Twelve Faces of Frustration? (Ch 1)
a) You wear a mask  b) You repeat your mistakes instead of growing through them  c) You retire from life  d) You work too few hours
**A:** d. The twelve include not being true to yourself, destructive imagination (no goal), not knowing how to relax, aiming at unhappiness, frustration as habit, not accepting weaknesses, no compassion, the mask, repeating mistakes, retiring from life, considering yourself a loser, and not accepting yourself for what you are.

**Q4 (SA).** What happened with the seventeen-year-old boy Maltz operated on in Latin America, and what does Maltz ask the reader? (Ch 1)
**A:** Maltz repaired the boy's cleft lip; the boy had shouted "I'm going to die!" on the table. Two weeks later, handed a mirror, he stared at a face he had never seen and cried "I'm going to live!" Maltz asks the reader: are you going to live? No surgeon can operate on emotional disfigurement, so "be your own plastic surgeon."

**Q5 (SA).** What is the "Tomorrow Illusion" (Mañana, Incorporated) and what is the remedy? (Ch 2)
**A:** The habit of deferring your life to a better tomorrow that never arrives; members "never get dividends." Remedy: do the constructive thing today and postpone only the destructive one ("wait till tomorrow, I'll give vent to my anger tomorrow").

**Q6 (SA).** How does Maltz reinterpret "conscience"? (Ch 2)
**A:** He rejects "conscience doth make cowards of us all" and says we make cowards of our conscience. Conscience is simply your opinion of yourself, audible after a blunder. "I can't live with my conscience" means "I can't live with myself," which is a fixable self-image problem.

## Section 2: Chapters 3-4 (Happiness, Crises, and Success)

**Q1 (SA).** What are the steps of the Happiness Habit in Ch 3, and what escape clause does Maltz build in? (Ch 3)
**A:** Decide happiness is now, not conditional; dig out buried assets; own your free eight hours and spend them on what *you* like; live in a climate of happiness; help someone; and start each morning with a small smile in the mirror and a resolution to do your best. Escape clause: "if you fail, you fail: there's no disgrace in it, and the next day, you try all over again."

**Q2 (SA).** Name the seven characteristics of the failure mechanism as listed in Ch 3, and say how they map onto F-A-I-L-U-R-E. (Ch 3)
**A:** Fear, Aggressiveness, Insecurity, Loneliness, Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness. Ch 24's acronym uses Anger and Inferiority for the second and third letters; the rest match.

**Q3 (SA).** What are the "wonderful computer" and the "decompression chamber"? (Ch 3)
**A:** The wonderful computer is Maltz's name for the goal-striving mechanism in the mid-brain (smaller than a hazelnut), which diagnoses your health, not your disease. The decompression chamber is a designated room in your mind where, under frustration, you regroup and give yourself another chance.

**Q4 (MC).** In Maltz's definition, success is best described as: (Ch 4)
a) accumulating achievements  b) a growing self-image ("a success to yourself")  c) beating competitors  d) avoiding all failure
**A:** b. "You will not be a success if you make a million dollars but leave your self-image impoverished."

**Q5 (SA).** Spell out S-U-C-C-E-S-S. (Ch 4)
**A:** Sense of direction, Understanding, Courage, Compassion, Esteem, Self-acceptance, Self-confidence.

**Q6 (SA).** What are the Four Principles of Relaxation and the four operating conditions for the servo-mechanism? (Ch 4)
**A:** Relaxation: forgive others; forgive yourself; see yourself at your best; keep up with yourself. Operating conditions: one thing, one thought, one goal at a time; live in the present; use anxiety creatively to reach the goal; if a situation defies solution, sleep *on* it, not *with* it.

**Q7 (T/F).** If you set a goal and fail one day, you have failed. (Ch 4)
**A:** False. "If you set a goal and fail one day, this is no failure at all." Reset the goal tomorrow. Maltz compares it to an athlete who cannot win every day; reaching most of your goals makes you a champion in the art of living.

## Section 3: Chapters 5-6 (Self-Respect, Understanding, Sincerity; Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness)

**Q1 (SA).** Why can't self-respect be obtained from other people's opinions, and how is it kept alive? (Ch 5)
**A:** "If you don't have self-respect, no one will give it to you." It is generated internally and spent outward; giving it to others keeps it replenishing. It is a lifelong job (a plant you keep watering), not a state you complete.

**Q2 (SA).** What specific "understanding" does Maltz say is key to dignity, and how does Goal Builder 2 practice it? (Ch 5)
**A:** That you have the right to live happily, pleasurably, and with self-respect. You walk a familiar street asking of each passerby "Does he believe he has the right to be happy?", then go to the mirror, write UNDERSTANDING, say "Do I have rights? Yes. Yes, I do," and erase the word but keep the feeling.

**Q3 (T/F).** Sincerity is just niceness and needs no particular precondition. (Ch 5)
**A:** False. Sincerity is the capacity to face reality without evasion, and it requires enough confidence in yourself to do so.

**Q4 (SA).** What is the "tell" that your self-respect is low, even when you admire others? (Ch 5)
**A:** Envy. If you downgrade yourself, you can still admire others, "but your respect will be tainted with envy," and others sense it.

**Q5 (SA).** What is the anxiety audit (Goal Builder 5), and what does Maltz predict it will show? (Ch 6)
**A:** Write down today's worries, then last week's, last month's, and a year ago's; check whether each was realistic. Maltz predicts many, "perhaps most," were unrealistic, giving you evidence of your own overreaction rate to apply to today's fears.

**Q6 (MC).** Which is NOT one of the Five Ways to Vanquish Emptiness? (Ch 6)
a) Stop sleepwalking  b) Resign from Mañana Incorporated  c) Renew your sense of direction  d) Wait for a sanction from others that you are somebody
**A:** d. The list also includes enriching your self-image and orienting outward by giving your self-respect away. Maltz says "No one else has to sanction that you are somebody."

**Q7 (SA).** When is anxiety useful and when is it dead weight? What does a goal do to uncertainty? (Ch 6)
**A:** Anxiety is creative while choosing and dead weight after the decision (a fifty-pound weight carried all day). "When you reach for a goal, you bend uncertainty to your will," turning an open-ended threat into a bounded problem.

## Section 4: Chapters 7-8 (Active Forgiveness and Opportunity; Mirror Watchers and Prison Walls)

**Q1 (SA).** What makes forgiveness "active" in Ch 7, and what must be included? (Ch 7)
**A:** It is a daily, all-out operation ("each day is D-day"), not a mumbled word followed by a catnap. Forgive others' faults, imperfections, wrongs done to you, and wrongs you only *imagine* they did; then forgive yourself for all your mistakes and flaws.

**Q2 (SA).** What is the torture-scene exercise in Goal Builder 8 meant to show? (Ch 7)
**A:** After picturing an enemy torturing a prisoner and noticing your recoil, Maltz turns it: "what about your own cruel self-torture? Is this not equally horrifying?" It makes you apply to yourself the standard you instantly apply to a stranger. Self-condemnation is torture you wouldn't tolerate from someone else.

**Q3 (T/F).** After a big loss, you should revise your self-image downward to stay realistic. (Ch 7)
**A:** False. "No matter what you lose... you must keep faith in yourself." Keep past successes ready on the "screen of your mind" and don't decide in advance that opportunity is for the other guy.

**Q4 (SA).** Describe Creative Mirror Watching and the diagnostic question it uses. (Ch 8)
**A:** Look at yourself honestly, turn away, and notice whether your face triggered a torrent of criticism; ask whether you feel that hostile when a friend walks into your home. Then close your eyes, picture a good friend's face, feel it, write FRIEND on the glass, and say "I will be a friend to myself." It looks behind the face at who you intend to become, not at flaws (prosecution) or surface (narcissism).

**Q5 (SA).** What does "stop cooking up failure" mean? (Ch 8)
**A:** Don't mentally prepare the botched interview or rejected pitch in vivid detail in advance. That is mental rehearsal in reverse and it programs the servo-mechanism with the dreaded outcome.

**Q6 (SA).** What is the point of the convicted-criminal caricature in Goal Builder 10? (Ch 8)
**A:** Overdrawing a snarling, riot-starting prisoner lets you feel how ludicrous it is to obsess over guilt and deny yourself self-respect; your real self-condemnation then looks like a role nobody cast you in. Maltz adds a fairness footnote: many inmates have constructive attitudes and deserve compassion.

## Section 5: Chapters 9-10 (Tranquility and the Active Life; Integrity, Survival, Goal Builders)

**Q1 (SA).** What does the "Daily Dozen for Tranquility" spell, and what is its stance on peace? (Ch 9)
**A:** TRANQUILITY: Tension, Resistance, Arrogant aggressiveness, Negation as a way of life, Quandary, Unbelief, Inferiority, Life of loneliness, Laissez-faire, Irritation, Trepidation, Yesterday Incorporated. Tranquility is active: you wage war on these forces; passivity leaves you helpless and tense.

**Q2 (SA).** In what order do effort and relaxation come in the tension drill? (Ch 9)
**A:** Effort first: brighten your day with something constructive, get up, fight off worries, live fully, then "after a good go at your goals, you are ready for your trip to the good land - tranquility." Chronic tension arises from trying to relax *instead of* pursuing goals.

**Q3 (MC).** In the Retirement Mechanism, "Retreat" is paired with: (Ch 9)
a) Return  b) Courage to Act  c) Responsibility  d) Friend
**A:** a. Other pairs include Eviction to Conviction, Mañana to Today, Ennui to Emancipation, No direction to Goal Orientation, and Traitor to Friend.

**Q4 (SA).** What are Goal Builders 11 and 12, and what does each have you say or write? (Ch 9)
**A:** 11, Tranquility: listen (in imagination or reality) to sounds that soothe you, then write TRANQUILITY and say "I will be tranquil today." 12, No Retirement Clock: listen to a ticking clock, write CLOCK, and say you must not use your time to retire from life or "kill" time.

**Q5 (SA).** Describe the Goal Builder program's structure and its progression. (Ch 10)
**A:** Fourteen Goal Builders, one per week, six days of application and one day of rest. Each typically has a seeing component, a mirror component (write the word in erasable pencil or lipstick), a spoken first-person statement, and an erasure. Progression: constructive thinking, constructive imaging, constructive action.

**Q6 (SA).** What is "survival with self-respect," and what is the five-part protocol (Goal Builder 14)? (Ch 10)
**A:** Surviving a crisis without downgrading your opinion of yourself. Accept the fear, nervousness and panic; accept yourself regardless of what the audience thinks; stop expecting yourself to fail; bring images of past successes to mind (they replace nervousness and cushion a real failure); then do your best.

**Q7 (T/F).** The exaggerated stadium scenario (125,000 people, 250,000 eyes) is meant to be realistic. (Ch 10)
**A:** False. It is a deliberate caricature; by rehearsing a crisis far worse than yours you return to your real problem correctly scaled. Maltz admits "this picture scares me, too."

## Section 6: Chapters 11-12 (Chief Purpose and 21 Days; The Self-Image)

**Q1 (SA).** What is the 21-day rule and why does Maltz frame it as he does? (Ch 11)
**A:** "For a person to experience a noticeable change in a mental picture, a minimum of twenty-one consecutive days must elapse." He refuses to debate the theory and hands the judgment to the reader: practice for 21 days, then judge for yourself.

**Q2 (SA).** What is "Divine Discontent"? (Ch 11)
**A:** Negative feedback you receive when you are not moving toward your ultimate goal (man is teleological). Chronic discontent is data about direction, not a character flaw.

**Q3 (SA).** Explain the two-ocean-liners image. (Ch 11)
**A:** Same engine, same sea: a crewed ship with a goal reaches port 9,999 times out of 10,000; one with no crew and no goal runs aground before clearing the harbor. The difference is the goal and the helmsman.

**Q4 (MC).** According to Ch 12, the self-image is best described as: (Ch 12)
a) a floor under your abilities  b) a ceiling on the effectiveness with which you can use your true potential  c) a fixed personality trait  d) the sum of your skills
**A:** b. Behavior, feelings and abilities are always consistent with it; performance outside the effectiveness range produces tension and a pull back.

**Q5 (T/F).** To change your self-image you must first actually improve yourself. (Ch 12)
**A:** False. It can be revised by repainting the mind-picture; a person "only puts to use the talents he already possesses."

**Q6 (SA).** When does positive thinking fail? (Ch 12)
**A:** "When it is compatible with a person's self-image, positive thinking does undoubtedly function, but when it is incompatible with the self-image, it absolutely will not work." Fix the picture first.

**Q7 (SA).** What do the circus elephant and the pike teach? (Ch 12)
**A:** Old limits can outlive their cause. The grown elephant stays at a stake it could uproot; the pike, after battering a glass partition separating it from minnows, starves surrounded by food once the glass is removed. Check whether the constraint still exists.

## Section 7: Chapters 13-14 (The Quiet Place; Goals and The Method)

**Q1 (SA).** How do you build and use the Quiet Place? (Ch 13)
**A:** Construct it once in imagination, furnished to your taste (a beach house, a cloud, a distant planet - the constraint is comfort, not realism). Two rules: completely isolated and nobody else knows where it is. Enter it at least once a day; it takes an instant, like picturing the Golden Gate Bridge.

**Q2 (SA).** What did Truman's "foxhole" show? (Ch 13)
**A:** Truman described a foxhole in his mind where he allowed nothing to bother him and retreated into it whenever anything bothered him. Maltz cites it as proof the technique scales to high-stakes work and is used mid-crisis, not only on weekends.

**Q3 (T/F).** Relaxation is a temperament some people have and others don't. (Ch 13)
**A:** False. It is learned, a skill with a training curve; and your response to events, not the events, determines whether you are calm or agitated.

**Q4 (SA).** State the Method (mental rehearsal) as Maltz specifies it. (Ch 14)
**A:** Ten minutes a day, alone, eyes closed, body still. Build one situation in vivid five-sense detail; be a participant, not an onlooker; see yourself succeed (past actions are irrelevant); "begin by knowing you have arrived"; hold the feeling. Twenty-one consecutive days.

**Q5 (MC).** Synthetic experience works because: (Ch 14)
a) the subconscious is gullible  b) the nervous system cannot distinguish a vividly imagined experience from a real one  c) affirmations are magic  d) it distracts you from failure
**A:** b. Successful affirming needs Desire, Information, and Repetition.

**Q6 (SA).** Give at least six of the nine rules for wording an affirmation. (Ch 14)
**A:** Strictly personal (I, me, my); positive; present tense; not progressive ("I am going to"); not comparative; avoid "able to" and "can"; balanced (no opposites or extremes); realistic (no absolutes or perfection); no time limits ("time limits are deadly; they create tension").

**Q7 (SA).** What does it mean that "you supply the goal; the mechanism supplies the how"? (Ch 14)
**A:** You define the objective and never specify the route; conscious micromanagement of the how is interference with the automatic guidance system.

## Section 8: Chapters 15-16 (Negative Feedback and Plan of Action; Happiness and Exercises)

**Q1 (SA).** Complete the line and give the three steps: "Negative feedback is never a ___." (Ch 15)
**A:** STOP sign. Steps: (1) be aware of it, (2) evaluate it as unwanted/unacceptable (the outcome, not yourself), (3) take measures to correct at once. Repeated, this becomes an automatic reflex.

**Q2 (SA).** What do the instrument-panel, tennis-net and golf-hazard images teach? (Ch 15)
**A:** A warning light is information, not judgment ("no one becomes upset"), and only an occasional glance at negatives is needed; attention stays on the objective. The tennis player doesn't watch the net, the golfer the water.

**Q3 (SA).** What are Maltz's three prohibitions for being effective with others, and what is the underlying diagnosis of inhibition? (Ch 15)
**A:** Avoid trying too hard to impress them; avoid doing or saying things for the effect you believe they'll have; avoid thinking about what they think of you. Inhibition is excessive negative feedback (over-monitoring), including hurt by what you *think* they think.

**Q4 (SA).** List the five parts of "Your Own Plan of Action." (Ch 15)
**A:** Crystallize your thinking; develop a written plan and a deadline; develop a real desire ("Is it really worth it to me?"); develop self-confidence from past successes; develop determination. Deadlines belong on plans, never on affirmations.

**Q5 (SA).** How does Maltz define happiness, and what is its relation to goals? (Ch 16)
**A:** Quoting Dr. John A. Schindler: "a state of mind in which our thinking is pleasant a good share of the time." It is a cultivable habit and a by-product of striving for goals, experienced in the present. Perpetual happiness is nonsense; gray days are normal.

**Q6 (SA).** What is the manipulation test? (Ch 16)
**A:** If you react automatically to outside circumstances that tell you to be angry or upset, you are being manipulated and letting outer conditions dictate how you feel. "If you learn the habit of happiness, you are the master instead of the slave."

**Q7 (MC).** Which is NOT one of the Mirror Magic six-day drills? (Ch 16)
a) Morning breathing and the question "Am I a friend to myself?"  b) Garbage disposal  c) Closing the front door on the past  d) Counting to twelve
**A:** d. (Counting to twelve is the anger protocol in Ch 22.) The traffic-light drill is also in the set: a red light stands for fear of failure holding you back, and green means move forward with the confidence of past successes. The drills attach to events that already happen daily.

## Section 9: Chapters 17-18 (Theater of the Mind and Origin Story; Relaxation and Habits)

**Q1 (SA).** What was the woman's reaction to her scar surgery, and what did Maltz discover? (Ch 17)
**A:** A woman in her mid-twenties had a deep indented scar from a car accident. After a successful removal, she said "I really don't see any improvement," and then, looking at the "before" photos, "It looks better, but I don't feel better." Her real wound was an unhappy romance that ended two years earlier, months before the accident; her self-image was poor.

**Q2 (SA).** Who are you in the theater of the mind? (Ch 17)
**A:** Producer, director, writer, actor or actress, hero or villain, the film technician in the booth, and the audience. There is no external critic in the room.

**Q3 (T/F).** Maltz says the subconscious is a second mind that judges your goals. (Ch 17)
**A:** False. It is "not a 'mind' but a goal-striving servomechanism" that is impersonal: fed success goals it works as a success mechanism, fed negative aims as a failure mechanism.

**Q4 (SA).** Why is worry "mental rehearsal running backwards"? (Ch 17)
**A:** Worry is imagination seeing what can happen to you before the event; it is the same technique with the opposite content. If you can worry, you can already visualize, so point it toward the goal.

**Q5 (SA).** What are the two roadblocks to relaxation in Ch 18? (Ch 18)
**A:** (1) Refusing to accept your limitations ("you can do only so much"; ask "what am I accomplishing at this pace?"). (2) Holding grudges against others and yourself. "Compassion and love are parents of relaxation"; you cannot have peace of mind while you hate.

**Q6 (SA).** What does the tense millionaire illustrate? (Ch 18)
**A:** A wealthy man with a mahogany dining room, two cars and staff who bolts his food, paces, lights and drops a cigar, and cannot relax; "he has even forgotten who he is." Asked who he is, he says only "Mr. X." Strain jams the mechanism, and material success does not buy relaxation. You must *see* him in your mind, not just think about him.

**Q7 (SA).** What are the three winning habits? (Ch 18)
**A:** Do the practice exercises regularly, like washing your hands; understand the importance of your mind (sustaining a single mental picture); realize the power of your self-image.

## Section 10: Chapters 19-20 (Happy Living and Emotional Scars; Masks and Accepting Weakness)

**Q1 (SA).** Name the Four Miracle Drugs for emotional scars. (Ch 19)
**A:** Accept your limitations; forget your mistakes; forgive others; see yourself at your best.

**Q2 (SA).** List the Seven Rules for Happy Living. (Ch 19)
**A:** Get the happiness habit; declare war on negative feelings; strengthen your self-image; learn how to laugh; dig out your buried treasures; help others; seek activities that make you happy.

**Q3 (T/F).** Maltz thinks any bitterness after an unfair blow is a sign of failure. (Ch 19)
**A:** False. A few days of resentment is understandable, "maybe even desirable," cleansing your system. What is forbidden is letting the scar permanently dominate your thoughts.

**Q4 (SA).** In the unfair-firing case, how does Maltz tell you to forgive the director? (Ch 19)
**A:** Not by deciding he was right, but by considering what fear or insecurity may have driven him (it took courage to hire someone inexperienced, and he may have feared responsibility for your mistakes), and because you are human and not always fair yourself. Then remove the scar and return to positive goals.

**Q5 (SA).** Why does accepting your weakness make you strong? (Ch 20)
**A:** It gives you a floor under you in a crisis: "You turn a crisis into an opportunity, not an opportunity into a crisis." Masks and the "tough guy" or "perfect woman" scripts are attempts to stand on nothing.

**Q6 (SA).** What question does Maltz ask about self-punishment, and what is the answer? (Ch 20)
**A:** "If you punish yourself for your mistakes, what do you gain?" Nothing; so be kind to yourself.

**Q7 (SA).** In the "Be Yourself" exercise, what are the three lists and what does the gap between them mean? (Ch 20)
**A:** Words you'd use to describe yourself; words you think others would use; words you'd like to describe you. The gap between 1 and 2 is the mask; the gap between 1 and 3 is working material for the self-image. You also write a biography of the person you'd like others to see, and a list of reasons it would be impossible (many are expired conditions still being obeyed).

## Section 11: Chapters 21-22 (Pressure, Individuality and Friends; Peace of Mind and Living Fully)

**Q1 (SA).** Why do some people stay calm under pressure, and what are the Bing Crosby and Whistler stories used for? (Ch 21)
**A:** "Their self-image holds up." Bing Crosby calmly picked up the dropped script and came in on cue. Whistler, discharged from West Point after "Silicon is a gas," later said "If silicon had been a gas, I would have been a general" - the point is adaptability: sometimes to succeed you must change your plans.

**Q2 (SA).** State the crisis protocol. (Ch 21)
**A:** Set your goals; believe in them; bring back your triumphs; don't be afraid to be nervous; recognize your power in a crisis. Intervene on the expectation of failure, not on the symptom.

**Q3 (SA).** List the Five Rules for Winning Friends. (Ch 21)
**A:** Be a friend to yourself; reach out to people; imagine you're the other person; accept the other fellow's individuality; try to meet others' needs (talk *to* people, not *at* them, and listen).

**Q4 (SA).** What is the daily vacation and why daily? (Ch 22)
**A:** A flight into the quiet room of your mind every day. Even with a 35-40 hour week (versus 60-80 hours in Maltz's youth) people are still tense; tension accrues daily and can't be settled in arrears by an annual vacation.

**Q5 (SA).** Describe "Delay Your Response" and its success criterion. (Ch 22)
**A:** Identify your over-reaction domains in advance (e.g., money on a tight budget). At the point where you know you can no longer check the anger, count slowly to twelve. At twelve you may still be irritated, but rage is subdued and your actions are controlled. The aim is control of action, not absence of feeling.

**Q6 (T/F).** Retiring from a job and retiring from your self-image are the same thing. (Ch 22)
**A:** False. Retiring from a job is optional; retiring from the self-image is always self-inflicted ("No police come in the middle of the night and enforce this indignity upon you; you do it to yourself"). Keep a goal every day.

**Q7 (SA).** What goes on inside the "pleasant room in your mind"? (Ch 22)
**A:** Rest, but also work: make peace with yourself, accept insecurities, re-picture cherished memories, set present goals, and imagine a future free of resentment and worry.

## Section 12: Chapters 23-24 (The Daily Dozen; Thoughts To Live By)

**Q1 (SA).** Name the first five of the Daily Dozen (12 Ways to a New Self-Image) and the twelve in order if you can. (Ch 23)
**A:** First five: Truth, Imagination, Relaxation, That Winning Feeling, Good Habits. Full list: Truth, Imagination, Relaxation, That Winning Feeling, Good Habits, The Aim of Happiness, Unmasking, Compassion, Accepting Your Weakness, Living with Your Mistakes, Being Yourself, Never Retiring.

**Q2 (SA).** How do success pictures relate to failure pictures? (Ch 23)
**A:** You don't delete failure pictures; you crowd them out by repeatedly visualizing the roles and situations you've relished, until success pictures "blot out" the failure pictures.

**Q3 (SA).** What is the practical forgiveness test in Daily Dozen item 3? (Ch 23)
**A:** Before renewing a years-old grudge, ask whether the act you condemn is one you, "in your own imperfection, might have committed."

**Q4 (SA).** What is the F-A-I-L-U-R-E acronym? (Ch 24)
**A:** Fear, Anger, Inferiority, Loneliness, Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness - a failure mechanism "made up of past defeats."

**Q5 (SA).** What does Maltz say is the only difficulty in therapeutic forgiveness, and why do we resist? (Ch 24)
**A:** Securing your willingness to cancel the debt with no reservations. We resist because we take comfort in condemnation: "As long as we condemn another, we can feel superior to him." True forgiveness sees that the debt itself was never valid.

**Q6 (SA).** What are the Five Roadblocks of Frustration, in short? (Ch 24)
**A:** (1) Worrying after the decision as well as before; (2) worrying about yesterday and tomorrow; (3) doing too many things at once; (4) wrestling problems around the clock (cure: sleep *on* it, not *with* it); (5) refusing to relax.

**Q7 (SA).** What lesson does the hurdler-turned-salesman carry, and what is the Yesterday Tomorrow Complex? (Ch 24)
**A:** A hurdle champion who became a mediocre salesman broke his leg trying to jump hurdles at a reunion; in the cast he recalled he became a champion through practice and overcoming failures, so he applied the same tenacity to selling and became top-flight within a year ("Failure teaches success"). The Complex: 60 percent of what we see deals with yesterday, 20 percent with tomorrow, leaving little for today (1,440 minutes a day).

## Cumulative Final Exam

1. **A friend has lost her job and says, "Positive thinking never works for me." Using the book, diagnose why and what to do.**
   *A:* Positive thinking incompatible with the self-image "absolutely will not work" (Ch 12). Fix the picture first through the Method (Ch 14): ten minutes a day, participant view, 21 consecutive days; affirmations that feel like lying come after.

2. **What is the single master framework of the book, and how does it explain both Maltz's surgical successes and his failures?**
   *A:* The self-image: behavior, feelings and abilities are consistent with it, a ceiling on effectiveness. Changing a face often changed the picture; the woman who said "It looks better, but I don't feel better" still held a poor self-image from an earlier broken romance (Ch 12, 17).

3. **Explain the servo-mechanism and what "you supply the goal, it supplies the how" means for how you set goals.**
   *A:* The subconscious is an impersonal, goal-striving machine: success mechanism when fed success goals, failure mechanism when fed negative aims. State the objective (present tense, positive, no time limits) and never specify the route (Ch 14, 17).

4. **Why is worry evidence that you can already use the Method?**
   *A:* Worry is mental rehearsal in reverse (Ch 17); "stop cooking up failure" (Ch 8) and point the same imagination at the goal.

5. **A colleague makes a serious error and says, "I'm just a failure." Apply negative feedback.**
   *A:* Negative feedback is never a STOP sign: be aware, evaluate the *outcome* (not the person) as unwanted, correct at once, then return attention to the objective (Ch 15); one day's failure is no failure (Ch 4); self-punishment buys nothing (Ch 20).

6. **Compare the Four Principles of Relaxation (Ch 4), the Two Roadblocks (Ch 18), and the Four Miracle Drugs (Ch 19). What do they share?**
   *A:* Forgiveness of others and self, accepting limitations, and seeing yourself at your best recur in all three; "compassion and love are parents of relaxation."

7. **Describe therapeutic forgiveness and why it is hard.**
   *A:* Cancel the debt with no reservations, seeing the debt was never valid; hard because condemnation lets us feel superior (Ch 24). Honesty check: could I have committed this act (Ch 23); a few days of bitterness are fine, permanent residence is not (Ch 19).

8. **You have stage fright before a big presentation. What does Maltz tell you to do, and what does he tell you not to do?**
   *A:* Set the goal, believe in it, bring back past triumphs, permit the nervousness, then act (Ch 10, 21). Do not fight the symptom; intervene on the expectation of failure.

9. **Compare the Quiet Place (Ch 13), the pleasant room and daily vacation (Ch 22), and the decompression chamber (Ch 3).**
   *A:* All are private mental retreats built in advance and entered often. The Quiet Place is furnished once and entered at least daily; Ch 22 stresses a daily vacation and that it is a working room too; Ch 3's is a room for regrouping under frustration.

10. **Applied task:** Pick one situation you handle badly and run the Method for 21 consecutive days (10 minutes, eyes closed, participant view, full sensory detail), mark each day off, and judge at day 21. Alongside it, pick one six-day Mirror Magic drill attached to a daily event.
