# Quiz Book: The Essential Psycho-Cybernetics (Maxwell Maltz)

> **Source note.** This quiz reflects the *skill's chapter notes* for "The Essential Psycho-Cybernetics: The Science of Success" (SKILL.md, glossary, patterns, cheatsheet and all 24 chapter files), **not the book's own wording**. Quotations are quotations *as recorded in those notes*. Where the notes are silent, the quiz is silent.

## How to use this quiz

- The notes cover an omnibus of five volumes in 24 chapter files. Because there are 24, chapters are **grouped in pairs** (12 sections). Each section has 10 questions: short answer (SA), true/false (T/F), multiple choice (MC), "explain in your own words" (OW), story/evidence recall (ST), and at least two scenario/application questions (SC).
- **Cover the answers**: answers sit in an "Answers" block after each question set. Say or write your answer first, then check.
- Score yourself: 2 points for a correct answer with the reason, 1 for the right idea with a gap, 0 otherwise. 80%+ in a section means move on; below 60% means re-read that chapter's notes.
- Finish with the **20-question cumulative final exam** at the end (answers included).
- A caution about numbering: "Goal Builder", "Book One...Five" and "Ch" numbers refer to the notes' sequential chapter files (Ch 1-24), not to each volume's original numbering.

## Section 1 - Ch 1-2: Overcoming Frustration; Winging Away and the Road to Self-Respect

**Q1 (SA).** What does "Psycho-Cybernetics" mean, where does the word come from, and what is the one-sentence definition used in these notes?

**Q2 (T/F).** "Frustration is an event that happens to you; you break it by changing your circumstances." True or false?

**Q3 (MC).** Which of these is **not** one of the Twelve Faces of Frustration?
(a) You wear a mask. (b) You retire from life. (c) You work too few hours. (d) You consider yourself a loser.

**Q4 (OW).** Explain the "consent" principle in your own words and why it is called the leverage point.

**Q5 (ST).** Tell the story of the seventeen-year-old with the cleft lip. What did he shout on the table, what did he cry two weeks later, and what question does Maltz turn on the reader?

**Q6 (ST).** What is the concentration-camp survivor image, and what is "tattooed" in the reader's case? Also: what did the woman who said the book "didn't do anything for me" do wrong?

**Q7 (SA).** Walk through Maltz's literal "operation on the face of your mind" (the mirror operation) in order. What are the "prune wrinkles"? Then tell the treasure story against himself and its point.

**Q8 (SA).** How does Maltz reinterpret "conscience," and what does he tell you to say instead of "I can't live with my conscience"? What does Mañana, Incorporated tell you to postpone and *not* postpone?

**Q9 (SC).** A project lead keeps saying, "Once the reorganization is over and things calm down, I'll finally set my goals and fix my work habits. Meanwhile I'm furious at the colleague who undermined me." Which concept applies, and what should she postpone versus do today?

**Q10 (SC).** A salesman misses a quota, blames his regional manager loudly, and then privately feels worthless. A coach asks which Maltz idea explains why scapegoating ends up hurting *him*, and what to do instead. Answer as the coach.

### Answers - Section 1

**A1.** *Psycho* = mind; *cybernetics* from the Greek *kybernetes*, "helmsman." Definition: **steering your mind toward a productive, useful, worthwhile, predetermined goal.** The ship image: the engine always runs; the question is who has the wheel and where the port is. (Ch 1, Ch 11)

**A2.** **False.** Frustration is a *habit of self-relation*, broken by changing your opinion of yourself. Maltz's example: happiness is not conditional on "Sally leaving you $30,000"; there are no "ifs" to happiness. (Ch 1)

**A3.** **(c).** The twelve faces include: not true to yourself; imagination used destructively (no goal); can't relax; aim is unhappiness; frustration as habit/self-hypnosis; not accepting weaknesses; no compassion; wearing a mask; repeating mistakes; retiring from life; considering yourself a loser; not accepting yourself (living in fantasy). Working "too few hours" is not on the list. (Ch 1)

**A4.** "No one can make you feel frustrated without your consent." Frustration requires your signature, and the same goes for unhappiness and loneliness, so the point of control is inside you. Because you grant consent, you can withhold it. (Ch 1, Ch 3)

**A5.** As an intern Maltz delivered a baby with a cleft lip; the father fainted and Maltz felt responsible, which pushed him toward plastic surgery. Later, in Latin America, a terrified seventeen-year-old shouted "I'm going to die!" on the table. Two weeks after the repair Maltz handed him a mirror; he stared at a face he had never seen and cried, "I'm going to live!" Maltz asks the reader: *Are you going to live?* Do you carry an emotional disfigurement no surgeon can operate on? Answer: "be your own plastic surgeon" with belief and compassion for yourself. (Ch 1)

**A6.** A survivor came to have the tattooed number removed from her arm to begin a new life; Maltz asks whether you have tattooed numbers on your *mental* arms that only you can remove. The woman who found the book useless had only **read** it; Psycho-Cybernetics is "positive doing," a practice, not a reading. (Ch 1)

**A7.** (1) Identify the scar: a specific blunder, hatred or bigotry. (2) Scrub in: go to a mirror alone. (3) Excise: refuse aloud to play games with yourself ("You're my friend. I like you."). (4) Close: forgive yourself for that specific item. (5) Post-op check: the "prune wrinkles" on the face of your mind are gone; Maltz says you are old at three or one hundred and three if you keep the negative feelings. **Treasure story:** a rumor said a million dollars was hidden on the eighteenth floor of his building; on a moonlit night he found his butler on the terrace in a white coat with pick and shovel hacking at the rock garden. Point: everyone digs outside for treasure buried in the self-image. (Ch 2)

**A8.** He rejects Shakespeare's "conscience doth make cowards of us all": *we make cowards of our conscience.* Conscience is your opinion of yourself, audible after a blunder. Restate "I can't live with my conscience" as "I can't live with myself," then treat it as a fixable self-image problem. Postpone **only the resentment** ("wait till tomorrow, I'll give vent to my anger tomorrow"); never postpone the goal. (Ch 2)

**A9.** **The Tomorrow Illusion / Mañana, Incorporated.** Reverse the deferral: do the constructive thing (set goals, fix habits) *today*, and postpone only the destructive feeling (the anger). Frustration is the thief of time; deferral gives negative feeling a permanent lease. (Ch 2; cheatsheet: members never get dividends)

**A10.** Scapegoating: refusing to blame yourself for a blunder and hunting for someone to hold responsible, "which ends with you attacking your own self-image" (Ch 1). Fix: restate the problem as an outcome to correct, stop chronic self-criticism first (Ch 2: once you stop criticizing yourself you stop criticizing others), and set a goal within your capabilities for tomorrow. Also run the Twelve Faces scan and name the one face you are wearing. (Ch 1-2)

## Section 2 - Ch 3-4: Happiness and Crises; On To Success!

**Q1 (T/F).** "Per Maltz, happiness is a reward that arrives once conditions improve, so you should wait for it." True or false? Give the correct view.

**Q2 (T/F).** An eighty-two-year-old attended Maltz's Monterey workshop because he was winding down for retirement. True or false?

**Q3 (MC).** In Ch 3 the "Seven Key Characteristics of Frustration" are Fear, ___, ___, Loneliness, Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness. The two blanks, versus the later F-A-I-L-U-R-E acronym, are:
(a) Aggressiveness/Insecurity in Ch 3, Anger/Inferiority in the final acronym. (b) Anger/Inferiority in Ch 3, Aggressiveness/Insecurity later. (c) Anxiety/Isolation in both. (d) Apathy/Ignorance in both.

**Q4 (SA).** What are the "wonderful computer" and the "decompression chamber"?

**Q5 (ST).** What happened with the ringing telephone at a lecture, and what principle does it illustrate?

**Q6 (SA).** Spell out S-U-C-C-E-S-S and give one clause of Maltz's gloss for each letter.

**Q7 (OW).** Explain "you cannot be a success unless you are a success to yourself," using the house-and-lawn analogy.

**Q8 (SA).** State the Four Principles of Relaxation and the four operating conditions for the servo-mechanism.

**Q9 (SC).** A product manager sets a goal to run daily, skips it one Tuesday, and concludes, "I'm a failure; this never works for me." Which Ch 4 ideas apply, and what should she do Wednesday?

**Q10 (SC).** An account executive loses one big pitch and then says: "I'm unlucky. Clients are cruel. I'll avoid pitching for a while." Using Face 9 and Face 11 from the Twelve Faces of Success, diagnose the thinking and prescribe the corrective.

### Answers - Section 2

**A1.** **False.** Happiness is a **habit** installed deliberately (like brushing teeth), not a conditional reward; "I'll be happy if X" is the trap. Install it with a fixed trigger (the mirror on waking): think of yourself kindly, smile, resolve to do your best today, accept the escape clause ("if you fail, you fail... the next day, you try all over again"), hold a "meeting with your self-image," guard your climate, and spend the free eight hours on activities *you* enjoy. (Ch 3)

**A2.** **False.** He said, "I'm eighty-two, and I'm practicing to be ninety." The story supports the anti-retirement theme: never retire from life. (Ch 4)

**A3.** **(a).** Ch 3 lists Fear, Aggressiveness, Insecurity, Loneliness, Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness; Ch 24 gives F-A-I-L-U-R-E with Anger and Inferiority. The notes treat these as the same roster; use "Anger/Inferiority" for the acronym. (Ch 3, Ch 24)

**A4.** The **wonderful computer** is the goal-striving mechanism in the mid-brain, smaller than a hazelnut and beyond any committee of scientists to build; it diagnoses your *health*, not your disease. The **decompression chamber** is a designated room in your mind where, under frustration, you regroup, reassert identity and give yourself another chance; build it before you need it. (Ch 3)

**A5.** At a lecture a telephone kept ringing and annoying Maltz; he simply took the receiver off the hook. Lesson: don't make a mountain out of a molehill; remove the small aggravation before it recruits you into a large one. (Ch 3)

**A6.** **S**ense of direction (a realistic goal within your limitations); **U**nderstanding (a mistake doesn't make you a failure; communicate with yourself first); **C**ourage (take chances, start toward another goal after a failure, act creatively); **C**ompassion (first for yourself, then others; your capacity to forgive); **E**steem (appreciate your worth; no one will give you self-respect); **S**elf-acceptance (don't imitate: "second fiddle to someone else's image"); **S**elf-confidence (calculated risks and recalled past successes; not conceit). (Ch 4)

**A7.** Success is a growing self-image, not accumulated results. You mow the lawn, paint the house and trim the shrubbery, then let weeds grow over the self-image. "You will not be a success if you make a million dollars but leave your self-image impoverished." (Ch 4)

**A8.** Relaxation: (a) forgive others, (b) forgive yourself, (c) see yourself at your best, (d) keep up with yourself (compare only with your own past). Servo-mechanism conditions: (a) one thing, one thought, one goal at a time; (b) live in the present; (c) use anxiety creatively to reach the goal; (d) if a situation defies solution, **sleep on it, not with it.** (Ch 4)

**A9.** "If you set a goal and fail one day, this is no failure at all." Reset the goal for the next day and the day after; Face 9 (live through mistakes) and the "escape clause" from Ch 3 (the next day you try all over again). Wednesday: try again, hold the same goal. An athlete cannot win every day; reaching most goals makes you "a champion in the art of living." (Ch 4, Ch 3)

**A10.** Face 9 (live through your mistakes): a blunder's only legitimate function is to stimulate you to rise above it. The pitch-loser draws four illegitimate conclusions (unlucky, people cruel, evade people, evade commitment), all of which deposit you in "a pit of despair and hatred"; note they are *conclusions about the world* from one data point about yourself. Corrective, Face 11 (consider yourself a winner): deliberately call on the confidence of past successes. The failure loop is self-sealing; the Success Cycle is the same loop with the sign flipped. (Ch 4)

## Section 3 - Ch 5-6: Self-Respect, Understanding, Sincerity; Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness

**Q1 (SA).** Why is self-respect "a lifetime job, not a state," and how does it stay replenished?

**Q2 (SA).** What specific "understanding" does Maltz mean in Goal Builder 2, and what are the steps of that exercise?

**Q3 (T/F).** "Sincerity is the capacity to be nice; confidence is optional." True or false?

**Q4 (MC).** Maltz's "tell" that your self-respect is low, visible in how you admire others, is: (a) boredom (b) envy (c) anger (d) fatigue.

**Q5 (OW).** Explain "living hopefully with anxiety" and its three-part method.

**Q6 (ST).** Describe the circling-airplane image and what it says about a life with no sense of direction.

**Q7 (SA).** List the Five Ways to Vanquish Emptiness.

**Q8 (SA).** Walk through Goal Builder 5 (Overcoming Resentment), including what the written audit is meant to prove. Then give the four tests in Goal Builder 6.

**Q9 (SC).** A VP, anxious about a rumored reorganization, reads it as proof she is not valued, and spends the weekend rehearsing grievances. Which Ch 6 concepts apply and what are the steps?

**Q10 (SC).** An employee with a "secure" job says he feels empty: lots of activity, no sense of why. A friend suggests a bigger house. Which Maltz diagnosis applies, and what is the minimal intervention?

### Answers - Section 3

**A1.** "When you feel self-respect, you still seek more, and when you find more, you keep your momentum alive and give your self-respect to others." Source it **internally and spend it outward**; sharing keeps it replenishing. It is like savings built week after week, more basic than money, and like a plant you keep watering as long as it lives. (Ch 5, Ch 10)

**A2.** The understanding that **you have the right to live happily, pleasurably and with self-respect.** Exercise: walk a familiar street; ask of each person passing, "Does he believe he has the right to be happy?"; accept that often you cannot tell; notice the clear cases at both ends (slouchers who avert their eyes vs. people who walk erect without swaggering); go home; write UNDERSTANDING on the mirror in erasable pencil or lipstick; say "Do I have rights? Yes. Yes, I do."; erase the word but keep the feeling. (Ch 5)

**A3.** **False.** Sincerity is the capacity to face reality without evasion, so it *requires* confidence ("enough belief in yourself"). Without confidence it collapses into evasion or brutality. (Ch 5)

**A4.** **(b) Envy.** If you downgrade yourself you can still admire others "but your respect will be tainted with envy," and others sense it. (Ch 5; Ch 21 repeats this for friendship)

**A5.** You don't eliminate uncertainty; you stop personalizing it. Method: (1) don't take uncertainties personally (the world is uncertain for everyone); (2) accept anxiety as creative *while choosing* and dead weight after; (3) translate theory into action, moving toward the objective with wisdom and determination in spite of uncertainty. "When you reach for a goal, you bend uncertainty to your will." (Ch 6)

**A6.** A plane leaves New York, whisks over Philadelphia, circles back, jets to Chicago, drifts toward Canada, circles back and never lands. Absurd, yet it is what a person with no sense of direction does with a life ("An airplane with no sense of direction is a horrifying thing to contemplate"). (Ch 6)

**A7.** (1) Stop sleepwalking (sleep eight hours soundly; when awake, stay awake and concentrate); (2) resign from Mañana Incorporated (members never get dividends); (3) renew your sense of direction (set goals daily, don't downgrade them); (4) enrich your self-image; (5) orient outward and give your self-respect away. (Ch 6)

**A8.** Write today's worries; then last week's; last month's; a year ago's if recalled. Check each: Was it realistic? Did it make sense? Maltz predicts many, "perhaps most," were unrealistic, and the result is evidence of your **overreaction rate** to apply to today's fears. Then mirror: write OVERCOME RESENTMENT and say you will reduce your anxieties and set constructive goals. Goal Builder 6 (NO EMPTINESS; "Time is my treasure"): write a goal and test it: Is it *your* goal? Does it mean something to you? Can you achieve it? Do you have at least a fighting chance? (Ch 6)

**A9.** She is **taking uncertainty personally** and **nursing grievances in fantasy** (the "torture of resentment in fantasy": physiological cost, no resolution). Intervene at the anxiety layer: run the written anxiety audit; spend anxiety *before* decisions; set a goal and move toward it (a goal converts an open-ended threat into a bounded problem). Resentment is downstream of unmanaged anxiety. (Ch 6)

**A10.** **Emptiness** (the E of F-A-I-L-U-R-E): "twentieth-century emptiness" is affluence with no direction, activity without purpose. The cure is directional, not emotional: set a daily goal (however small) that passes the four tests, concentrate fully on what he does, and give involvement/self-respect outward. A bigger house won't help because the empty person "lacks goals." (Ch 6; cheatsheet: write one goal, however small)

## Section 4 - Ch 7-8: Opportunity and Forgiveness; Mirror Watchers and Prison Walls

**Q1 (SA).** What makes forgiveness "active" in Maltz's treatment, and which wrongs does it include?

**Q2 (T/F).** "Maltz says mumbling 'I forgive him' wipes the slate clean." True or false?

**Q3 (OW).** Explain "each day is D-day."

**Q4 (ST).** What is the Philadelphia cab driver story and what lesson did Maltz draw?

**Q5 (SA).** Give the steps of Goal Builder 8 (Forgiveness), including the torture-scene turn and the arrow.

**Q6 (MC).** In Creative Mirror Watching, the calibrating question is: (a) "Am I as good as the other people in the room?" (b) "Do you feel this hostile when a friend walks into your home?" (c) "What would a stranger say about me?" (d) "How much do I weigh?"

**Q7 (SA).** Name the three misuses of the mirror that the notes warn about, and what "creative" means.

**Q8 (ST).** Describe the prison caricature exercise (Goal Builder 10) and its purpose. What fairness footnote does Maltz add about real inmates?

**Q9 (SC).** A founder is about to pitch and mentally rehearses the investor's cold stare, the stumbling answer and the silence after. Which Ch 8 anti-pattern is this, and why is it dangerous?

**Q10 (SC).** After losing a promotion, a manager tells herself, "Opportunity is for other people; I'm realistic," and begins avoiding stretch assignments. Which Ch 7 ideas apply and what four moves does Maltz give for moving toward opportunity?

### Answers - Section 4

**A1.** It is a **daily, all-out operation**, not a word: forgive others for their faults, imperfections, wrongs done to you, **and wrongs you only imagine they did**, then forgive yourself for lifetime mistakes and human flaws. "No half effort, no tentative stumble — a real thrust, all-out." (Ch 7)

**A2.** **False.** "Is forgiveness a magical word...abracadabra?" No. Passive forgiveness ("lie down to take a catnap") returns you to "the passive void" and half-living. (Ch 7)

**A3.** A framing device: the war against the invisible enemy (negative feelings) for the invisible goal (self-respect) using invisible, peaceful weapons (ideas, feelings, images), "bloodless, constructive, and without casualties," fought against self-depreciation, not people. (Ch 7)

**A4.** A Philadelphia cab driver told Maltz his ex-wife had phoned at 3 a.m., the first contact in twenty years since the divorce, only to say hello. Maltz pocketed his prepared notes and spoke off the cuff: what you refuse to cancel does not decay, it waits. (Ch 7)

**A5.** Mirror; draw an arrow and say "From forgiveness to self-respect" (self-respect depends on forgiving yourself). Diagnose honestly whether you cannot stop self-condemnation. Imagine a torture scene ("So, you won't talk, eh?"), notice your recoil, then turn it: "What about your own cruel self-torture? Is this not equally horrifying?" Erase the arrow, write FORGIVENESS, say "I will try to forgive myself. I will try to forgive others. We are all human and imperfect." The next step is self-respect; the sequence is one-directional. (Ch 7)

**A6.** **(b).** Step to the mirror, look in honesty, walk away and notice the critical torrent; ask whether you'd be this hostile to a friend; return, picture a specific good friend, write FRIEND, and say you will look at yourself with kind eyes. (Ch 8)

**A7.** Narcissism (admiring the surface), superficial apathy, and critical self-destruction/prosecution (cataloguing flaws; the most common). "Creative" means looking *behind* the face to "the mature, dynamic, and compassionate person you want to become." (Ch 8)

**A8.** Sit in a quiet place and imagine you are a convicted criminal playing the heavy: shaking bars, beating your plate to stir a riot, snarling, attacking the kindly warden. The overdrawn picture makes you *feel how ludicrous* it is to obsess over guilt and deny yourself self-respect. Footnote: many inmates Maltz met in penitentiaries have constructive attitudes, want another chance and "deserve our compassion. Self-respect is their right too"; the Hollywood criminal is a distortion, which is why it works as a mirror for yours. (Ch 8)

**A9.** **Cooking up failure**: pre-rehearsing the bad outcome in vivid detail is mental rehearsal running in reverse and "works just as well," programming the servo-mechanism with exactly what you dread. Catch it and rehearse the intended outcome instead. (Ch 8; Ch 14, Ch 17)

**A10.** Ch 7: opportunity is only visible to someone whose self-image hasn't ruled them out; "pre-emptive withdrawal disguised as realism" closes the door. Four moves: (1) refuse to renounce your self-image whatever happens; (2) keep past successes in imagination ready for the screen of your mind; (3) hold faith when you lose and stand up to crises with calm; (4) don't decide in advance opportunity is for the other guy. (Ch 7)

## Section 5 - Ch 9-10: Tranquility and the Active Life; Integrity, Survival, Goal Builders

**Q1 (SA).** Spell out TRANQUILITY (the Daily Dozen for Tranquility).

**Q2 (T/F).** "Tranquility is won by withdrawing from stress." True or false?

**Q3 (OW).** Explain why Maltz schedules relaxation *after* a go at your goals.

**Q4 (ST).** Retell Maltz's French Resistance analogy and its lesson about "resistance."

**Q5 (SA).** What is the Retirement Mechanism and name at least five paired alternatives. What is the note about unnamed items?

**Q6 (SA).** Describe the Goal Builder program: structure, sequence and common shape.

**Q7 (ST).** Retell the Goal Builder 14 (Survival) caricature, including the numbers.

**Q8 (SA).** What are the five steps of "survival with self-respect," and why do past successes serve a "dual purpose"?

**Q9 (SC).** A newly retired executive says, "I've earned it; I'll just coast; it's the generation gap, anyway." Which ideas apply?

**Q10 (SC).** A junior engineer is terrified before presenting to 300 people and plans to "fight the nerves" and tell himself he won't fail. Using Goal Builder 14, what does Maltz say to do instead?

### Answers - Section 5

**A1.** **T**ension, **R**esistance, **A**rrogant aggressiveness, **N**egation as a way of life, **Q**uandary, **U**nbelief, **I**nferiority, **L**ife of loneliness, **L**aissez-faire, **I**rritation, **T**repidation, **Y**esterday Incorporated. (Ch 9)

**A2.** **False.** Tranquility is *active*, won by combat against twelve named forces; passivity renders us helpless and ties us up in tension. (Ch 9)

**A3.** Relaxation is the reward on the far side of goal-directed action. People try to relax *instead of* pursuing goals, which fails because unpursued goals are themselves a tension source. Tension drill: think of something constructive, get off your back, fight off worries as expected, live in the world; "after a good go at your goals, you are ready for your trip to the good land — tranquility." (Ch 9)

**A4.** The French Resistance dynamiting a munitions train bound for the front was a good thing; but "when you sabotage your worthwhile daily goals...in resistance of your legitimate satisfactions, that is not so good." Same skill, wrong target: check which side you're sabotaging. (Ch 9)

**A5.** Ten components of withdrawal from life, each with an alternative: Retreat to **Return**, Eviction to **Conviction**, Tendency to withdraw to **Courage to Act**, then **Imaginative Power**, **Reaffirmation**, **Responsibility**, Mañana to **Today**, Ennui to **Emancipation**, No direction to **Goal Orientation**, Traitor to **Friend**. In the notes the first labels for the 4th-6th alternatives are given only as parentheticals (loss of imagination, self-negation, abdication); do not treat them as Maltz's wording. "No police come in the middle of the night"; you do it to yourself. (Ch 9, Ch 22)

**A6.** Fourteen weeks, one goal builder per week, **six days of application then one day of rest**. Sequence: Self-Respect, Understanding, Sincerity, Defeating Uncertainty, Overcoming Resentment, Getting Rid of Emptiness, Opportunity, Forgiveness, Creative Mirror Watching, Tearing Down Prison Walls, Tranquility, No Retirement Clock, Integrity, Survival. Shape: seeing component, mirror (write the word in erasable pencil or lipstick), spoken first-person sentence, erase (keep the feeling). Progression: constructive thinking to imaging to action. (Ch 10)

**A7.** You must deliver a speech; workmen enlarged the stadium to hold **125,000** if it doesn't rain; you can't recognize yourself in the mirror, forget your name, pray for rain, brush your teeth with soap and wash your face with toothpaste, check that you have a shoe on each foot, and your terror peaks at **250,000 eyes**. Maltz breaks frame: it's a caricature, "I am a veteran lecturer, and this picture scares me, too." Overshooting the simulation lets you right-size the real problem. (Ch 10)

**A8.** (1) Accept the fear, nervousness and panic; (2) accept yourself regardless of the audience; (3) stop expecting yourself to fail; (4) bring images of past successes to mind; (5) do your best. Past successes displace nervousness now and, if you do fail, "make acceptance of this failure easier." (Ch 10)

**A9.** Retiring from a job is optional; **retiring from life/self-image is self-inflicted** and a mechanism of Retreat, Eviction, Mañana, Ennui, Traitor; the generation gap is a communication failure, not a reason to retire from the young. Anti-retirement protocol: a goal every day, however small (Goal Builder 12: "I must not resort to killing time"). (Ch 9, Ch 22)

**A10.** Don't fight the nervousness; say aloud: "I will accept my fear. And my nervousness. And my panic"; accept yourself no matter what the crowd thinks; stop expecting yourself to fail; recall past successes; do your best. The intervention is on the **expectation**, not the symptom. (Ch 10, Ch 21)

## Section 6 - Ch 11-12: Psycho-Cybernetics Defined and the 21 Days; The Self-Image as Ceiling

**Q1 (SA).** What is the "21-day rule" and why is it significant to the book's claims?

**Q2 (T/F).** "In Book Three, Maltz tells you to argue the theory fully before trying it." True or false?

**Q3 (OW).** What is "Divine Discontent" and what practical reading does it give to chronic restlessness?

**Q4 (ST).** Retell the two ocean liners and explain what they isolate.

**Q5 (SA).** Define the self-image and state its relation to behavior, feelings and abilities.

**Q6 (MC).** Maltz says positive thinking that is incompatible with the self-image: (a) works slowly (b) works if repeated enough (c) absolutely will not work (d) works only at night.

**Q7 (ST).** What are the elephant and the pike stories and what do they show?

**Q8 (SA).** Explain the effectiveness range / comfort zone, including why success can feel destabilizing and why performance reverts.

**Q9 (SC).** A manager performs superbly in rehearsals and badly in front of the board, and her company sends her to another skills course. What does Ch 12 say, and what are the steps of the diagnostic?

**Q10 (SC).** A new hire says, "I tried affirmations for a month and they felt like lying; they did nothing." Which Ch 12 principle explains it, and what does the notes' decision rule say to do first?

### Answers - Section 6

**A1.** "For a person to experience a noticeable change in a mental picture, a minimum of twenty-one consecutive days must elapse." It makes the book **falsifiable**: "No amount of debate...can give you proof...if you follow them for twenty-one days, you will be able to judge the outcome for yourself." Mark off consecutive days; don't evaluate before day 21. (Ch 11)

**A2.** **False.** "Avoid debating": debate substitutes for the trial that settles the question. Also "Confusion is helpful": disorientation means the old picture is loosening. (Ch 11)

**A3.** Man is teleological, motivated toward an ultimate meaning; when not moving that way he gets negative feedback, which Maltz calls **Divine Discontent**. Chronic discontent is *data about direction*, not a character defect. (Ch 11)

**A4.** One liner, fully crewed and with a goal, reaches port **9,999 times out of 10,000**; the other, with no crew and no goal, engines running, runs aground before clearing the harbor. Same engine, same sea; the difference is entirely the goal and the helmsman. (Ch 11)

**A5.** "The overall average of the various attitudes we hold towards our capabilities in a multitude of areas." Behavior, feelings and abilities are always consistent with it; it is the **ceiling on the effectiveness** with which you can use your true potential. It is area-specific. (Ch 12)

**A6.** **(c).** "When it is compatible with a person's self-image, positive thinking does undoubtedly function, but when it is incompatible with the self-image, it absolutely will not work." Fix the self-image first. (Ch 12)

**A7.** An elephant chained as a calf to a stake it couldn't pull out stays put as an adult who could uproot a tree: the chain is gone, the picture remains. A pike separated from minnows by glass batters itself until it stops; remove the glass and it starves surrounded by food. Both show a self-image built from *former* conditions; check whether the glass is still there. (Ch 12)

**A8.** Your self-image in a given area sits on a scale surrounded by an effectiveness range; you perform effectively only inside it, and performance outside produces **tension**, which the mechanism resolves by pulling you back. ### Answers - Section 7

**A1.** Construct it in imagination (plan, paint, furnish to your taste; beach house, cloud, deep in the earth, distant planet; the constraint is that *you* feel at home). Rules: **completely isolated** from the outside world and **nobody else knows where it is**. Entry is instant, "the same instant it takes to picture the Golden Gate Bridge." **A2.** **False.** Shared, it becomes a topic; private, it stays a refuge. (Ch 13)

**A3.** Harry Truman described "a foxhole in my mind where I allow nothing to bother me," and retreated into it whenever anything bothered him, used mid-presidency, which shows the technique scales to high stakes. Marcus Aurelius: men seek retreats in the country but "it is in your power, whenever you shall choose to retire into yourself." (Ch 13)

**A4.** Your state is set by how you respond, not by events; that is why a technique like the quiet place or daily recitation can work at all. (Ch 13)

**A5.** Ten minutes daily, alone, eyes closed, body still. Choose one situation; build it in all five senses and environmental detail; be inside as participant; imagine yourself successful (past actions irrelevant); shift to the *feeling* ("Begin by knowing you have arrived"); mark the day; 21 consecutive days. Essentials: (1) strong desire; (2) immobility induced at will ("the greater energies of the mind are available only when the body is still and the door to the senses is closed"); (3) the felt experience of having achieved it, daily for 21 days. (Ch 14)

**A6.** "Not as an onlooker (objectively), but as a participant (subjectively). We actually must be there." A goal you *think of* is a possibility; a goal you *think from* is "overpoweringly real." Watching from outside gives a pleasant picture and no change. (Ch 14)

**A7.** The nervous system cannot distinguish a real experience from a vividly imagined one, so you can manufacture success experience before you have any. Affirming requires **Desire, Information, Repetition**. "Nothing succeeds like success." (Ch 14)

**A8.** **(b).** The nine rules: personal (I/me/my); positive; present tense; not progressive ("I am going to"); not comparative; avoid "able to"/"can"; balanced; realistic (no absolutes); no time limits ("Time limits are deadly; they create tension"). (Ch 14)

**A9.** Useless if first built mid-crisis: build it in calm, in detail, once. Telling her team converts it from refuge to topic. Truman used his foxhole mid-presidency, but it was built and ready. (Ch 13; patterns: trade-offs)

**A10.** The first is an **onlooker** (most common execution error): switch to participant. The second violates rules: **progressive** ("going to"), **comparative** ("better than"), and a **time limit** (by next March), all creating tension that jams the mechanism. ### Answers - Section 8

**A1.** "Negative feedback is never a STOP sign"; its concern is to alter and adjust direction, not halt it. Three steps: **be aware** of the negative; **evaluate** it (the *outcome*) as unwanted and unacceptable; **correct at once**. Repeat until it becomes an automatic reflex stored as data. (Ch 15)

**A2.** **False.** "An occasional glance at the negative indicators is all that is required"; concentrate on the positive. Nobody gets upset at a dashboard light; the upset is an optional, expensive addition. (Ch 15)

**A3.** (1) Avoid trying too hard at the conscious level to impress; (2) avoid doing or saying anything for its effect on them; (3) avoid thinking about what they think or feel about you. Inhibition = *excessive* negative feedback, including hurt by "what he thinks they think" (a loop with no external input). (Ch 15)

**A4.** (1) Crystallize your thinking; (2) written plan **and deadline**; (3) real desire ("Is it really worth it to me?"); (4) self-confidence from past successes; (5) determination. Deadlines belong on **plans**, never on affirmations. (Ch 15)

**A5.** A tennis player doesn't consciously attend the net; a golfer, the traps and water; a bowler, the channels; a pole-vaulter, the bar. They concentrate on objectives. Hazards exist but aren't where attention goes. (Ch 15)

**A6.** A self-audit across Know-how, Energy, Time, Imagination, People, Research and Development, Leadership, Intellectual Efficiency, Ambition, Responsibility, Conviction of Success; plus a life review of Social, Mental, Family Life, Spiritual, Physical, Financial. (Ch 15)

**A7.** "A state of mind in which our thinking is pleasant a good share of the time." Mechanically, contentment improves health, efficiency, brain competence, memory and sensory sharpness. It's a **by-product of goals** ("No one is really ever alive unless he is striving for a series of goals") and must be experienced now. Also: perpetual happiness is nonsense; there are gray days. (Ch 16)

**A8.** Six-day drills attached to existing events: morning breathing then mirror question "Am I a friend to myself?"; garbage disposal (emptying the wastebaskets while discarding mental garbage); closing the door (symbolically closing it on the past mistake). Traffic light: a red light is your fear of failure holding you back; expect green; then move forward "with the confidence of past successes." (Ch 16)

**A9.** Failure reading: verdict, repeated review, attention pinned to the warning light. Servo reading: (1) aware, the light is on; (2) evaluate the *outcome* as unwanted, no commentary on worth; (3) correct at once; (4) return attention to the objective; (5) repeat until automatic. "Our future...[is] not decided by constantly turning over and reviewing the past." (Ch 15)

**A10.** Excessive negative feedback / over-monitoring. Remedy: the three prohibitions, not more self-scrutiny. "Just For Today": live through the next twelve hours; equal in TIME; refuse to worry about what might happen ("I am going to spend my time making things happen"). Also mark off 21 days of the daily recitation. (Ch 15-16)

**3.** **False.** "It is not necessary actually to improve yourself"; repainting the mind-picture releases talents you already possess (Ch 12).

**4.** **(b).** Aware, evaluate, correct at once; then glance at negatives, concentrate on positives (Ch 15).

**5.** That the operative image is inside, not on, the face: "as a plastic surgeon, my 'failures' really taught me the vast importance of a person's self-image." The cheek-scar woman: "It looks better, but I don't feel better" (Ch 17).

**6.** Ten minutes daily, alone, eyes closed, body still; one situation in full sensory detail; inside it as participant; ignore the past; feel already being that person; 21 consecutive days, then judge (Ch 14).

**7.** Worry is imagination before the event; if you can worry you can visualize; only the content is wrong. Same for "cooking up failure" (Ch 8, 17).

**8.** Forgive others, forgive yourself, see yourself at your best, keep up with yourself (Ch 4). Roadblocks: refusing limitations and holding grudges (Ch 18). Easy does it: strain jams the mechanism; compassion and love are parents of relaxation.
