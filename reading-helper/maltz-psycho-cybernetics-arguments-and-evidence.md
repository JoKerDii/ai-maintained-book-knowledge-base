# The Essential Psycho-Cybernetics: Key Arguments, Stories & Evidence by Chapter
*Maxwell Maltz*

Each chapter has: **Argument** (the claim), **Stories & evidence** (the anecdotes, cases and images the book uses), **Key numbers**, and **So what** (the lesson). Built from the chapter files, glossary, patterns and cheatsheet in the `maltz-psycho-cybernetics` skill, so it reflects that summary of the book, not Maltz's own wording. Chapter numbers follow the skill (24 files covering an omnibus of five separately published volumes). Note: this is not a book of studies. Maltz's evidence is mainly clinical anecdote, vivid analogy and exercises the reader is told to test personally; he cites no experiments in these notes.

---

## The Big Arc in One Paragraph
Maltz, a plastic surgeon, noticed that fixing a face sometimes transformed a personality and sometimes changed nothing, and concluded that the operative image is inside the face, not on it. Book One (Ch 1-4) diagnoses frustration as a habit of self-relation and defines success as a growing self-image (S-U-C-C-E-S-S). Book Two (Ch 5-10) builds self-respect through forgiveness, mirror work and fourteen weekly Goal Builders. Book Three (Ch 11-16) is the systematic course: define Psycho-Cybernetics, state the self-image as a ceiling, then teach the Quiet Place, the Method (ten minutes of mental rehearsal for 21 days), negative feedback as course correction, and the happiness habit. Book Four (Ch 17-23) retells the origin story and adds the theater of the mind, relaxation, emotional scars, acceptance of weakness, pressure, friends and the Daily Dozen. Book Five (Ch 24) distills everything into two acronyms (F-A-I-L-U-R-E and S-U-C-C-E-S-S), therapeutic forgiveness and the Five Roadblocks. Material repeats across volumes by design.

## Ch 1. The Art of Overcoming Frustration
**Argument:** Frustration is a habit of self-relation, not an event, and it is broken by changing your opinion of yourself, not your circumstances. Psycho-Cybernetics means steering your mind to a productive, predetermined goal; it is a practice, not a reading ("positive doing").

**Stories & evidence**
- **The cleft-lip origin:** as an intern Maltz delivered a baby with a cleft lip; the father fainted and Maltz felt responsible, pushing him toward plastic surgery.
- **The seventeen-year-old in Latin America:** shouted "I'm going to die!" on the table; two weeks later, handed a mirror, he stared at a face he had never seen and cried "I'm going to live!" Maltz asks the reader whether they carry an emotional disfigurement and says to be your own plastic surgeon.
- **The concentration-camp survivor** who had a tattooed number removed from her arm: do you have tattooed numbers on your mental arm?
- **The woman who said the book "didn't do anything for me"** had only read it.
- **"Sally and the $30,000":** happiness is not conditional on external windfalls.
- **Twelve Faces of Frustration** (diagnostic checklist).

**Key numbers:** twelve faces; one goal a day within your capabilities.

**So what:** Run the Twelve Faces scan, name the one face you wear today, set one achievable goal, and remember frustration needs your consent.

## Ch 2. Winging Away / The Road to Self-Respect
**Argument:** Frustration is kept alive by the Tomorrow Illusion and by misreading conscience (really the self-image punishing you). Forgiveness, treated as a procedure, dissolves both.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Conscience reinterpreted:** against Shakespeare, "we make cowards of our conscience"; "I can't live with my conscience" means "I can't live with myself."
- **The mirror operation:** a surgeon's routine (cap, gown, mask, gloves, incision) applied to the face of the mind: identify the scar, scrub in at the mirror, excise ("You're my friend. I like you."), close, post-op check.
- **The treasure story against himself:** a rumor of a million dollars on the eighteenth floor of his building; Maltz found his butler on the terrace in a white coat with pick and shovel hacking at the rock garden. Everyone digs outside for treasure buried in the self-image.
- **The tennis doubles-partner image** of self-criticism ("Let's play ball together").
- **Compliment people while they are alive**, not at the funeral.

**Key numbers:** none stated; the "Mañana Incorporated" members get no dividends.

**So what:** Postpone only your resentment, never your goal. Stop criticizing yourself first (criticism of others stops as a side effect), and treat forgiveness as an operation, not a word.

## Ch 3. Happiness and Unhappiness / Winning Out Over Crises
**Argument:** Happiness is a habit you install deliberately, and a crisis is survivable because you carry a built-in servo-mechanism ("your wonderful computer") that works if you stop jamming it with worry.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The ringing telephone:** at a lecture a phone kept ringing; Maltz took the receiver off the hook. Remove the small aggravation before it recruits you into a big one.
- **The morning protocol:** think kindly of yourself on waking, smile at the mirror, resolve to do your best, accept in advance that if you fail you try again tomorrow.
- **The wonderful computer:** the goal-striving mechanism in the mid-brain, smaller than a hazelnut, which no committee of scientists could build.
- **Seven key characteristics of frustration** (Fear, Aggressiveness, Insecurity, Loneliness, Uncertainty, Resentment, Emptiness); resentment is "the gout of the mind."
- **The decompression chamber** in the mind.

**Key numbers:** the free eight hours outside work and sleep; seven characteristics.

**So what:** Attach happiness to a fixed daily trigger (the mirror on waking), protect the free eight hours for activities you like, and build the decompression chamber before you need it.

## Ch 4. On To Success!
**Argument:** Success is a growing self-image ("you cannot be a success unless you are a success to yourself"), and S-U-C-C-E-S-S is the repair checklist. Its mechanics: relax, one thing at a time, and let a Success Cycle run.

**Stories & evidence**
- **S-U-C-C-E-S-S:** Sense of direction, Understanding, Courage, Compassion, Esteem, Self-acceptance, Self-confidence.
- **Twelve Faces of Success** as the inverse of Ch 1's list.
- **The house-and-lawn analogy:** you mow the lawn and paint the house but let weeds grow over the self-image.
- **The eighty-two-year-old in Maltz's Monterey workshop:** "I'm eighty-two, and I'm practicing to be ninety."
- **The athlete who cannot win every day:** reaching most of your goals makes you a champion in the art of living.
- **The "emotional sunglasses"** worn even at the pillow.

**Key numbers:** seven ingredients, twelve faces, four principles of relaxation, four operating conditions.

**So what:** Define success internally, run the Four Principles of Relaxation (forgive others, forgive yourself, see yourself at your best, keep up with yourself), and treat one day's failure as no failure at all.

## Ch 5. Self-Respect / Understanding / Sincerity
**Argument:** Self-respect is a need, assembled from understanding (that you have rights), sincerity (facing reality without evasion), and daily reaching for the best in yourself. It cannot be imported from others' opinions.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Goal Builder 2 (Understanding):** walk a familiar street asking whether each person believes he has the right to be happy; some slouch and avert their eyes, others walk erect without swaggering; then write UNDERSTANDING on the mirror and say "Do I have rights? Yes. Yes, I do."
- **Funhouse mirrors:** harmless as amusement, crippling as self-distortion.
- **Self-respect as savings:** "even more basic than money."
- **Envy as the tell:** admiration tainted by envy.

**Key numbers:** fourteen goal builders, one per week, six days on, one off.

**So what:** Generate self-respect internally and spend it outward; run one Goal Builder at a time; read envy as a low-self-respect signal.

## Ch 6. Uncertainty / Resentment / Emptiness
**Argument:** Each enemy of self-respect is mechanically fixable: uncertainty yields to a goal, resentment to a rationally audited anxiety, emptiness to direction.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The circling airplane:** a plane leaves New York, goes over Philadelphia, circles back, heads to Chicago, drifts toward Canada, circles back and never lands. That is a life with no sense of direction.
- **The anxiety audit (Goal Builder 5):** list today's, last week's, last month's and last year's worries; Maltz predicts most were unrealistic.
- **Goal Builder 6 (Getting Rid of Emptiness):** write a goal and test it: is it yours, does it mean something to you, can you achieve it, do you have a fighting chance?
- **Emptiness as a locked airport locker** holding your integrity.
- **Five Ways to Vanquish Emptiness** (stop sleepwalking, resign from Mañana Inc., renew direction, enrich self-image, orient outward).

**Key numbers:** five ways; Yesterday Tomorrow Complex of 60% yesterday and 20% tomorrow.

**So what:** Do not personalize uncertainty, spend anxiety before the decision and not after, and give yourself at least one goal that means something to you.

## Ch 7. Opportunity / Real Forgiveness
**Argument:** Forgiveness is active, a daily all-out operation ("each day is D-day"), not a word you mumble; and opportunity is visible only to someone whose self-image hasn't already ruled them out.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The Philadelphia cab driver:** his ex-wife phoned at 3 a.m. after twenty years of silence just to say hello; Maltz put away his lecture notes and spoke off the cuff. What you refuse to cancel does not decay, it waits.
- **Half-living:** the middle-aged man who answers "I'm still alive; I get by."
- **Goal Builder 8 (Forgiveness):** an arrow on the mirror, "From forgiveness to self-respect"; the torture-scene exercise ("Is this not equally horrifying?" about your own self-condemnation).
- **Grudges as history:** Suspicion, Resentment and Greed joined forces to pin Forgiveness to the mat.

**Key numbers:** none stated.

**So what:** Forgive daily, include wrongs you only imagined, forgive yourself for specific items, and never renounce your self-image after a loss.

## Ch 8. Mirror Watchers / Tear Down Those Prison Walls
**Argument:** The mirror works only in its creative form (looking behind the face at who you intend to become); misused it becomes narcissism or prosecution. Most confining walls are self-installed.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The friend test:** compare your commentary on your reflection with your reaction when a friend enters your home; then picture a good friend's face and write FRIEND on the glass.
- **Goal Builder 10 (convicted-criminal caricature):** a snarling prisoner who shakes the bars, beats his plate with a fork and attacks the kindly warden, so overdrawn that guilt obsession looks ludicrous. Footnote: inmates Maltz lectured include many with constructive attitudes who deserve compassion.
- **The maladjusted pigeon** performing a response after its conditions have gone.
- **The woman who couldn't talk:** inhibition presents as incapacity and dissolves when the self-image changes.
- **Cooking up failure** as reverse rehearsal.

**Key numbers:** none stated.

**So what:** Look at yourself as at a friend, write then erase the word, and test each "wall" before accepting it.

## Ch 9. Tranquility / The Active Life
**Argument:** Tranquility is won actively, by combat against twelve named forces; relaxation is the reward after goal-directed effort. Retirement from life is a ten-part mechanism with constructive alternatives.

**Stories & evidence**
- **TRANQUILITY acronym:** Tension, Resistance, Arrogant aggressiveness, Negation, Quandary, Unbelief, Inferiority, Life of loneliness, Laissez-faire, Irritation, Trepidation, Yesterday Incorporated.
- **French Resistance analogy:** dynamiting a munitions train is good; sabotaging your own worthwhile goals is the same skill on the wrong target.
- **Retirement Mechanism pairs:** Retreat/Return, Eviction/Conviction, Withdraw/Courage to Act, Mañana/Today, Ennui/Emancipation, No direction/Goal Orientation, Traitor/Friend, and others.
- **Goal Builders 11 (Tranquility, soothing sounds) and 12 (No Retirement Clock, listening to a ticking clock).**

**Key numbers:** twelve forces; ten retirement components.

**So what:** Schedule relaxation after a real go at your goals, attack manufactured tension (not unavoidable tension), and keep a goal every day however small.

## Ch 10. Integrity / Survival / Goal Builders
**Argument:** Book Two closes with its payload: fourteen Goal Builders turn ideas into physical practice, and survival with self-respect means getting through a crisis without downgrading your opinion of yourself.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The program structure:** one per week, six days on, one off; sequence from Self-Respect through Survival; each has seeing, mirror, spoken and erasure parts.
- **Goal Builder 14 (Survival):** a speech to a stadium enlarged for 125,000 people, you forget your name, brush your teeth with soap, think of 250,000 eyes. Maltz: "Obviously, it's a caricature... this picture scares me, too."
- **The survival statement:** "I will accept my fear. And my nervousness. And my panic."
- **Past successes as failure insurance:** they displace nervousness now and ease acceptance of an actual failure.
- **Self-respect as a plant** you keep watering as long as it lives.

**Key numbers:** 14 goal builders; 125,000 people; 250,000 eyes.

**So what:** Serialize the practice, speak the sentence to the mirror, simulate a crisis worse than yours to right-size it, and accept the fear explicitly.

## Ch 11. Chief Purpose / Two Images / As a Man Thinks
**Argument:** Book Three is a 21-day program built on one definition (steering your mind to a predetermined goal) and one rule: you can only prove it by doing it. Chronic discontent is directional feedback.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The man at the airline counter** who says "I don't know," or "a ticket to anywhere you choose."
- **The giant earth-moving machine:** hands on the wheel or run into a ditch.
- **The two ocean liners:** a crewed ship with a goal reaches port 9,999 times out of 10,000; one with no crew and no goal runs aground before clearing the harbor.
- **Divine Discontent:** man is teleological and gets negative feedback when off heading.
- **The opening promise:** "Now! You can teach yourself how to use Psycho-Cybernetics in 21 days!" with judgment handed to the reader.
- **"Avoid debating"** and **"Confusion is helpful."**

**Key numbers:** 21 consecutive days; 9,999 of 10,000.

**So what:** Name the port before touching the wheel, commit to 21 days before judging, and run the method rather than debating it.

## Ch 12. Self-Image / Perception / The Process of Thinking
**Argument:** The self-image is the ceiling on effectiveness; behavior, feelings and abilities are always consistent with it. You can change it by repainting the picture, without first improving yourself, and positive thinking incompatible with it will not work.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The circus elephant:** chained as a calf to a stake, the grown elephant stays put though it could uproot a tree.
- **The pike and the minnows:** after battering a glass partition the pike stops trying and starves once the glass is removed.
- **"PARIS IN THE / THE SPRING":** most readers miss the doubled word, showing we see what we expect.
- **Effectiveness range:** performance outside it produces tension and a pull back.
- **Observation, Retention, Selection** as the creative-thinking sequence.

**Key numbers:** none stated.

**So what:** Work on the picture before the skill, read tension at the edge of performance as a boundary marker, and check whether the glass is still there.

## Ch 13. The Quiet Place / Relaxation
**Argument:** Relaxation is learned and is the enabling condition for everything else; the core tool is a mental retreat built once and entered daily.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Marcus Aurelius** on retreats: you can retire into yourself whenever you choose.
- **Truman's foxhole:** "a foxhole in my mind where I allow nothing to bother me."
- **The construction protocol:** a beach house on an uncharted Pacific island, a cloud, deep in the earth or a distant planet; total isolation and privacy; entry as quick as picturing the Golden Gate Bridge.
- **The daily statement** read each day for 21 days.

**Key numbers:** at least once a day; 21 days for the recitation.

**So what:** Build the place before the crisis, keep it private, and enter it daily; your response, not events, sets your state.

## Ch 14. Goals / Synthetic Experience / The Method
**Argument:** The nervous system cannot tell a vividly imagined experience from a real one, so you can manufacture success experience and reprogram the self-image. You supply the goal; the mechanism supplies the how.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The Method:** ten minutes daily, eyes closed, one situation in sensory detail, as participant, "begin by knowing you have arrived," 21 consecutive days.
- **Synthetic experience** requires Desire, Information, Repetition.
- **Nine affirmation rules** and a Descriptive Statement checklist ("Is my goal high enough? Reach out!").
- **Controlled-attention drill:** review the day in reverse as you fall asleep.
- **"Act the way you want to be (in your imagination), and you'll be the way you act."**
- **The law works impersonally, like gravity.**

**Key numbers:** 10 minutes; 21 consecutive days; nine rules.

**So what:** Think from the goal, not about it, avoid time limits on affirmations, and never micromanage the how.

## Ch 15. Negative Feedback / Plan of Action / Tired People
**Argument:** Mistakes are steering corrections, never a STOP sign. Excess attention to the negative indicator, or to what others think, is the malfunction.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Three steps:** be aware, evaluate the outcome as unwanted, correct at once.
- **The instrument panel:** "When one of the panels lights up, no one becomes upset."
- **The tennis player and the net; the golfer and the water.**
- **Three prohibitions** with others: don't try to impress, don't act for effect, don't think about what they think.
- **Plan of Action** (five parts) and **Personal Balance Sheet** (eleven headings plus six life areas).
- **Tired people:** fatigue from unresolved negative feeling and goalless effort.

**Key numbers:** three steps; five-part plan.

**So what:** Glance at negatives, concentrate on positives, correct at once, and put deadlines on written plans, not on self-image work.

## Ch 16. Happiness / Exercises and Techniques
**Argument:** Happiness is a cultivable habit of thinking, a by-product of striving for goals, and it is the way to stop being manipulated by circumstances. Book Three ends with concrete drills.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Dr. John A. Schindler's definition:** "a state of mind in which our thinking is pleasant a good share of the time."
- **Mirror Magic:** morning breathing plus "Am I a friend to myself?", emptying the wastebaskets, closing the front door on yesterday, the red traffic light as fear of failure.
- **The daily 21-day recitation** ("This is the beginning of a new day...") and **"Just For Today"** (twelve hours; "I am going to spend my time making things happen").
- **Emerson:** "Do the thing, and you will have the power."

**Key numbers:** 21 days; six-day drills; twelve hours; "mark off 21 consecutive days."

**So what:** Attach drills to events that already happen, expect gray days, and don't wait for perfect conditions.

## Ch 17. Theater of the Mind / Truth / The Power of Imagination
**Argument:** The origin story in full: correcting faces transformed some patients, but the exceptions taught Maltz that the operative image is inside. The subconscious is an impersonal servo-mechanism that becomes a success or failure mechanism depending on goals fed.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The successes:** fearful people became bold, a mean adolescent won acceptance, a listless middle-aged man felt hope rise.
- **The woman with the cheek scar:** mid-twenties, scar from a car accident; "I really don't see any improvement"; "It looks better, but I don't feel better." The real scar was an unhappy romance two years earlier.
- **The theater of the mind:** you are producer, director, writer, actor, technician and audience.
- **The method actor playing a king** and **worry as imagination running backwards.**
- **The movie queen standard** that makes young girls certain they are ugly.

**Key numbers:** none beyond the two-year romance.

**So what:** Feed the mechanism goals rather than inferiority data, and use the imagination you already use for worry.

## Ch 18. Relaxation / Why Not Be a Winner? / Habits
**Argument:** "Easy does it." Strain is the modern failure; relaxation is a precondition for success, blocked by refusing limitations and holding grudges.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The tense millionaire:** mahogany dining room, drumming the table, gobbling food "as if his hands were two shovels," lighting and dropping a cigar, ending as "Mr. X."
- **You can't push yourself into sleep.**
- **Franklin:** "He that can take rest is greater than he that can take cities."
- **Kennedy at the podium** as visible relaxation under high stakes.
- **Habits** applied to drinking, weight, smoking, overwork and dress.
- **Three winning habits** and **dead aim on the target.**

**Key numbers:** two roadblocks; three winning habits.

**So what:** Ask "what am I accomplishing at this pace?", forgive, and treat relaxation as a readout of the self-image.

## Ch 19. Seven Rules for Happy Living / Cast Off Your Emotional Scars
**Argument:** Emotional scars are deeper than physical ones and keep generating new injury until you resolve to heal them; seven rules and four miracle drugs are the remedy.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The unfair firing:** hired by a publishing company after clerical bank work, criticized by an impatient director, fired; Maltz's ruling is a few days of bitterness is fine, then forgive by considering the director's fear.
- **The wounded man** who vows to refuse anyone any favor and takes it out on his sister, the postman, the cat.
- **The ointment of kindness** applied gently.
- **Seven Rules** (including learning to laugh and helping others) and **Four Miracle Drugs.**

**Key numbers:** seven rules; four drugs; "a few days" of resentment.

**So what:** Do not hold a grudge against yourself, and heal the scar rather than waiting for others to acknowledge it.

## Ch 20. Masks / Sex and the Self-Image / Accept Your Weakness
**Argument:** Accepting weakness gives you a floor to stand on; masks and imported standards are attempts to stand on nothing, and self-punishment buys nothing.

**Stories & evidence**
- **"You turn a crisis into an opportunity, not an opportunity into a crisis."**
- **The tough guy and the perfect woman** as masks.
- **The homely boy who got the dance with the pretty girl:** a self-image that permitted him to ask.
- **"You're as beautiful as you feel."**
- **Be Yourself exercise:** three description lists, a biography, and a written list of reasons change is impossible.

**Key numbers:** three lists.

**So what:** Be kind to yourself, treat the gap between self-description and perceived image as the size of your mask, and check which "impossibilities" are expired. (The skill notes the social framing of the sex chapter is dated.)

## Ch 21. Individuality / Functioning Under Pressure / Winning Friends
**Argument:** People stay calm under pressure because their self-image holds up; friendship starts from being a friend to yourself.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Bing Crosby drops his script** ten seconds before air and comes in on cue.
- **Whistler at West Point (1854):** "Silicon is a gas"; later, "If silicon had been a gas, I would have been a general."
- **Overcoming stage fright:** the actress is helped by the crisis protocol (goal, belief, triumphs, permit nerves, go) rather than reassurance.
- **Five Rules for Winning Friends.**
- **Maltz's first patient** as a turning point.

**Key numbers:** five rules.

**So what:** Intervene on the expectation of failure, not the fear symptom; talk *to* people, not *at* them.

## Ch 22. Peace of Mind / You Can Live Fully At Any Age
**Argument:** Take a daily vacation in a pleasant mental room, delay your response to anger, and never retire from your self-image.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The pleasant room** rebuilt from a childhood retreat, a working room for accepting insecurities and setting goals.
- **Daily vs annual vacation:** workweeks fell from 60-80 hours in Maltz's youth to 35-40 and people are still tense.
- **The $180 dentist bill:** the linoleum and TV are absorbed, then the bill triggers the count to twelve.
- **Maltz's own routine past sixty:** alternating days of surgery and writing.
- **"Revitalizing cream"** for the self-image.

**Key numbers:** count to 12; $180; 60-80 versus 35-40 hours.

**So what:** Know your over-reaction domains in advance and aim for control of action, not absence of feeling.

## Ch 23. Daily Dozen / Rave Notices
**Argument:** Twelve concepts (the same as Book One's Faces of Success) are daily instruments; repetition crowds out failure pictures with success pictures.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Twelve items:** Truth, Imagination, Relaxation, That Winning Feeling, Good Habits, The Aim of Happiness, Unmasking, Compassion, Accepting Your Weakness, Living with Your Mistakes, Being Yourself, Never Retiring.
- **Quotes:** Shelley, Emerson ("Self-trust is the first secret of success"), Aristotle and Ovid on habits.
- **The grudge test:** the act may be one you would have committed.
- **Kennedy**, months dead, still vivid in the theater of the mind.
- **Rave notices:** "You've re-written the script."

**Key numbers:** twelve items.

**So what:** Run the twelve as a daily pass, stoke the winning feeling, and remember habits can be changed with work.

## Ch 24. Thoughts To Live By
**Argument:** Book Five distills the system into two acronyms, defines forgiveness as cancelling an invalid debt, and diagnoses frustration through five roadblocks.

**Stories & evidence**
- **F-A-I-L-U-R-E and S-U-C-C-E-S-S**, with Schopenhauer on compassion and a Shaw adaptation on keeping the self-image "clean and bright."
- **Therapeutic forgiveness:** "As long as we condemn another, we can feel superior to him"; the surgical standard of no halfway measures.
- **The hurdler who became a salesman:** broke his leg at a reunion, thought in the cast, returned with the tenacity that made him a champion, and became top-flight in a year.
- **Maltz's own failure:** failed organic chemistry as a Columbia premed, retook it over the summer and earned an A.
- **Casals at ninety-three:** "Every day I am reborn."
- **The Daily Requirements of Man** (a second twelve).

**Key numbers:** 60% yesterday and 20% tomorrow; 1,440 minutes in a day; five roadblocks; an A.

**So what:** Cancel the debt with no reservations, sleep on a problem rather than with it, and remember that fearing mistakes is the greatest failure.
