# Chaos Monkeys: Key Arguments, Stories & Evidence by Chapter
*Antonio García Martínez*

Each chapter has: **Argument** (the claim), **Stories & evidence** (what the book uses to support it), **Key numbers**, and **So what** (the lesson). Built from the full text of the chapter files in the `martinez-chaos-monkeys` skill (plus its glossary, patterns and cheatsheet), so it reflects that summary of the book, not the book's own wording. Chapter numbering follows the skill's 14 groupings. Figures are period data (2007-2016).

---

## The Big Arc in One Paragraph
Martínez, a Goldman quant turned ad-tech founder and Facebook product manager, argues that Wall Street and ad tech are one business, pricing human perception, in which incumbents prefer owned, inefficient markets to open ones (Ch 1). He tells a memoir in three parts: a doomed startup and the escape into Y Combinator (Ch 2-3), the founding of AdGrok, its survival of an incumbent's lawsuit, and its sale as an acqui-hire (Ch 4-7), and his years inside Facebook, where he sees the culture, the weak value of Facebook's own data, the "great folly" of Sponsored Stories, the awakening to identity joins, and the defeat of the open exchange (FBX) by the closed stack (Ch 8-13). The epilogue (Ch 14) shows that neither side of that fight saved Facebook: mobile News Feed ads did, for structural reasons nobody predicted, and success stories are narratives written afterward. Running throughout are practical playbooks: count miracles, have a leader, price per head, verify what you vouch for, measure incrementality, and vest before you leave.

## Ch 1. The Undertakers of Capitalism / The Human Attention Exchange
**Argument:** Wall Street and ad tech are the same business, putting a price on human perception, and both are being taken over by machines. Markets are run by whoever makes prices; incumbents with information asymmetry keep markets inefficient; and quants and engineers are the enablers, not the stars.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The April 13, 2012 Zuck meeting:** with the IPO imminent and revenue plateauing, three proposals; Zuck said no to Like-button browsing data and yes to retargeting ("but no to using social plugins"). Decisions made on "gut feel, the residue of whatever historical politics were in play."
- **Goldman and CDS:** Goldman declined to move CDS to exchanges because transparency only invites competition; it preferred "owning a small market than merely participating in a larger one."
- **SLK:** hundreds of traders and two programmers (2000) became two traders and twenty quants (2007), prefiguring ad tech's RTB and later Uber/Airbnb/Instacart.
- **The DUI gambit:** offering the officer a taxi under observation to reframe encounter as haggling; the root of "negotiate from weakness."
- **The ID lottery:** IDs in a $2,000 pot sold above the $200 fair value, with the premium rising as fewer remained; markets deviate predictably when payouts are large.
- **Facebook geography and Sheryl as gatekeeper:** proximity to Zuck as status; the "Supreme Court of Sheryl."
- **The throwaway RTB engine** built at Adchemy for the CEO's pitch later made him "light-years ahead" of Facebook's product team.

**Key numbers:** SLK 2000 vs. 2007 (hundreds of traders and 2 programmers vs. 2 traders and 20 quants); Goldman average pay 2005 $521K vs. grad stipend $19K (cheatsheet); IDs pot $2,000 / ten IDs = $200 fair value.

**So what:** Ask "who makes the price?" to find where power sits. Expect incumbents to resist transparency, advertiser first-party data to beat publisher data, and negotiate from weakness by reframing the encounter.

## Ch 2. Knowing How to Swim / Abandoning the Shipwreck
**Argument:** You can diagnose a doomed startup from inside, you should leave it, and you get into Y Combinator by selling the team, not the idea. Counting the miracles an idea needs tells you whether it is a business, a startup, or a bad idea.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Adchemy's four symptoms:** a purged Team page, ~$6M/month of lead-gen revenue acting as a sedative, rotating beta-customer logos, and staff who stayed despite abuse (Murthy broke the author's finger with a baseball and mocked him for months; employees still bought stock).
- **The five-miracle idea:** a bar-code-to-local-ad product; the pivot ended at "Plan J."
- **The YC application and interview:** a two-day deadline (March 1-29, 2010), four partners firing overlapping questions in ten minutes, no demo reached; the "Goldman Sachs of advertising" tagline and the Craigslist hack as answers.
- **PG's call:** "I should probably ask him first" was the wrong answer.
- **The escape from Adchemy:** harassment, retention bribes, and an ICE threat over an F-2 slip; H-1B visas as "indentured servitude."
- **The fourteen-line creed** of Silicon Valley capitalism.
- **British Trader and Zoë:** pregnant after weeks; Zoë born March 2010 during YC applications.

**Key numbers:** ~$6M/month Adchemy lead-gen revenue; five miracles for AdGrok's first plan; YC interview of ten minutes with four partners.

**So what:** Before any pitch, investment or job offer count miracles (zero = business, one = startup, two+ = pass). Treat retention pressure as confirmation to leave, and settle cofounder commitment before the offer.

## Ch 3. War Face / Like Marriage / Speed Is a Feature / D-Day
**Argument:** Inside YC, success depends on network, prestige and leadership, not on the product; Google's auction prints money while the small-business last mile is a graveyard; and attention can be manufactured through provocation and timing. Every real startup problem is a people problem.

**Stories & evidence**
- **What YC sells:** partners, the YC-founder network (rebuild ~80% of the stack from YC companies), prestige, Demo Day.
- **Google's auction:** bid x click probability; ROAS = revenue / cost - 1; "nevada cheap divorce" at 47%; "mesothelioma" at ~$90/click; Google as "the bouncer at the door."
- **The graveyard:** Clickable ($32M), Lexity ($6M), Trada ($19M) all died on the SMB last mile.
- **Equal thirds:** decision paralysis and rotating two-against-one factions; PG diagnosed the leadership gap in ten minutes; ~six of thirty batch companies had disputed leadership.
- **The New York post:** Tuesday-morning Hacker News post; #1 within minutes; Scoble tweet; the server melted; traffic jumped from ~a dozen views/day to 50,000; Business Insider and 20/20 called.
- **Rapportive:** its Prototype Day pitch dismayed him, but two years later both companies had the same acqui-hire exit ("this too shall pass").
- **Demo Day mechanics:** ~30 companies, 2.5 minutes each, no actual demos; the T-shirt arms race.
- **Chaos monkey:** Netflix's random server-killer as metaphor for Uber, Airbnb, Tinder.

**Key numbers:** Google ~$70B; 47% ROAS example; ~$90/click; $32M/$6M/$19M raised by dead SMB-tools companies; 50,000 views; 30 companies at 2.5 minutes each.

**So what:** Pick one leader with 51%+ or a real mind-meld; avoid the Mom-and-Pop market unless you partner with someone who owns it; engineer attention (and have servers ready); don't overrate peers' triumphs or your troubles.

## Ch 4. Conclave of Angels / Hill of Sand / Widening Gyre / ¡No Pasarán!
**Argument:** Seed fund-raising is about caps, dilution and finding the person with real decision-making power; and a larger, richer incumbent is not necessarily more dangerous than a small team with nothing to lose. The Valley's founding myths are "forgotten crimes."

**Stories & evidence**
- **Russ Siegelman vs. Chris Sacca:** the old-school angel who wants 2-4x and invests his own money vs. a micro-VC with a "fund of bosses" and a gold-plated name.
- **Cap math:** a $100K note at a $3M cap = 3.3% regardless of the A (vs. 1% at a $10M A and 0.1% at $100M without a cap).
- **Reading tells:** Russ's headshake at a $4M cap; Sequoia's polite "white space" pass ("a truth that is also a polite lie"); Bill Tai's "would you sell for $50M?" and the wrong smirk.
- **The suit (Sept 10, 2010):** filed by Wilson Sonsini personally against all three founders and the company, with ~$2,000 in AdGrok's bank.
- **The Death Clock:** hidden and then "cooked."
- **The Fenwick deal:** $250K fronted for a small equity slice plus options, a mispriced option ("a one-in-a-million lottery ticket to win $250,000... for $250,000"); Rodger Cole, "the Undertaker."
- **Forgotten crimes:** Gates/Kildall, Jobs/Wozniak ($350), Zuckerberg and the idea he was hired to build; Balzac's line.
- **The round:** four investors, ~$500K, at a $2M cap; cap cut from $4M after the suit.

**Key numbers:** YC 2010 caps: $3-4M middle, $6M good, ~$8M stellar (Hipmunk); AdGrok closed at $2M, ~22% sold, ~$500K; litigation ~$500K to trial, ~$1.5M total over 18 months, ~$20K/month, ~$800/hr (cheatsheet); solo lawyers at $400/hour vs. partners at $700/hour; Fenwick $250K.

**So what:** Negotiate the cap, ask "Do you have decision-making power?", groom your lead before Demo Day, fund-raise alone, and expect that the incumbent's employees are mercenaries while a cohesive, enraged small team can outlast them.

## Ch 5. Dog Shit Sandwich / Victory / Launching!
**Argument:** A tiny startup can beat a funded incumbent's lawsuit by attacking the giant's dependencies (its investors and its prospective partners) rather than the legal claims. After surviving, the harder test is launch, the Trough of Despair and the search for product-market fit.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Knife to balls, October 2010:** an Adchemy employee's drinks-fueled leak of a Bing deal; LinkedIn search for a two-hop connector; PG's threat to cut Adchemy's VCs (August, Mayfield) out of Demo Day; Sam Altman got Microsoft BD to call the litigation "problematic" ("the decapitated horse's head in Murthy's bed"); Dalal seen dressing down Murthy.
- **PG's principle:** "reward your friends and punish your enemies"; his instruction to firewall the team ("Come alone... keep them out of it").
- **Deal flow:** the 2010 shift (angel glut, micro-funds, YC, AWS) made YC the "water sluice" big VCs had to kowtow to.
- **Resolution:** Adchemy offered to dismiss in late October; settled February 2011; Adchemy ended in 2014 after $130M+ raised with a fire-sale "acquisition" and employees who lost savings.
- **The launch (March 7, 2011):** a paid PR agency, *Internet Retailer* and *Direct Marketing News*, a small sign-up wave.
- **Trough of Despair:** the YC whiteboard curve.
- **British Trader:** move-out December 2010; a second pregnancy.

**Key numbers:** Adchemy raised $130M+; dismissal late October 2010 / settled February 2011; 90%+ of surviving startups killed by the trough; launch March 7, 2011.

**So what:** Ask "who needs what from whom?"; keep existential crises out of the team's headspace; remember focus and endurance (and enemies) are the scarce inputs; and don't believe the launch spike.

## Ch 6. Dates @Twitter / Acquisition Chicken / Getting Liked / Getting Poked
**Argument:** Acquisition talks are a seduction and negotiation manual: stalk everyone, be ready to demo, run formal and clandestine channels, and create an auction before the no-shop. The acquirer's interest and your VC's incentives both distort your judgment.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Twitter courtship (March 2011):** lunch with @jess (Jessica Verrilli); the impromptu Bain demo saved by MRM's hosted demo; Kevin Weil's "Maybe you should join us... like in acquiring you"; the verbal $5M on March 29.
- **Deep Tweet:** the composite insider source (Epicenter Café) who said "create an auction."
- **Orrick, not Kinko's:** getting a first-rate corporate firm.
- **Sacca's reappearance:** silent for months, then calling at all hours once Twitter was in play; "No fucking way should you engage on this offer"; the author hid Facebook from him.
- **Facebook, March 30:** the Google bluff on Amin Zoufonoun; the parking-garage board call; interviews within 48 hours.
- **Facebook interview day (April 1):** Alon Amit, Rohit Dhawan, Jared Morgenstern, Justin Shaffer, Gokul ("What's the first thing you would change?" -> "a conversion-tracking system").
- **Ads teams as future leaders at Twitter:** Weil, Roetter, Bain, Verrilli.

**Key numbers:** Twitter's "$5 million thing" for three hires; engineers $0.5-2M each in four-year vesting equity; no-shop typically 60 days; ~400 Twitter employees; five PM archetypes.

**So what:** Preparation is stalking; phone calls mean yes and emails mean no; a bluff works when unverifiable; do not let the investor with a stake in the buyer run your process; and treat "cultural fit" with suspicion.

## Ch 7. Forking Paths / Retweets / The Dotted Line / Endgame
**Argument:** Acquirers buy people at a target price per head and do not care how founders and investors split it; founders own nothing until they vest; and the whole startup may have been "a long, stressful job interview." The optimal play would have required deceiving cofounders.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Facebook as legalized crack:** CityVille hit 100M users in a month; a Florida mother shook her baby to death over FarmVille notifications; the Cuban grandmother's dominoes now unused because his mother plays FarmVille.
- **The Whereoscope template** (Mick Johnson) for shopping a company and forcing two partial bidders into one room.
- **The split deal:** April 5, Twitter ~$10M; Facebook wanted only the CEO (Amin: "mixed" on Argyris and Matt, MRM a definite no); the lie to cofounders; the South Park reckoning; the *desengaño* (April 14); term sheets from both (April 15).
- **Sacca's last call (April 16):** "You're going to fuck up this entire deal!"
- **April 18 walkout:** Satya Patel; Twitter's callback within an hour for $5M+ for two engineers; the lawyer's "accept now, resign first"; the 3:07 Caltrain.
- **The Getaround race:** Tesla Roadster vs. a Mini to Stinson Beach.
- **Consideration math:** investors got ~$1M of ~$10M in Twitter's default term sheet.

**Key numbers:** Twitter ~$10M, then $5M+ for two engineers; investors ~$1M vs. ~$2M pro rata; Facebook offer at ~$32/share pre-IPO = ~$2.3M, about 25% AdGrok stake after split; founders' stakes 20-40% vesting; CityVille 100M users in a month.

**So what:** Think of an acqui-hire as N separate job offers; read the counterparty's hand from their tempo; resign before you sign; lie to investors and acquirers if you must but never deceive cofounders; and remember vesting governs every exit decision.

## Ch 8. Boot Camp / Product Masseur / Google Delenda Est / Leaping Headlong
**Argument:** Facebook's onboarding is conversion to a belief system, producing true believers; the PM's real job is shit umbrella and tiebreaker; and early revelations (the cheap impressions and Project Chorizo) show Facebook's data had not yet been turned into money.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Onboarding:** Cox's "New York Times of You"; Pedram's "leave your old culture behind"; Chamath's "just fucking do it"; posters on every wall; Faceversaries and Facebook "deaths."
- **Engineering boot camp:** six weeks of bug-fixing in PHP; first act: changing the Like button text to an obscenity on a dev server.
- **Zuck as old-school genius:** a Jefferson/Napoleon/Jim Jones figure whose vision converts followers ("true believers are scarier than the greedy because they can't be bought"); Chris Putnam's virus -> hired, not prosecuted.
- **Ads org 2011:** a house "out of parts selected at random from a Home Depot."
- **Lockdown:** Google Plus (June 2011, ad-free, subsidized by AdWords); the neon sign; Cato's "Carthage must be destroyed"; Google's lot empty on Sunday, Facebook's full; Google's instant counteroffers.
- **Kitten:** the hashtag decision and the two-month launch (August 1, 2011).
- **Project Chorizo:** "A mile-long trainful of meat cows... one measly sausage."

**Key numbers:** Ads 2011: ~30 engineers, 1 designer, ~a dozen products, 6 PMs; keywords = 20% of revenue; six-week boot camp; clickthrough ~0.05% (cheatsheet).

**So what:** Be the tiebreaker, layer new features on old paradigms, ship with a bias for action, build a dashboard you know to the decimal ("you make what you measure"), and measure commitment by who shows up on Sunday.

## Ch 9. One Shot, One Kill / Twice Bitten / Ads Five-Oh / Narcissism of Privacy
**Argument:** Facebook's data was hard to monetize because social talk is commercially inert; at Facebook's scale only platform-wide ideas clear the revenue bar; and the privacy debate mistakes embarrassment for value. Advertisers want purchases and browsing from outside Facebook, and Facebook buys that data rather than selling its own.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The noisy bar:** transcripts of every conversation with anonymous IDs; "Obama" in an Alabama bar preceded by an expletive; sarcasm; Tigrinya.
- **Failed niches:** political-district targeting, a Hispanic cluster, travel-intender geodata.
- **Testing on slices of humanity:** New Zealand as "Aotearoa... Facebook test set"; engineers horse-trading country-sized test populations.
- **Ireland:** the audit, 4.5M people "wouldn't even fill a Facebook test set"; **Christmas Eve 2011**: the UPDATE on live targeting tables behind ~$2B in spend, Hong Ge watching, then beer.
- **Sheryl's ops meeting:** the kitten-as-porn-placeholder demo; the author's up-and-to-the-right slide on ads reviewed vs. flat human hours.
- **The 2011 referendum:** 30% participation threshold; ~90% opposed; almost nobody voted; taken "under advisement."
- **The 49ers-jersey anecdote** explained by a game weekend and a broad campaign.
- **Scalps@Facebook** and the safety work Facebook doesn't publicize.

**Key numbers:** new ads idea must move ~5% of revenue (~$100M/year then); ~$2B in spend behind the tables; 30% threshold = ~300M people; ~90% of voters opposed; Ireland 4.5M people; Noah born September 2011; $700/month sailboat.

**So what:** Use technical depth to detect engineer bullshit; verify what you vouched for even in production; prefer unsexy, programmatic, platform-wide ideas; and treat "Facebook is showing me X" as a category error (the advertiser addressed you).

## Ch 10. Are We Savages? / O Death / Barbaric Yawn / Going Public
**Argument:** Facebook's culture is a loop of bold bets, fast failure, correction and ritualized memory; big companies decelerate founders and price offers through vesting math; and mission cultures at scale resemble revolutionary regimes with a hidden two-class wealth split.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The art experiment (Dec 2011):** unlimited spray cans; two days of vandalism; Zuck's message ("I trusted you to make art and you vandalized the place"); blue tape; "bluetape" a code-review macro. David Choe commissioned (again).
- **The move-out riot:** thirty minutes to vacate; the SPAIN plaque pried off with a rigging knife.
- **Memento mori:** Sun logos left on doors and behind the Facebook sign; the red book (*Facebook Was Not Originally Created to Be a Company*) and posters like THIS JOURNEY 1% FINISHED.
- **Track racing vs. commuting:** Facebook autobahn, Apple/Google the 101, Oracle a Delhi intersection.
- **The shuttle buses:** protesters smashing windows; Google "spies" on the wrong bus.
- **IPO tent (Feb 1, 2012):** an email from Zuck; "stay focused on our mission."
- **Haves and have-mores:** the NR250 group; 401(k) matching arrived late because "the IPO was the pension"; the People's Republic comparison with his Cuban cousins' revolution.

**Key numbers:** four-year vesting, 25% at one year, then quarterly; halve the headline, halve again for stock vs. options; $2.4M Diamond Heights listings browsed during the speech.

**So what:** Build blue-tape loops (permission to experiment, ruthless pruning, an artifact that encodes the lesson); keep a memento mori; value acqui-hire offers at about a quarter of the headline; and read "we don't talk about money" as the tell of a two-class company.

## Ch 11. Flying Saucers / Monetizing the Tumor
**Argument:** Facebook's pre-IPO monetization plan (Open Graph feeding Sponsored Stories) was a two-miracle idea sustained by cognitive dissonance until Sheryl said brands would not keep paying for Likes. The rush for revenue then collided with Growth, revealing that engagement and money are anticorrelated.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Festinger's 1954 flying-saucer cult:** the cult grew stronger when the prophecy failed.
- **The two revenue streams c. 2010:** brands (Starbucks, Burberry) buying page Likes; direct-response advertisers barely present except game developers (energy-drink Likes predicting mob-game conversions).
- **Social context lift:** 40-60% on a ~0.05% base vs. >=0.1% for worst display and ~1% for well-targeted ads: "piddly-boo."
- **Gavage launching** and partners reporting no difference.
- **Fog:** a VP's magnum of champagne with a Post-it targeting 10% of revenue from Sponsored Stories by end-2011; never approached.
- **FMC (Feb 2012):** Timeline Hallway; Paul Adams's *Grouped* thesis contradicted by Facebook research; Alicia Keys under the blue whale.
- **Growth team:** retargeting on exchanges before Ads knew they existed; Risk-board map of countries yet to "tip."
- **LOX:** the logout page for a few hundred thousand dollars; run only in the US, not Brazil.
- **Running out of humans:** 1.8B addressable at 1.44B (Q1 2015).

**Key numbers:** ~$10M per brand Likes deal; 40-60% lift on a 20x deficit; 10% revenue target; ~3B internet users minus ~600M China, minus 25% = ~1.8B; 1.44B at ~13% growth.

**So what:** Count the miracles in your monetization plan; measure lift against the best alternative; listen when partners say it isn't working; and remember you can optimize for engagement or revenue in a given moment, not both.

## Ch 12. Great Awakening / Barbarians at the Gates / IPA > IPO / IPO Reevaluation / Flash Boys
**Argument:** Advertising is name-calling, and the real war among platforms is over who controls the joins between names (postal address, device ID, cookie). Facebook's 2012 awakening produced Custom Audiences and FBX, and the supposedly "flat" IPO was the best in tech history because it screwed the bankers, not the employees.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Data brokers as the real Big Brother:** Acxiom, Experian, Epsilon, Merkle, Neustar; Nielsen's PRIZM segments.
- **Direct mail economics:** ~$17B postage, $50B+ industry, ~$1,000 CPMs vs. ~$1 online.
- **On-boarding:** Datalogix, Neustar, LiveRamp; pixels on newsletters and dating sites; hashed joins as "privacy theater."
- **How Facebook learned:** "mooched" from Turn, MediaMath, BlueKai, Acxiom, Epsilon; Experian sent a junior team with a box of cookies to a meeting about cookies.
- **Selling FBX internally (March 13, 2012):** Zach Coelius of Triggit as the outside "voice of the market" for an hour; a written case within days.
- **The build:** five weeks, three engineers, a parallel ads system; first bid June 15, 2012 via TellApart.
- **Flash Boys:** 120 ms bid window; NC-Amsterdam ~23 ms one way at light speed, ~60 ms in practice.
- **The unforgivable sin:** outside bidders beat Facebook's data in its own auction.
- **IPO night:** a mandated hackathon, "IPO IPA," single malt at 5 a.m., Zuck: ring the bell, then get back to work.
- **Pay:** 75,000 shares over four years, $175K salary, 5,000 shares cashed up front.

**Key numbers:** match rates up to ~90%; $100M promised 2012 revenue; open ~$42, close ~$38.37, ~1% fees vs. up to 7%; package ~$971K/year at the IPO price, ~$550K after tax.

**So what:** Think of every channel as an addressing scheme and every platform war as a fight over the join table; read any IPO with "who gets the pop?"; ship parallel systems fast with tiny teams; and expect an incumbent to protect asymmetry when outside data beats its own.

## Ch 13. Full Frontal Facebook / Microsoft Shrugged / Ad Majorem / Adiós, Facebook
**Argument:** Facebook chose the closed stack (Custom Audiences plus the Atlas ad server) over the open exchange (FBX), repeating Goldman's choice of monopoly and information asymmetry over innovation. Incrementality is the honest measure of a new ads product, and the author chose "to do something" over "to be someone."

**Stories & evidence**
- **Great powers:** no partners, only rivals and temporary allies; Amazon's A9 engineers "got it, instantly."
- **Google Plus's inflated usage:** 400M registered / 100M active by counting anyone who clicked a Plus button; Larry Page's ultimatum; Vic Gundotra as the internal Emmanuel Goldstein.
- **The Omnicom meeting (Jan 2013):** the suited retargeting executive and Gokul's non-answer.
- **"The Spartans lost at Thermopylae":** a manager answers a resource plea with a movie reference.
- **LiveBoz (April 4) and April 12:** Boz's recommendation; Sheryl: "that's what we'll do"; Gokul fled the room.
- **Atlas:** Microsoft's aQuantive write-down; "it's not what you pay, it's what they cost you."
- **Ad servers:** Google counts clicks, Facebook needs viewthroughs; IAB/MMA containers vs. native formats.
- **Court politics and negative selection** of middle managers; the review "You're a very divisive figure."
- **Exit (April 15-16):** vacation over vesting day, desk cleared, disk wiped, HR with a $30,000-for-silence document; Goodfellas "getting made."

**Key numbers:** April 12, 2013 decision; Atlas ~$0.1B of Microsoft's $6.2B aQuantive purchase after a $6.1B write-down; Atlas ~20% share; CA dollars discounted by half; $30,000 for a year of silence; Google Plus 400M/100M.

**So what:** Ask "is this dollar incremental?" before crediting any dashboard; treat the ad server (source of truth) as the strategic asset; expect middle management to side with power; and sequence your exit around the vest while assuming the company does the same.

## Ch 14. Pandemonium Lost / Epilogue
**Argument:** Neither FBX nor Custom Audiences saved Facebook; mobile News Feed ads did, for structural reasons (data scarcity and weak inventory on mobile) nobody predicted. Success stories are post-hoc narratives ("ten dice, one winner"), and compensation form, tax timing and lockouts, not talent, decided who got rich.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Plantar fascia rupture and the MoPub essay:** the Medium post circulated by the deal's principals, reopening a door; giving Twitter the full whiteboard ("if in doubt, show more"); the advisory offer (a thousand shares a quarter, ~$160K/year).
- **A grudge job:** advising Twitter while employed by Nanigans, Facebook's largest ads partner.
- **Why mobile won:** browsers block third-party cookies, apps silo data, Apple polices device IDs; mobile's savvy monetizers charge users directly, leaving exchanges with tiny bars, interstitials and Grindr; Audience Network proved "the one-eyed Facebook man was king."
- **Land rush:** the Louisiana Purchase analogy; revenue kept growing after saturation.
- **Ten dice:** seven fail, two do OK, one becomes transformational; the press writes the narrative fallacy.
- **Buying your way out:** Instagram and WhatsApp.
- **FBX's fate:** a "perpetual motion machine of cash," Facebook's periscope into programmatic competitors; Goldman moved CDS clearing to ICE in 2013.
- **Google's Punic defeat:** Gundotra's April 2014 exit.
- **Compensation and karma:** AdGrok engineers rich at Twitter; the author taxed at $38 on stock sold near $20.
- **The boat:** after his mother's death (summer 2014), he outfitted *Ayala*, a forty-foot cutter.

**Key numbers:** MoPub ~$350M; FBX ~$500M by early 2014 (all incremental); Instagram $1B (2012), WhatsApp $19B (2014); Q2 2013 mobile actives up 50%+, mobile revenue roughly doubled; Twitter post-lockout price above $50; Facebook restricted stock taxed at $38 basis, lockout expiring near $20.

**So what:** Being right about the technology is not the same as being right about what saves the quarter; market structure (who can see what data) decides a dataset's value; discount any founder's retrospective genius; and "man plans and God laughs."
