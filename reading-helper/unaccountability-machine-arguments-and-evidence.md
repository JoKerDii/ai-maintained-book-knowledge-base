# The Unaccountability Machine: Key Arguments, Stories & Evidence by Chapter
*Dan Davies*

Each chapter has: **Argument** (the claim), **Stories & evidence** (what the book uses to support it), **Key numbers**, and **So what** (the lesson). Built from the full text of the chapter files (plus glossary, patterns and cheatsheet) in the `unaccountability-machine` skill, so it reflects that summary of the book, not the book's own wording. The skill's notes carry few statistics, so "Key numbers" sections are short by nature.

---

## The Big Arc in One Paragraph
Davies argues that over the last century the developed world industrialised decision-making, so that important institutions run on processes and standardised information rather than on individuals reacting to circumstances, and accountability (the link between deciders and the decided-upon) atrophied. Ch 1 names the mechanism, the accountability sink; Ch 2-4 supply the toolkit from Stafford Beer and Ross Ashby (black boxes, POSIWID, requisite variety, variety engineering) and treat organisations as non-human decision-making systems. Ch 5 builds the Viable System Model (five functions) as a diagnostic, with the Decerebrate Cats intermission showing what happens when the higher functions are cut. Ch 6-7 turn the lens on economics (the Ricardian Vice, markets as computing fabric) and accounting (business hallucination), with the Chile intermission showing the road not taken. Ch 8 explains how the Friedman doctrine, debt and the leveraged buyout gave corporations a single maximising goal; Ch 9 traces the consequences (reversed polarity, lost translation capacity, the one-bit scream of populism); Ch 10 prescribes viability over maximisation, guaranteeing the debts of controlled companies, red handles for the decided-upon, and acceptance that robots will make decisions while feedback is repaired.

## Introduction
**Argument:** Over the last century the developed world industrialised decision-making: important institutions are run by processes and systems operating on standardised information, not by individuals reacting to circumstances. The result is the atrophy of accountability. To understand it, treat outcomes as the output of decision-making systems rather than conspiracy or cock-up, and read history through three revolutions (managerial, cybernetic, neoliberal).

**Stories & evidence**
- **Dugdale vs Truman vs Trump:** Sir Thomas Dugdale resigned in 1954 over the Crichel Down affair (a fairly trivial malpractice concerning requisitioned Dorset farmland), though it was never clear he was personally involved. Truman kept "The Buck Stops Here" on his desk in 1953. Sixty-six years later, asked who was responsible for a government shutdown, Trump said "the buck stops with everybody."
- **The three revolutions:** the managerial revolution happened and was forgotten; the cybernetic revolution looked promising but never arrived; the neoliberal revolution of the early 1970s succeeded.
- **Cybernetics' brain drain:** the founders discovered information representation, which proved commercially decisive for electronic circuits, so the talent went to the computer industry.
- **Chile 1973:** the neoliberal/cybernetic debate did happen, "with helicopters and guns at La Moneda."

**Key numbers:** Dugdale 1954; Truman 1953; 66 years between Truman's desk sign and Trump's answer; the neoliberal revolution in the early 1970s; 11 September 1973.

**So what:** Ask where a decision went, not who erred. Read a system's consistent output as its purpose. Look for the rule book behind the algorithm. Cover the three revolutions before reaching for character flaws.

## Ch 1. 'Something's Up'
**Argument:** The twenty-first-century polycrisis is at root a crisis of accountability. Organisations have built accountability sinks (a powerless human interface plus an unappealable policy) that absorb negative feedback; the resulting inability to learn from unanticipated situations guarantees periodic catastrophic failure.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Schiphol squirrels (April 1999):** 440 ground squirrels from Beijing landed on a KLM flight without import papers; they could not go on to Athens or be sent back, and were put through a shredder built for worthless male chicks. The machine had earlier taken 200 more squirrels, dozens of turtles and a flock of parakeets. A sensible-looking ministry policy, a delegating airline, a low baseline of destruction accepted, no feedback channel, and the pet-squirrel craze as the unanticipated input. Staff were "formally correct"; the alleged fault was an "assessment mistake," i.e. failing to disobey.
- **The common law as a good sink:** you can't argue with a judge, but precedent is weighted by judges' seniority and reputation and promotion reflects decisions made over time. Fundamentalism and strict constructionism try to remove that channel.
- **Bill Black's control fraud**, extended by Davies to self-organising control fraud in banks (unrealistic targets plus underinvestment in compliance).
- **Peter Dale Scott's parapolitics / the deep state** (Turkish shorthand originally) as the part of government where accountability is intentionally diminished.
- **Placeholder numbers:** the 3 per cent deficit rule and 2 per cent inflation target as examples of numbers with no analysis behind them becoming fundamental constraints; junior officials' round numbers becoming continental policy.
- **Kings vs priests:** a king's orders can be argued with; a priest interprets the Word. Discontent grows when king-shaped structures are run by priest-like managers.
- **The IMF as scapegoat:** clever in a journal, indefensible to a hungry crowd.

**Key numbers:** 440 squirrels (plus 200 earlier); 99 per cent obedience vs the hundredth case; 3 per cent deficit rule; 2 per cent inflation target.

**So what:** Ask what the frontline person can change. Write down the assumptions a policy encodes. Build an exceptions route and check it is used. Keep a slow reputational feedback channel in any sink you must retain. Expect crises on a schedule (a Minsky-shaped dynamic). Recognise legitimate reasons for sinks (liability shields, underwriting, avoiding corrosive interpersonal conflict) before removing them; naive de-sinking brings back caprice and unfairness.

## Ch 2. Stafford Beer
**Argument:** Beer built management cybernetics on one move: define a complex system by its behaviour, draw a box around it, and refuse to open it. From that follow the black box axiom, homeostasis, POSIWID, and a management theory based on neither control nor delegation but on accountability via the resource bargain.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Beer's 1947 army unit:** an army psychologist with no medical qualification was handed illiterate soldiers diagnosed with "psychopathological personalities." They couldn't write home or handle 4 + 3, yet scored darts aloud and computed accumulator bets instantly. He chose variables, plotted trends, refused to assume, and followed feedback, producing a "crash course" method that raised the educational age of troops. His superiors told him it was called operational research.
- **Pre-Partition India:** he had earlier tracked shifting allegiances the same way.
- **The platoon told to take a bridge:** the model of a resource bargain, with two expected messages.
- **Homeostat and feedback examples:** ship's gyroscopic stabiliser (stabilising feedback) vs Hendrix amplifier (destabilising feedback).
- **Soviet planning:** adding up different grades of wire, switches and fastenings got the system into serious trouble.
- **Hayek on prices:** prices derive from transactions through which knowledge percolates; the central planner's dilemma.
- **Beer's Sigmoots:** informal cross-level meetings where anyone could bang a shoe on the table.
- **Applied backwards to Ch 1:** the purpose of academic publishing is to generate citations for academics and profits for publishers; the purpose of the Schiphol pet hotel was to shred squirrels.

**Key numbers:** 1947; the soldiers could not handle 4 + 3 yet scored darts aloud ("21 — that's 15 and a double 3 to go").

**So what:** Treat complex systems as black boxes and study behaviour. Judge organisations by what they consistently produce, separately from intentions. When change stalls, look for homeostatic forces. Structure autonomy as a resource bargain. Expect the consultant's paradox: a good diagnosis says people's own organisation prevents them achieving their goals, which triggers self-defence.

## Ch 3. Aliens Among Us
**Argument:** Treat corporations and large organisations as non-human decision-making systems whose outputs must be attributed to the system, not to any individual. Individual accountability shrinks not because managers are sneaky but because complexity forces everyone, insiders included, to treat the organisation as a black box. "Systems don't make mistakes and don't have intentions," so conspiracy-versus-cock-up is the wrong question.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Fox News and the voting-machine defamation case:** internal emails show individuals reacting to audience figures, share price and angry viewers, each assuming someone else would stop anything catastrophic. Yet the audience didn't arrive believing in stolen elections by chance: local decisions to stir up rage accumulated into a system-wide commitment. A real decision that nobody made.
- **Boeing and Mark Forkner:** charging the one pilot with legible paperwork while corporate intent goes unproven.
- **Ashby's four-part example:** four mutually connected parts have twenty circuits; knowing all twenty still doesn't describe the whole.
- **Searle's Chinese room**, read as an argument about accountability; **Bostrom's paperclip maximiser** as the model for firms that prioritise financial results over extinction-level harm.
- **Overconfident partial expertise:** financiers' "it's different this time"; the State Department underperforming a parrot trained to say "don't start a war."
- **Explainability:** research is funded to explain refusals, almost never favourable decisions.

**Key numbers:** four parts and twenty circuits (Ashby).

**So what:** Attribute systemic outputs to the system by name. Expect accountability to fall as organisations grow. Distrust confidence in proportion to how much you know about a subset of a complex system. Treat "we need explainability" as a claim about who is allowed to see inside. Apply the AI-ethics agenda to corporations, which crossed the black-box threshold centuries ago.

## Intermission: Computing Ponds and Rabbit Holes
**Argument:** The cyberneticians kept trying to build computation from living or physical "fabric" (ponds, petri dishes, compass needles) rather than algorithms written in advance. The appeal is a system with no comprehensible logic that nonetheless gives good answers, which is in direct tension with explainability.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Beer's computing pond:** a tank of algae, larvae and Daphnia, with iron filings ingested by the Daphnia so they would respond to a magnetic field. Initial conditions were meant to correspond to a computing problem and the pond's evolution to represent the solution; neither correspondence was specified. It failed because the water filled with tiny permanent magnets, collapsing any developing organisation.
- **Beer and Gordon Pask's ferrous sulphate petri dish**, "tuned" with feedback to discriminate frequencies.
- **Electronic turtles/tortoises** that drive toward or flee light, showing complex behaviour from simple connections.
- **The conjurer and the rabbit:** explanation demystifies.

**Key numbers:** None in the notes.

**So what:** Apply the pencil-and-paper test to impressive AI. Expect explainability and surprise to trade off. A substrate's own dynamics are a failure mode. Opaque methods are justified only when they do what conventional methods cannot.

## Ch 4. How to Psychoanalyse a Non-human Intelligence
**Argument:** Diagnose an organisation by checking whether its regulators have as much variety as what they regulate. Ashby's law of requisite variety is load-bearing: a system achieves stability only if every source of environmental variability is matched by equal or greater variety in the regulator.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The squirrel survival facility:** squirrel-plus-cage as a black box (weight, body temperature, heart rate, ambient temperature, light/dark), a survival set, a regulator. Lighting is a one-bit switch; temperature is matched by a continuous heater or attenuated into "too hot/too cold" (a thermostat); a hundred cages need thermostats (amplification); staff and husbandry policies attenuate. "That is a squirrel farm, and it is also an organisation."
- **Ashby's homeostat:** four compass needles acting as voltmeters; a dragged needle cycles through settings (a veto), a mid-range needle stops. Four inputs of thirty states each give 810,000 combinations, yet it reached stability quickly and recovered when disturbed.
- **Wiener's automated gunsight:** ataxia (too weak, gun trails) and purpose tremor (too strong, oscillation).
- **Vehicle controls test:** train (one handle), car (steering wheel), plane (joystick).
- **Rubik's Cube smashed into 26 sub-cubes** as a failed split of a non-separable problem.
- **Beer's gibe** about hiring the greatest geniuses to memorise the phone book.
- **Wiener vs Shannon on information:** entropy with a negative sign vs unpredictability; British cyberneticians followed Wiener, hence "variety."

**Key numbers:** 810,000 combinations (4 inputs x 30 states) in the homeostat; one-bit lighting switch; twenty-six sub-cubes.

**So what:** Check requisite variety at every regulatory link before blaming people. Fix mismatches by attenuating input, amplifying the regulator through subsystems, or splitting separable problems. Prefer comparison to measurement. Every black-box boundary is an act of attenuation, so say what you decided not to look at. Match observation frequency to the rate variety is generated. The accountability sink is a variety amplifier, "a solution as much as a problem."

## Ch 5. Cybernetics Without Diagrams
**Argument:** Any organisation capable of surviving indefinitely must contain five functions (operations, regulation, integration, intelligence, philosophy), each matched in information-handling capacity to the variety it faces, plus channels fast and legible enough to turn data into action. When a big system fails, find which function is missing, under-resourced or mismatched.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Analogies for the five systems:** musicians, conductor, tour manager, artistic director and Elton John; or soldiers, quartermaster, battlefield commander, reconnaissance and field marshal. Identity as information management: a consistent identity lets you refuse Wagner's Ring cycle for an Elton John tour.
- **The 2008 crisis read cybernetically:** a central bank as a System 1 of government with independence as its resource bargain. The 2000s named the calm ("Great Moderation," "NICE times," "Goldilocks economy") while a debt bubble and house-price spiral built. Forecasting (inflation forecast targeting) was here-and-now extended. Warnings in 2006-07 stayed data; Lehman's bankruptcy was the algedonic squeal. The root failure was System 5, an identity that defined certain information as none of their business.
- **Train driver's emergency brake** as the model for the algedonic channel.
- **System 2/3 test:** System 2 is the management everyone agrees is necessary; System 3 is the one people complain about.

**Key numbers:** Five systems; warnings in 2006-07.

**So what:** Diagnose by function, not org chart. Size System 3 to residual variety. Treat "problem child" units as unrecognised viable systems. Build System 4 around what is not yet relevant. Check channels for bandwidth, speed and translation capacity. Protect red handles. When 3 and 4 are chronically mismatched, look at System 5.

## Intermission: Decerebrate Cats
**Argument:** An organisation that has stopped attending to some kinds of information looks alive but has lost the ability to act purposively; it survives only while its environment stays unchanged, and collapses at the first real shock.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The decerebrate cat:** with its higher brain severed it still walks, rights itself, eats and grooms, but cannot generate its own responses to change and needs sheltering.
- **Beer's university example:** teaching happens, terms start and end, accounts balance, but the capacity to decide what the institution is for has gone. A shock (funding regime change, demand collapse) leaves it with no function to respond.
- **Davies' corollary:** if you see a company actively decerebrating itself, update your resume.

**Key numbers:** None in the notes.

**So what:** Judge health by what an organisation attends to, not whether operations run. Treat disappearing planning and horizon-scanning as an early warning. The crisis is deferred, never avoided.

## Ch 6. Economics and How It Got That Way
**Argument:** Economics is best understood as a variety-engineering technology: a rhetoric and modelling style that attenuates society to price and quantity so it can always give a definite answer. That made it the identity-creating function (System 5) of modern governance and generates predictable blind spots.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Ricardo's England and Portugal:** cloth costs 100 hours in England and 90 in Portugal; wine 120 and 80. Portugal is better at both, but relative prices (cloth worth nine-eighths of a barrel of wine in Portugal, five-sixths in England) make specialising in wine pay. Airtight and non-trivial (Samuelson could name no other proposition in the social sciences that was both). The vice is the leap to claims about real trade, where adding commodities, inputs, brands, firms, investment, strategy or noting that countries import goods similar to those they export lets you generate any conclusion.
- **Competitive-markets model of discrimination:** "shows" it can't survive competition, so competition is treated as a substitute for anti-discrimination law.
- **Electricity example:** the four conditions (commodity good, observable marginal cost curve, independent spot markets, negligible strategic interaction); marginal cost curve as the one concept engineers independently invented (a load balancing table).
- **Four sources of "market as computing fabric":** Smith's invisible hand, Hayek's socialist calculation debate, post-war equilibrium proofs, financial-economics market efficiency.
- **Safety statement:** "This aircraft has a faulty stall sensor which might cause it to crash" is not a statement about expected costs.
- **Economics' vocabulary:** "ad hoc" as an insult; microfoundations, homo economicus, representative individual.

**Key numbers:** 100 and 90 labour-hours (cloth), 120 and 80 (wine); nine-eighths vs five-sixths; four conditions.

**So what:** Read any economic argument as an attenuation device and ask what it licensed you to stop thinking about. Separate tractability assumptions from assumptions that smuggle in the conclusion. Never infer optimality from a market outcome. Route information not expressible as expected cost (safety, red handles) through separate channels. Judge economics on the practical deficiencies of the systems it builds, not on moral failings of economists.

## Ch 7. If You're So Rich, Why Aren't You Smart?
**Argument:** Managers are the System 1 of a structure whose higher functions are supplied by economists, but the channel is broken because economists don't speak accounting, the language of management. Inside firms, financial-reporting numbers built for one purpose get used for decisions they were not designed for, so companies hallucinate.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The skincare factory:** bulk barrels for construction workers and small tubs for the beauty market at ten times the price, from essentially the same cream. Marketing as a period cost flatters the luxury line; fixed packaging cost allocated by direct labour penalises the bulk line. Consultants compare in-house manufacturing (carrying beauty overheads) with a cheap external supplier; manufacturing is sold; marketing and R&D costs don't vanish; two years later consultants explain the profit slump.
- **Chicken efficiency:** a fast-food chain judged managers on the proportion of each bird sold; managers stopped frying an hour before closing, lost sales, and hit 100 per cent.
- **One death, three encodings:** liver failure, mercury poisoning, murder.
- **Einstein reads the phone book:** 1960s firms computerised existing paper bookkeeping instead of rethinking it; "the decision nobody made."
- **Consultants as accountability sinks and "brains for hire":** renting middle-manager expertise costs the same hours at higher rates.
- **The Chandler pattern; Machiavelli's constraint** (a prince not wise cannot be well advised).

**Key numbers:** Ten times the price (beauty tubs); 100 per cent chicken efficiency; one hour before closing.

**So what:** Ask what decision each number was collected for. Allocate a cost to a manager only if stopping the output would avoid it and the manager controls that part of the bargain. Disagreements over allocation are disagreements about the production process. Beat Ricardians with data collected for another purpose or an internal inconsistency. Budget for excess management capacity. "Learn the numbers by looking at the business, never the business by looking at the numbers."

## Intermission: Meanwhile, in Chile
**Argument:** Cybersyn was not computerised central planning but a large-scale management-by-exceptions system built on the viable system model, and it was so badly out of scale to the Chilean economy that it could never have been a fair test.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Cybernet:** several hundred repurposed telex machines (found idle in a government building) carrying production data, more reliable than phoned numbers and leaving a record. During a trucking strike it let the government monitor deliveries and secure essential supplies.
- **Cyberstride:** statistical filter classifying departures as blip, trend change or new normal; the automated descendant of Beer's hand-drawn steelworks charts.
- **Operations Room:** about a dozen managers with wall displays (hand-drawn slides projected; Beer said CRT flicker was distracting).
- **Checo:** economic simulator, planned but never delivered.
- **Interventors** placed in nationalised factories undercut devolved autonomy; Chile nationalised faster than it could manage.
- **The coup:** ended the experiment; it didn't get to fail informatively.
- **Violence as a variety amplifier:** one guard with a gun controls a prison.

**Key numbers:** Several hundred telex machines; about a dozen managers.

**So what:** Build the exception filter before the command centre. Compare channel capacity to environmental variety first. Expect reaction from the regulated. Cheap instrumentation can beat the war room. Design experiments that can fail legibly.

## Ch 8. Enter Friedman
**Argument:** The Friedman doctrine solved a real crisis (corporations at the end of the 1960s had lost any sense of purpose amid an explosion of environmental variety) by supplying a single maximising goal and a personal accountability sink, at the price of denying that the organisation exists as a decision-making system. Debt and the leveraged buyout gave it teeth.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The doctrine's three moves:** look through the corporation to individuals; substitute a "representative shareholder" black box; invite the executive to split into a person and a businessman. Social responsibility is "the moral equivalent of taxation."
- **Jensen and Meckling:** managerial behaviour, agency costs, ownership structure (debt and stock options as the fix).
- **The buyout example:** corporate jets, country club memberships, lavish expenses, obsolete product lines, cosy relationships with sclerotic customers. The firm puts up 10 per cent, borrows 90, buys the company, makes it borrow to repay the acquisition debt. Interest is tax-deductible where dividends are not. Perks stop; risk sits with workers, suppliers, customers and managers; limited liability caps the buyer's downside at 10 per cent.
- **"Fiduciary duty to maximise shareholder value":** cited to Friedman, but courts have never found such a duty; semantic drift from increase to maximise and profits to shareholder value.
- **Efficient market hypothesis as ideology; Economic Value Added** (Joel Stern); private equity's strong System 5.
- **Pre-emptive self-buyout** and definition creep once wasteful targets ran out.
- **Capitalism vs managerialism:** the unremarked class struggle of the 1970s-80s that managers lost.

**Key numbers:** 10 per cent equity / 90 per cent borrowed; downside capped at the initial 10 per cent.

**So what:** Ask what information a single-maximising-end doctrine licenses you to throw away. Read heavy debt as an information problem first. "Fiduciary duty" outside law is exhortation in costume. Look for the personal accountability sink. Structures beat individuals: neither Friedman nor Welch is the villain. Expect definition creep when legitimate targets run out.

## Ch 9. The Morbid Symptoms
**Argument:** Having cut away the middle management and civil-service capacity that absorbed volatility, the corporate and political systems reversed the old bargain and made the working class the shock absorber. When that broke, the only channel left to the population was a one-bit scream, which is what populism is.

**Stories & evidence**
- **The Welch/GE model:** always make the quarterly number; costs are controllable in a quarter, revenues not; layers of middle management go; annual rank-and-yank of the bottom 10 per cent kept for its threat; GE offset a one-off $2bn gain by closing factories and making over 1,000 people redundant; financial services grew until subjective estimates dominated earnings; the mortgage arm "jacked up the volume without controls" and misrepresented loan quality, ending in a $1.5bn Justice Department settlement.
- **Levy, Marx, Kalecki, Keynes, Friedman's natural rate / NAIRU:** unemployment as variety attenuator, giving the Great Moderation.
- **Time inconsistency (Prescott and Kydland):** independent central banks as Odysseus tied to the mast; read cybernetically as accountability channels working "too well."
- **Outsourcing as a one-bit channel:** "going as anticipated" vs "it's stopped working."
- **Case and Deaton's deaths of despair; Marmot's Whitehall studies:** grade predicts mortality independent of income, smoking and family history.
- **Allende's "el pueblo"** as the System 5 box.
- **Pre-crisis blind spot:** only government deficits monitored; private-sector debt unwatched.
- **Elections and polls:** proportional systems carry more information than first-past-the-post; a monthly poll has a shorter lag than an annual one.

**Key numbers:** Bottom 10 per cent (rank and yank); $2bn one-off gain; over 1,000 redundancies; $1.5bn settlement; one bit.

**So what:** Before cutting "redundant" management, ask which boundary it sits at. Ask who absorbs volatility. Build bandwidth for "conditions are changing." Watch what the control system doesn't monitor. "If you consistently demand the impossible, you will inevitably get the unethical." Respond to the fact of the scream, not its content. Make recommendations.

## Ch 10. What Is to Be Done?
**Argument:** Quieten the signal that drowns out all others by making anyone taking control of an operating company guarantee its debts, so corporate decision-making can return to viability rather than maximisation. Stop letting a maximising discipline sit at the top of governance, and build real channels for the decided-upon to signal intolerability.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Elizabeth Warren's eight-part private equity plan:** only one point (guarantee the debts) is needed cybernetically. Limited liability is a resource bargain introduced for US joint-stock corporations in the 1820s, not a principle of natural justice; keep its original purpose for small non-controlling investors.
- **Galbraith's technostructure and Herbert Simon's behavioural theory of the firm:** profits as a constraint, not a goal to be maximised.
- **Brian Eno's 77 Million Paintings:** a purposive non-maximiser; businesses as artists, not paperclip maximisers.
- **Keynes' dentists:** economists as competent technicians.
- **Transformer networks** as variety amplifiers for two-way communication.
- **Beer's household dial** (the original red-handle alert, never built beyond his son's shed prototype) and the Cwarel Isaf meeting with Brian Eno.
- **Oil company example for POSIWID.**
- **Only cybernetic problems are soluble.**

**Key numbers:** 8-part Warren plan, of which 1 point is needed; 1820s.

**So what:** Fix systems by closing the sink, not by writing rules that must out-vary the situations they govern. Set survival conditions, not maximisation targets. Pair every maximiser with a supervisor and a red handle. Demote economics to a competent technical trade. Use AI as a variety amplifier. Accept that robots will make decisions; repair feedback because people are less angry when listened to. "I blame the system" will have to be said literally.
