# The Genius Myth: Key Arguments, Stories & Evidence by Chapter
*Helen Lewis*

Each chapter has: **Argument** (the claim), **Stories & evidence** (what the book uses to support it), **Key numbers**, and **So what** (the lesson). Built from the chapter files in the `lewis-genius-myth` skill, so it reflects that summary of the book, not the book's own wording.

---

## The Big Arc in One Paragraph
Lewis's thesis is to grant acts of genius but deny a class of geniuses. The Introduction and Ch 1-2 show that "genius" is a manufactured status: Shakespeare, Vasari's fables, and the Romantics built the templates. Ch 3-7 follow the attempt to turn genius into a measurable, inherited quantity (Galton, Terman, Burt, Eysenck, Shockley, vos Savant), which led to eugenics, fraud, and sperm banks. Ch 8 lists the distortions that really decide who gets the label. Ch 9-12 take apart the seductive myths: the lone rebel, the licence for monsters, the invisible great wife, and the estates that manage reputations after death. Ch 13-15 use the Beatles and biopics to show luck, co-authors, and the deficit model. Ch 16-18 bring it to Silicon Valley, Edison, and Musk, and Ch 19 explains why the myth persists and prescribes attaching "genius" to works, not persons.

## Introduction: The Genius Myth
**Argument:** "Genius" is not an objective category but a myth-making process: societies anoint exceptional people to display what they value, then grant those "special people" latitude denied to everyone else. Acts of genius are real; a class of geniuses is not.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Had to was:** Greek/Roman *genius* was a visiting spirit or guardian (*daimonion*, *furor divinus*); the Renaissance and the Romantics made it an identity.
- **The Musk referendum:** arguing whether Musk is a genius is really arguing about what society values and tolerates; the right exaggerates, the left denigrates.
- **Berners-Lee vs. Musk test:** the more original inventor is less famous because he doesn't perform the role of genius.
- **Eysenck's tell:** posterity is likelier to call you a genius if you behave oddly, die young or very old, or disregard normal restrictions.
- **Story requirement:** a person becomes a "genius" only when a story is woven around them.

**Key numbers:** none stated.

**So what:** Ask who tells the story and why. The label converts taste into pseudo-fact and eccentricity into entitlement.

## Ch 1. Avonian Willy
**Argument:** Even the most deserving genius needed people who worked to make him one. Reputation is manufactured and monetized.

**Stories & evidence**
- **First Folio:** two actor-friends assembled it seven years after Shakespeare's death; without it eighteen plays (including *Macbeth* and *The Tempest*) vanish.
- **Restoration rewrites (Davenant):** flying witches, a happy-ending *Lear*, ballet in *The Tempest*. Irreverence kept the work alive.
- **Garrick's 1769 Stratford Jubilee:** odes to "Avonian Willy," mulberry-tree relics, no performances of the plays; bardolatry is born. Macklin called Garrick's cult a "golden calf."
- **Biographical vacuum:** a 40-page life written a century later; the vacuum fills with conspiracy (Oxford, Bacon, "a woman") or relic-worship.
- **Estates as brands:** Einstein's "priests" hid his first child until 1986; the university licensing his image has earned about $250M; Picasso's name on a Citroen; "Genius Bar," Genius.com, "Genius" pills.

**Key numbers:** 7 years (Folio after death); 18 plays at risk; 1769; about $250M (Einstein image licensing); 1986.

**So what:** Find the preservers (Heminges and Condell) and marketer (Garrick) in any canon. When the toothpicks outnumber the performances, canonization has become commerce.

## Ch 2. Secular Saints
**Argument:** Vasari's *Lives* turned artist biography into fable and planted templates we still use; the Romantics then added nature, madness, illness, and moral exemption.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Vasari's archetypes:** the art monk (Michelangelo, boots fused to his skin), the scatter-brained polymath (Leonardo), the prodigy who corrects the master, and the arrogant visionary vindicated (Brunelleschi and the egg).
- **Story-fitness selection:** Artemisia Gentileschi's rape-and-revenge legend is remembered; Sofonisba Anguissola, court painter to Philip II who died at 93, is not.
- **Gauss test (Hayes):** the schoolboy story first appears in 1856, gets the arithmetic series in 1938, goes viral with E. T. Bell's gothic version, and drops the whips as readers get squeamish.
- **Romantic additions:** Rousseau's *Emile*; Kant's "nature's elect"; Byron ("we of the craft are all crazy"; the incest scandal absorbed into legend); tuberculosis as the genius's disease; Weininger's suicide in Beethoven's death-room.
- **Walden debate:** Thoreau's laundry; the premise that the genius must return to nature is itself the myth.

**Key numbers:** 1856 and 1938 (Gauss versions).

**So what:** Treat every anecdote as a fable: find the earliest source, what was added, what vanished, and the invariant. Prodigy stories discourage the rest of us as "the less distinguished classmates."

## Ch 3. Galton's Good Breeding
**Argument:** Galton replaced the Romantic lightning strike with genius as rare, innate, inherited, and measurable, founding both modern statistics and eugenics. His method confused reputation with ability and couldn't separate genetics from nepotism.

**Stories & evidence**
- ***Hereditary Genius* (1869):** list eminent men, count eminent relatives, conclude ability is inherited. Eminent: 1 in 4,000; illustrious: 1 in a million.
- **Nepotism confound:** Merivale on the Atkyns judges ("a snug little party of jobbers"). Galton's nephews-vs-sons control was admitted to be beyond him.
- **Environment omitted:** de Candolle's 18 factors (rickets, lead, sex bars, religious exclusion, serial pregnancy, colour bars, independent wealth...).
- **Gauss counter-example:** Galton's statistics depend on Gauss, whose family gave no hint of genius; Gauss praised Sophie Germain's "superior genius" for overcoming obstacles.
- **Carlyle's Great Man theory:** history as the biography of great men, written defensively against an age that "accounts for" heroes.
- **Havelock Ellis and the Austen Problem:** cut short entries from a biographical dictionary and you lose Jane Austen.
- ***Kantsaywhere*:** Galton's utopia of breeding licences and labour colonies; Pearson and Fisher held the Galton chair, with buildings renamed in 2020.

**Key numbers:** 1 in 4,000 eminent; 1 in a million illustrious; 95% "mediocre"; 18 factors.

**So what:** Before accepting "talent runs in families," control for nepotism and access. Check whether your selection method rewards drama over quality.

## Ch 4. Terman's Termites
**Argument:** IQ testing was invented to help struggling children and hijacked to rank humanity. Terman's study of high-IQ children produced eminent but not illustrious adults, rejected two future Nobelists, and showed childhood tests can't find genius, though the label bent the children's lives.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Binet vs. the single number:** Binet feared "brutal pessimism"; Simon called Stern's single mental age a betrayal.
- **Flynn Effect:** a 1900s child says "dogs hunt rabbits," a modern one "both are mammals."
- **Goddard:** moron/imbecile/idiot bands; the debunked Kallikak study; legacy in the 1924 Immigration Act, *Buck v. Bell* (1927), and Nazi sterilization laws.
- **Terman's study (1921-):** healthier than average, 90% college-bound, 1,500 papers, 100+ patents, no genius. Terman's 1947 admission: no Shakespeare or Newton since Jamestown.
- **Shockley and Alvarez** both rejected as boys, both later won physics Nobels.
- **Thumb on the scale:** teacher nominations skewed to confident, eloquent boys; poor and minority schools under-sampled; "royal jelly" label.
- **Cox Miles' ruler method:** IQs for the dead from encyclopedia entry length (Goethe 210, Newton 190, Cervantes and Copernicus 105, Shakespeare absent).
- **Worked cases:** Jess Oppenheimer (*I Love Lucy*) logged as conceited with no sense of humour; "Sara Ann," a physics graduate who never worked as one.

**Key numbers:** about 1,500 children; IQ 140 then 135; about 3.3 points per decade (Flynn); 1921; 90% college-bound; 100+ patents.

**So what:** Precocity is not adult originality. "Gifted" predicts comfortable eminence, not breakthrough, and labelling a child is an intervention.

## Ch 5. Cyril and Hans: Twin Flames
**Argument:** Burt and his pupil Eysenck produced falsified or "unsafe" data in service of hereditarian views and were protected for decades by the mythology that a genius may bend the rules.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Too-perfect correlation:** Burt's twin correlations (0.944 together, 0.771 apart) unchanged to three decimals while his sample grew from 21 to 53 pairs.
- **Phantom collaborators:** "Miss Howard" and "Miss Conway" published only in Burt's own journal while he edited it; over half its forty contributors appear to be Burt pseudonyms.
- **Genius exemption:** Eysenck's defense that geniuses may fudge figures; if wrong, he wasn't a genius. Hearnshaw's "justifiable prerogative of a great man" reappears as Corr's "Sometimes you need a pariah."
- **Eysenck's ecosystem:** tobacco funding (GBP 800,000), cancer-prone personality, astrology, gout as a genius correlate (rich middle-aged men both eat richly and have careers).
- **Pelosi's 30-year pursuit:** 1992 *BMJ* article softened; a 2019 open letter listing 61 papers; King's College judged 26 co-authored papers "unsafe."
- **Eleven-plus:** Burt's political legacy of selection that stratifies by class.
- **Harden's middle ground:** intelligence is partly heritable; ranking worth by it is the error.

**Key numbers:** 0.944 and 0.771; 21 to 53 pairs; twenty coincidences; GBP 800,000; 61 papers; 26 papers; 30 years.

**So what:** Unchanging decimals are fingerprints of fraud. Spot the defender's script, not just the man. Institutions protect stars; correction lags stature by decades and comes from outsiders.

## Ch 6. William Shockley and the Genius Sperm Bank
**Argument:** Rejected from Terman's study, Shockley won a Nobel through scenius, then believed his own genius label, strayed into eugenics as an omni-expert, alienated everyone, and ended as a footnote.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Scenius:** Paul Graham's Leonardo and Florence line; Milan, 300 km away, made no Leonardo. Shockley's Florences were California, Caltech, and Bell Labs (nine Nobels, monopoly-funded freedom).
- **Insulation by identity:** pulled over unshaven with a loaded gun, he got the gun back.
- **Credit grab:** Brattain and Bardeen built the point-contact transistor; Shockley, excluded from the patent, threatened revolt and became "the father of the transistor." Bardeen left and won a second Nobel.
- **Management:** "climbing by moonlight and unroped," lie-detector tests, then the Traitorous Eight left, leading to Fairchild and Intel.
- **Omni-expertise cascade:** a physicist at a genetics conference; "1 IQ point per 1% Caucasian ancestry"; moral condemnation lets him pose as a persecuted empiricist.
- **Persecuted becomes persecutor:** his own scores were 129 and 125 (mother 161), yet he proposed $1,000 per IQ point below 100 as a sterilization bonus.
- **Sperm bank (Repository for Germinal Choice):** Shockley the only laureate to admit donating, at about 70; no laureate donations produced a child; Doron Blake (IQ 180) concluded you can't breed for good people.
- **Syl Jones interview and *Playboy* 1980:** a self-image that blocks self-correction.

**Key numbers:** nine Nobels at Bell Labs; IQ 129 and 125; $1,000 per IQ point; 69th percentile (sciences) and 45th (quadratics) at college entry; Blake's IQ 180.

**So what:** Achievement is talent x place x time x identity; the myth reports only the first. Refute cranks as wrong, not evil.

## Ch 7. Marilyn and Me
**Argument:** A childhood test score sat unnoticed for thirty years; once printed, an obscure woman became "the smartest person in the world." IQ can't identify genius, but the label can manufacture one, and high-IQ societies show a number doesn't make you right or able to run a meeting.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Marilyn vos Savant:** 178th of 613 in her high-school class, married at 16, divorced twice; after the 1985 *Guinness* entry came "Ask Marilyn" and public omni-opinions. Her score itself is contested (228 vs. the school board's ceiling of 167).
- **Galaxy-brainedness:** Jarvik calling relativity, the Big Bang, and black holes wrong; high-IQ polls on foreign policy were useless.
- **Mensa and others:** a new "aristocracy of brains" (fireplace tiles, yellow map pins); randomly assigned volunteer roles collapsed; Mega, Triple Nine, Cincinnatus, and Prometheus fought schisms and lawsuits; members' own preferred label was "Outsider."
- **Towers' three groups:** well-adjusted, marginal, and dropouts ("the psychological walking wounded").
- **Lewis sits the Mensa test:** it rewards speed, dated assumptions, and arguable synonyms.
- **Langan's Koko post:** IQ used to compare Somalis to gorillas.
- **Mega Society log:** credentialism by number resolved by fiat.
- **Guinness retires the category (1990);** GWAS shows even eye colour is polygenic.
- **Hawking:** "People who boast about their IQ are losers."

**Key numbers:** 178th of 613; scores of 228 vs. 167; 1985; 1990; 30 years.

**So what:** The label is a lens that creates its own object. Count the schisms as the empirical refutation of "high IQ means works of genius."

## Ch 8. So You Want to Be a Genius?
**Argument:** The label is distorted by the Potus problem, multiples, the adjacent possible, the Matthew Effect, a taste for weirdness, hedgehogs, and white-elephant art, and plain luck.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Eysenck's list flipped:** male, lose a parent before 10, born in February, die at 30 or 90 but never 60: features picked from biographies afterward.
- **Potus problem:** *The West Wing*; Shakespeare's complexity now baseline; zero once took centuries.
- **Multiples (Ogburn & Thomas, 1922):** telephone, natural selection, calculus, aeroplane, logarithms, Neptune; credit goes to the one who claimed harder (Newton smearing Leibniz; Wallace naming his book *Darwinism*).
- **Adjacent possible:** printing press (moveable type + paper + ink + wine press); YouTube (cameras + broadband); Babbage ahead of time; Edison as manufacturer.
- **Timing:** even Einstein and Picasso lost contact with the implications of their own revolution.
- **Matthew Effect and forty-first seat:** Descartes, Pascal, Molière, Rousseau, Diderot, Flaubert, Zola, Proust; science advances one funeral at a time.
- **Be weird:** Franklin's belief in transmigration; ninety-nine insane ideas and one gravity.
- **Hedgehog vs. fox (Berlin):** Tetlock's foxes forecast better.
- **White elephant vs. termite art (Farber):** *Ulysses*, awards campaigns.
- **Luck (Fleming):** brother, water polo, rifle club; Dubos: "every decision is like a murder."

**Key numbers:** 148 simultaneous inventions; 1922; eight missed Académie names.

**So what:** Run the six-step distortion audit: Potus, multiples, adjacent possible, Matthew, weirdness, luck. What survives is the act; what falls away was the myth.

## Ch 9. The Rebel
**Argument:** The lone heretic vindicated against the establishment flatters contrarians from scepticism into conspiracism: "if everyone says I'm wrong, I must be right." For every Newton of mathematics there's a Newton of alchemy.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Galileo gambit:** ivermectin "truthers" (Heying, Weinstein), Mark Rylance on garlic and chemo, Freeman Dyson on climate.
- **They didn't all laugh:** Edison's phonograph was an instant hit; Columbus didn't prove the earth round; Semmelweis (handwashing, died in an asylum) is the exception that makes the myth.
- **Impressionists:** Leroy's "wallpaper in its early stages"; *Impression, Sunrise* sold for 800 francs.
- **Galileo unmythed:** "*Eppur si muove*" first appears in 1757; he sought papal permission, died in his bed, and was a superb lens-maker (3x to 8x to 15x to 20x); Kepler borrowed his telescope.
- **Dyson's *Scientist as Rebel*:** exemplar Thomas Gold (right about the ear, wrong about moon dust and abiogenic oil); phlogiston, mesmerism, phrenology, polywater.
- **Ivermectin timeline:** early scepticism reasonable, then Merck says no evidence and *Nature* reports the evidence evaporating; contrarians whose identity is heterodoxy can't update, and speculation spirals to vaccine-Nuremberg rhetoric.
- **Theft as fame-maker:** *Impression, Sunrise* (1985), *Mona Lisa* (1911).

**Key numbers:** 1757; 800 francs; magnification 3x, 8x, 15x, 20x.

**So what:** Opposition is not evidence. Source "they laughed at him," set an update trigger before the evidence arrives, and remember rebels have power and consequences.

## Ch 10. Monsters and Tortured Artists
**Argument:** The tortured-genius aura works as a licence that stifles doubt, at any scale. Orwell's rule stands: being an artist is no excuse for abuse.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Orwell on Dalí:** benefit of clergy; neither denounce the cad nor defend the untouchable genius. If Shakespeare's hobby were raping children, we would not tell him to carry on for another *King Lear*.
- **Chris Goode mechanism:** a cultivated sensitive-outsider persona; work that eroticized adult-child relationships; an all-male, largely unpaid ensemble recruited at 18 to early thirties with nudity and sexual contact written into the advert; "check-ins" as consent theatre; critics fearing the *Mary Whitehouse* trap; queerness and left politics as shield. Arrested for child-abuse images, died by suicide on bail, then erased from the internet.
- **Smuggled argument:** calling him a genius told viewers they were cool enough to see the deeper message.
- **Weinstein and Dederer's question:** "What do we do with the art of monstrous men?" Polanski: Oscars, "not rape rape," "philistine collusion," until MeToo.
- **Stephens:** contradiction, not causality; "all his plays were suicide notes" sentimentalizes addiction and abuse; "dividuals."

**Key numbers:** ensemble aged 18 to early 30s (no other figures).

**So what:** Apply the propaganda test. Prefer friends to fans, who can hold contradiction. Reckon; don't erase.

## Ch 11. Great Wives
**Argument:** Behind most acknowledged geniuses stands a wife who feeds, copies, edits, manages, shields, and mirrors him, and whose sacrifice is written out or recast as hindrance.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Tolstoy and Sofia:** married at 18 to a 34-year-old, sixteen pregnancies, copied *War and Peace* seven times, supplied material (her grief for a dead son became Anna's "eternal fear"); he wrote a play condemning wet nurses when she couldn't breastfeed; "a genius, and a parasite."
- **Reader thesis (Wilson):** the marriage broke when the disciple Chertkov (2,000 letters, secretary-copyists, a secret will) replaced her as reader. She was admitted only after he lost consciousness; her memoir was suppressed for eight decades.
- **Wife Glitch (Schaffer):** anticipates needs, fills weaknesses, "and means it." Institutions can substitute (nunneries for Hildegard, a Cambridge college for Newton, a Tokyo hospital for Kusama).
- **Wife as mirror:** Gertrude Stein's *The Autobiography of Alice B. Toklas*.
- **Vera Nabokov:** agent, translator, secretary, driver, substitute lecturer, pistol in handbag after *Lolita*.
- **#thanksfortyping (Holsinger):** thanks for typing whole manuscripts five times while nursing and teaching; an economist thanking his professor-wife for "managing the entire infrastructure of our lives."
- **Einstein's contract for Mileva Marić:** laundry, three meals in his room, no personal relations; later replaced and written out by the "Einstein priests."
- **Adam Smith's dinner:** cooked by his mother, then a cousin. *New Yorker* cartoon: "significantly less impressive once you realise these guys had free child care."

**Key numbers:** 16 pregnancies; 7 copies of *War and Peace*; 2,000 letters; 8 decades of suppression; age 18 vs. 34.

**So what:** Ask "who typed it?" and "who cooked the dinner?" The male dominance of "genius" partly reflects who gets a wife. Ask what you owe those harmed in the production of the work you admire.

## Ch 12. Keepers of the Flame
**Argument:** Legacies are made and unmade by keepers (widows, heirs, executors, friends, magazines) who select, suppress, sanitize, price, and package.

**Stories & evidence**
- ***Life* and the Irascibles:** the 1949 headline and 1951 group portrait created abstract expressionism as a movement, excluding Krasner; Hedda Sterne regretted being the one woman included.
- **Lee Krasner:** painted small in a bedroom while Pollock had the barn; after his 1956 crash took the barn and painted seventeen-foot canvases; as widow sold one or two Pollocks a year and forced up prices.
- **Sonia Orwell:** fourteen weeks a wife, thirty years a widow running the estate.
- **Jo van Gogh-Bonger:** widowed at about 29 with a baby, 200+ paintings in a boarding house in Bussum; paired art and letters, won over a critic who first called it "nearly vulgar," staged a 484-work show in 1905, bought back *Starry Night* ("uncomfortable is good"); diary withheld until 2009; descendants on the museum board.
- **Hamilton's guardians:** Donne's son, Marvell's "widow," Pope's feud with a pirate publisher, Boswell for Johnson, Burns sanitized (a revisionist biographer got a bullet in the post).
- **Copyright as control:** the Beckett estate forbids women in male roles; le Carré's womanizing was published eight years later.
- **Reversals:** Pollock "Stanley Kowalski with a paintbrush"; Krasner now the critical darling (Whitechapel, Whitney, MoMA, Barbican), but the market lags.
- **Grocer story:** *Life* said the grocer "bought" a Pollock; he took it against unpaid debts.

**Key numbers:** $2M (*Blue Poles*, 1973); $140M (*No. 5*); Pollock $200M (2015) vs. Krasner record $11M (2019); 484-work show (1905); 14 weeks and 30 years (Sonia Orwell); 2009.

**So what:** Use the Jo checklist for any estate. Ask who holds the papers, who sits on the board, and who was cropped from the group photo.

## Ch 13. A Cover Version
**Argument:** Two films from the same premise (a man is the only one who remembers the Beatles) give opposite answers: *Yesterday* says talent will out; *Cover Version* says talent isn't enough without persona, era, manager, producer, and luck. Each author wrote the theory of success his own life taught.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Counterfactual test:** In *Cover Version*, "In My Life" from an unknown twentysomething doesn't land; the protagonist becomes Townes Van Zandt (died at 52).
- **"Star Wars wouldn't sell today"** (Barth).
- **Auteur fetish:** "One Man Only" campaign; Ed Sheeran's real co-writers; 60,000 songs a day.
- **Omitted Fifth and Sixth Beatles:** Martin and Epstein.
- **The authors' lives:** Curtis (Harrow, Oxford, *Blackadder*, *Four Weddings*, "never been knocked out") vs. Barth (twenty-five unproduced scripts, one *Simpsons* episode).
- **Credit:** Curtis says he never read the script; Barth got a shared "Story by" credit and a share of net profit that "lost money" by Hollywood accounting; Alan Smithee.
- **Age:** at thirty-two "you are no longer a person who *could be*."

**Key numbers:** 25 unproduced scripts; 60,000 songs a day; 12 notes; age 52 (Van Zandt); age 32.

**So what:** Value a hit as work x persona x moment x sponsor x luck. The more comforting story outsells the truer one.

## Ch 14. Alchemy
**Argument:** The Beatles became *the* band through near-misses, collaborators, a demographic and media moment, and the right lifecycle: one member to die young and one to live long. Genius is a story with a beginning, middle, and end.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Near-miss biography (Craig Brown):** bombers on one night and not the next; Ringo's peritonitis; Paul failing Spanish; conscription dodged; Epstein arriving as they considered quitting; Sutcliffe staying in Hamburg; Ringo's emigration forms.
- **Origin smoothing:** John "barely noticed" Paul (1967) vs. "immediately impressed" (1983).
- **Losing it (Brown):** John and Paul "lost it" too; Barth: old bands "don't look right."
- **Decca reconsidered:** mostly covers plus three forgettable originals; Martin heard potential. *Please Please Me* was raw and sub-three-minutes; Little Richard was "way ahead."
- **Adjacent possible:** television, Little Richard, post-war stability, George Martin (classically trained, Goons producer), Boomer bulge, charts, *Ed Sullivan*.
- **Age curves:** Beard (peak by 40); Simonton (steep rise, gentle decline); Darwin at 50, Copernicus at 70, Disraeli PM at 64. Galenson's conceptual (Picasso) vs. experimental (Cezanne).
- **Perspiration:** Gladwell's Hamburg hours (the "10,000" spuriously precise); *Get Back* shows songs emerging from aimless jamming.
- **When to die (Simonton):** Mozart young, Goethe very old, never sixty; the Beatles ended at their peak (George was 27); Lennon murdered 1980; McCartney as patriarch and catalogue manager (Glastonbury at 80).

**Key numbers:** George was 27; peak by 40; "never sixty"; 1980; Glastonbury at 80.

**So what:** Count the coin flips. Early rejection isn't proof of hidden genius. Don't rank the dead partner above the living one, because death mythologizes.

## Ch 15. The Deficit Model
**Argument:** Genius is said to extort a price, with oddness as currency. Biopics are today's Vasari: inspiration as flash, genius as unworldly, suffering as payment, which feels just, sells tickets, and warps real lives.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Examples of deficit:** Beethoven deaf, Milton blind, Mozart a child who couldn't handle adulthood, Picasso needing women's "blood."
- **Nash (*A Beautiful Mind*):** the bar-scene "vision"; the spy plot as delusion; Nash said supernatural ideas came "the same way" as mathematical ones.
- **Hawking (*The Theory of Everything*):** skates over marriages and abuse allegations; best work came before deterioration; Penrose, equally impressive and later a Nobel laureate, stays obscure; Wilczek: idolization "corrosive."
- **Turing:** already a Cambridge Fellow at Bletchley, but the film makes him friendless.
- **Oppenheimer:** a rare attempt at genius as organizational.
- ***The Laws of Madness* (Laudor):** schizophrenia, Yale Law Review editor, a 1995 *NYT* profile, a $600K+ memoir deal and $1.5M film rights (Ron Howard; Brad Pitt); off medication he killed his girlfriend; *A Beautiful Mind* was released three years later.
- **Nobel's rule of three** vs. CERN's hundreds.

**Key numbers:** $600K+ memoir; $1.5M film rights; 1995; three years later; Nobel cap of 3.

**So what:** Use the Hawking test: suffering that follows the work is narrative, not cause. Intelligence does not save you; the unmade shadow film is the corrective.

## Ch 16. Disruptors
**Argument:** Silicon Valley is a site of scenius built by public institutions yet mythologized as rugged individualism and "disruption." The 2020s turned from wonder to cynicism.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Scenius conditions:** no non-competes in California, Stanford, DoD funding (GPS via Naval Research; the internet via DARPA), post-war wealth attracting scientists.
- **Disruption (Christensen):** cheap rivals dislodge bloated incumbents; reality includes lobbying, weak antitrust, and Facebook buying thirty companies in two years (Instagram, WhatsApp).
- **Genius asshole:** Jobs the "Genius Entrepreneur," Wozniak the "Genius Engineer"; Wozniak: a nicer Apple "may never have made the Macintosh." Boy-kings: daughters named after Roman emperors, laser eye surgery, guns, gold, and potassium iodide.
- **Cynical turn:** Swisher's *Burn Book*; Gladwell mocked in 2010; crypto as con (SBF), NFTs, social networks, generative AI.
- **Geopolitics:** Musk, Altman, and Thiel as arguments that America still leads, as Shakespeare argued for England. Menlo Park, New Jersey gave its name to Menlo Park, California.

**Key numbers:** thirty companies in two years (Facebook).

**So what:** Every "self-made" founder stands on DARPA, Stanford, and a tax code. Ask "who bought whom?" Both wonder and cynicism are myth responses.

## Ch 17. Thomas Edison: The Lightbulb Moment
**Argument:** At Menlo Park the Romantic genius gave way to the modern workaholic. Edison's real conceptual breakthrough was the phonograph; the lightbulb was incremental and team-built; the grid was underrated; and the myth was co-produced by reporters with shares in his company.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Phonograph (1877):** an accidental byproduct of hunting telephone diaphragms; the lab ignored it for six months; the "flash" was a midnight-dinner collaboration. *Scientific American* demo ("How do you do?").
- **Myth revisions:** deafness as superpower; schooling argument; "Tom" Edison (he was "Al").
- **Access-for-hype machine:** a *Herald* writer received eight shares in Edison Electric; filament explorers (one claimed 98 days in the same clothes).
- **Lightbulb de-mythed:** Davy (1802-09), Jobard (1838), Swan (1860); Sprengel pump; Batchelor's carbonised thread (40 hours); platinum "for ever" filaments burned out in minutes; lost the current war to Westinghouse; company becomes GE.
- **Had it, then didn't:** ore-milling, litigation, a movie camera actually an employee's, the lab fire, the electric-car battery killed by the Model T; couldn't grasp radio.
- **Sage phase:** opinions on women, Turks, the soul; the "Edison Test" (Einstein reportedly flunked it); his name invoked against the New Deal.
- **Selfish genius:** Tesla left after a reneged bet; neglected first wife; a failson lending the name to the "Magno-Electric Vitalizer."
- **Latimer footnote:** one of the few Black engineers hired, for patent-fight opposition research.

**Key numbers:** 1877; six months ignored; 40 hours (Batchelor's filament); 8 shares; 98 days.

**So what:** Credit the manufacturing and the grid; use the reporter-with-shares test (who profits from calling this person a genius?). Early-peaking conceptual innovators calcify as the myth grows.

## Ch 18. Elon Musk: Extremely Hardcore
**Argument:** Musk shows acts of genius (reusable rockets, making EVs desirable) and has succumbed to being a special person. Genius is not transferable: SpaceX was physics, Tesla manufacturing, Twitter social and psychological.

**Stories & evidence**

## Ch 19. Conclusion: An Idea That Won't Stay Dead
**Argument:** We used to have God, now we have geniuses: an ineffable quality like sanctity that every era tries and fails to pin down. The myth survives because it makes better stories and does social work. The prescription is to return "genius" to acts and works and nurture the many seeds exclusion has wasted.

**Stories & evidence**
- **Secularization arc (Fara):** possession, then secular saint, then Victorian number, then granular data.
- **Four functions:** space for the weird, envy management, reconciliation with monstrosity, admiration for the one who gets away with it (Picasso as pure id, Trump as analogue, Larkin's shit in the shuttered chateau).
- **Picasso reversed:** revered because of his selfishness; he made himself the sun.
- **Two wastes (Gould, Spencer):** scorched by indulgence; far more extinguished by lack of support. "Before he can remake his society, his society must make him."
- **Why Great Man theory won't die:** better stories; Vasari's readers wanted gossip; Nobel's cap of three vs. CERN's hundreds.
- **AI and the artist:** LLMs are prediction machines; Baldwin's connection across time; "substitute ChatGPT for Dostoevsky and the magic is gone."
- **Impossible standards:** private lives are fully accessible, yet the archetypes stagger on.

**Key numbers:** none stated beyond Nobel's three.

**So what:** Attach "genius" to the painting, the transistor, *War and Peace*. Don't debunk into nihilism; replace the myth's functions. "There are many seeds of genius in the world. We must nurture as many as we can."
